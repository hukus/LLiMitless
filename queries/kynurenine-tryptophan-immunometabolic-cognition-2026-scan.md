# Kynurenine / Tryptophan Immunometabolic Cognition - 2026 Scan

This branch belongs in the immunometabolism and glutamate/NMDA comparator layer, not in any tryptophan-supplement promotion lane. The 2025 dementia meta-analysis and the older AD meta-analyses show that kynurenine-pathway biology is altered in AD, but the direction is mixed across compartments and the cognitive associations are often inconclusive. Schizophrenia data are more mechanistically coherent: higher kynurenic acid can track worse cognition or worse CBF response to tryptophan challenge, and a 2025 patient-derived model now links KYNA to microglia-mediated synapse engulfment, which makes the branch useful for state-restoration hypotheses and target-validity checks, not for general enhancement claims.^[raw/articles/springer-2025-kynurenine-pathway-dysregulation-cognitive-impairment-dementia.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-36754068-kynurenine-pathway-alzheimers-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/springer-2023-kynurenine-pathway-schizophrenia-cognition-review.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-37118058-tryptophan-challenge-schizophrenia-cognition-cbf.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40165559-kynurenic-acid-synapse-elimination-schizophrenia.md]

The main practical update since 2024 is that the pathway now has a cleaner split between biomarker association and intervention tests. A completed schizophrenia crossover challenge study (`TrypNAC-II`) is explicitly trying to block kynurenine-to-kynurenic-acid conversion with high-dose NAC plus tryptophan, and the registry now shows results posted. A first-in-human KMO inhibitor study (`GSK3335065`) showed pathway engagement in healthy volunteers but was stopped after a serious ventricular tachycardia event, which keeps the drug-development lane safety-first even before cognition enters the picture. The 2014 KAT II review, the 2016 KMO review, and the 2024 inflammatory-disorder target review all reinforce that the enzyme story is context-sensitive rather than a simple "lower KYNA everywhere" rule.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct04013555-kat-ii-schizophrenia-trypnac-ii.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-24562494-targeting-kat-ii-psychiatric-diseases-review.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-26589832-kynurenine-3-monooxygenase-review-structure-mechanism-inhibitors.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39120289-kynurenines-target-inflammatory-disorders-review.md]

Standalone entity pages now split the branch into [[kynurenine]], [[kynurenic-acid]], [[quinolinic-acid]], [[kat-ii-inhibitors]], [[gsk3335065-kmo-inhibitor]], and [[ido-tdo-kynurenine-modulation]]. The 2021 quinolinic-acid schizophrenia study gives the QA node disease-biomarker support, while the 2026 gut-brain-axis review adds microbiome / indole / enzyme-flux context without turning the branch into a direct cognition treatment.^[raw/articles/pmc-8113521-quinolinic-acid-cognitive-deficits-schizophrenia.md]^[raw/articles/springer-2026-tryptophan-metabolism-gut-brain-signaling-review.md]

What changed since 2024:

- The 2025 dementia meta-analysis weakened the older "quinolinic acid is simply high in AD" storyline: quinolinic acid was lower in AD CSF in the pooled analysis, and cognitive correlations were mostly non-significant.
- The AD literature now looks more compartment-specific than pathway-wide: peripheral and CSF directions can diverge, and plasma/serum are not interchangeable.
- Schizophrenia remains the cleanest cognition-linked phenotype for the branch, especially around KYNA, memory, and CBF.
- A 2025 schizophrenia mechanistic paper now adds microglia-mediated synapse elimination to the KYNA story, which makes KAT-side inhibition more plausible as a schizophrenia comparator than as a general enhancer.
- A dedicated quinolinic-acid schizophrenia biomarker source now anchors the QA page, but its cross-sectional design keeps causality unresolved.
- A 2026 gut-brain-axis review adds microbiome, indole, IDO/TDO, KAT, and KMO context while staying translational rather than cognition-interventional.
- The target-review layer is more explicit now: KAT II, KMO, and broader KP inflammatory reviews all say the relevant enzyme direction can flip by disease context.
- The 2025 D-amino acid oxidase inhibitor meta-analysis adds a separate but adjacent comparator signal: modest pooled schizophrenia symptom and cognitive benefit, but enough heterogeneity that sodium benzoate and luvadaxistat still need compound-level interpretation.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40328785-daao-inhibitors-schizophrenia-meta-analysis.md]
- The pathway is best treated as a comparator for inflammation-linked state deterioration, not as a direct supplement target.

## Evidence Tiering

| Layer | What it says | Branch interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Systematic review / meta-analysis | 2025 cognitive impairment / dementia meta-analysis found lower CSF tryptophan, 3-HK, AA, and QA in AD dementia, higher CSF KYNA, lower blood TRP, KYNA, XA, and 3-HAA, and largely inconclusive cognitive correlations. | The pathway is altered, but the "more QA equals more dementia cognition loss" story is not stable enough to rank as a simple biomarker. |
| Systematic review / meta-analysis | 2023 AD meta-analysis found lower peripheral TRP, higher peripheral KYN/TRP, higher central KA/KYN, and no consistent QA change. | AD looks like a compartment-specific TRYCAT shift, not a uniform toxic load increase. |
| Systematic review | 2023 schizophrenia cognition review concluded the pathway affects cognition in schizophrenia, but the evidence remains limited. | Use as a target-validity branch, not as proof of a treatment. |
| Human challenge RCT | TRP challenge in schizophrenia / healthy controls robustly raises KYN and KYNA and can shift CBF, with only a trend toward visuospatial memory worsening in healthy controls. | Good mechanistic stress test for KYNA-sensitive circuitry; not a broad nootropic result. |
| Prospective cohort | Post-stroke plasma kynurenines were not associated with PSCI odds, but AA, KA, and KA/QA tracked some domain-specific cognitive measures. | The direction is phenotype- and domain-specific rather than global. |
| Human first-in-human drug study | GSK3335065 partially inhibited KMO in healthy volunteers, but the program stopped after a serious ventricular tachycardia event. | Important for target engagement and safety, not for cognition efficacy. |
| Human meta-analysis | The 2025 DAAO inhibitor meta-analysis in schizophrenia reported modest symptom and cognitive benefit, but the class was heterogeneous and likely compound-specific. | Keep sodium benzoate and luvadaxistat in the comparator lane rather than as a single uniform branch. |
| Target review / mechanistic review | KAT II and KMO reviews, plus the 2024 KP inflammatory-disorder review, frame the pathway as context-sensitive: KAT II inhibition can lower KYNA and raise transmitter tone in preclinical models, while KMO remains a druggable branch-point enzyme in neurodegeneration and inflammation. | Useful for target positioning, but still not human cognition efficacy. |
| Human patient-derived mechanistic study | A 2025 schizophrenia study found KYNA promotes microglia-mediated synapse engulfment and linked KYNA-producing KATs to schizophrenia risk and synaptic genes. | Strengthens the case that elevated KYNA can be cognitively harmful in schizophrenia, but it is still non-interventional. |
| Human clinical biomarker study | A 2021 schizophrenia / MDD / healthy-control study found plasma quinolinic acid correlated negatively with composite cognition in schizophrenia but not MDD. | Supports [[quinolinic-acid]] as a disease-biomarker node, not a causal intervention target. |
| Gut-brain-axis review | The 2026 review frames tryptophan metabolism through kynurenine, indole, and serotonin routes, with IDO/TDO/KAT/KMO and microbiome control points. | Adds source depth for [[ido-tdo-kynurenine-modulation]] and gut-microbiome context, but no cognition endpoint. |
| Interventional registry | TrypNAC-II is the most direct human test of KAT II inhibition logic in schizophrenia, with cognitive and MRI endpoints and posted registry results. | Keep it in the watchlist until a publication confirms the direction of effect. |

## Effect Size Snapshot

- 2025 dementia meta-analysis:
  - CSF tryptophan SMD `-0.26`
  - CSF 3-hydroxykynurenine SMD `-0.21`
  - CSF anthranilic acid SMD `-0.28`
  - CSF quinolinic acid SMD `-0.38`
  - CSF kynurenic acid SMD `+0.18`
  - Blood tryptophan SMD `-0.39`
  - Blood kynurenic acid SMD `-0.31`
  - Blood xanthurenic acid SMD `-0.34`
  - Blood 3-hydroxyanthranilic acid SMD `-0.42`
- 2023 AD meta-analysis:
  - Peripheral KYN/TRP ratio SMD `+0.216`
  - Peripheral TRP SMD `-0.520`
  - Central KA/KYN ratio increased
  - Peripheral KA decreased
  - Quinolinic acid: no consistent change
- 2023 tryptophan challenge in schizophrenia / controls:
  - 6 g TRP caused >20-fold KYN increase and >130-fold KYNA increase by 240 min
  - Cognitive performance was mostly unchanged, with only a trend toward worse visuospatial memory in healthy controls
- 2023 post-stroke prospective study:
  - AA better episodic memory at baseline
  - KA/QA ratio associated with episodic memory
  - KA associated with processing speed in women only

## Safety / Adverse Events

- The biggest practical safety issue is not the biomarker literature; it is the drug-development and self-experimentation lane.
- GSK3335065 was stopped after a serious ventricular tachycardia event in one volunteer, even though causality remained confounded.
- NAC and tryptophan challenge work should not be read as a DIY recommendation: the protocol was designed to test pathway biology under supervision, not to establish a safe general-use regimen.
- For any future KMO or KAT II program, the safety profile should stay tied to cardiovascular monitoring, psychiatric state, and biomarker targeting rather than to supplement-style optimism.

## Responder Phenotypes

- Schizophrenia with elevated KYNA or KYNA-sensitive cognitive impairment is the clearest mechanistic responder lane.
- Post-stroke patients may show domain-specific associations rather than a global PSCI signal.
- AD / MCI likely need compartment-specific biomarker enrichment if this branch ever becomes an intervention target.
- Age effects matter: kynurenine-pathway metabolites drift with age, so age correction is not optional.

## Protocol Parameters

- TRP challenge studies: oral tryptophan 6 g.
- TrypNAC-II: NAC 140 mg/kg up to 15 g, then tryptophan 6 g, crossover placebo-controlled design.
- GSK3335065: single IV bolus in healthy volunteers; pathway engagement observed at 1.3 mg in the reported subject.
- Stroke cohort: plasma kynurenines sampled at baseline and followed longitudinally over 3 years after stroke.

## Open Contradictions

- The older "quinolinic acid is always high in AD and explains cognition loss" model does not hold cleanly in the 2025 meta-analysis.
- Blood and CSF can point in different directions, which makes a single peripheral biomarker dangerous to overinterpret.
- Schizophrenia challenge studies support KYNA-sensitive circuitry, but the same pathway does not yet produce a reliable human enhancer effect.
- The intervention story is still ahead of the evidence story: there are target-engagement signals, but no selective KMO/KAT II cognition win in hand.
