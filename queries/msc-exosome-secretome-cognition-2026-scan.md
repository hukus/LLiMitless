# MSC / Exosome / Secretome 2026 Scan

## Summary

- The branch is real but still very small: one published open-label phase I/II exosome AD trial, a not-yet-recruiting 2026 intranasal exosome dose-escalation/expansion study, a not-yet-recruiting secretome trial in moderate dementia, and a not-yet-recruiting hMSC add-on pilot in MCI/early AD.^[raw/articles/pubmed-37859748-msc-exosome-ad-trial.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct07457125-cb-exo-a600-ad.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct06632470-hucmsc-secretome-moderate-dementia.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct07512362-hmscs-mab-mci-early-ad.md]
- The 2023 exosome trial reported short-term cognitive movement in the medium-dose arm and no adverse events, but it was small, open-label, and had no significant amyloid/tau separation across arms.^[raw/articles/pubmed-37859748-msc-exosome-ad-trial.md]
- The 2025 reviews are mechanistically optimistic but still read as translation papers rather than efficacy proof.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40142948-msc-exosome-cognitive-impairment-review.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40951706-msc-exosome-ad-review.md]
- FDA guidance is unambiguous: there are no FDA-approved exosome products, and these products are not approved for Alzheimer's disease or other neurological disorders.^[raw/articles/fda-consumer-alert-regenerative-medicine-stem-cells-exosomes.md]^[raw/articles/fda-public-safety-alert-unapproved-stem-cell-exosome-products.md]
- The cleanest branch-level conclusion is that there is a live clinical-registry lane, but the evidence is still far too fragile for a cognitive-enhancement claim.

## What Changed Since 2024

- The 2023 phase I/II exosome trial is now the main human anchor for the branch and gives the wiki a concrete efficacy datum instead of only preclinical theory.
- A 2025 cognitive-impairment review and a 2025 AD-specific review both expanded the mechanistic and translational rationale, but neither adds controlled human efficacy.
- A 2026 ClinicalTrials.gov record shows the exosome lane is still active in AD, with a new intranasal dose-escalation/expansion protocol registered for 2026, not yet recruiting, and results still pending.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct07457125-cb-exo-a600-ad.md]
- A separate moderate-dementia secretome trial and a stem-cell adjunct trial in MCI/early AD are both not yet recruiting, which confirms movement in the broader regenerative-medicine lane but does not constitute efficacy proof.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct06632470-hucmsc-secretome-moderate-dementia.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct07512362-hmscs-mab-mci-early-ad.md]
- FDA's 2019 and 2020 alerts remain the strongest counterweight: the products are not approved, and unapproved exosome marketing has already been associated with serious adverse events.

## Evidence Tiering

| Tier | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Human open-label phase I/II | One small AD exosome trial with short-term cognitive movement in the medium-dose arm |
| Human registry | New exosome, secretome, and hMSC adjunct trials remain registered; the exosome and secretome records are not yet recruiting |
| Review / mechanistic | MSCs and exosomes are framed as neuroprotective, anti-inflammatory, and BBB-crossing platforms |
| Regulatory safety | FDA says no approved exosome products and warns against misleading disease claims |

## Effect Size Snapshot

- 2023 exosome trial: ADAS-cog decreased by 2.33 points and basic MoCA increased by 2.38 points in the medium-dose arm at week 12, with ADAS-cog down 3.98 points by week 36.
- No significant amyloid or tau differences were reported across arms.
- The effect is too small and uncontrolled to treat as durable efficacy.

## Safety / Adverse Events

- The 2023 exosome trial reported no adverse events, but the sample was tiny.
- FDA's safety alerts emphasize that unapproved stem cell and exosome products have already produced serious harms in the real world.
- Current regulatory messaging is stronger than the trial-level safety signal.

## Responder Phenotypes

- The only human exosome efficacy anchor is mild to moderate AD.
- The current secretome trial uses moderate dementia as its population.
- The stem-cell adjunct registry is being pushed into MCI and early AD on top of anti-amyloid monoclonal therapy.

## Protocol Parameters

- 2023 exosome trial: intranasal administration, twice weekly, 12 weeks, three dose arms.
- 2026 exosome registry: intranasal exosome drops, dose-escalation plus expansion phase, twice weekly for 12 weeks, not yet recruiting.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct07457125-cb-exo-a600-ad.md]
- Secretome trial: 3 ml intramuscular secretome every two weeks for four months, not yet recruiting, with vitamin B12 comparator and MMSE / AD8 / GFAP readouts.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct06632470-hucmsc-secretome-moderate-dementia.md]
- Autologous MSC AD trial: four IV infusions, last known recruiting status unknown / results not posted.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct04228666-hb-admscs-ad.md]
- hMSC adjunct trial: one infusion added to background monoclonal antibody treatment in MCI / early AD, not yet recruiting.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct07512362-hmscs-mab-mci-early-ad.md]

## Open Contradictions

- The only published AD exosome trial shows short-term cognitive movement, but it is open-label and not mechanistically separated from expectation or regression to the mean.
- Reviews are optimistic about BBB transport, neuroinflammation, and regeneration, but FDA safety guidance says the products are unapproved and marketing claims are often misleading.
- Stem-cell, exosome, and secretome products are often marketed together, but they are not the same intervention class.
- The live registry lane keeps the branch active, but results are not yet mature enough to resolve whether any signal is durable or reproducible.
