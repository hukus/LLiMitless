# Rapamycin Cognitive-Aging 2026 Scan

[[rapamycin-sirolimus]] still has a strong mechanistic and preclinical aging story, but the human cognition branch remains weak. The best direct human studies so far are an older healthy-adult feasibility RCT and a 2025 AD/early-dementia phase 1 trial; neither produced a cognition win, and the 2025 trial also failed to show CSF penetration while still raising several AD/inflammatory biomarkers in the primary Communications Medicine article.^[raw/articles/pubmed-29408453-rapamycin-older-human-cohort-cognitive-effects.md]^[raw/articles/nature-s43856-025-00904-9-rapamycin-adrd-phase1-clinical-trial.md]

The branch is now more biomarker-active than it was in 2024. A 2025/2026 pharmacokinetic companion study in early AD found moderate blood-level variability, a 48-hour post-dose sample that tracked trough best, and concentrations below immunosuppressive levels, which helps with dosing but not efficacy. A 2026 placebo-controlled immune-aging study in older adults found reduced p21 in immune cells after low-dose rapamycin, which strengthens the human geroprotection story without adding a cognition endpoint. The 2025 six-month open-label pilot in early AD stayed null on prespecified FDG-PET regions, but exploratory regional increases and concentration-response correlations mean it is better read as target-engagement ambiguity than as a clean negative.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41046300-rapamycin-intermittent-pk-early-ad.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41524558-rapamycin-aging-cell-dna-damage-immune-resilience.md]^[raw/articles/medrxiv-2025-12-08-25340853-rapamycin-six-month-ad-pilot.md]

What changed since 2024:

- The human branch moved from "interesting but thin" to "still thin, but now with a negative/neutral cognition readout in both healthy older adults and MCI/early AD."^[raw/articles/pubmed-29408453-rapamycin-older-human-cohort-cognitive-effects.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40394335-rapamycin-adrd-phase1-open-label.md]
- The 2025 phase 1 trial added an important constraint: rapamycin was not detectable in CSF even with daily dosing, so CNS exposure remains unproven in this setting, and the primary article also reported increases in CSF p-tau181, GFAP, NfL, and several plasma inflammatory/vascular markers after treatment.^[raw/articles/nature-s43856-025-00904-9-rapamycin-adrd-phase1-clinical-trial.md]
- The 2025 open-label pilot shifted the conversation toward target-engagement and biomarker questions rather than cognition, because the prespecified FDG-PET regions did not move.^[raw/articles/medrxiv-2025-12-08-25340853-rapamycin-six-month-ad-pilot.md]
- The community-use signal is now better documented. A 333-person survey found that almost all off-label users were pursuing healthy longevity / anti-aging, with weekly dosing the norm, but this remains self-selected survey data.^[raw/articles/pubmed-37191826-rapamycin-off-label-healthspan-survey.md]
- The 2025 healthy-adult review keeps the branch honest: despite compelling animal data, human evidence is still insufficient to call rapamycin a proven senotherapeutic or aging-delay intervention.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40778880-rapamycin-off-label-healthy-adults-review.md]
- Standalone [[rapamycin-sirolimus]] now separates the prescription safety gate, longevity survey signal, and cognition-null human pilot evidence from broader senolytic claims.

## Evidence Tiering

- Preclinical: still the strongest part of the story, with AD-model behavioral/pathology improvements.
- Healthy older adults: safety/feasibility was acceptable, but cognition stayed flat.
- MCI / early AD: the 2025 phase 1 trial was open-label and biomarker-heavy, but cognition and CSF penetrance were not convincing.
- Early AD imaging pilot: null on prespecified metabolic and perfusion regions, with exploratory regional signals only.
- Pharmacokinetic companion: weekly dosing appears monitorable and below immunosuppressive levels in early AD, but that is exposure support rather than efficacy evidence.
- Immune-aging experimental medicine: low-dose rapamycin moved a genome-stability marker in older adults, which strengthens the geroprotection story but still does not answer cognition.
- Community use: substantial off-label longevity use exists, but it is not efficacy evidence.

## Safety / Adverse Events

- 2018 healthy older-adult RCT: facial rash, stomatitis, GI issues, and lower erythrocyte indices, without short-term clinical harm.^[raw/articles/pubmed-29408453-rapamycin-older-human-cohort-cognitive-effects.md]
- 2025 phase 1 ADRD trial: 20 mostly mild adverse events, with increased systolic BP, increased HbA1c, decreased erythrocyte parameters, and a biomarker pattern that looks more inflammatory than clearly disease-modifying.^[raw/articles/nature-s43856-025-00904-9-rapamycin-adrd-phase1-clinical-trial.md]
- 2025 pilot: no serious adverse events reported.^[raw/articles/medrxiv-2025-12-08-25340853-rapamycin-six-month-ad-pilot.md]
- Human safety is still context-dependent because transplant and oncology dosing do not map cleanly onto low-dose off-label use in healthy adults.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40778880-rapamycin-off-label-healthy-adults-review.md]
- The official sirolimus label still carries immunosuppression, infection/malignancy, hyperlipidemia, renal, wound-healing, pneumonitis, embryo-fetal, and infertility warnings, so low-dose off-label use is not the same risk object as the longevity community often implies.^[raw/articles/dailymed-sirolimus-2026-label.md]

## Responder Phenotypes

- No validated cognition responder phenotype has emerged.
- The best hypotheses remain disease-state enrichment, inflammatory aging, and perhaps APOE4/dementia-prevention use, but these are still hypotheses rather than demonstrated selectors.
- Delivery and exposure matter: the 2025 trial's lack of CSF detectability suggests that pharmacokinetics may be a bigger bottleneck than target plausibility.
- The PK companion suggests 48-hour post-dose blood sampling may be the most informative practical monitoring point if a future trial needs fixed-dose simplification, but it does not identify a cognition responder phenotype.

## Protocol Parameters

- 2018 healthy older-adult RCT: 1 mg daily, placebo-controlled, 8-16 weeks.
- 2025 phase 1 ADRD trial: 1 mg/day for 8 weeks, open-label, single site, NCT04200911.
- 2025 early-AD pilot: 7 mg weekly for 26 weeks, open-label.
- 2025/2026 PK companion: mostly 7 mg weekly, 26 weeks; 48-hour post-dose sample correlated best with trough.
- 2026 immune-aging study: low-dose rapamycin for 4 months in older adults, placebo-controlled.
- Off-label practice in the survey/review literature often clusters around weekly or intermittent dosing, but no cognition-validated dose-response curve exists.

## Open Contradictions

- Animal cognition benefit is substantial enough to keep rapamycin in the translational queue, but the human branch has not yet converted that into a credible cognitive signal.
- The 2025 phase 1 trial raised AD/inflammatory biomarkers while failing to show cognition or CSF penetration, which could mean target mismatch, dose mismatch, or a harmful biomarker response.
- The early-AD pilot shows that imaging biomarkers can move differently from the prespecified regions of interest, so exploratory positives should not be mistaken for a disease-modifying win.
- The 2026 PK and immune-aging studies strengthen the argument that low-dose rapamycin can do something measurable in humans, but the measurable thing is still not cognition.
- Off-label longevity enthusiasm is real, but it currently outruns cognition-specific human proof.
