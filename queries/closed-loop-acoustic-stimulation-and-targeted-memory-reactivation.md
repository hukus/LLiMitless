# Closed-Loop Acoustic Stimulation and Targeted Memory Reactivation

This branch now has a clearer pattern than it did in 2024: when closed-loop auditory cueing is aligned to the right sleep phase, it can move slow oscillations, sigma power, spindle timing, or phase-specific memory traces, and it sometimes improves task retention. The new 2024 Brain Sciences CLAS paper adds a second positive lab-learning signal beyond the motor-memory result: verum CLAS improved both language recall and discovery learning over multiple nights, which makes the branch look broader than rote recall alone, but still not broadly cognitive. A 2025 preprint now adds a separate responder layer: pre-sleep alpha/theta power and sleep-onset latency predicted who benefited from auditory stimulation and how much slow-wave activity increased. The 2024 REM CLAS paper further shows that alpha/theta phase targeting can shift REM power and frequency, which strengthens the phase-dosing logic even though it remains a physiology-only result. The 2026 odor-cued declarative-memory paper adds a clean null with a small encoding-strength residue, which sharpens the claim that reactivation is task- and trace-dependent rather than a generic sleep booster. The personalized TMR paper adds a second 2025 proof-of-concept that adaptation matters too: cue counts can be tuned to recall difficulty during stable NREM sleep, but the win still looks task-specific rather than broadly cognitive. Lab-delivered, phase-specific motor and paired-associate tasks look better than home delivery, and some TMR paradigms move electrophysiology without moving the final behavioral endpoint. The 2025 posterior-alpha paper widens the branch beyond sleep consolidation: closed-loop auditory stimulation during working-memory retention can lateralize alpha oscillations in a behavior-linked way, which strengthens target-validation without turning the branch into a general enhancement claim. The 2026 Imaging Neuroscience paper adds a mechanistic clue that cue-evoked reactivation can be detected, is temporally compressed, and tracks spindle coupling, while the 2025/2026 Parkinson's disease nap and TMR studies show that in older or disease-context samples, sleep physiology can move without a motor-memory win.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39595901-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation-language-discovery-learning.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39208441-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation-alpha-theta-rem-phase-dependent-power-frequency.md]^[raw/articles/medrxiv-2025-11-12-bedtime-brain-state-clas-sleep-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41656173-odor-cued-targeted-reactivation-declarative-memory-null.md]^[raw/articles/nature-2025-phase-specific-enhancement-motor-memory-consolidation-closed-loop-tmr.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40102385-phase-specific-enhancement-motor-memory-consolidation-closed-loop-tmr.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40928755-lateralised-modulation-posterior-alpha-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40691439-personalized-targeted-memory-reactivation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41497605-closed-loop-acoustic-stimulation-cathodal-tdcs-retention.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41229102-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation-ecological-memory-null.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40983248-targeted-reactivation-multiple-cues-nap.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40885484-tmr-word-meaning-priming-null.md]^[raw/articles/imaging-neuroscience-2026-targeted-memory-reactivation-elicits-temporally-compressed-reactivation-linked-to-spindles.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41001836-post-learning-nap-motor-memory-consolidation-parkinsons-disease.md]^[raw/articles/medrxiv-2026-04-22-26351552-targeted-memory-reactivation-sleep-parkinsons-disease.md]

A 2026 Clinical Neurophysiology paper in children with Rolandic epilepsy and controls adds the newest phase-window clue: auditory stimulation during background activity or SO upstate increased slow oscillations and SO-spindle complexes, and the event-rate change tracked sleep-dependent motor-sequence consolidation. That makes phase timing look like part of the dose rather than a cosmetic detail.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41435615-auditory-evoked-changes-slow-oscillations-spindles-memory-consolidation-children-epilepsy-controls.md]

The safest synthesis is that `closed-loop` is a control property, not a guarantee of efficacy. Phase, cue count, sleep state, and whether the task is fragile enough to benefit all matter. The newer 2025/2026 papers make that visible rather than merely plausible.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40102385-phase-specific-enhancement-motor-memory-consolidation-closed-loop-tmr.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40575621-tmr-rem-negative-images-lpp.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40983248-targeted-reactivation-multiple-cues-nap.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41110657-auditory-stimulation-sleep-varies-thalamocortical-spindle-phase.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41656173-odor-cued-targeted-reactivation-declarative-memory-null.md]^[raw/articles/medrxiv-2026-04-22-26351552-targeted-memory-reactivation-sleep-parkinsons-disease.md]

**What changed since 2024**

- Slow-wave closed-loop TMR now has a direct phase-comparison result in humans: up-phase cueing beat down-phase cueing for motor performance, with corresponding SO and sigma changes, plus task-related fMRI changes in striato-motor and hippocampal networks.^[raw/articles/nature-2025-phase-specific-enhancement-motor-memory-consolidation-closed-loop-tmr.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40102385-phase-specific-enhancement-motor-memory-consolidation-closed-loop-tmr.md]
- A 2024 Brain Sciences CLAS study extended the positive signal into language and discovery learning, with 35% better word recall and 26% better discovery-learning performance after multi-night verum CLAS, though the sponsor-adjacent COI keeps the result interpretation-sensitive.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39595901-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation-language-discovery-learning.md]
- A 2026 odor-cued TMR study found no overall declarative-memory benefit during NREM sleep; only an encoding-strength-dependent residue remained, which is a cleaner null than the older "physiology positive, behavior flat" cases because the cue was being tested directly against remembered material.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41656173-odor-cued-targeted-reactivation-declarative-memory-null.md]
- A 2025 home down-phase PTAS feasibility study showed that the same control logic can run for seven consecutive nights in an unsupervised setting with good data quality and tolerability, but it did not test cognition or mood as the primary endpoint.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41033050-down-phase-targeted-auditory-stimulation-home-setting-feasibility.md]
- A 2025 preprint showed that pre-sleep alpha/theta EEG features and sleep-onset latency predicted slow-wave enhancement and next-day memory/attention response to auditory stimulation, which is the closest thing in this branch to a pre-intervention responder screen.^[raw/articles/medrxiv-2025-11-12-bedtime-brain-state-clas-sleep-cognition.md]
- A 2024 REM sleep paper showed alpha/theta CLAS can alter phase-dependent power and frequency in both phasic and tonic REM, which keeps the phase story alive even outside NREM.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39208441-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation-alpha-theta-rem-phase-dependent-power-frequency.md]
- Personalized TMR now shows that cue frequency can be individualized by recall difficulty during stable NREM 2 and NREM 3 sleep, which makes the branch more design-sensitive than a generic "cue during sleep" story.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40691439-personalized-targeted-memory-reactivation.md]
- A 2025/2026 spindle-phase paper showed that auditory stimulation only recruited coordinated spindle activity at the rising and peak spindle phases, which makes the cue window part of the dose.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41110657-auditory-stimulation-sleep-varies-thalamocortical-spindle-phase.md]
- A 2026 Clinical Neurophysiology paper in children with Rolandic epilepsy and controls showed that auditory stimulation during background activity or SO upstate can increase slow oscillations and SO-spindle complexes, and that those event-rate changes positively track sleep-dependent motor-sequence consolidation.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41435615-auditory-evoked-changes-slow-oscillations-spindles-memory-consolidation-children-epilepsy-controls.md]
- Closed-loop auditory stimulation also has a wakeful working-memory retention paper now: opposite alpha-phase cues over opposite cortices induced asymmetric posterior alpha modulation, and that asymmetry tracked behavioral asymmetry.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40928755-lateralised-modulation-posterior-alpha-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation.md]
- A 2026 mechanistic paper showed TMR-evoked reactivation is temporally compressed and spindle-linked, with detectability correlating with memory improvement, which strengthens the timing-dose logic even though it is still task-specific.^[raw/articles/imaging-neuroscience-2026-targeted-memory-reactivation-elicits-temporally-compressed-reactivation-linked-to-spindles.md]
- Home delivery remains a major failure mode: a real-world CLAS study increased slow-oscillation amplitude but did not move memory or vigilance.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41229102-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation-ecological-memory-null.md]
- A 2025 randomised controlled trial in people with Parkinson's disease found that a post-learning nap did not improve motor memory consolidation versus wakefulness, although slow-wave amplitude and slope still tracked performance change in the PD group.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41001836-post-learning-nap-motor-memory-consolidation-parkinsons-disease.md]
- A 2026 medRxiv preprint found that auditory TMR during a nap modulated slow-wave and spindle density in PD and older controls but still did not improve motor retention or dual-tasking.^[raw/articles/medrxiv-2026-04-22-26351552-targeted-memory-reactivation-sleep-parkinsons-disease.md]
- Ecological measurement is feasible even when efficacy is not: a 2025 insomnia study showed smartphone cognitive testing plus EMA can work over 28 days in older adults, but completion was imperfect and some participants used score-optimizing strategies that could invalidate results.^[raw/articles/bmc-digital-health-2025-smartphone-cognitive-testing-ema-insomnia.md]
- Another 2025 TMR paper found no boost to word-meaning priming or contextual recall during a nap, even though cue-evoked spindle/beta activity still rose, which strengthens the branch-level distinction between sleep reprocessing and behavioral enhancement.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40885484-tmr-word-meaning-priming-null.md]
- More stimulation is not automatically better: a nap study found no significant memory benefit from once-versus-five-times cueing, even though sigma and delta predictors still differentiated the conditions.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40983248-targeted-reactivation-multiple-cues-nap.md]
- REM TMR can alter emotional processing markers without altering memory performance, which separates neural reprocessing from overt cognition.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40575621-tmr-rem-negative-images-lpp.md]
- Timing within the spindle envelope matters: spindle-phase and spindle-targeting work shows the cue window is part of the dose, and early delivery can terminate the spindle rather than help it.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40626105-targeting-sleep-spindles-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41110657-auditory-stimulation-sleep-varies-thalamocortical-spindle-phase.md]

## Safety / Adverse Events

- No major new safety signal stands out in these studies, but the practical burden is real: audio cueing can fragment sleep if timing is off.
- The Brain Sciences CLAS study used a prototype commercial system and discloses advisor-level ties to the vendor, so the bigger concern is credibility and transportability rather than a new medical adverse-event signal.
- The 2025 home PTAS feasibility study is reassuring on burden and data capture, but it remains a delivery study rather than a cognitive-efficacy study.
- The 2026 pediatric epilepsy/control paper is not a safety red flag, but it is a reminder that disease-context sleep architecture can shape the apparent effect size and the generalizability of cue timing.
- Spindle-targeting work shows that early cueing can terminate the spindle, so the control loop can overshoot its own target.
- Home protocols are especially vulnerable to awakenings, habituation failure, and lower compliance.
- Disease-context naps add another caution: if baseline sleep architecture is already atypical, cueing benefit may be easy to lose even when physiology moves.
- Remote cognitive testing can be feasible, but it needs guardrails against strategic responding and drop-off; feasibility is not the same as a valid efficacy readout.^[raw/articles/bmc-digital-health-2025-smartphone-cognitive-testing-ema-insomnia.md]
- REM odor cueing altered electrophysiology but did not worsen arousal or valence in the pilot study, which is reassuring but not the same as proving safety across longer use.
- The bigger safety risk for the wiki is interpretive, not medical: physiology gains are easy to overread as cognitive enhancement.

## Responder Phenotypes

- `Phase-window` responders are the clearest subgroup in the new lab studies.
- `Pre-sleep EEG` responders are now plausible: alpha/theta power and sleep-onset latency predicted CLAS response in the 2025 preprint.
- `Home-compatible` delivery is a different question from responder status; repeated unsupervised nights can work without proving benefit.
- `Background-activity` and `SO-upstate` timing windows look like a separate responder dimension rather than a simple yes/no CLAS phenotype.
- `Multi-night lab-learning` responders may exist for language and discovery-learning tasks, but that pattern still needs independent replication.
- `Challenging-memory` items appear more likely to benefit from personalized cueing than already-stable memories.
- `Alpha-phase-sensitive` retention may be a distinct closed-loop phenotype in wakeful tasks.
- `Encoding-strength` appears to be a distinct responder dimension: weakly encoded material may fail to benefit even when the same cue works on stronger traces.
- `Disease-context` responders may show sleep-physiology movement without behavioral gain, especially in PD and older-adult nap paradigms.
- `Learners` or participants who can maintain stable sleep during cueing are more likely to show any benefit at all.
- `Sleep-disturbance-sensitive` users may lose the signal in home delivery.
- `Emotional-memory` targets can show a neural response even when overt memory is unchanged.

## Protocol Parameters

- The 2025 Nature Communications study enrolled 31 healthy young adults and compared up-phase, down-phase, and no-reactivation cueing during overnight sleep.
- The 2024 Brain Sciences CLAS study enrolled 20 analyzed healthy adults across six overnight sessions and compared verum versus control CLAS while participants performed language and discovery learning tasks.
- The 2026 odor-cued declarative-memory study enrolled 32 healthy adults and cued one odor during non-REM sleep after learning two object-location sets with distinct odors.
- The home down-phase PTAS study used five healthy adults over seven consecutive nights, with 58 analyzable recordings and alternating 10-second ON/OFF windows.
- The 2026 pediatric Clinical Neurophysiology study enrolled children with Rolandic epilepsy and controls across two nap visits, with auditory or sham stimulation during background activity versus SO upstate and motor-sequence typing as the behavioral endpoint.
- The 2025 posterior-alpha study used closed-loop auditory stimulation during a working-memory retention task in healthy adults and targeted opposite alpha phases over opposite cortices.
- The personalized TMR study enrolled 36 healthy adults and adjusted cue counts by recall difficulty during stable NREM 2 and NREM 3 sleep while testing word-pair consolidation.
- The 2025 Parkinson's disease nap RCT enrolled 32 PD participants and 32 healthy older adults, comparing a post-learning nap with wakefulness after finger-tapping sequence learning.
- The 2026 medRxiv TMR preprint enrolled 20 PD participants and 20 healthy older adults and delivered auditory TMR during a 2-hour nap after finger-tapping learning.
- The 2026 Imaging Neuroscience mechanistic paper studied 48 participants on a serial reaction time task and used slow-wave sleep TMR with classifier-based reactivation detection.
- The ecological CLAS study delivered one night of home auditory stimulation in 34 participants and measured both slow oscillations and behavior.
- The nap cue-count study cued 40 of 60 object-location memories once or five times during a 90-minute nap in 31 participants, then inserted an interference task before final testing.
- The REM pilot studied 16 healthy young adults and replayed an odor cue during REM sleep after pairing it with negative images in wake.
- The spindle-targeting study used 10 healthy adults across six nights to validate pre-offset spindle targeting without gross sleep disruption.
- The REM phase-targeting paper studied 18 healthy young adults and phase-locked alpha/theta stimulation during REM sleep.
- The bedtime-brain-state preprint studied 28 healthy adults under mild sleep restriction and used pre-sleep EEG features to predict auditory-stimulation response.

## Open Contradictions

- Physiological success does not guarantee behavioral success: home CLAS and REM TMR both moved neural markers without a parallel memory win.
- Lab delivery is more permissive than home delivery, which means the intervention may be highly context-dependent.
- The new home feasibility study reinforces that context gap: repeated home delivery is possible, but it still does not demonstrate a home cognitive benefit.
- Multi-night lab delivery can be positive even when home delivery is null, so setting and monitoring burden are part of the dose.
- Wakeful closed-loop retention is another reminder that a precise control signal can still give only a narrow, asymmetry-linked effect rather than a broad gain.
- Disease context matters too: the 2025 PD nap RCT and 2026 PD TMR preprint both moved sleep physiology without a motor-memory win.
- Declarative-memory TMR may depend on initial encoding strength, so a branch-level null can still hide a subgroup signal without becoming a general enhancer.
- Cue repetition is not monotonic: once-versus-five-times cueing did not create a significant behavioral difference.
- Phase is part of the dose: the same class of stimulation can help, do nothing, or disrupt the sleep event depending on timing.
- Within-stage phase is also part of the dose: REM alpha/theta targeting can move physiology even when behavior is not measured.
- Pre-sleep state is a separate axis from chronotype, so brain-state prediction should not be collapsed into trait timing.
- Emotional reprocessing and declarative memory are not the same endpoint, so REM TMR cannot be generalized from one to the other.
- Some language and semantic tasks may be especially resistant to TMR-style gains even when sleep reprocessing is measurable.
