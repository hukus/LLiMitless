# Active-Comparator and Caffeine-Scheduling Confound Audit

This audit is the protocol-level companion to [[pharmacology-comparator-map]] and [[anti-tolerance-periodization-in-cognitive-enhancement]]. Its job is to separate three things that are easy to collapse into one signal:

1. a truly superior intervention,
2. withdrawal or deficit reversal,
3. and a comparator schedule that changed the participant state before the task even started.

The strongest pattern in the current wiki is that caffeine, nicotine, stimulant holidays, sleep restriction, meal timing, and repeated testing all move the measurement context. If the comparator is active, the control is part of the intervention design. If the comparator is a washout, the washout itself may be the intervention. If the endpoint is repeated often, practice effects can overwhelm small treatment gains.^[raw/articles/pubmed-22992376-caffeine-attention-withdrawn-consumers.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-30861208-expectancy-caffeine-withdrawal-balanced-placebo-design.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-20846444-practice-effects-frequent-repetitive-cognitive-testing.md]

## What Changed Since 2024

- The caffeine literature now makes it harder to pretend the comparator is inert. Acute caffeine still improves attention, but repeated dosing under sleep loss and chronic sleep restriction shows that the state being measured is itself moving across days.^[raw/articles/pubmed-22992376-caffeine-attention-withdrawn-consumers.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-35791877-repeated-low-dose-caffeine-sleep-deprivation-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-38830861-repeated-caffeine-intake-sleep-restriction-gm.md]
- Nicotine is now more clearly a state-restoration comparator than a generic enhancer: in abstinent smokers, the question is often whether nicotine is reversing withdrawal, not whether it is creating a clean new benefit.^[raw/articles/pubmed-38582919-nicotine-cognitive-function-smokers-abstinence.md]
- Sleep-loss and meal-state papers make the comparator problem broader than drugs. Postprandial glucose regulation, glucose expectancy, and glycemic extremes all show that timing and metabolic context can move cognition or the readout around it.^[raw/articles/pubmed-11502230-placebo-expectancy-glucose-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39579950-breaking-up-sitting-postprandial-glucose-regulation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41522270-glucose-extremes-cognitive-function-type1-diabetes.md]
- Repeated cognitive testing is now impossible to ignore as a schedule confound. Serial gains in the early phase can be practice effects, not intervention effects, especially in short studies.^[raw/articles/pubmed-20846444-practice-effects-frequent-repetitive-cognitive-testing.md]

## Summary

The audit conclusion is simple: if the comparator changes sleep pressure, nicotine state, caffeine withdrawal, meal timing, or task familiarity, then the study is no longer a clean head-to-head cognition comparison. It is a state-manipulation study.

Caffeine is the clearest example. In withdrawn consumers, acute caffeine still improves attention and encoding, but that does not mean every apparent gain in a caffeine block is a true enhancement signal.^[raw/articles/pubmed-22992376-caffeine-attention-withdrawn-consumers.md] Balanced-placebo data show that expectancy matters for craving and that withdrawal symptoms are driven mainly by caffeine deprivation itself, so schedule changes can easily masquerade as efficacy.^[raw/articles/pubmed-30861208-expectancy-caffeine-withdrawal-balanced-placebo-design.md]

Nicotine shows the same structural problem. In smokers after abstinence, nicotine can look cognitively helpful because it is relieving abstinence-related decrement rather than lifting a stable baseline. That is useful comparator evidence, but only if the abstinence state is explicitly logged.^[raw/articles/pubmed-38582919-nicotine-cognitive-function-smokers-abstinence.md]

Repeated caffeine under sleep loss is a good reminder that "works in one block" does not equal "stable enhancer." Under acute sleep deprivation, repeated low-dose caffeine preserved reaction time and detections, while chronic sleep restriction studies show repeated caffeine changes brain-state readouts across days. The right interpretation is schedule-sensitive resilience, not universal enhancement.^[raw/articles/pubmed-35791877-repeated-low-dose-caffeine-sleep-deprivation-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-38830861-repeated-caffeine-intake-sleep-restriction-gm.md]

Meal timing and glucose state belong in the same audit. Glucose expectancy can change some cognitive outcomes, breaking a naive "substrate equals effect" assumption, while postprandial walking and glycemic extremes show that metabolic state can correlate with attention-related markers and with cognitive risk more generally.^[raw/articles/pubmed-11502230-placebo-expectancy-glucose-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39579950-breaking-up-sitting-postprandial-glucose-regulation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41522270-glucose-extremes-cognitive-function-type1-diabetes.md]

## Evidence Tiering

- Withdrawal-reversal evidence: caffeine and nicotine studies in abstinent or withdrawn participants.
- Schedule-manipulation evidence: repeated caffeine under sleep deprivation and chronic sleep restriction.
- Comparator-holiday evidence: methylphenidate weekend holiday trial.
- Measurement-confound evidence: practice effects in serial testing.
- Metabolic-state confound evidence: glucose expectancy, postprandial glucose, and glycemic extremes.

## Effect Size Snapshot

- Caffeine withdrawal study: acute caffeine improved reaction time, target detection, and encoding in both withdrawn and non-consumer groups.
- Balanced-placebo caffeine study: expectancy shifted craving; withdrawal symptoms and cognition mainly tracked caffeine deprivation.
- Repeated low-dose caffeine during total sleep deprivation: reaction time and correct detections improved versus placebo.
- Repeated caffeine during sleep restriction: brain response changed across days, indicating the state itself was not constant.
- Nicotine after abstinence: attention and reaction-time effects were measurable in abstinent smokers.
- Methylphenidate weekend holidays: insomnia fell, appetite interference trended lower, and there was no significant immediate symptom rebound.
- Practice effects study: early repeated testing produced clinically relevant gains, with Cohen's d roughly 0.36-1.19 across measures.
- Glucose expectancy study: some cognitive measures were expectancy-sensitive, not just substrate-sensitive.

## Safety/Adverse Events

- Caffeine confounds are not just statistical. Sleep disruption, anxiety, dependence, and withdrawal can change the participant state before the task starts.^[raw/articles/pubmed-27612937-caffeine-effects-cognitive-physical-occupational-performance.md]
- Nicotine brings dependence, cardiovascular effects, nausea, and sleep effects into the comparator layer.
- Methylphenidate holidays reduced insomnia in the ADHD trial, which is helpful for tolerability but also shows why the control schedule matters.
- Sleep restriction and sleep debt are themselves performance harms, so they are state challenges, not sham controls.
- Glucose extremes are a cognition risk in their own right, especially where hypoglycemia or large variability is present.

## Responder Phenotypes

- Habitual caffeine consumers and withdrawn consumers.
- Non-habitual caffeine users under sleep deprivation.
- Smokers after abstinence.
- ADHD children on methylphenidate.
- Healthy adults undergoing serial cognitive testing.
- Participants with glycemic variability or explicit postprandial load.

## Protocol Parameters

- If caffeine is not the intervention, keep the caffeine window fixed and do not change dose or timing mid-study.
- If caffeine is the intervention, stabilize baseline intake first so withdrawal does not masquerade as benefit.
- If nicotine or other stimulants are in the design, declare the abstinence or washout state before the first block.
- If the study uses active comparators, keep dose, route, and time-of-day matched across conditions.
- If the battery is repeated frequently, use alternate forms and a run-in period before interpreting gains.
- If meal timing or glucose state matters, standardize first meal, fasting window, and postprandial test timing.

## Open Contradictions

- A comparator can be active and still be the right comparator. That does not make the result invalid, but it changes the interpretation.
- A washout can be biologically meaningful even when it is called control.
- Caffeine can be both a valid comparator and a confound at the same time.
- Nicotine can show true attention effects and still be mostly a withdrawal-reversal signal in smokers.
- Practice effects can create apparent improvement even when the intervention has no meaningful effect.
- Meal timing can move cognition directly or by changing the surrounding physiology.
