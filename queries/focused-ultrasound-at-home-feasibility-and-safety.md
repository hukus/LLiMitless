# Focused Ultrasound At-Home Feasibility and Safety

This scan did not find a validated at-home or DIY transcranial focused ultrasound protocol. The current human literature is built around trained operators, MRI-based or neuronavigation-based targeting, formal acoustic parameter reporting, skull modeling, hydrophone calibration, and explicit safety thresholds. A wearable prototype exists, but it still depends on the same professional workflow, so "wearable" does not mean "consumer safe." The newest direct human efficacy signal is a 2026 pilot RCT in mild AD, which supports supervised feasibility and short-term safety but not at-home translation. The newest field-maturity and safety-governance signals make the same point from another angle: 177 registered TUS neuromodulation trials now exist, but device information appears in only 32% of registrations, and the field is actively responding to a serious adverse event around low-frequency, high-output, incompletely parameterized ultrasound.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39449176-wearable-steerable-transcranial-low-intensity-focused-ultrasound-system.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-38410648-itrusst-standardised-reporting-transcranial-ultrasound-stimulation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40470501-transcranial-ultrasound-stimulation-parameters-neurological-diseases.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-36215339-hydrophone-measurements-for-biomedical-ultrasound-applications.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-28777075-numerical-evaluation-of-the-skull-for-human-neuromodulation-with-transcranial-focused-ultrasound.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-31401074-safety-of-transcranial-focused-ultrasound-stimulation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41046632-transcranial-ultrasound-stimulation-ad-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41856430-transcranial-ultrasound-neuromodulation-registry-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41916485-low-frequency-ultrasound-sae-response.md]^[raw/articles/itrusst-2026-open-letter-ultrasound-neuromodulation-adverse-event.md]

## What Changed Since 2024

- The reporting consensus is now explicit about what must be measured and disclosed: transducer/drive system, drive settings, free-field acoustics, pulse timing, in situ estimates, and intensity parameters.^[raw/articles/pubmed-38410648-itrusst-standardised-reporting-transcranial-ultrasound-stimulation.md]
- The new safety consensus gives concrete nonsignificant-risk thresholds for mechanical and thermal exposure, but only under assumptions that already exclude several real-world risk states.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41072763-itrusst-biophysical-safety-transcranial-ultrasound-stimulation.md]
- The 2019 safety review already warned that adverse effects, though rare, could include hemorrhage, tissue damage, unintentional BBB opening, and long-term behavioral effects when intensity or rate climbed too high.^[raw/articles/pubmed-31401074-safety-of-transcranial-focused-ultrasound-stimulation.md]
- The 2025 parameter review shows that published human TUS work still spans a wide parameter range and remains insufficiently standardized for a plug-and-play home protocol.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40470501-transcranial-ultrasound-stimulation-parameters-neurological-diseases.md]
- A wearable system has appeared, but it still uses MRI, neuronavigation, skull modeling, and volunteer screening, which keeps it firmly in research/professional territory.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39449176-wearable-steerable-transcranial-low-intensity-focused-ultrasound-system.md]
- The 2026 mild-AD pilot RCT adds the first direct randomized human efficacy/safety signal in this branch: 9 patients, 30 supervised sessions, no serious clinical or radiographic adverse events, and only preliminary cognitive stabilization.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41046632-transcranial-ultrasound-stimulation-ad-rct.md]
- A 2026 BBB-opening mechanism paper reinforces the broader safety lesson: low-pressure and high-pressure focused ultrasound are not interchangeable, because transient opening and persistent injury separate by pressure window.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41723237-safe-focused-ultrasound-bbb-opening-tight-junctions.md]
- A 2026 registry analysis shows rapid field growth but weak protocol transparency: 177 unique trials were found, 150 were registered in the last five years, cognitive indications accounted for 11%, and only 32% of trial records reported device information.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41856430-transcranial-ultrasound-neuromodulation-registry-analysis.md]
- A 2026 peer-reviewed safety response and ITRUSST open letter add a concrete adverse-event boundary: low-frequency, high-output, repurposed, incompletely parameterized ultrasound is not interchangeable with conservative, purpose-built low-intensity neuromodulation.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41916485-low-frequency-ultrasound-sae-response.md]^[raw/articles/itrusst-2026-open-letter-ultrasound-neuromodulation-adverse-event.md]

## Evidence Tiering

| Tier | What it shows | Practical read |
|---|---|---|
| Safety consensus | Thresholds and contraindication assumptions | Useful for research governance, not home instruction |
| Reporting consensus | Required exposure and system reporting | Implies specialist calibration and documentation burden |
| Systematic review | Real-world parameter ranges and dose-response signals | Too heterogeneous for DIY simplification |
| Pilot RCT | Repeated supervised TUS in mild AD with no serious clinical or radiographic AEs | Feasibility signal only; not home evidence |
| Wearable prototype | Wearability is technically possible | Still research-grade, not consumer-validated |

## Effect Size Snapshot

There is no home-use efficacy estimate to report. The relevant human literature is mostly mechanistic, feasibility, or early translational work rather than a controlled home protocol with cognition outcomes. The closest direct human signal is a 2026 mild-AD pilot RCT in which ADAS-cog stayed relatively stable in the active group versus worsening in placebo at 52 weeks, while MMSE favored active treatment at 24 and 52 weeks; the sample was only nine patients, so this remains hypothesis-generating rather than practice-changing.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41046632-transcranial-ultrasound-stimulation-ad-rct.md]

## Safety / Adverse Events

- The 2025 consensus only labels TUS nonsignificant-risk within explicit mechanical and thermal bounds and only when the participant lacks key contraindications.
- The review literature still treats rare hemorrhage, tissue damage, BBB opening, and off-target biologic effects as the adverse outcomes that define the safety envelope.
- The 2026 mild-AD pilot RCT reported no serious clinical or radiographic adverse events, but the trial was small and clinic-based rather than home-based.
- The 2026 serious-adverse-event response makes frequency, device class, cavitation margin, and full acoustic disclosure non-negotiable safety variables, especially for low-frequency 200-340 kHz exposures and repurposed high-output systems.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41916485-low-frequency-ultrasound-sae-response.md]
- For DIY use, the important gap is not just dose selection; it is verification of delivered acoustic field, skull effects, target placement, and screening for contraindications.

## Responder Phenotypes

- No validated at-home responder phenotype is established.
- Research studies suggest target, frequency, and task coupling matter, but those effects have not been translated into a self-administered home protocol.

## Protocol Parameters

- Current human studies use MRI-based targeting or other professional targeting workflows.
- The reporting consensus requires documentation of transducer and drive system, drive settings, free-field acoustic parameters, pulse timing, in situ estimates, and intensity parameters.
- The parameter review reports 220-650 kHz fundamental frequencies and 0.5-31 W/cm^2 spatial peak pulse-average intensity across included studies.
- The wearable prototype still uses neuronavigation, LIFU planning, MRI skull assessment, simulations, and water testing to establish safe steering range.
- The 2026 mild-AD pilot RCT used 30 sessions over 6 weeks (15 min/day, 5 days/week) in a supervised clinical setting; that schedule is not a home protocol by itself.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41046632-transcranial-ultrasound-stimulation-ad-rct.md]
- Registry-level growth does not solve the protocol problem: most trials still do not disclose device information in their registry record, so a public trial listing is not enough to reproduce or self-administer the exposure.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41856430-transcranial-ultrasound-neuromodulation-registry-analysis.md]
- Hydrophone calibration and skull modeling are not optional implementation details; they are part of the exposure chain that determines whether the device is actually delivering what the label claims.^[raw/articles/pubmed-36215339-hydrophone-measurements-for-biomedical-ultrasound-applications.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-28777075-numerical-evaluation-of-the-skull-for-human-neuromodulation-with-transcranial-focused-ultrasound.md]

## Open Contradictions

- Wearable hardware is becoming more feasible, but operational feasibility is not the same as validated home safety.
- The 2026 pilot RCT makes the branch look more clinically real, but it also shows the current evidence is still built on supervised sessions, imaging surveillance, and tiny samples rather than consumer self-administration.
- Promotional language around precision and depth access can outrun the actual translational evidence.
- The safety consensus gives thresholds, but those thresholds do not create a consumer protocol by themselves.
- The serious-adverse-event debate should not be overgeneralized into "all tFUS is unsafe," but it also cannot be ignored; it shows that low-frequency, high-output, repurposed systems can sit in a different risk regime than purpose-built low-intensity neuromodulation hardware.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41916485-low-frequency-ultrasound-sae-response.md]^[raw/articles/itrusst-2026-open-letter-ultrasound-neuromodulation-adverse-event.md]
