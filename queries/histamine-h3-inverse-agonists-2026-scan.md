# Histamine H3 Inverse Agonists 2026 Scan

Histamine H3 inverse agonists and antagonists are a wake-promoting class that increases histaminergic tone and downstream monoaminergic/cholinergic signaling. In practice, the class looks more like a state-restoration tool than a broad cognitive enhancer. [[samelisant]] now has a published phase-2 narcolepsy result, so the class is no longer protocol-only; the new signal is still disorder-context wakefulness evidence rather than a healthy-adult cognition win.

The human evidence is strongest for pitolisant in hypersomnolence disorders. Outside that context, the class has not produced convincing general cognition benefit: MK-0249 failed in adult ADHD, GSK239512 was neutral overall in stable schizophrenia, and no healthy-adult enhancement trial surfaced in this pass.

The latest development lead is [[samelisant]], and the public record now includes a published phase-2 narcolepsy result. That makes the class relevant to wakefulness and fatigue control, not yet to validated enhancement of attention, memory, or executive function in well-rested adults.

For a broader comparator frame, see [[healthy-adult-cognition-signals]] and [[comparisons/pharmacology-comparator-map]].

## Summary

- Pitolisant has the clearest approved human wakefulness signal.
- OSA data support daytime sleepiness reduction, but not a clean cognition win.
- Adult ADHD and schizophrenia studies do not support a general enhancer claim.
- Samelisant now has published phase-2 narcolepsy evidence, but it still looks more like a wakefulness comparator than a cognitive enhancer.
- Rare activating mood effects remain a low-frequency but plausible safety concern.

## Evidence Tiering

- RCT / trial evidence: pitolisant OSA, MK-0249 ADHD, GSK239512 schizophrenia, samelisant phase-2 protocol and published narcolepsy results.
- Meta-analysis: pitolisant sleepiness outcomes in narcolepsy and OSA.
- Regulatory / label evidence: pitolisant label.
- Case report: pitolisant-associated mania.

## Effect Size Snapshot

- Pitolisant meta-analysis: better ESS, mean sleep latency, and EQ-5D versus placebo in narcolepsy/OSA populations.
- Pitolisant OSA RCT: ESS and fatigue improved; wake-maintenance testing did not.
- MK-0249 ADHD: no meaningful separation from placebo on symptom outcome.
- GSK239512 schizophrenia: small positive composite estimate, but not a convincing overall cognitive effect.
- Samelisant narcolepsy: statistically significant ESS improvement versus placebo, with supportive patient/clinician scales; the abstract does not expose the numeric delta.

## Safety / Adverse Events

- Insomnia is the most recurrent class issue.
- Pitolisant also carries nausea, anxiety, and QT-prolongation cautions on the U.S. label.
- Samelisant's published phase-2 AE pattern includes insomnia, abnormal dreams, nausea, and hot flush.
- The mania case report suggests a low-frequency activation risk that should stay on the watchlist.

## Responder Phenotypes

- Best-supported phenotype: people with excessive daytime sleepiness, especially narcolepsy and OSA.
- Possible but unproven: other hypersomnolence states such as idiopathic hypersomnia.
- Not established: well-rested healthy adults seeking general cognitive enhancement.

## Protocol Parameters

- Pitolisant OSA RCT: clinical dosing in a disorder-context sample; the key readout was sleepiness rather than cognition.
- MK-0249 ADHD: 5 to 10 mg/day in a crossover design.
- GSK239512 schizophrenia: exploratory phase II cognitive dosing, but no class-wide useful effect emerged.
- Samelisant phase 2: 2 mg and 4 mg once daily for 14 days in narcolepsy; 190 randomized.

## Open Contradictions

- The class clearly improves sleepiness in hypersomnolence disorders, yet that does not translate into a validated healthy-adult cognition enhancer.
- A small positive composite result in schizophrenia does not survive the broader null pattern across domains and trials.
- Wakefulness gains can look like cognitive gains unless the study separates arousal from memory, transfer, and executive function.

## What Changed Since 2024

- Samelisant moved from preclinical promise into a public phase-2 narcolepsy record with a positive EDS endpoint.
- The pitolisant evidence base now includes stronger disorder-context wakefulness support, but still not a broad cognition story.
- Recent safety surveillance is enough to keep mood activation and insomnia visible in the class summary.
