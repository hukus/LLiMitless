# Regulatory And Sports Visibility Map

This page is the practical status layer for branches where approval, scheduling, anti-doping visibility, and product-quality risk change how cognition evidence should be read.

It is not an efficacy ranking. The key question is whether the branch is a prescription medicine, a controlled wake-promoter, an unapproved research chemical, a dietary supplement, or a device-like intervention whose intended use does not match the cognition claim.

## Summary

The main update since 2024 is that the status layer is now clearer than the marketing layer.

- Methylene blue is still a medical antidote/antidote-adjacent prescription product in both U.S. and EU references, and the current label language keeps serotonin syndrome and G6PD risk front and center.^[raw/articles/dailymed-methylene-blue-injection-label-2026.md]^[raw/articles/ema-methylthioninium-chloride-proveblue-2026.md]
- Solriamfetol now has both U.S. and EU wakefulness labels, and it also shows up in the 2026 WADA specified-stimulant bucket, which makes it a high-visibility comparator for fatigue-rescue claims.^[raw/articles/dailymed-sunosi-solriamfetol-tablet-label.md]^[raw/articles/ema-sunosi-solriamfetol-2026.md]^[raw/articles/wada-2026-prohibited-list-general.md]
- Phenylpiracetam now has an explicit WADA sports-visibility entry and a small but denser Russian asthenia / recovery literature, so it belongs in the comparator layer as a phenotype-bound rescue candidate rather than a generic enhancer.^[raw/articles/wada-2025-prohibited-list-fonturacetam.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40047835-fonturacetam-asthenia-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/pharmateca-624929-actitropil-reactive-asthenia-young-patients.md]^[raw/articles/rusmedreview-2023-actitropil-chronic-cerebral-ischemia.md]
- Prescription wake-promoters such as modafinil and armodafinil remain schedule-controlled in the U.S. and belong in the comparator layer, not the supplement layer.^[raw/articles/dailymed-modafinil-tablet-label.md]^[raw/articles/dailymed-armodafinil-tablet-label.md]
- [[lisdexamfetamine]] / Vyvanse now has a separate prescription-stimulant status line because it is a Schedule II prodrug amphetamine with ADHD and adult BED indications, not a supplement, weight-loss drug, or casual cognition aid.^[raw/articles/dailymed-2026-vyvanse-lisdexamfetamine-label.md]
- Unapproved afinil-style analogs now have a separate status lane through [[unapproved-modafinil-analog-eugeroic-watchlist]] because FDA/ORA analytical work places adrafinil, CRL-40,940, CRL-40,941, and N-methyl-difluoromodafinil in a supplement-screening / hidden-API context, while WADA 2026 directly names adrafinil, fladrafinil, flmodafinil, hydrafinil / fluorenol, and modafinil in S6.A. [[ce-123]] remains preclinical-only and should still be treated as unapproved/status-risk.^[raw/articles/wada-2026-stimulant-list-afinils-pemoline-tesofensine.md]
- Pitolisant is prescription-only in both the U.S. and EU, and I did not find it on the 2026 WADA prohibited list in this pass, so its sports status still deserves a molecule-specific check before publication-grade use.^[raw/articles/dailymed-wakix-pitolisant-tablet-label.md]^[raw/articles/ema-wakix-pitolisant-2026.md]^[raw/articles/wada-2026-prohibited-list-general.md]
- Approval geography now needs its own field: GV-971 is China-approved but globally unconfirmed, while KarXT/Cobenfy is U.S.-approved for schizophrenia yet still investigational in Alzheimer agitation, so label status and registry status cannot be collapsed into one line.^[raw/articles/nmpa-2019-gv971-approval.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct04520412-gv971-green-memory-global.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct05058040-gv971-postmarketing-safety.md]^[raw/articles/fda-cobenfy-approval-2024.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct07011745-karxt-adagio2-alzheimers-agitation.md]
- Peptides and bioregulators now have a sharper regulatory split: FDA's current compounding-risk page explicitly flags BPC-157, MOTS-c, Selank acetate, Semax, Dihexa acetate, and TB-500/LKKTETQ as compounding-risk or no-human-exposure items, while the July 23-24, 2026 PCAC agenda puts BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, emideltide/DSIP, Semax, and Epitalon on the 503A bulk-substance review track. WADA separately flags BPC-157 under S0, CJC-1295 / ipamorelin / GHRPs under GH-axis S2 language, MOTS-c under S4.4.1, and thymosin-beta-4 derivatives such as TB-500 under S2.3; WADA's surveillance PDF also marks EPITALON (EPITHALON) as not approved for human use. The dedicated overlay is now [[peptide-regulatory-live-watch-board]].^[raw/articles/fda-bulk-drug-substances-peptide-risk-2026.md]^[raw/articles/fda-compounder-supplier-quality-warning-2026.md]^[raw/articles/fda-warning-letter-genogenix-epithalon-2026.md]^[raw/articles/fda-2026-advisory-epitalon-semax.md]^[raw/articles/fda-2026-pcac-tb500-bpc157-kpv-motsc-bulk-substances.md]^[raw/articles/fda-unii-epitalon.md]^[raw/articles/fda-orphan-epitalon-retinitis-pigmentosa.md]^[raw/articles/wada-prohibited-list-peptide-entries-2026.md]^[raw/articles/wada-2026-prohibited-list-peptide-gh-axis-expanded.md]^[raw/articles/wada-internet-surveillance-epitalon-2026.md]
- Dietary supplements remain a different regulatory species from drugs, and FDA still does not approve them for safety or effectiveness before sale; hidden-ingredient risk is part of the interpretation layer, not an edge case.^[raw/articles/fda-dietary-supplements-regulatory-baseline-2026.md]^[raw/articles/fda-hidden-ingredients-supplement-risk-2026.md]
- [[lithium-orotate]] has a supplement wrapper but inherits prescription-lithium safety logic because the relevant active ion is lithium and the live LiO-AD protocol excludes kidney/thyroid problems, thiazides, and chronic daily NSAID use. Treat it as a monitored clinical hypothesis, not a casual nootropic supplement.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct07459959-lio-ad-record-2026-05-04.md]^[raw/articles/dailymed-lithium-oral-solution-medication-guide.md]
- [[nicotinamide-mononucleotide]] / NMN now has a clearer U.S. dietary-supplement status than it did during the earlier drug-preclusion dispute: FDA's 2025 citizen-petition response says NMN is not excluded from the dietary-supplement definition under the revised race-to-market interpretation. That does not mean FDA has approved NMN for safety, efficacy, cognition, or label accuracy, and a 2024 label-claim study found some NMN products with no detectable NMN.^[raw/articles/fda-2025-nmn-dietary-supplement-petition-response.md]^[raw/articles/springer-2024-nmn-urolithin-label-claim-testing.md]
- [[urolithin-a]] has a cleaner food-ingredient status layer than many longevity supplements because FDA GRN 791 has a no-questions response for specified urolithin A food uses and Mitopure exposes a branded product identity. That still does not equal FDA approval for cognition or all generic urolithin A products, and label-claim / marketplace-quality issues remain interpretation risks.^[raw/articles/fda-gras-2026-urolithin-a-grn-791.md]^[raw/articles/mitopure-2026-urolithin-a-product-identity.md]^[raw/articles/springer-2024-nmn-urolithin-label-claim-testing.md]
- [[paraxanthine]] is a newer stimulant ingredient with self-affirmed GRAS reporting for ENFINITY up to 300 mg in selected foods/beverages and explicit consumer-product stimulant warnings. Status should be read as product/dose context, not FDA efficacy review or proof that it is non-stimulant.^[raw/articles/nutraingredients-2021-enfinity-paraxanthine-self-affirmed-gras.md]^[raw/articles/rarebird-2026-paraxanthine-coffee-product-identity.md]
- [[methylliberine]] / Dynamine is a branded stimulant-stack ingredient with synthetic-identity toxicology and self-affirmed-GRAS/product-status reporting. Treat this as a product/dose identity gate, not as FDA-reviewed nootropic efficacy or proof that caffeine + theacrine + Dynamine stack results transfer to methylliberine alone.^[raw/articles/pmc-6930730-methylliberine-dynamine-toxicology.md]^[raw/articles/compoundsolutions-2026-dynamine-product-identity.md]^[raw/articles/nutritionaloutlook-2019-dynamine-self-affirmed-gras.md]
- [[mucuna-pruriens]] has a supplement wrapper but a drug-like interpretation problem because the relevant payload is L-DOPA. Product assays and prescription levodopa label context should gate any nootropic reading before community motivation anecdotes are considered.^[raw/articles/pmc-9361182-mucuna-supplement-levodopa-content.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-5808387-commercial-mucuna-levodopa-hplc.md]^[raw/articles/dailymed-2026-carbidopa-levodopa-safety-label.md]
- [[p-synephrine]] / bitter orange sits in the sports-visible stimulant-adjacent supplement lane: NCCIH notes NCAA stimulant status and label/adulterant problems, while WADA monitors synephrine in competition without listing it as prohibited in this pass.^[raw/articles/nccih-bitter-orange-synephrine-safety.md]^[raw/articles/wada-2026-monitoring-program.md]
- [[yohimbine]] has a supplement wrapper but NCCIH places the emphasis on serious adverse events, label variability, and antidepressant interactions; treat it as an adrenergic risk comparator before considering any focus or fat-loss anecdote.^[raw/articles/nccih-2025-yohimbe-usefulness-safety.md]
- [[higenamine-hordenine-octopamine]] is a preworkout-amine cluster where the status signal is stronger than cognition evidence: FDA's directory and warning-letter update flag the ingredients, WADA names higenamine as prohibited at all times and octopamine as in-competition prohibited, and supplement testing shows label-quantity uncertainty.^[raw/articles/fda-2026-dietary-supplement-ingredient-directory-higenamine-hordenine-octopamine.md]^[raw/articles/fda-2022-higenamine-hordenine-octopamine-warning-letters.md]^[raw/articles/wada-2026-higenamine-octopamine-prohibited-list.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-30188222-higenamine-supplement-quantity.md]^[raw/articles/jama-2023-botanical-sports-supplement-label-accuracy.md]
- [[tesofensine]] / Tesomet now has a separate status lane because the formal story is obesity and rare-obesity development, not cognition approval. A 2024 sponsor release says the Mexican obesity application had not yet been approved, while the 2025 Saniona annual report frames Tesomet as an investigational orphan-designated program; WADA 2026 also names tesofensine in S6.B, so sports visibility is explicit. Sibutramine and nomifensine supply withdrawn-class caution rather than nootropic precedent.^[raw/articles/saniona-2024-mexico-tesofensine-not-approved.md]^[raw/articles/saniona-2025-annual-report-tesomet-tesofensine.md]^[raw/articles/wada-2026-stimulant-list-afinils-pemoline-tesofensine.md]^[raw/articles/fda-2015-sibutramine-hidden-ingredient-withdrawn.md]^[raw/articles/pubchem-2026-nomifensine-withdrawn-hemolytic-anemia.md]
- [[phentermine]] now has a separate prescription-anorectic status lane because it is a Schedule IV short-term obesity drug with stimulant-like focus anecdotes, not a supplement or cognition drug. [[diethylpropion]], [[phendimetrazine]], and [[benzphetamine]] now keep the sibling-label risks explicit, while phentermine/topiramate adds an explicit cognitive-impairment warning and [[fenfluramine]] / current FINTEPLA REMS supply the serotonergic anorectic safety precedent.^[raw/articles/dailymed-2024-phentermine-label.md]^[raw/articles/dailymed-2025-qsymia-cognitive-impairment-label.md]^[raw/articles/dailymed-2026-sympathomimetic-anorectic-labels.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-9271479-fenfluramine-phentermine-valvular-disease.md]^[raw/articles/dailymed-2026-fenfluramine-fintepla-rems.md]
- [[pemoline]], [[cyclazodone]], and [[n-methyl-cyclazodone]] now have a pemoline-adjacent status lane. Pemoline has FDA label and approval-withdrawal records tied to liver-toxicity risk and is named by WADA 2026 in S6.B; cyclazodone/NMC are unapproved gray-market stimulants with PubChem identity, NMC toxicology, and DILI case-report context rather than cognition approval.^[raw/articles/fda-2002-cylert-pemoline-label-liver-warning.md]^[raw/articles/federalregister-2023-cylert-pemoline-approval-withdrawal.md]^[raw/articles/wada-2026-stimulant-list-afinils-pemoline-tesofensine.md]^[raw/articles/clinical-toxicology-2022-n-methyl-cyclazodone-toxicity.md]
- Research-only psychoplastogens should be treated as sports-visible by default if they are not otherwise approved, because WADA S0 covers non-approved pharmacologic substances.^[raw/articles/wada-2026-prohibited-list-general.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40354769-non-hallucinogenic-psychedelics-mood-anxiety-systematic-review.md]
- Device-like interventions should be read by family and indication, not by the generic word "device": the transcranial focused-ultrasound literature now splits lesioning, BBB opening, and low-intensity circuit stimulation into distinct clinical tools, and the safety literature keeps the calibration / parameter question front and center.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41610842-transcranial-focused-ultrasound-in-the-human-brain.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-31401074-safety-of-transcranial-focused-ultrasound-stimulation.md]

## Interpretation Rules

1. If it is a prescription wake-promoter, keep it in the comparator layer and check control status before calling it a benign enhancer.
2. If it is a dietary supplement or botanical, check product identity and hidden-ingredient risk before talking about efficacy.
3. If it is an unapproved research chemical, assume WADA S0 risk until proven otherwise.
4. If it is a device, ask what indication it is actually cleared for. A mechanism is not an authorization.
5. If label status and registry status diverge, keep both fields visible and do not let one erase the other.

## Open Contradictions

- I confirmed modafinil in the 2026 WADA S6.A stimulant list in this pass, but the map still does not attempt to cover every federation-specific rule set.
- Pitolisant did not surface on the 2026 WADA prohibited list in this pass, but absence from that list is not the same thing as a universal sports clearance.
- Device-like interventions still need a dedicated authorization/clearance pass by device family; this page only states the interpretation rule.
- EMA status here is a cross-check, not a full jurisdictional survey.
- Approval geography and registry status can point in opposite directions for the same molecule, as GV-971 and KarXT show.
- Peptide sourcing is still a mixed evidence layer: FDA identity, compounding-risk, and enforcement records exist, but they do not settle human-grade quality for all marketed analogs or suppliers.
