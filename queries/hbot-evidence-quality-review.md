# HBOT Evidence Quality Review

This is a strict-bias review of the HBOT cognition/recovery literature. The short version is that HBOT still looks biologically plausible and sometimes signal-rich, but the cleaner the design gets, the smaller and less consistent the cognitive effect becomes.

## What Changed Since 2024

- The 2020 severe-TBI randomized trial was retracted in January 2024, so it should no longer be counted as valid efficacy evidence.
- The 2025 meta-analysis reported large pooled cognitive gains, but it only included 4 studies (250 patients) and mixed randomized trials with retrospective cohorts; the trial subgroup was weaker for executive function and information processing speed.
- The older 2023 systematic review found 10 eligible studies but still judged the evidence limited and heterogeneous.
- The 2026 comprehensive review of 2015-2024 literature again described mixed outcomes and called for standardized protocols and longer follow-up.
- The 2014 sham-controlled PCS trial was null on symptom and secondary cognitive/psychomotor outcomes, and the 2015 sham-controlled TBI cognition trial was null overall as well.
- The civilian HOT-POCS placebo-gas study is now completed, but the registry still shows no posted results and only 12 enrolled participants; the record's last posted update was 2025-10-14.
- The net effect is that the strongest-looking HBOT signal is now more obviously dependent on protocol choice, comparator choice, and population.

## Summary

- HBOT has the clearest clinical rationale in recovery phenotypes such as TBI and persistent post-concussion symptoms.
- The best sham-controlled trials were null overall on cognition and PCS symptoms.
- The best positive PCS trial used a no-treatment control, not a sham control.
- The newest meta-analysis is encouraging on paper, but it is not a clean answer because it blends small randomized and non-randomized studies.
- Safety is not trivial: ear discomfort and other adverse effects are common enough that dose and pressure matter.

## Evidence Tiering

- Meta-analysis: the 2025 TBI neurocognitive review is positive, but its design mix limits interpretability and the randomized subset does not reproduce every domain-level gain.
- Systematic review: the 2023 TBI cognition review found the evidence limited and heterogeneous.
- Narrative review: the 2026 TBI structure review again called the literature mixed and emphasized protocol standardization.
- Sham-controlled RCT: the 2015 TBI study was null overall despite a few subgroup signals.
- Sham-controlled PCS trial: the 2014 military study was null on symptoms and secondary functional/cognitive/psychomotor outcomes.
- Randomized crossover without sham: the 2013 PCS trial was positive, but expectancy and period effects remain a real concern.
- Protocol/registry: HOT-POCS is the design HBOT needed for a cleaner answer, but the completed registry still has no efficacy results posted as of the latest registry update.
- Retraction-sensitive evidence: the 2020 severe-TBI trial should be excluded.

## Effect Size Snapshot

- 2025 meta-analysis reported statistically significant improvements in memory (MD 10.13), attention (MD 7.99), general cognition (MD 7.47), executive function (MD 7.16), information processing speed (MD 7.48), and motor skills (MD 5.19), but the two randomized studies alone were less convincing for executive function and information processing speed.
- The 2023 systematic review found 10 eligible studies but still judged the evidence limited and heterogeneous.
- The 2015 sham-controlled trial found no statistically significant overall between-group difference.
- The 2014 sham-controlled PCS trial found no signal on symptoms or secondary functional/cognitive/psychomotor outcomes.
- The 2013 PCS crossover trial reported significant gains in cognition and quality of life after HBOT relative to a no-treatment period.
- My inference: the apparent effect shrinks materially once you insist on a sham comparator and exclude retracted data.

## Safety / Adverse Events

- The broad HBOT adverse-effects meta-analysis found more adverse events with HBOT than control: 30.11% vs 10.43%.
- Ear discomfort was the most common adverse effect signal.
- Adverse events were more frequent with more than 10 sessions and with chamber pressure above 2.0 ATA.
- For cognition-oriented use, that makes pressure, session count, and ear/sinus screening central to the risk discussion.

## Responder Phenotypes

- Not established.
- The 2015 sham-controlled trial suggested possible responder patterns by concussion history, time from event to treatment, loss of consciousness, and select test components.
- The 2013 PCS crossover trial was in chronic mTBI/persistent PCS years after injury, which may be the phenotype most likely to show a signal.
- The 2025 meta-analysis does not provide a validated responder model.

## Protocol Parameters

- 2013 PCS crossover trial: 40 sessions, 1.5 ATA, 60 minutes, 100% oxygen.
- 2015 sham-controlled trial: 30 exposures, 2.4 ATA HBOT vs 1.3 ATA air sham.
- HOT-POCS protocol: 20 sessions, 2.0 ATA, 100% oxygen vs a placebo gas mixture of 10.5% oxygen and 89.5% nitrogen.
- 2025 meta-analysis: included studies used roughly 40-60 sessions and 60-90 minute sessions, which underscores heterogeneity.

## Open Contradictions

- Positive meta-analysis versus null sham-controlled RCT.
- Positive no-treatment crossover trial versus null sham-controlled trial.
- Retrospective/cohort signals versus no clean blinded replication.
- Retraction-aware synthesis versus older pages that still need to be read with the invalidated 2020 trial removed.
