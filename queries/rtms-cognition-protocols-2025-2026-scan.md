# rTMS Cognition Protocols 2025-2026 Scan

## Summary

rTMS is now best understood as a protocol family, not a single intervention. The 2024-2026 literature keeps DLPFC as the default target, but the newest reviews make three points hard to ignore: target matters, pulse load matters, and adjunctive use often looks better than stand-alone claims. The most decision-relevant shift since 2024 is that the field has moved from "does rTMS work?" to "which target, which dose curve, and which combination context?"^[raw/articles/pubmed-40676541-rtms-cognitive-impairment-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41191186-rtms-cognitive-emotional-symptoms-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41192010-rtms-non-pharmacological-interventions-cognitive-disorders.md]

The newest dose-response paper is the most useful protocol clue so far. Across AD and PD, the mean number of sessions was 14 and the mean total pulse load was 23,300, but cognition did not improve in a simple linear way as pulse count rose. In AD, MoCA and MMSE peaked in the mid-20k to high-20k pulse range, which is the clearest sign yet that there is a usable window rather than an unlimited upward slope.^[raw/articles/frontiers-2025-rtms-dose-response-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]

## Protocol Families

| Family | Evidence anchor | Current read |
|---|---|---|
| High-frequency DLPFC | PSCI and MCI meta-analyses | The default research recipe; still the clearest baseline comparator for cognition studies |
| Precuneus / AD network targeting | AD protocol design paper | Moves the field toward disease-network targeting and away from a one-site-fits-all assumption |
| Dose-window optimization | 2025 dose-response meta-analyses | Suggests intermediate cumulative pulse loads can be better than unbounded escalation for some endpoints |
| iTBS / high-intensity stroke comparator | Stroke cognition meta-analysis | Supports the idea that protocol family matters as much as target selection |
| Adjunctive rTMS | Sham-controlled add-on meta-analysis | Combination context can preserve or amplify the signal better than stand-alone enhancement claims |

What changed since 2024:

- DLPFC remains the anchor target, but the DLPFC versus non-DLPFC comparison is no longer a trivial footnote.
- Precuneus work in AD is maturing into explicit induction-plus-maintenance protocol design.
- Dose-response work suggests some outcomes peak at intermediate total pulse counts rather than improving monotonically.
- Healthy-adult evidence still exists, but the signal is small and highly heterogeneous.
- Add-on designs now look more convincing than stand-alone enhancement claims, especially in AD/MCI.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40065917-rtms-precuneus-alzheimers-study-design.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39866659-rtms-neuroenhancement-healthy-adults.md]

## Evidence Tiering

- Meta/systematic review: strongest layer here, especially for AD/PD and combined cognitive disorders.
- Randomized trials: enough to suggest clinical signal, but the field still depends on small, heterogeneous RCTs.
- Protocol/design papers: increasingly important for target selection and maintenance scheduling.
- Healthy-adult review evidence: present, but still much less stable than disease-cohort evidence.

## Effect Size Snapshot

- AD/PD meta-analysis: MoCA MD 2.13 and MMSE MD 1.16 favor rTMS; HAMA also improved, while ADL did not clearly separate.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40676541-rtms-cognitive-impairment-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]
- Dose-response meta-analysis: MoCA, HAMA, and ADL showed bell-shaped pulse-response curves, with approximate optima around 16,153, 12,138, and 17,237 total pulses; MMSE kept trending upward across the sampled range.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41191186-rtms-cognitive-emotional-symptoms-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]
- Frontiers dose-response meta-analysis: in AD, MMSE and MoCA peaked around 28,793 and 25,201 total pulses, respectively, which is the clearest signal that a moderate cumulative dose may be better than escalation for escalation's sake.^[raw/articles/frontiers-2025-rtms-dose-response-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]
- Add-on meta-analysis: rTMS as an adjunct produced a modest immediate global-cognition benefit (SMD 0.38) and a smaller but persistent 4-8 week signal (SMD 0.34).^[raw/articles/pubmed-41192010-rtms-non-pharmacological-interventions-cognitive-disorders.md]
- Network meta-analysis: rTMS was reported as superior to sham and to aducanumab/lecanemab/donanemab on cognition, but that claim still rests on indirect comparisons and short follow-up.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40386876-rtms-with-aducanumab-lecanemab-donanemab.md]
- Healthy-adult literature: effects exist, but the 2025 review still frames them as heterogeneous and sensitive to outlier handling.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39866659-rtms-neuroenhancement-healthy-adults.md]
- Protocol constants across the most usable cognition studies: multi-session designs dominate; DLPFC is still the most common target; and the best-looking disease-cohort signals cluster around high-frequency or iTBS-like families rather than low-intensity one-off exposure.^[raw/articles/pmid-38113027-psci-hf-rtms.md]^[raw/articles/pmid-37759936-mci-rtms.md]^[raw/articles/pmid-31901449-rtms-guidelines.md]

## Safety / Adverse Events

The recent reviews do not surface a new safety alarm, and the network meta-analysis suggests tolerability/acceptability is generally better than the anti-amyloid comparator drugs. The real safety problem is narrower and more practical: sham control, reporting quality, and the fact that protocol complexity makes it easy to overinterpret a small positive study.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40386876-rtms-with-aducanumab-lecanemab-donanemab.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41192010-rtms-non-pharmacological-interventions-cognitive-disorders.md]

## Responder Phenotypes

- AD and MCI remain the clearest disease cohorts for cognition-relevant signal.
- PD appears responsive in the broader neurodegenerative meta-analytic literature, but the effect sizes are not obviously interchangeable with AD.
- Healthy adults can show task-specific effects, but the variance is high and the effect is not a general-purpose enhancer signal.
- Stroke cognition may respond to high-intensity and iTBS-style protocols, which is useful as a comparator for time-efficient dosing.

## Protocol Parameters

- Target: DLPFC remains the default target in the current cognition literature, but the precuneus is now an explicit AD network target and non-DLPFC sites are part of the comparison set.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40676541-rtms-cognitive-impairment-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40065917-rtms-precuneus-alzheimers-study-design.md]
- Frequency/type: excitatory or high-frequency protocols dominate the cognition literature; healthy-adult and stroke work keeps iTBS in the picture as a time-efficient variant.^[raw/articles/pubmed-37526325-rtms-age-related-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40769285-rtms-stroke-cognitive-impairment.md]
- Pulse count: the newest dose-response work argues for intermediate total pulse loads for MoCA, HAMA, and ADL, while MMSE may benefit from higher cumulative dose rather than a midrange plateau.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41191186-rtms-cognitive-emotional-symptoms-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]
- Pulse count: the Frontiers analysis makes the window more concrete, with AD cognition peaking around 25k-29k total pulses and an overall mean of 23,300 pulses across the dataset.^[raw/articles/frontiers-2025-rtms-dose-response-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]
- Session count: the evidence base is uniformly multi-session, but the exact count is not stable enough to summarize as a single best number; the practical takeaway is that one-off sessions are not the relevant comparison.
- Combination context: adjunctive rTMS with cognitive training, Tai Chi, or tDCS appears more reproducible than stand-alone enhancement claims in AD/MCI.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41192010-rtms-non-pharmacological-interventions-cognitive-disorders.md]
- Practical protocol range: the current literature keeps circling roughly a two-week to one-month course with a mid-20k total pulse window in AD-like cohorts, but the healthy-adult and stroke comparators warn that the best window is phenotype-dependent rather than universal.^[raw/articles/frontiers-2025-rtms-dose-response-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40769285-rtms-stroke-cognitive-impairment.md]

## Open Contradictions

- Meta-analyses agree that rTMS can move cognition in some cohorts, but they disagree on how much of the signal is target-specific versus dose-specific.
- The DLPFC is still the default target, but the field is now testing whether network hubs such as the precuneus are better disease-specific choices in AD.
- Healthy-adult enhancement remains plausible but not decisive, especially once outliers and montage differences are handled.
- The combination literature is promising, but it is still underpowered and heterogeneous enough that treatment hierarchy claims remain provisional.
- The dose-response papers are not fully aligned on the exact pulse optimum, which means "intermediate dose window" is robust while the exact window should still be treated as provisional.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41191186-rtms-cognitive-emotional-symptoms-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]^[raw/articles/frontiers-2025-rtms-dose-response-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]
