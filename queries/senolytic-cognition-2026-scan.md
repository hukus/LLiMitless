# Senolytic Cognition 2026 Scan

## Summary

- [[dasatinib-quercetin]] now has two published human cognition-adjacent signals: STAMINA in older adults at risk for Alzheimer's disease and a small phase 1 mild-AD biomarker study. Both are open-label and too small for efficacy claims, but they are no longer purely preclinical.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40010154-senolytics-cognition-mobility-at-risk.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40274471-dasatinib-quercetin-phase1-mild-alzheimer-biomarkers.md]
- The strongest human signal remains feasibility and biomarker movement rather than validated cognitive benefit. STAMINA reported a mean MoCA increase of 1.0 point overall (95% CI -0.7, 2.7), with a larger gain in the lowest baseline subgroup (+2.0 points, 95% CI 0.1, 4.0), but there was no control group for functional outcomes.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40010154-senolytics-cognition-mobility-at-risk.md]
- The mild-AD phase 1 paper confirms CNS exposure to dasatinib and shows exploratory biomarker shifts, but the biomarker direction is mixed rather than cleanly pro-cognitive.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40274471-dasatinib-quercetin-phase1-mild-alzheimer-biomarkers.md]
- A 2025 sex-differences review now gives the branch a clearer modifier hypothesis: senolytic response may depend on sex, estrogen context, senescent-cell burden, and treatment timing, which matters for how future cognition data should be stratified.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40103928-sex-senescence-senolytics-cognition.md]
- The preclinical ladder is more supportive than the human ladder: rats showed persistent memory improvement, a 2026 HFD/palmitate mouse study restored hippocampal-dependent cognition after senolytic treatment, and NHPs showed reduced inflammaging and better metabolic markers without cognition testing.^[raw/articles/pubmed-35042834-dasatinib-quercetin-aged-rats-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41895394-senolytic-therapy-hfd-hippocampal-senescence-cognitive-decline-mice.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-37261678-dasatinib-quercetin-nonhuman-primates-aging-inflammation.md]
- [[fisetin]] is still a frailty / inflammation program in humans. The main active older-adult trial uses inflammatory and frailty outcomes, not cognition, so [[fisetin]] should not be upgraded to a cognition candidate on current human evidence.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct03675724-fisetin-frailty-inflammation-older-adults.md]
- [[foxo4-dri]] now adds a peptide-senolytic branch with stronger preclinical/mechanistic identity than human evidence. The 2017 Cell paper and 2025 Nature Communications mechanism paper make the FOXO4-p53 axis real enough to track, but ClinicalTrials.gov API searches returned zero records for `FOXO4-DRI` and `Proxofim` on the 2026-05-05 data refresh, and no human cognition evidence is captured.^[raw/articles/sciencedirect-2017-foxo4-dri-targeted-apoptosis-senescent-cells.md]^[raw/articles/nature-2025-foxo4-dri-p53-tad2-mechanism.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-2026-foxo4-dri-search-no-record.md]
- [[quercetin]], [[fisetin]], and [[dasatinib-quercetin]] now have standalone entity pages, but D+Q remains combination-level and safety-first until controlled human cognition data exist.
- Safety is the gating issue: dasatinib inherits oncology-grade warnings, so any senolytic cognition protocol needs much tighter monitoring than a nutraceutical stack.^[raw/articles/dailymed-dasatinib-tablet-label.md]

## What Changed Since 2024

- 2025 produced the first published STAMINA pilot result in older adults at risk for AD.
- 2025 also produced a biomarker-focused follow-up from the first mild-AD senolytic trial, making the human branch more concrete even though the evidence is still very small.
- 2025 added a protocol for older adults with schizophrenia, schizoaffective disorder, and treatment-resistant depression, which moves the concept into accelerated-aging psychiatry rather than pure dementia research.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40443429-dasatinib-quercetin-cognitive-decline-mental-disorders-protocol.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct05838560-dasatinib-quercetin-accelerated-aging-mental-disorders.md]
- 2025-2026 registry freshness shows two live pipeline branches: ALSENLITE remains active/not recruiting for tau-PET-positive MCI/AD, and the mental-disorders D+Q protocol is recruiting with repeated MRI and biomarker follow-up.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct04785300-alsenlite-senolytics-alzheimers.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct05838560-dasatinib-quercetin-accelerated-aging-mental-disorders.md]
- 2026-04-30 freshness pass: AFFIRM-LITE still reads as support-only because its primary endpoint remains frailty/inflammation rather than cognition.
- The branch now has a better translational ladder, but it still lacks a randomized cognition efficacy trial.
- Standalone [[dasatinib-quercetin]] now separates senolytic-combination evidence from [[quercetin]]-alone flavonol evidence.

## Evidence Tiering

- Human controlled cognition: none yet.
- Human open-label / feasibility: STAMINA and the mild-AD phase 1 senolytic trial.
- Human registry pipeline: ALSENLITE, the D+Q mental-disorders protocol, and [[fisetin]] frailty / inflammation trials.
- Human review layer: sex-difference framing suggests senolytic response may not be sex-neutral.
- Preclinical obesity / metabolic-state rescue: 2026 HFD/palmitate mouse work adds a clearer mechanistic cognition lane, but still not a human effect.
- FOXO4-DRI peptide branch: mechanism and disease-model extensions only; no human registry record or cognition endpoint captured.
- Nonhuman primate: inflammaging / metabolic effects only.
- Rodent: direct spatial-memory improvement with washout durability.
- Mechanistic: senescence marker suppression, anti-inflammatory shifts, and synaptic / histone changes.

## Effect Size Snapshot

- STAMINA: MoCA +1.0 point overall; +2.0 points in the lowest baseline subgroup; TNF-α change about -3.0% with an inverse correlation to MoCA change (r = -0.65, p = 0.02).^[raw/articles/pubmed-40010154-senolytics-cognition-mobility-at-risk.md]
- STAMINA abstract details from the full text mirror the raw note but add uncertainty intervals: MoCA +1.0 points overall (95% CI -0.7, 2.7), +2.0 points in the lowest baseline subgroup (95% CI 0.1, 4.0), TNF-α -3.0% (95% CI -13.0, 7.1), and about 10% of telephone-screened individuals completed the intervention (n = 12).
- Mild-AD phase 1: no clean cognition effect size in the abstract; biomarker changes were exploratory and mixed.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40274471-dasatinib-quercetin-phase1-mild-alzheimer-biomarkers.md]
- Rat: cognition improved and remained better after treatment cessation, but the effect is not directly translatable to humans.^[raw/articles/pubmed-35042834-dasatinib-quercetin-aged-rats-cognition.md]
- 2026 HFD/palmitate mouse: D+Q reduced hippocampal senescence and restored hippocampal-dependent cognition and synaptic proteins; no controlled human analogue exists yet.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41895394-senolytic-therapy-hfd-hippocampal-senescence-cognitive-decline-mice.md]

## Safety / Adverse Events

- Dasatinib carries label warnings for myelosuppression, bleeding, fluid retention, cardiovascular toxicity, pulmonary arterial hypertension, QT prolongation, severe dermatologic reactions, tumor lysis syndrome, and hepatotoxicity.^[raw/articles/dailymed-dasatinib-tablet-label.md]
- STAMINA used heavy screening exclusions for cardiac, hematologic, renal, hepatic, and interacting-drug risk, which is a practical reminder that this is not a casual self-experiment candidate.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct05422885-dasatinib-quercetin-stamina.md]
- The STAMINA pilot reported no serious adverse events related to the intervention, but that is only small-study reassurance.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40010154-senolytics-cognition-mobility-at-risk.md]

## Responder Phenotypes

- Best current human phenotype: older adults with slow gait, MCI, and low baseline MoCA.
- Mild-AD phase 1 participants show that CNS exposure and biomarker movement are possible, but responder biology is still undefined.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40274471-dasatinib-quercetin-phase1-mild-alzheimer-biomarkers.md]
- Sex may be a real effect modifier, at least in the preclinical / review literature, so future D+Q cognition work should stratify or at minimum check sex and hormone status.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40103928-sex-senescence-senolytics-cognition.md]
- ALSENLITE is enriched for tau-PET-positive MCI / AD, which is a cleaner disease phenotype than the broader older-adult pilot.
- The mental-disorders protocol targets accelerated-aging phenotypes in schizophrenia / schizoaffective disorder / TRD, so it is best read as a psychiatric-aging translational branch rather than an AD branch.
- [[fisetin]] is currently being tested in frailty / inflammation phenotypes rather than cognition phenotypes.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct03675724-fisetin-frailty-inflammation-older-adults.md]
- AFFIRM-LITE is a randomized, placebo-controlled study of [[fisetin]] 20 mg/kg/day for 2 consecutive days, but its primary endpoint is percent decrease in blood inflammation markers rather than cognition.

## Protocol Parameters

- STAMINA: 100 mg dasatinib + 1250 mg [[quercetin]] orally for 2 consecutive days every 2 weeks over 12 weeks.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40010154-senolytics-cognition-mobility-at-risk.md]
- Mild-AD phase 1: intermittent D+Q over 12 weeks; biomarker sampling was the main analytic focus.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40274471-dasatinib-quercetin-phase1-mild-alzheimer-biomarkers.md]
- ALSENLITE: dasatinib 100 mg + [[quercetin]] 1000 mg daily for 2 consecutive days every 15 days, 6 cycles total, 77 days.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct04785300-alsenlite-senolytics-alzheimers.md]
- Mental-disorders protocol: about 1 year total participation, 8 total doses across weeks 1 to 4, baseline MRI, week-10 MRI, and blood draws at baseline, weeks 1, 2, 3, 4, 10, and endpoint.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct05838560-dasatinib-quercetin-accelerated-aging-mental-disorders.md]
- NHP: 5 mg/kg dasatinib + 50 mg/kg [[quercetin]] on 2 consecutive days monthly for 6 months, with caloric restriction layered in month 6.^[raw/articles/pubmed-37261678-dasatinib-quercetin-nonhuman-primates-aging-inflammation.md]
- Rat: 8 weeks of D+Q, with persistent post-treatment benefit.^[raw/articles/pubmed-35042834-dasatinib-quercetin-aged-rats-cognition.md]
- AFFIRM-LITE: [[fisetin]] versus placebo for frailty / inflammation, not cognition.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct03675724-fisetin-frailty-inflammation-older-adults.md]
- AFFIRM-LITE registry status: enrolling by invitation; last update posted 2025-11-20; estimated primary completion 2026-04.

## Open Contradictions

- The human branch is still too small to know whether the MoCA signal is real or mostly regression to the mean / expectancy / practice effect.
- The biomarker direction is mixed in the mild-AD phase 1 paper, so a simple "senolytic = anti-inflammatory = cognitive" chain is not yet proven.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40274471-dasatinib-quercetin-phase1-mild-alzheimer-biomarkers.md]
- The [[fisetin]] human program is not cognition-first, which limits how much it can support a cognition-enhancement claim today.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct03675724-fisetin-frailty-inflammation-older-adults.md]
- Open-label pipelines remain the dominant human format, so expectancy and practice effects still sit on top of any true biological signal.
- Sex and hormone status may change the direction or magnitude of response, but current human datasets are too small to stratify robustly.
- The freshest registry signal is still support-only: a placebo-controlled [[fisetin]] frailty trial, not a cognition efficacy study.
- The newest 2026 mouse paper strengthens the metabolic rescue hypothesis, not the broad healthy-enhancement case.
- The FOXO4-DRI branch is even less translated than D+Q: the mechanism is sharper, but human trial, dose-ranging, safety, and cognition data are absent in this pass.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-2026-foxo4-dri-search-no-record.md]
