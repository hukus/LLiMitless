# AMPAkines and Glutamatergic Plasticity Enhancers - 2026 Scan

## Summary

AMPAkines had a real translational story: strong rodent and nonhuman-primate cognition data, plus a plausible synaptic-plasticity mechanism that made them look like a clean route to memory enhancement. The human record is now mixed rather than simply empty. [[cx717]] has a 2025 adult-ADHD phase 2A publication with a positive secondary repeated-measures analysis at 800 mg BID, but the primary paired t-test was only trend-level, the study was disease-state and sponsor-linked, and older healthy-human sleep-loss / shift-work trials stayed mixed to negative. [[cx516]] failed in schizophrenia and fragile X syndrome, [[tak-653]] remains target-engagement only, [[cx1739]] is safety/respiratory-development only, and [[cx691-farampator]] is preclinical. The older high-impact / research-chemical lane now has standalone routing through [[idra-21]] and [[cx614]]: IDRA-21 is animal/primate-positive but human-absent, while CX614 is BDNF/plasticity-positive but safety-ceiling dominated.

What changed since 2024:

- A 2025 review of AMPA and kainate receptor PAMs explicitly says most clinical studies over the past decades failed or were stopped because of adverse events or limited efficacy, which is the clearest recent synthesis of the field's stalled translation.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40650226-ampa-kainate-modulators-cns-review.md]
- A 2024 mechanistic paper now explains why the older high-impact compounds were hard to develop: they can drive Gq-linked ER calcium release and hyperexcitability, whereas low-impact CX516/CX717 did not show the same linkage and CX1739 remained safe at very high doses.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39304968-high-impact-ampakines-toxic-calcium-release.md]
- A high-impact research-chemical branch is now explicit. IDRA-21 has older rat and nonhuman-primate cognition signals plus modern gray-market/anecdote visibility, but no human cognition trial; CX614 has strong BDNF / dendritic-translation / TrkB signaling biology and also a clearer high-impact hyperexcitability warning.^[raw/articles/pubmed-7815345-idra21-rat-cognition-ampa-desensitization.md]^[raw/articles/sciencedirect-2004-idra21-rhesus-delayed-matching.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-3073152-huperzine-a-idra21-young-macaques.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-6774091-cx614-neurotrophin-expression.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-2761758-cx614-bdnf-release-mrna-translation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39304968-high-impact-ampakines-toxic-calcium-release.md]
- A 2025 adult-ADHD phase 2A paper changed the CX717 branch: 800 mg BID was not significant on the primary paired t-test, but a secondary repeated-measures endpoint analysis favored CX717 over placebo on total ADHD-RS and inattentive / hyperactivity subscales.^[raw/articles/sciencedirect-2025-cx717-adhd-phase2a.md] This supports disease-state tracking, not broad healthy-adult enhancement.
- A 2022 healthy-volunteer TAK-653 study showed a modern low-impact AMPAR PAM can move CNS excitability and some NeuroCart measures, but it still stopped short of a cognition-efficacy signal.^[raw/articles/pubmed-36153330-tak653-tms-cns-effects.md]
- A 2025 first-in-human CX717 safety and PK paper gave the low-impact ampakine story a modern restart, and a 2025 CX1739 healthy-volunteer paper extended that low-impact safety/PK lane, but both were still development studies rather than efficacy breakthroughs.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39892449-cx717-safety-pharmacokinetics.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39302241-cx1739-safety-pharmacokinetics.md]
- A 2025 CX691/farampator paper extended the low-impact lane into antipsychotic-like rescue logic and a spatial-memory readout in rats, which is useful translationally but still preclinical and company-linked.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40245529-cx691-farampator-antipsychotic-like-low-impact-ampakine.md]
- A 2026 juvenile global cerebral ischemia paper keeps a narrow rescue phenotype alive: delayed type II ampakines raised hippocampal BDNF, restored LTP, and improved learning/memory after injury, which is promising for recovery biology but still not human enhancement evidence.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41612671-type-ii-ampakines-juvenile-global-cerebral-ischemia.md]
- The translational center of gravity has shifted from "cognition enhancer" to "low-impact salvage program": the current human package is mostly ADHD symptom work, PD, PK, and safety, while the remaining plausible niche is state-bound rescue or diagnosed vulnerability states rather than rested healthy-adult enhancement.
- No recent healthy-adult cognition trial surfaced that clearly reverses the older negative pattern. The modern literature is still mostly trying to salvage the class by lowering AMPAR impact, tightening the safety margin, and looking for state-sensitive or disorder-specific readouts.
- The original translational promise still matters because CX717 did show strong primate performance effects, especially under sleep deprivation, but that promise has not transferred cleanly into human cognitive enhancement.^[raw/articles/pubmed-16104830-cx717-nonhuman-primate-sleep-deprivation.md]
- June 2026 routing update: AMPAR-PAM anecdotes and gray-market claims now need an excitatory-ceiling and product-identity gate before they can affect rankings.

For comparator framing, see [[pharmacology-comparator-map]] and [[most-promising-methods-for-cognition]]. For the broader intervention landscape, see [[medicine-cognition-summary]] and [[neurocognitive-interventions-under-study]].

## Why Translation Stalled

- The class sits close to an excitatory ceiling: the historical fear was not theoretical, and the modern low-impact chemotypes are explicitly trying to avoid the seizure / overactivation edge rather than prove a stronger efficacy signal.
- The human signal is phenotype-bound, not universal. Sleep loss, shift work, and disorder-context rescue are the only places where the branch ever looked plausibly useful, and even there the studies are mixed or negative.
- The development story has drifted toward target engagement and safety packages. That keeps the chemistry alive, but it does not close the cognition-efficacy gap.
- The most defensible remaining niche is rescue under vulnerability states, not broad enhancement in rested healthy adults.

## Evidence Tiering

- Preclinical / translational positive: CX717 improved performance and reversed sleep-deprivation impairment in nonhuman primates.
- Preclinical / nonhuman-primate positive: IDRA-21 improved rat learning/memory tasks and nonhuman-primate delayed matching / visual-recognition performance, but no human cognition trial is captured.
- Preclinical mechanism-positive but safety-negative: CX614 increased BDNF/plasticity signaling in cultured neuron and slice systems, but high-impact AMPAkine biology also carries hyperexcitability concerns.
- Preclinical rescue-positive: delayed type II ampakines improved hippocampal BDNF, LTP, and memory after juvenile global cerebral ischemia.
- Human disease-state signal: CX717 800 mg BID showed a positive secondary repeated-measures ADHD-RS analysis in adults with ADHD, but the primary paired t-test was only trend-level and the trial does not generalize to healthy cognition.
- Human experimental medicine mixed-to-negative: CX717 did not show consistent prespecified benefit in acute sleep deprivation or simulated night shift work.
- Human RCT negative: CX516 failed in schizophrenia and fragile X syndrome.
- Human registry/protocol only: the CX516 MCI program surfaced as a progress report rather than a final efficacy paper.
- Human pharmacodynamic-positive but not efficacy-proven: TAK-653 increased TMS-evoked excitability and improved some NeuroCart metrics in healthy volunteers.
- Human safety/PK positive for development: low-impact CX717 and CX1739 now have modern tolerability packages, but that is not the same as cognition efficacy.
- Preclinical rescue-positive with a schizophrenia-adjacent twist: CX691/farampator now adds antipsychotic-like activity and a spatial-memory readout, but it is still an animal paper and should not be read as human cognition proof.

## Effect Size Snapshot

- CX516 schizophrenia: no difference in the composite cognitive score or any test at weeks 4 or 8; the reported between-group effect sizes were small and inconsistent.
- CX717 sleep deprivation: prespecified analyses were null; exploratory repeated measures suggested an attention gain only at 1000 mg.
- CX717 shift work: no reversal of alertness/performance decline at 200, 400, or 1000 mg.
- CX717 adult ADHD: 800 mg BID trended on the primary paired t-test but separated on secondary repeated-measures total ADHD-RS and inattentive / hyperactivity subscale analyses; 200 mg BID was not significant.
- CX717 primates: dose-dependent performance improvement plus reversal of sleep-deprivation impairment, which is the strongest signal in the entire branch.
- IDRA-21 animals/primates: rat water-maze/passive-avoidance and pharmacologic-impairment reversal, plus rhesus delayed matching-to-sample and young-macaque visual-recognition improvements; still no human effect-size bridge.
- TAK-653 healthy volunteers: some NeuroCart measures moved in the predicted direction, but the outcome set was physiological / psychopharmacologic rather than a memory endpoint.
- CX1739 healthy volunteers: no efficacy endpoint, only tolerability and dose-proportional PK.
- CX614 mechanism studies: BDNF and TrkB/plasticity readouts moved in slices/cultures, while prolonged exposure also reduced AMPAR subunit expression; this is a mechanism packet rather than behavioral cognition evidence.

## Safety / Adverse Events

- CX717 was generally tolerable, but the 2025 safety study still found headache, dizziness, and nausea.
- In the 2025 adult-ADHD paper, CX717 was reported as well tolerated, with sleep disturbance and headache the most frequent adverse events and no significant cardiovascular changes in the captured abstract.
- CX1739 was also generally tolerable, with headache and nausea the prominent adverse events.
- CX717 reduced slow-wave sleep in human studies, which matters because a "cognition enhancer" that degrades recovery sleep can simply trade one problem for another.
- CX516 in schizophrenia produced fatigue, insomnia, and epigastric discomfort.
- Fragile X trial safety was acceptable overall, but the efficacy signal was absent.
- The class's historical development problem was never only efficacy; it was always efficacy plus a narrow safety margin for too-strong AMPAR potentiation.
- High-impact AMPAkines now have a plausible mechanistic toxicity explanation rather than a vague "too excitatory" story: a Gq/ER-calcium-release pathway with hyperexcitability and convulsion liability.
- IDRA-21's early partial-modulator neurotoxicity paper is reassuring only inside that older cell-culture comparison; it does not establish human safety, chronic use safety, or stack safety.^[raw/articles/pubmed-9192690-idra21-partial-modulator-neurotoxicity.md]
- IDRA-21 has visible gray-market presence in smart-drug surveillance and Reddit self-experimentation, so product identity and dose accuracy are part of the safety signal rather than side details.^[raw/articles/sciencedirect-2024-smart-drugs-japan-idra21.md]^[raw/articles/edqm-2025-mssip007-nootropics-idra21.md]^[raw/articles/reddit-2020-2026-idra21-ampakine-experience-threads.md]
- TAK-653 did not show a major subjective-drug-effect burden in healthy volunteers, but the signal was still not a cognition win.

## Responder Phenotypes

- The closest thing to a responder phenotype is diagnosed or state-based rather than trait-based: adult ADHD, sleep-deprived, or high-load conditions seem more plausible than rested healthy baseline enhancement.
- The modern low-impact development story is also state-based: healthy-volunteer CNS-PD studies suggest measurable target engagement, but not a clear enhancer phenotype.
- A separate recovery phenotype exists in juvenile injury biology: delayed type II ampakines helped after global cerebral ischemia in mice, so the remaining niche is rescue rather than baseline enhancement.
- Even there, the human evidence is weak enough that no stable responder atlas exists.
- Psychiatric and fragile-X cohorts did not convert the biological rationale into measurable cognitive benefit.

## Protocol Parameters

- TAK-653 healthy-volunteer CNS-PD study: 0.5 mg and 6 mg single doses in a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover design with TMS and NeuroCart readouts.
- CX717 adult ADHD phase 2A: 200 mg BID and 800 mg BID across two 3-week crossover periods with a 2-week washout; primary endpoint was ADHD-RS at 3 weeks.
- CX717 human cognition studies: 100, 300, and 1000 mg single doses in acute sleep deprivation; 200, 400, and 1000 mg in simulated night shift work.
- CX717 safety/PK study: single doses up to 1600 mg, repeated dosing up to 800 mg BID, and a 300 mg QD elderly cohort.
- CX1739 safety/PK study: 100 to 1200 mg single-dose escalation and 300 to 600 mg BID multiple-dose ascending regimens for 7 to 10 days.
- Juvenile global cerebral ischemia mouse rescue: delayed type II ampakine dosing at 14 days after injury.
- IDRA-21 animal/primate packet: rats received oral IDRA-21 in the 4-120 umol/kg range in the water-maze work; the rhesus delayed matching paper used oral dosing and the later macaque visual-recognition paper cites earlier 0.15-10 mg/kg monkey dosing context.
- CX516 schizophrenia RCT: 900 mg three times daily for 4 weeks.
- CX516 fragile X: 4-week randomized controlled treatment after a 1-week placebo lead-in.
- CX516 MCI trial: international placebo-controlled design, but the PubMed record available here is only a progress report.

## Open Contradictions

- The rodent and primate literature still looks stronger than the human efficacy record, even after the newer adult-ADHD signal.
- The low-impact ampakine strategy is a real safety correction, but there is still no proof that the safer chemistry will translate into a meaningful cognition effect.
- The newer low-impact studies show adult-ADHD symptom movement, target engagement, and tolerability, which keeps the branch alive, but they do not solve the healthy-cognition efficacy gap.
- The CX691/farampator paper adds a newer low-impact rescue lane, but it is preclinical, company-linked, and still far from a human cognition bridge.
- The 2026 juvenile ischemia paper keeps a narrow recovery niche alive, but it does not generalize to healthy-adult enhancement.
- Some early small studies suggested promise, yet the more rigorous human RCTs in schizophrenia and fragile X were negative.
- CX717's adult-ADHD secondary-analysis signal sits beside older negative healthy-human sleep-loss evidence, so the class should be read as phenotype-bound rather than as globally failed or globally validated.
- The field may still have a niche in ADHD, sleep-loss countermeasures, respiratory-drive rescue, or injury-state recovery, but that is not the same as broad healthy-adult enhancement.
- IDRA-21 is a good example of why animal/primate cognition plus anecdotes should not outrank human-absent safety uncertainty.
- CX614 is a good example of why "BDNF up" and "plasticity signaling" should not be promoted without behavior, chronic safety, and excitability constraints.
