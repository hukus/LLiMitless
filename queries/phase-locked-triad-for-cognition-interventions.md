# Phase-Locked Triad for Cognition Interventions

## Summary

The current evidence supports a three-layer timing model rather than a single "best time" rule. Chronotype or circadian phase can change the response window, session timing can matter even within the same day, and some interventions need alignment to an endogenous phase marker such as a spindle, slow oscillation, or theta rhythm. A 2025 chronotype review adds that chronotype usually does not have a large main effect on cognition, but synchrony effects still show up in attention, inhibition, and memory, especially in older adults. A 2025 CLAS preprint goes one step further and suggests that pre-sleep EEG state can predict both slow-wave enhancement and next-day cognitive response. That makes the useful unit for several cognition branches not just `device` or `drug`, but `device/drug + when + phase`.^[raw/articles/pubmed-34344864-chronotype-and-tdcs-timing-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-30035659-tdcs-time-of-day-episodic-memory-retrieval.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-35321778-tacs-insomnia-time-of-day.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40102385-phase-specific-enhancement-motor-memory-consolidation-closed-loop-tmr.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40626105-targeting-sleep-spindles-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41110657-auditory-stimulation-sleep-varies-thalamocortical-spindle-phase.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40307237-closed-loop-theta-hippocampal-connectivity.md]^[raw/articles/chronobiology-international-2025-chronotype-synchrony-human-cognitive-performance-systematic-review.md]^[raw/articles/medrxiv-2025-11-12-bedtime-brain-state-clas-sleep-cognition.md]

What changed since 2024 is that the timing story is no longer only chronotype or chronotherapy folklore. A direct tDCS study now ties motor learning and cognitive performance to circadian-preferred timing, an episodic-memory paper shows morning-session sensitivity, a small tACS insomnia study hints at state sensitivity during polysomnography, and phase-specific closed-loop sleep work shows that the exact internal event matters within the sleep window itself. The 2025 chronotype review also softens the old "chronotype predicts cognition" claim by showing more synchrony than main-effect signal, while the new CLAS preprint shifts attention toward pre-sleep brain state as a response marker.^[raw/articles/pubmed-34344864-chronotype-and-tdcs-timing-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-30035659-tdcs-time-of-day-episodic-memory-retrieval.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-35321778-tacs-insomnia-time-of-day.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40102385-phase-specific-enhancement-motor-memory-consolidation-closed-loop-tmr.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40626105-targeting-sleep-spindles-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41110657-auditory-stimulation-sleep-varies-thalamocortical-spindle-phase.md]^[raw/articles/chronobiology-international-2025-chronotype-synchrony-human-cognitive-performance-systematic-review.md]^[raw/articles/medrxiv-2025-11-12-bedtime-brain-state-clas-sleep-cognition.md]

The best current interpretation is inference, not a validated universal rule: timing misalignment can create apparent nulls, while phase alignment can make the same intervention look meaningfully better. The triad is strongest for tDCS/tACS and sleep-based closed-loop stimulation, weaker for broad healthy-adult enhancement, and still not proven as a three-way responder biomarker in one trial.

## Triad Model

| Layer | Question | Current evidence | Failure mode if ignored |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trait / circadian phase | When is the person naturally better or more vulnerable? | Chronotype and circadian preferred time matter for some tDCS, light, and sleep-loss contexts.^[raw/articles/pubmed-34344864-chronotype-and-tdcs-timing-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41113437-chronotype-cognitive-outcomes-neural-dynamics-perioperative.md] | The sample averages out a responder subgroup. |
| Session phase | At what clock time was the intervention delivered? | Morning tDCS and circadian-preferred sessions outperformed non-preferred timing in the direct timing papers.^[raw/articles/pubmed-30035659-tdcs-time-of-day-episodic-memory-retrieval.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-34344864-chronotype-and-tdcs-timing-cognition.md] | A good protocol looks inconsistent across replications. |
| Endogenous oscillation phase | Was the cue delivered on the right internal event? | Slow-wave up-state cueing, spindle-phase targeting, and theta-locked stimulation all show phase-sensitive effects.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40102385-phase-specific-enhancement-motor-memory-consolidation-closed-loop-tmr.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40626105-targeting-sleep-spindles-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41110657-auditory-stimulation-sleep-varies-thalamocortical-spindle-phase.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40307237-closed-loop-theta-hippocampal-connectivity.md] | The intervention becomes biologically active but behaviorally weak or even disruptive. |

## Evidence Tiering

- Human chronotype-by-time-of-day timing evidence: strongest for tDCS, with direct circadian-preferred versus non-preferred comparisons and morning-session memory effects.^[raw/articles/pubmed-34344864-chronotype-and-tdcs-timing-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-30035659-tdcs-time-of-day-episodic-memory-retrieval.md]
- Human chronotype/synchrony review evidence: a 2025 systematic review found mostly no main chronotype effect but recurring synchrony effects, especially for attention, inhibition, memory, and older adults.^[raw/articles/chronobiology-international-2025-chronotype-synchrony-human-cognitive-performance-systematic-review.md]
- Human timing-sensitive, but not chronotype-stratified, evidence: tACS insomnia work and perioperative chronotype review material support the idea that time-of-day and state matter even when chronotype is not measured well.^[raw/articles/pubmed-35321778-tacs-insomnia-time-of-day.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41113437-chronotype-cognitive-outcomes-neural-dynamics-perioperative.md]
- Human phase-specific closed-loop evidence: up-phase TMR and spindle-phase auditory stimulation show that within-window timing can change the direction of the effect.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40102385-phase-specific-enhancement-motor-memory-consolidation-closed-loop-tmr.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40626105-targeting-sleep-spindles-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41110657-auditory-stimulation-sleep-varies-thalamocortical-spindle-phase.md]
- Human pre-sleep state prediction evidence: pre-sleep alpha/theta power and sleep-onset latency predicted CLAS responsiveness and next-day cognition in a 2025 preprint.^[raw/articles/medrxiv-2025-11-12-bedtime-brain-state-clas-sleep-cognition.md]
- Physiology-first phase-locking evidence: theta-synchronized stimulation improved hippocampal network connectivity, which is an important mechanism anchor but not a healthy-adult performance proof.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40307237-closed-loop-theta-hippocampal-connectivity.md]

## Responder Phenotypes

- Eveningness versus morningness, especially when the outcome is sleep phase, mood, or a chronotherapeutic response.
- Synchrony effects, especially in older adults and on attention/inhibition/memory tasks, even when chronotype has little main effect.
- Sleep debt or circadian misalignment, which can amplify the value of timing-aware interventions.
- Pre-sleep EEG state, especially alpha/theta power and sleep-onset latency, as a possible CLAS response marker.
- Sleep-stage dependent responders, where the main modifier is not trait chronotype but whether the cue lands in the correct slow-oscillation or spindle window.
- Perioperative or otherwise circadian-disrupted patients, where the review literature suggests timing may matter more than it does in rested healthy volunteers.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41113437-chronotype-cognitive-outcomes-neural-dynamics-perioperative.md]

## Protocol Parameters

- tDCS timing paper: testing at 8:00 and 19:00, with better performance at circadian-preferred times.^[raw/articles/pubmed-34344864-chronotype-and-tdcs-timing-cognition.md]
- tDCS episodic-memory paper: morning stimulation favored recollection accuracy, including a randomized morning-versus-afternoon comparison.^[raw/articles/pubmed-30035659-tdcs-time-of-day-episodic-memory-retrieval.md]
- tACS insomnia paper: 0.75 mA, 0.75 Hz, 5 minutes, bilateral frontal, delivered during polysomnography.^[raw/articles/pubmed-35321778-tacs-insomnia-time-of-day.md]
- REM CLAS paper: alpha and theta phase targeting during REM sleep induced phase-dependent power and frequency changes in 18 healthy young adults.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39208441-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation-alpha-theta-rem-phase-dependent-power-frequency.md]
- Closed-loop TMR paper: slow-wave up-phase versus down-phase cueing during overnight sleep.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40102385-phase-specific-enhancement-motor-memory-consolidation-closed-loop-tmr.md]
- Spindle-targeting auditory stimulation: spindle-detected delivery before spindle offset, with a warning that early delivery can terminate the spindle it is trying to engage.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40626105-targeting-sleep-spindles-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation.md]
- Theta-locked stimulation: lateral temporal cortex stimulation phase-locked to hippocampal theta in neurosurgical patients.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40307237-closed-loop-theta-hippocampal-connectivity.md]
- Pre-sleep CLAS prediction paper: 8 minutes of pre-sleep EEG before lights off were enough to predict responsiveness with machine-learning models.^[raw/articles/medrxiv-2025-11-12-bedtime-brain-state-clas-sleep-cognition.md]

## Open Contradictions

- Chronotype matters in some chronotherapy and light settings, but fixed-time light can still work without chronotype matching, so trait timing is not a universal personalization key.
- Chronotype itself often has no main effect in the new review, so synchrony is a better label than "chronotype main effect" for many cognition studies.
- Phase-specific sleep stimulation is more concrete than chronotype personalization, which means the strongest timing signal may live inside sleep-state alignment rather than clock-time scheduling.
- tACS and tPBM still look too sparse for chronotype-based personalization, so the triad is not yet a general device rule.
- Pre-sleep brain state may matter more than chronotype for CLAS responsiveness, but that is a state marker, not a trait marker.
- The strongest evidence is for mood, sleep timing, and memory consolidation; broad healthy-adult executive enhancement remains thinner.
- The triad is an inference from separate source lines rather than a direct three-way interaction trial.
