# tDCS and tACS Parameter-Response Scan

This scan turns the current tDCS/tACS branch from a generic "does it work?" question into a parameter map: target, montage, frequency band, phase lag, current density, session count, and whether stimulation is paired with training or used alone. The strongest 2025-2026 signal is that tDCS effects are not flat across settings: the broad 2025 meta-analysis reports significant overall cognitive effects across 69 studies and 5,545 participants, while the dosage meta-analysis still points to current density, duration, session count, and target as the practical levers. The tDCS branch now has a second responder-phenotype signal on top of age and dose: focal language-network stimulation can depend on baseline cognition, with older adults who start worse showing the clearest naming or fluency benefit. The tACS branch now has a sharper disease-state split and a sharper timing story: theta-band and maintenance-oriented protocols do more of the work in healthy adults, a 45° versus 180° phase-lag protocol shows that inter-network phase is itself a dose variable, and a five-session bifrontal study shows that some gains only emerge weeks later. A 2026 older-cognitive-impairment meta-analysis shows memory and overall cognition benefit but no attention effect. Region-specific mapping also suggests the story is more anatomical than frequency-only.^[raw/articles/pubmed-38060075-tdcs-cognitive-functions-review.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39905569-tdcs-dosage-parameters.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39438426-tacs-working-memory-meta-2025.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40097468-phase-lagged-tacs-executive-default-mode-working-memory.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41461804-bifrontal-tacs-long-term-working-memory-episodic-memory.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41034500-tacs-older-adults-meta-2025.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41507453-tacs-cognitive-impairment-meta.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40933163-theta-tacs-working-memory-healthy-adults.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41784599-tacs-long-term-memory-mapping.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40174444-hdtdcs-region-specific-cognition-older-adults.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40347355-focal-tdcs-picture-naming-baseline-performance.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41870727-tdcs-verbal-fluency-baseline-cognition-site.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41449818-tdcs-auditory-false-perceptions-null.md]

For timing interpretation, keep clock-time, chronotype, and endogenous phase-locking separate; the three-layer framing is captured in [[phase-locked-triad-for-cognition-interventions]].

**What changed since 2024**

- tDCS moved from a vague adjunctive option to a parameter-sensitive intervention: the broad review found overall cognitive effects, but current density, duration, session count, target location, timing, intensity, population, and age all moderate the result.
- New focal language-network studies in 2025-2026 suggest baseline cognition also matters: older adults with poorer naming or fluency started to show the clearest gains, which is a stronger responder-phenotype claim than age alone.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40347355-focal-tdcs-picture-naming-baseline-performance.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41870727-tdcs-verbal-fluency-baseline-cognition-site.md]
- tACS moved from a speculative oscillation tool to a branch with two visible subchannels: theta-band working-memory effects and gamma-band AD/MCI memory protocols.
- tACS now has a cleaner impairment-cohort signal: the 2026 meta-analysis shows benefit for memory and overall cognition in older adults with cognitive impairment, but not for attention.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41507453-tacs-cognitive-impairment-meta.md]
- New evidence also suggests older age increases tDCS response variability, so average effects likely hide responders.
- New 2026 mapping also suggests both tDCS and tACS are target-dependent in ways that are stronger than the surface summary implied: HD-tDCS shows distinct M1, DLPFC, and cerebellar effects, and tACS shows region-by-frequency interactions rather than a single theta story.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40174444-hdtdcs-region-specific-cognition-older-adults.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41784599-tacs-long-term-memory-mapping.md]

## Evidence Tiering

| Tier | tDCS | tACS |
|---|---|---|
| Meta-analysis | 2024-2025 meta-analyses now isolate density, duration, sessions, and target effects | 2025 meta-analyses isolate theta, gamma, online/offline, and AD vs healthy splits |
| Randomized trial | vmPFC vs dlPFC stress-WM study; montage crossover studies | 40 Hz dementia crossover trial; active gamma protocol studies in AD/MCI and healthy older adults |
| Registry / active trial | Parameter-rich optimization still sparse | Several active/completed gamma-branch trials now track memory and oscillation outcomes |

## Effect Size Snapshot

| Modality | Signal | Magnitude |
|---|---|---|
| tDCS | Broad cognitive-function review | Significant overall effects across 69 studies / 5,545 participants, with moderation by timing, population, duration, intensity, site, and age |
| tDCS | Immediate global cognition in older adults with impairment | SMD 0.16 overall in one 2024 meta-analysis; MMSE SMD 0.51 immediately and 2.29 at follow-up in the 2025 dosage meta-analysis |
| tDCS | Parameter subgroups | Better signals at current density <= 0.06 mA/cm2, duration > 20 min, <= 15 sessions, and temporal targets |
| tDCS | Age effect | Active-response variability about 21% higher in healthy older vs young adults |
| tDCS | Baseline cognition | Older adults with poorer naming or fluency appear more likely to benefit from focal left IFG or related language-network stimulation |

| tACS | Working memory overall | g = 0.06 overall; g = 0.102 for n-back; g = 0.207 for theta tACS on n-back |
| tACS | Maintenance | offline stimulation showed a small benefit for WM maintenance (g = 0.127) |
| tACS | Older adults memory | SMD 0.66 immediate memory and 0.68 delayed memory, with the clearest benefit in AD rather than healthy older adults |

## Safety / Adverse Events

- The recent tDCS and tACS literature still reports mostly mild, transient effects such as tingling, itching, or local discomfort, with no new cognition-specific safety signal emerging in these scans.
- Trial registries continue to exclude epilepsy/seizure risk and implanted devices for gamma tACS studies.
- The bigger safety issue is often indirect: overconfident stacking can worsen sleep, irritability, or fatigue and erase the small signal these methods can produce.

## Responder Phenotypes

- Older adults with AD appear more likely than healthy older adults to show a visible tACS memory signal.
- Older adults with cognitive impairment may show more tDCS benefit on global cognition than MCI or dementia subgroups, but not on memory or executive function.
- High-stress working-memory conditions may favor vmPFC-targeted tDCS over generic dlPFC targeting.
- Older age itself likely increases response variance, so individual-level responders matter more than group means.
- Baseline cognition may be a second responder gate: low-starting naming or fluency can matter as much as age or montage.
- Healthy-adult theta tACS looks positive before publication-bias correction but nearly null after correction, while the 2026 field-map paper suggests site-by-frequency effects that are not captured by a simple pooled estimate.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40933163-theta-tacs-working-memory-healthy-adults.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41784599-tacs-long-term-memory-mapping.md]
- tDCS target choice is now more obviously phenotype-dependent: M1, DLPFC, and cerebellum do not behave like interchangeable versions of the same treatment.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40174444-hdtdcs-region-specific-cognition-older-adults.md]
- tACS phase-lag and follow-up timing now complicate the usual "frequency plus site" framing: the same branch can be positive only with a specific inter-network phase or only at a delayed time point.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40097468-phase-lagged-tacs-executive-default-mode-working-memory.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41461804-bifrontal-tacs-long-term-working-memory-episodic-memory.md]
- tDCS model-shaped montages still do not guarantee the intended perceptual/cognitive effect, so anatomical plausibility should not be mistaken for behavioral proof.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41449818-tdcs-auditory-false-perceptions-null.md]

## Protocol Parameters

### tDCS

- Current density at or below 0.06 mA/cm2 was associated with better global cognition in the 2025 dosage meta-analysis.
- Session duration above 20 minutes and up to 15 sessions looked better than shorter or more prolonged schedules.
- Temporal targets outperformed left DLPFC for global cognition in the older-impaired meta-analysis.
- Montage category and reference-electrode location were not significant in that meta-analysis, but a healthy-adult crossover study found a broad-frontal montage improved 2-back reaction time.
- Focal language-network studies now show that a left IFG or TPJ setup can be the right target only in the right baseline phenotype; older low-baseline naming/fluency performers are the clearest responders.
- In a stress-induced WM task, vmPFC stimulation beat dlPFC stimulation for behavior.
- A healthy-participant null shows that a model-shaped montage can still fail on a false-perception task, so anatomical plausibility alone is not enough to predict effect direction.

### tACS

- Theta-band tACS is the clearest working-memory frequency signal in the 2025 meta-analysis, especially for n-back tasks.
- Offline tACS seems better for WM maintenance than a pure stand-alone online claim.
- Gamma-band 40 Hz protocols are now the major AD/MCI branch, with precuneus or frontal/frontotemporal targets under active study.
- One registry protocol uses 40 Hz at the precuneus with 3.2 mA peak-to-peak for four 12-minute blocks.
- Another 40 Hz dementia trial used 1.5 mA peak-to-peak over left DLPFC with contralateral supraorbital reference during simultaneous cognitive exercises.
- Healthy-adult theta tACS shows a modest uncorrected WM signal, but bias correction pushes the estimate toward null.
- The 2026 memory-mapping paper argues for region- and frequency-specific interpretation instead of a single generic tACS mechanism, including negative or nonlinear associations in some frontal and temporal sites.
- Phase lag is now part of the dose: 45° and 180° inter-network offsets produced different WM and connectivity patterns in a 2025 task-fMRI study.
- Some tACS gains appear delayed rather than immediate: five consecutive bifrontal sessions improved WM and episodic memory only at 4 weeks, not right after treatment.

### HD-tDCS

- A 2025 HD-tDCS trial in older adults separated M1, DLPFC, and cerebellar effects rather than treating all frontal stimulation as equivalent.
- M1 and DLPFC improved flexibility and processing speed, while cerebellar stimulation selectively improved working memory.
- This is the clearest reminder that target selection is part of the dose, not just an implementation detail.

## Open Contradictions

- tDCS montage matters in specific trials, but montage category was not significant in the 2025 dosage meta-analysis.
- tDCS combined with training is often assumed to be better, but the older-impaired meta-analysis favored tDCS alone while the post-stroke rehabilitation meta-analysis favored combined tDCS + cognitive rehab.
- tDCS broad-review positivity conflicts with the durability/null story in older-adult and aMCI branches, so the field is clearly not flat but also not uniformly durable.
- tDCS baseline-performance gating conflicts with any simple average-effect framing: the same site can help or not help depending on how low the subject starts.
- tACS appears positive in AD and small-to-moderate in some healthy-younger WM settings, but the healthy-older-adult signal is weaker or null.
- tACS memory gains are clearer than executive-function gains, yet the working-memory meta-analysis still describes the overall effect as minimal.
- Healthy-adult theta tACS can look positive before publication-bias correction and nearly null after it, which makes moderator choice and publication-bias handling central to interpretation.
- The 2026 memory-mapping paper suggests theta and gamma effects may reverse across regions, so "theta works" is too blunt a conclusion.
- tACS enhancement and tACS treatment are different questions: the healthy-adult branch is fragile, but the older-cognitive-impairment branch now looks more treatment-like.
