# CBT-I and Neuromodulation Sequence Effects on Cognition

## Summary

No direct human `CBT-I -> neuromodulation` cognition RCT surfaced, but the 2025 literature makes the sequencing question more concrete. A 2025 insomnia-phenotype rTMS trial improved both sleep quality and cognitive flexibility, and a 2025 chronic-insomnia fNIRS study showed rTMS shifting prefrontal activation alongside symptoms; that means the insomnia state is clearly part of the stimulation signal, not just background noise.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40276332-rTMS-cognitive-flexibility-insomnia-symptoms.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40267527-rTMS-chronic-insomnia-fnirs.md]

The best current inference is still sleep-first: stabilize sleep if cognition is the endpoint, then test stimulation as a second-stage amplifier. That remains an inference, not a proven rule. The closest explicit order evidence still comes from `CBT-I` followed by `PAP` in comorbid insomnia/sleep apnea and an older rTMS-then-CBT case report in tinnitus/insomnia.^[raw/articles/pubmed-34648425-cbt-i-pap-sequence-daytime-performance.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-28484405-rTMS-cbt-tinnitus-insomnia-combined-approach.md]

## What Changed Since 2024

- CBT-I now has explicit cognition-facing trials, so sleep treatment can no longer be treated as cognition-irrelevant.^[raw/articles/pubmed-32128593-dcbt-i-cognitive-function-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-35691208-cbt-i-objective-sleep-cognition.md]
- rTMS now has a 2025 insomnia-phenotype cognition trial, which strengthens the idea that sleep state and stimulation state interact.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40276332-rTMS-cognitive-flexibility-insomnia-symptoms.md]
- A 2025 chronic-insomnia fNIRS study and the 2025 mPFC-rTMS sham-controlled trial both show that stimulation can alter insomnia symptoms and prefrontal function, not just subjective sleep.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40267527-rTMS-chronic-insomnia-fnirs.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40774161-mpfc-rTMS-chronic-insomnia.md]
- The closest explicit sequencing evidence is still `CBT-I` plus `PAP` and an older rTMS-then-CBT case report, not a direct CBT-I-plus-brain-stimulation cognition trial.^[raw/articles/pubmed-34648425-cbt-i-pap-sequence-daytime-performance.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-28484405-rTMS-cbt-tinnitus-insomnia-combined-approach.md]

## Effect Size Snapshot

- Direct combo effect size: none found.
- The 2025 insomnia-phenotype rTMS trial reported better sleep quality, better cognitive-flexibility scores, lower switch cost, and follow-up ERP differences in the active arm versus sham.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40276332-rTMS-cognitive-flexibility-insomnia-symptoms.md]
- The 2025 chronic-insomnia fNIRS study reported stronger prefrontal activation after rTMS alongside symptom improvement.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40267527-rTMS-chronic-insomnia-fnirs.md]
- mPFC-rTMS insomnia: response rate 51.7% and remission rate 44.8% in the active arm versus 20.8% and 16.7% in sham.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40774161-mpfc-rTMS-chronic-insomnia.md]
- rTMS-then-CBT case report: single-case improvement, not a generalizable effect size.^[raw/articles/pubmed-28484405-rTMS-cbt-tinnitus-insomnia-combined-approach.md]

## Safety/Adverse Events

- CBT-I remains the lowest-risk component in the sequence, with the main issue being temporary sleep restriction discomfort rather than device-related harm.
- rTMS and tDCS generally have mild acute adverse effects in the broader literature, but combining them with sleep deprivation or insomnia can magnify irritability, fatigue, and attribution error.^[raw/articles/pubmed-32843299-rtms-sleep-deprivation-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-33524838-tdcs-sleep-deprivation-recovery.md]
- The main safety lesson here is sequencing, not toxicity: a sleep-fragmented state can make a stimulation protocol look better or worse than it really is.

## Responder Phenotypes

- Chronic insomnia with daytime cognitive complaints.
- Undergraduates or high-demand adults with insomnia symptoms and executive-control complaints.
- Chronic insomnia with prefrontal dysfunction or abnormal task activation.
- Older adults with insomnia and objective sleep-state misperception.
- Sleep-deprived high-demand participants where the goal is acute countermeasure rather than durable enhancement.
- Potential future responders with both insomnia and another cognition-limiting condition, such as MCI, chronic pain, or OSA.

## Protocol Parameters

- CBT-I trials in this layer use 4-8 weekly sessions or 8 weekly group sessions, with follow-up windows out to 24 weeks in the dCBT-I study.^[raw/articles/pubmed-32128593-dcbt-i-cognitive-function-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-35691208-cbt-i-objective-sleep-cognition.md]
- The CBT-I plus PAP sequencing trial used CBT-I first in one arm and concurrent delivery in another arm.^[raw/articles/pubmed-34648425-cbt-i-pap-sequence-daytime-performance.md]
- The rTMS sequence analog used 10 sessions of rTMS followed by 10 CBT sessions.^[raw/articles/pubmed-28484405-rTMS-cbt-tinnitus-insomnia-combined-approach.md]
- The insomnia-phenotype rTMS cognition trial used 1 Hz left DLPFC stimulation for 2 weeks, 10 sessions total.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40276332-rTMS-cognitive-flexibility-insomnia-symptoms.md]
- The chronic-insomnia fNIRS study used 1 Hz right DLPFC stimulation for 2 weeks.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40267527-rTMS-chronic-insomnia-fnirs.md]
- The mPFC-rTMS insomnia trial used 20 sessions.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40774161-mpfc-rTMS-chronic-insomnia.md]
- The sleep-loss comparator studies used 24 h to 30+ h deprivation windows, which is a very different state from a stabilized insomnia cohort.^[raw/articles/pubmed-32843299-rtms-sleep-deprivation-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-33524838-tdcs-sleep-deprivation-recovery.md]

## Open Contradictions

- It is still unclear whether sleep should always be stabilized before stimulation, or whether some hyperarousal phenotypes might benefit from stimulation first.
- rTMS during sleep deprivation may represent an acute rescue strategy, not a durable cognition strategy.
- CBT-I can temporarily restrict sleep during treatment, which could confound any simultaneous cognition readout.
- The current evidence does not answer whether concurrent CBT-I plus neuromodulation is better, worse, or merely harder to interpret than serial treatment.
