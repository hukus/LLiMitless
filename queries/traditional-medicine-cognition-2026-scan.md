# Traditional Medicine Cognition 2026 Scan

This pass focused on standardized botanical or traditional-medicine extracts with actual human cognition endpoints, not broad folklore or multi-herb formulas with no chemistry control. The main pattern is simple: the best recent signals come from standardized extracts in narrow phenotypes, while broad healthy-adult enhancement remains weak or null. Broader Chinese herbal medicine meta-analyses are a separate, formula-level lane: they look positive in MCI and as an adjunct to donepezil, but the chemistry is not yet as reproducible as a named single extract. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-38425647-chinese-herbal-medicine-mci-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39134455-chinese-herbal-medicine-donepezil-mci-meta-analysis.md]

## Summary

- The cleanest recent positive human signal I found is standardized grape extract in healthy older adults, where a 250 mg/day extract improved immediate and delayed memory plus several broader cognitive scores.
- Aronia melanocarpa extract now has two small cognition RCTs in different age bands, and both point in the same narrow psychomotor-speed / reaction-time direction. That makes it the clearest reproducible near-pass in the branch, even though the effect is still too narrow to call it a general nootropic.
- Pomegranate now has a 2020 juice-memory stabilization trial and a 2025 extract RCT, which makes it interesting but still split across product identities.
- Ashwagandha now has a 2025 randomized MCI pilot with a standardized extract and Sominone that reported improvements in memory and working memory after 30 and 60 days.
- Ginseng now has both a 2024 standardized sprout-extract RCT in subjective memory impairment and a 2026 systematic review / meta-analytic package that keeps the branch modest but real, while sharpening the point that species and ginsenoside profile matter.
- Bacopa still has a plausible signal, but the strongest controlled evidence is older and phenotype-dependent; the 2025 review suggests the branch is now limited more by extract composition, bioavailability, and dose standardization than by a lack of mechanistic interest.
- Ginkgo now has a stronger standardized-extract story: the 2025 mild-dementia subgroup meta-analysis and the 2026 pooled-data meta-analysis in cerebral-infarction-related dementia both lean positive, but review quality and sponsor homogeneity are still worth keeping visible. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-39895346-ginkgo-egb761-mild-dementia-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40121884-ginkgo-egb761-overview-systematic-reviews.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-13023401-ginkgo-egb761-cerebral-infarction-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39476880-ginkgo-faers-safety.md]
- Saffron has cognition-positive signals in MCI and CABG recovery contexts, but the evidence is still phenotype-specific and not a general-purpose nootropic story.
- Curcumin now has a modest pooled signal in meta-analysis, but the branch is still formulation-sensitive and the older AD RCT remains a cautionary comparator. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-40308636-curcumin-cognitive-function-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-23107780-curcumin-ad-rct.md]
- Phlai essential-oil inhalation now has two small human trials, one mood/stress pilot and one acute cognition RCT, which is enough to merit a low-confidence standalone node even though the route is aromatherapy rather than an oral extract. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-31618177-phlai-mood-stress-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41614069-phlai-cognition-rct.md]
- Broader Chinese herbal medicine meta-analyses in MCI and as an adjunct to donepezil are positive, but they remain formula-level rather than fixed single-extract evidence. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-38425647-chinese-herbal-medicine-mci-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39134455-chinese-herbal-medicine-donepezil-mci-meta-analysis.md]
- The Acacia catechu + Scutellaria baicalensis extract trial is a useful null/weak comparator because the study showed no clear between-group advantage despite within-group time effects.

## Standalone-Page Candidates

- `Aronia melanocarpa extract`: two product-specific cognition RCTs now exist across different age bands, and both point to a narrow psychomotor-speed / reaction-time signal. The product is standardized enough to track as its own entity, and it is the clearest near-pass for a standalone page.
- `Pomegranate`: the cognition literature now has both a juice-memory stabilization trial and a newer extract trial, but the product identity is split enough that the branch still reads better as a comparison entry.
- `Grape polyphenol products`: the branch has multiple RCTs across grape extract, grape-blueberry extract, and grape-seed procyanidins, but the products are not yet interchangeable enough to merge into one clean standalone page without more normalization work.
- `Phlai essential-oil blend`: two small human trials now exist, one mood/stress pilot and one acute cognition RCT, so the exposure is specific enough for its own page even though the evidence is still acute and sensory-confounded.

## Evidence Tiering

### Stronger recent human signals

- `Grape extract`: the 2024 RCT in healthy older adults is the most compelling recent standardized-botanical cognition result in this branch. It is also easy to interpret because the product was a named extract with a fixed daily dose and multiple cognitive readouts.
- `Ashwagandha`: the 2025 MCI pilot is promising because it is recent, standardized, and directly cognition-facing.

### Older but still relevant standardized-extract signals

- `Bacopa`: older RCTs support memory and attention-related endpoints, but the literature is not newly crowded with clean standalone cognition trials.
- `Ginseng`: the new meta-analysis and sprout-extract RCT make this a small but real older-adult branch, especially for subjective memory complaints and impaired phenotypes.
- `Ginkgo`: older RCTs and MCI work support a phenotype-restricted signal, especially for cognitive control and memory-related impairment.
- `Saffron`: cognition-positive studies exist, but they cluster in impaired or recovery contexts rather than healthy enhancement.

### Mixed or null branches

- `Acacia + Scutellaria`: within-group improvements did not translate into a clear placebo-separated cognitive benefit.
- `Curcumin`: the meta-analytic signal is now positive, but the older AD RCT was negative and formulation heterogeneity keeps the branch from graduating to a clean win.

## Current Read

### 1) Ashwagandha

The 2025 MCI pilot is the first recent result that makes ashwagandha look like more than a stress herb. The caveat is that this is still a small study, so the real question is whether the signal reproduces in larger, better-powered MCI or chemotherapy-cognitive-dysfunction cohorts. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-40099725-ashwagandha-mci-rct.md]

The ongoing chemotherapy-cognitive-dysfunction trial is useful because it tests a recognized impairment phenotype rather than healthy enhancement. That matters because it may tell us whether the extract is more of a recovery aid than a general nootropic. ^[raw/articles/ctgov-nct04092647-ashwagandha-cognitive-dysfunction.md]

### 2) Bacopa

Bacopa still looks biologically plausible, but the best direct cognition evidence is older. The standardized-extract RCT in age-associated memory impairment showed memory-domain improvements, and the active registry work is now pushing Bacopa into Gulf War Illness and vascular-oxidative-stress phenotypes rather than healthy-adult enhancement. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-20703343-bacopa-aami-rct.md]^[raw/articles/ctgov-nct04927338-bacopa-gwi.md]

The 2025 review adds that the main bottleneck is not whether bacopa is "traditional" but whether a given product has consistent bacosides, bioavailability, and enough dose standardization to interpret a cognition outcome. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-40507208-bacopa-monnieri-preclinical-clinical-bioavailability.md]

That pattern is consistent with a compound that may help when fatigue, stress, or memory vulnerability are already present, but it does not yet justify broad healthy-adult claims.

### 3) Ginseng

Ginseng now has both a product-specific RCT and a fresh meta-analysis. The 2024 sprout-extract trial in subjective memory impairment improved verbal learning, figure recall, MoCA-K, and sleep quality, while the 2026 meta-analysis in older adults found modest gains on MMSE and ADAS-Cog with a subgroup signal around 4.5 g/day. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-38931306-panax-ginseng-sprout-extract-smi-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41863264-ginseng-cognitive-function-meta-analysis.md]

The practical read is still conservative: the effect is small, phenotype-sensitive, and product-dependent, but it is now strong enough to keep ginseng in the standardized-extract branch instead of treating it as folklore.

### 4) Ginkgo

Ginkgo now has a better impairment-phenotype story than it did a year ago. The 2016 EGb 761 pilot improved cognitive control / task-switch cost in subjective memory impairment, the 2025 mild-dementia subgroup meta-analysis found benefit at 240 mg/day, and the 2025 overview of systematic reviews was generally favorable for neurocognitive disorders. That said, the overview quality was poor and the FAERS safety layer still argues against treating ginkgo as automatically benign. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-27147264-ginkgo-egb761-smi-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39895346-ginkgo-egb761-mild-dementia-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40121884-ginkgo-egb761-overview-systematic-reviews.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39476880-ginkgo-faers-safety.md]

The practical read is that EGb 761 is one of the more defensible traditional extracts to study, but it should be treated as a phenotype-specific intervention with explicit adverse-event monitoring.

### 5) Grape extract

The 2024 Cognigrape trial is the strongest recent healthy-adult result in this branch. It used a named standardized extract, a fixed 250 mg/day dose, and showed improvements across immediate and delayed memory, language, attention, and broader neuropsychological batteries. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-39335927-grape-extract-cognition-rct.md]

If this wiki needs a traditional-medicine candidate with a modern, reproducible chemistry story, grape extract is near the top of the current list.

### 6) Phlai essential-oil blend

The phlai branch is different from the oral standardized-extract lane because the exposure is inhaled essential oil, not a swallowed capsule. That said, it now has a 2019 mood/stress pilot and a 2026 cognition RCT, both with fairly clear product characterization. The most defensible read is that acute state modulation is real enough to keep studying, but the branch is still too small and route-specific to promote into a general cognition enhancer. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-31618177-phlai-mood-stress-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41614069-phlai-cognition-rct.md]

### 7) Saffron

Saffron is more phenotype-specific than the internet narrative suggests. The cognition-positive evidence is concentrated in MCI and CABG recovery, not in healthy enhancement, so it belongs in a recovery or impairment branch rather than a universal nootropic branch. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-29185780-saffron-cabg-cognition-rct.md]

### 8) Curcumin

Curcumin is no longer a pure null branch. The 2025 meta-analysis found a modest improvement in global cognition across nine RCTs, but the effect sits on top of an older negative AD trial and a formulation problem that still makes product identity matter more than the ingredient name. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-40308636-curcumin-cognitive-function-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-23107780-curcumin-ad-rct.md]

### 9) Chinese herbal medicine

Broader Chinese herbal medicine meta-analyses in MCI are interesting because they are still cognition-positive even when the intervention is not a single standardized extract. The 2024 placebo-controlled MCI meta-analysis found MMSE and MoCA gains, and the 2024 donepezil-adjunct meta-analysis found similar MMSE/MoCA improvements with no clear AE penalty, but the formula-level heterogeneity is why this branch stays separate from the standardized-extract ranking. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-38425647-chinese-herbal-medicine-mci-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39134455-chinese-herbal-medicine-donepezil-mci-meta-analysis.md]

### 10) Acacia + Scutellaria

This is a useful reality-check study because it used a standardized product, a placebo control, and biomarkers. The trial found no clear between-group cognitive advantage, so it should be filed as a weak/null branch rather than an enhancement win. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-39701593-acacia-scutellaria-rct.md]

## What Changed Since 2024

- A new standardized ashwagandha MCI pilot appeared in 2025 and made the extract look more cognition-relevant than it did in the older anxiety/sleep literature.
- Standardized grape extract added a recent healthy-older-adult signal that is cleaner than most of the older botanical literature.
- Aronia melanocarpa extract now has two cognition RCTs across different age bands, which is enough to move it out of the "single-study curiosity" bucket and makes it the clearest near-pass for a standalone page.
- Pomegranate added both a 2020 juice-memory RCT and a 2025 extract trial, but the branch is still split between product forms.
- Ginseng now has both a product-specific sprout-extract RCT and a 2026 review package that keeps the branch modest but real while emphasizing species and constituent specificity.
- The Acacia catechu + Scutellaria baicalensis extract trial gave the wiki a recent null/weak comparator in a clean RCT design.
- Ginkgo now has both a 2025 mild-dementia subgroup meta-analysis and a 2026 pooled-data meta-analysis in cerebral-infarction-related dementia, which strengthens the impairment-phenotype case while also showing how sponsor-homogeneous data can inflate confidence.
- Curcumin now has a modest pooled signal in the 2025 meta-analysis, so the branch is better framed as formulation-sensitive and translationally limited rather than flatly null.
- Phlai essential-oil inhalation is now a low-confidence but real traditional-medicine cognition node, with acute state-modulation data and a GC-MS-characterized blend.
- Broader Chinese herbal medicine meta-analyses in MCI and as an adjunct to donepezil also surfaced, but they remain formula-level rather than fixed single-extract evidence.

## Safety / Practical Notes

- Standardized extracts are not automatically safe because they are traditional.
- Ginkgo deserves explicit AE tracking because of the FAERS signal.
- Curcumin deserves a bioavailability note and GI-tolerability caution.
- Ashwagandha and bacopa should not be treated as generic stress supplements when the target is cognitive dysfunction; the phenotype matters.
- Combination products are weaker evidence than single standardized extracts, even when they look promising inside a supplement label.
- Inhaled aromatic blends like phlai should be treated as a separate route class because sensory and expectancy effects are harder to rule out than with oral capsules.
- Formula-level CHM should stay separated from standardized extracts so the wiki does not overstate reproducibility.
