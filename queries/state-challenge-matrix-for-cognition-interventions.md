# State-Challenge Matrix for Cognition Interventions

The main lesson of this pass is that challenge-state rescue is real, but it is not the same thing as baseline enhancement. The clearest human signals now cluster under sleep loss, hypoxia, perioperative inflammation, and a few metabolic-load states. When the challenge changes, the signal can disappear: ketones look better at rest under hypoxia than during hypoxia plus exercise, and taVNS can modulate stress physiology without a clean cognitive win.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39190580-ketone-monoester-hypoxic-exposure-resting.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40315254-ketone-ester-hypoxia-null.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40908377-taVNS-mental-stressor.md]

Use the state labels below as the shared taxonomy for trial design, measurement, and recommendation pages. The intervention can change from page to page; the participant state should not be implicit.

## State Taxonomy

| Canonical label | Includes | Why it stays separate |
|---|---|---|
| Rested baseline | Well-slept, non-challenged comparator state | Comparator state for baseline enhancement claims, not a rescue state |
| Sleep pressure / sleep restriction | Acute sleep loss, chronic restriction, sleep banking, sleep extension | Sleep loss changes vigilance and executive control, so rescue effects should not be pooled with rested-baseline gains |
| Acute stress / autonomic load | Mental stressors, threat paradigms, stress-reactive arousal, taVNS stress studies | Autonomic and subjective state can move without a broad cognition win |
| Hypoxia / oxygen constraint | Acute hypoxia, altitude-like exposure, hypoxia plus exercise | Oxygen limitation is the perturbation, so rest-state and exercise-state results should stay separate |
| Postprandial / dysglycemic load | Meal timing, postprandial sitting, glucose excursions, CGM-linked load | Metabolic timing is part of the challenge, not just a background covariate |
| Exercise / heat fatigue | Exertional load, heat stress, load carriage, physical fatigue | Physical strain is a distinct rescue problem from sleep loss or hypoxia |
| Perioperative / inflammatory recovery | Surgery, anesthesia recovery, delirium, postoperative cognitive dysfunction | Acute recovery-state rescue, not broad healthy-adult enhancement |
| Injury recovery / neurologic deficit | TBI, concussion, stroke, rehab, MTBI | Recovery cohorts are more fragile and should not be merged with healthy-adult rescue work |

When a study fits multiple labels, choose the one that best matches the manipulated state. If the design intentionally crosses states, name both and do not average away the difference.

## Summary

- Sleep-loss rescue is now more convincing than a generic nootropic story: creatine has a direct sleep-deprivation RCT, rTMS has a sleep-deprivation RCT, tDCS has a pilot recovery-sleep study, and sleep banking / extension sit in the same resilience lane.^[raw/articles/pubmed-38418482-creatine-sleep-deprivation-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-32843299-rtms-sleep-deprivation-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-33524838-tdcs-sleep-deprivation-recovery.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41892386-sleep-banking-cognitive-motor-impairments-sleep-restriction.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40984126-sleep-extension-cognitive-performance-military-aged-adults.md]
- Hypoxia now has an explicit state split: resting acute hypoxia is ketone-responsive, but hypoxia plus exercise stays cognitively null.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39190580-ketone-monoester-hypoxic-exposure-resting.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40315254-ketone-ester-hypoxia-null.md]
- Acute stress has a clearer autonomic and target-response readout. taVNS can shift stress physiology, and prefrontal tDCS now has a target split under stress-induced working-memory impairment, but the direct behavioral cognition effect remains narrow or context-specific.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40908377-taVNS-mental-stressor.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40958978-taVNS-stress-regulation-eeg-questionnaire.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40292419-tdcs-stress-working-memory-2025.md]
- Perioperative inflammation is the strongest current rescue branch. Intranasal insulin now has RCTs plus two 2026 meta-analyses showing less POD and/or POCD, while a daridorexant feasibility trial keeps the DORA branch in the monitoring lane rather than the efficacy lane.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39216470-intranasal-insulin-perioperative-cognitive-function-older-adults.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40359848-intranasal-insulin-postop-sleep-cardiac-surgery.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40374039-daridorexant-delirium-feasibility-trial.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41146035-intranasal-insulin-hip-fracture-delirium.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41720686-intranasal-insulin-perioperative-delirium-meta.md]^[raw/articles/sage-2026-intranasal-insulin-postoperative-delirium-meta.md]
- Injury recovery now has a small but important split: tPBM showed a cognition-positive mTBI crossover signal, whereas HBOT remains anchored to review and registry-level evidence without posted efficacy results for the strict placebo-gas question.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40485299-tpbm-mtbi-cognitive-postconcussion-ptsd.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41180753-hbot-neurocognitive-deficits-traumatic-brain-injury.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct05643482-hbot-post-concussion-syndrome.md]
- The heat / exercise branch is still mostly a design watchlist. Creatine has a recruiting exertional-heat cognition trial, but the actual result has not posted yet.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct07472426-creatine-heat-exercise-cognition.md]
- Postprandial and dysglycemic load is a state-control problem first and an enhancement problem second. Movement breaks and meal timing can shift glucose and some neurocognitive markers, but the behavioral signal remains small.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39579950-breaking-up-sitting-postprandial-glucose-regulation.md]

## State Matrix

| Challenge state | Best current interventions | What moves | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rested baseline | No broad winner; protocol-sensitive neuromodulation only | Small task-specific effects, but no stable universal enhancer | Keep this as the comparator state, not the target state |
| Sleep loss / sleep pressure | Creatine, rTMS, tDCS pilot, sleep banking, sleep extension | PVT, processing speed, n-back accuracy, mood/HPA markers, later performance preservation; PVT remains the most sensitive anchor | Strongest healthy-adult rescue lane |
| Acute stress | taVNS, prefrontal tDCS | Autonomic markers, perceived stress, EEG arousal, stress-induced working memory; PASAT and broad direct cognition endpoints remain weak or context-specific | State modulation more than enhancement |
| Hypoxia | Ketone monoester | Resting-hypoxia code substitution and oxygen saturation; hypoxia-plus-exercise cognition stays null | Challenge-specific rescue |
| Heat / exercise load | Creatine is the main watch item | Trial pending; current evidence is mostly protocol and physiology, not final cognition | High-priority gap, not a settled branch |
| Postprandial / dysglycemic load | Movement breaks, meal timing, glucose stabilization | Postprandial glucose and P3-related neurocognitive markers | Better state control than enhancement |
| Injury / recovery / perioperative inflammation | Intranasal insulin, tPBM, HBOT watchlist | Delirium, postoperative cognitive dysfunction, inflammatory markers, sleep recovery, post-concussion symptoms | Strongest rescue branch in the current wiki, but still phenotype- and protocol-sensitive |

The important distinction is endpoint class. If a study mainly preserves performance under challenge, that is resilience. If it only moves a biomarker, that is physiology. Neither should be relabeled as broad baseline enhancement without a rested comparator or a delayed transfer readout.

## Evidence Tiering

- RCT level sleep-loss rescue: creatine and rTMS.
- Review-level sleep-preservation support: sleep banking and sleep extension.
- RCT level hypoxia rescue: ketone monoester at rest.
- RCT level acute-stress state modulation: taVNS.
- RCT and meta-analysis level perioperative rescue: intranasal insulin.
- RCT and protocol level postprandial control: movement breaks and exertional-heat creatine.
- The matrix is not trying to rank every enhancer. It is trying to prevent a rescue effect from being sold as a baseline win.

## Effect Size Snapshot

- Creatine under sleep deprivation: acute 0.35 g/kg dosing improved cognitive performance and processing speed with parallel changes in cerebral high-energy phosphates.^[raw/articles/pubmed-38418482-creatine-sleep-deprivation-cognition.md]
- rTMS under sleep deprivation: 24 h sleep deprivation reduced n-back accuracy; 10 Hz left DLPFC rTMS improved HPA-axis and frontal activation readouts.^[raw/articles/pubmed-32843299-rtms-sleep-deprivation-cognition.md]
- tDCS under sleep deprivation: a pilot used active stimulation during acute sleep deprivation to probe both performance and recovery sleep, which makes it a useful comparator for "stimulation during challenge" rather than "stimulation after recovery."^[raw/articles/pubmed-33524838-tdcs-sleep-deprivation-recovery.md]
- Sleep deprivation task sensitivity: PVT stayed more sensitive than a continuous visuomotor tracking task, so tracking belongs as a supplemental endpoint rather than a replacement for vigilance readouts.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41999492-sleep-deprivation-pvt-visuomotor-tracking.md]
- Ketone monoester at rest under hypoxia: DANA code substitution improved by 6.8 correct responses per minute, and oxygen-saturation decline was attenuated by 6.4 percentage points in the hypoxia window.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39190580-ketone-monoester-hypoxic-exposure-resting.md]
- Ketone monoester during hypoxia plus exercise: oxygen desaturation improved, but cognition did not.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40315254-ketone-ester-hypoxia-null.md]
- Stress-induced WM tDCS: vmPFC outperformed dlPFC for working memory and cortisol blunting under stress, which is a reminder that target selection can matter as much as state selection.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40292419-tdcs-stress-working-memory-2025.md]
- Perioperative intranasal insulin meta-analyses: the PubMed-indexed analysis reported postoperative delirium RR 0.39 and postoperative cognitive dysfunction RR 0.51, with lower inflammatory markers; the later SAGE analysis reported postoperative-delirium RR 0.36 across 9 RCTs and 1039 surgical patients.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41720686-intranasal-insulin-perioperative-delirium-meta.md]^[raw/articles/sage-2026-intranasal-insulin-postoperative-delirium-meta.md]
- Daridorexant delirium feasibility: postoperative delirium symptoms were numerically lower, but the pilot is too small for efficacy and sedation remains a real tradeoff.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40374039-daridorexant-delirium-feasibility-trial.md]
- mTBI tPBM: a 17-participant randomized crossover study reported better visual working memory, verbal learning, sleep quality, post-concussion symptoms, pain, and PTSD symptoms after real stimulation.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40485299-tpbm-mtbi-cognitive-postconcussion-ptsd.md]
- HBOT in TBI: the current evidence is still dominated by reviews and registry entries without posted efficacy results for the placebo-gas question.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41180753-hbot-neurocognitive-deficits-traumatic-brain-injury.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct05643482-hbot-post-concussion-syndrome.md]
- Postprandial movement breaks: inhibitory control did not significantly differ, but the active condition improved P3 amplitude and lowered postprandial glucose iAUC.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39579950-breaking-up-sitting-postprandial-glucose-regulation.md]
- taVNS under mental stress: autonomic signals moved, but PASAT performance did not.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40908377-taVNS-mental-stressor.md]

## Safety/Adverse Events

- Creatine is still the low-burden option, but heat, sleep loss, and hydration context matter when the state itself is stressful.
- Ketone esters add GI burden and can shift glucose and acid-base state, so they are not inert in hypoxia or exercise.
- taVNS and tDCS are usually tolerable, but sensory leakage, arousal effects, and target specificity complicate blinding and interpretation more than toxicity does.
- Intranasal insulin is not risk-free: the branch needs attention to dose, delivery device, and hypoglycemia ceiling even when serious events are rare.
- Daridorexant adds next-morning sedation as a competing adverse effect in the delirium setting, so the challenge state and the sleep-debt state cannot be conflated.
- tPBM and HBOT both sit in fragile recovery cohorts where protocol differences can dominate apparent efficacy.
- Postprandial movement breaks are low risk, which is one reason they are useful as a control-state intervention rather than just a mechanism study.

## Responder Phenotypes

- Sleep-deprived, time-pressured, or high-demand adults are the clearest creatine and rTMS candidates.
- Sleep-loss sensitivity is endpoint-specific, so vigilance should stay separate from continuous tracking tasks unless the protocol is explicitly severe-sleep-loss focused.
- Hypoxia-sensitive or altitude-like contexts are the best ketone phenotype, but only when the task is not load-bearing exercise.
- Stress-reactive participants with measurable autonomic responsivity are the best taVNS phenotype; stress-induced WM impairment is a separate tDCS phenotype.
- Older, perioperative, insulin-resistant, or delirium-prone participants are the clearest intranasal-insulin phenotype.
- People with unstable glucose or prolonged sitting are the best postprandial-control phenotype.
- mTBI and other recovery cohorts are the clearest tPBM/HBOT phenotype, but the generalization boundary is still narrow.

## Protocol Parameters

- Creatine sleep-loss rescue: 0.35 g/kg single oral dose during 21 h total sleep deprivation.
- rTMS sleep-loss rescue: 2 sessions of 10 Hz rTMS, 40 trains of 50 pulses, left DLPFC, during 24 h sleep deprivation.
- Ketone monoester rest-hypoxia rescue: 0.65 g/kg split dose before 20 min normoxia / hypoxia exposure.
- Ketone monoester exercise-hypoxia null: 573 mg/kg with 40 g dextrose before weighted ruck exercise in hypoxia.
- taVNS stress-regulation study: 15 min left cymba conchae stimulation before and during PASAT; other stress studies used 30 min or longer protocols.
- tDCS stress study: target-specific prefrontal stimulation under stress-induced working-memory impairment; use this as a montage/target comparator, not a generic tDCS result.
- Movement-break postprandial study: 3 min walking every 30 min during 3.5 h sitting.
- Perioperative intranasal insulin studies: 20 IU to 40 IU around surgery, with the meta-analysis pooling 20-40 IU regimens.
- Daridorexant feasibility: 50 mg nightly for the first three postoperative nights in a cardiac-surgery delirium pilot.
- Creatine heat/exercise trial: 20 g/day for 7 days before exertional heat testing in active adults.
- mTBI tPBM crossover: small placebo-controlled study in 17 adults with mild TBI.
- HBOT post-concussion registry: completed with no posted results, so efficacy remains unresolved.

## Open Contradictions

- The ketone branch is explicitly state-dependent: rest-state hypoxia is positive, hypoxia plus exercise is null.
- taVNS can move stress physiology without improving the tested cognitive endpoint, so arousal and cognition must stay separate.
- Sleep-loss endpoints are not interchangeable: PVT is more sensitive than continuous tracking in total sleep deprivation.
- Stress, recovery, and injury branches are increasingly phenotype-specific rather than class-wide.
- Intranasal insulin looks strongest perioperatively, but the chronic MCI/AD story remains much weaker.
- Heat/exercise is still more of a study design than a demonstrated cognitive branch.
- Sleep banking and sleep extension are useful resilience concepts, but their direct cognition evidence is still mostly review-level.
