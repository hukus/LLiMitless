# taVNS Cognition, Stress-Resilience, and Inflammation Scan

## Summary

The 2025-2026 taVNS literature is now split into four layers: a small but direct healthy-adult memory signal, a still-mixed stress-resilience signal, a stronger disease-context sleep/biomarker signal, and an inflammation branch that looks more credible than the cognition branch.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39039354-taVNS-memory-nonclinical-adults.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41046500-taVNS-associative-memory-healthy-adults.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40908377-taVNS-mental-stressor.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39936474-taVNS-cortisol-stress-inflammation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40629377-taVNS-primary-insomnia-bf-fc.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39854697-taVNS-inflammation-sah.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41428140-taVNS-stroke-inflammatory-biomarkers.md]

The newest healthy-adult pilot found an associative-memory interaction without P300 or P600 changes, the corrected 2-week RAVLT trial found improved immediate recall and short-term memory but no delayed recall, and a 2026 protocol confirms that pooled meta-analytic synthesis is still pending. Autonomic modulation remains the most reproducible readout, but a 2025 continuous-stimulation study and a 2025 stress-regulation EEG study suggest taVNS can also shift arousal and long-latency processing without clearly improving task performance. The 2025 insomnia review still says there is no consensus on duration, site, or parameters, and the 2026 military-exercise field study reads as state rescue under sleep deprivation rather than broad enhancement.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41046500-taVNS-associative-memory-healthy-adults.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39039354-taVNS-memory-nonclinical-adults.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-42030358-taVNS-cognitive-functions-protocol.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39124651-taVNS-autonomic-function.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40130364-taVNS-long-latency-sensory-processing.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40958978-taVNS-stress-regulation-eeg-questionnaire.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40535058-tavns-insomnia-clinical-practice-recommendations.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41973311-taVNS-military-exercise-sleep-deprivation.md]

## Evidence Tiering

- Meta/review: the 2021 healthy-adult meta-analysis provides the old baseline, the 2026 protocol shows the field still has not pooled the newer studies, and the 2026 neuropsychiatric systematic review remains heterogeneous.
- RCTs in healthy adults: the corrected 2-week RAVLT trial and the 2025 associative-memory pilot both lean positive, but the acute-stress PASAT study remains null, the stress-regulation EEG study is closer to a state-regulation result than a cognition win, and the short-burst memory/pupil study is hard to interpret because of sensory confounding.
- RCTs in disease states: primary insomnia, post-stroke depression, subarachnoid hemorrhage, and large-vessel stroke suggest sleep, mood, and inflammation relevance, but not direct healthy-enhancement proof.
- Mechanistic studies: autonomic-function and continuous-stimulation work shows that placement, geometry, sham design, and sensory arousal matter as much as the stimulation label.

## Effect Size Snapshot

- Healthy cognition: no stable pooled estimate from the newer literature yet.
- Historical baseline: the 2021 healthy-adult meta-analysis reported a small overall effect size (g = 0.21), with stronger signals for executive function and accuracy, but also heavy parameter sensitivity.
- Two-week memory trial: improved immediate recall and short-term memory, but not delayed recall.
- Associative-memory pilot: a timepoint interaction on associative-memory performance in 14 healthy adults, with no P300/P600 change.
- Acute stress cognition: no measurable PASAT improvement in 41 healthy volunteers.
- Stress-endocrine signal: 12 healthy adults showed lower salivary cortisol during taVNS than sham during mental stress, but that is not the same as improved cognition.
- Military-exercise field study: a 4-minute taVNS session with individualized intensity 12-26 mA modestly attenuated vigilance decrements and negative mood under 24-hour sleep deprivation, but did not improve response inhibition.
- Autonomic and arousal response: RMSSD, electrodermal activity, self-reported arousal, and some EEG markers moved, which supports state modulation but still does not equal a broad enhancer effect.
- Disease-context outcomes: insomnia, mood, and inflammatory biomarkers look more promising than direct cognitive endpoints.

## Safety And Tolerability

- The healthy volunteer studies did not surface a major safety signal in the abstracts.
- The main practical risk is not obvious toxicity but interpretability: stimulation intensity, auricular location, and sham design can create sensory artifacts that mimic efficacy.
- A 2025 sleep-deprivation alertness study found that earlobe stimulation itself improved alertness and moved HR/HRV, so the common sham control is active enough to matter when interpreting taVNS sleep-loss results.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39727264-taVNS-sleep-deprivation-alertness-sham.md]
- A 2026 military-exercise field study now suggests that short taVNS can rescue vigilance under sleep deprivation, which reinforces the state-rescue framing rather than any claim of broad enhancement.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41973311-taVNS-military-exercise-sleep-deprivation.md]
- The 2-week daily 4-hour protocol is also operationally heavy, so adherence and tolerability matter even if acute adverse events stay mild.
- Disease-context trials in SAH and stroke are not interchangeable with healthy-adult enhancement, so safety and efficacy should not be generalized upward.

## Responder Phenotypes

- Best-supported inference: a phenotype with measurable autonomic responsiveness may be more likely to show a signal, but that is still only an inference from the existing studies.
- The literature now suggests three possible responder axes: baseline autonomic tone, sleep/insomnia phenotype, and inflammation/stress load.
- The literature has not yet established a reliable cognition-responder profile by chronotype, baseline HRV, inflammatory burden, or mood state.
- If a responder atlas is built later, the first candidates should be baseline HRV, stress load, sleep quality, basal forebrain connectivity, and task-specific memory salience.
- The corrected RAVLT trial and the associative-memory pilot both hint that memory-specific tasks may be more responsive than broad executive batteries, but that is still a hypothesis rather than a settled phenotype.
- The military-exercise field study suggests that a sleep-loss state challenge may be a more productive responder filter than rested baseline for short-burst taVNS.

## Protocol Parameters

- 2-week memory trial: daily 4-hour auricular stimulation at tragus or earlobe.
- Associative-memory pilot: taVNS or sham during the 10 minute rest between repeated encoding/retrieval blocks, with one-week washout.
- Short-burst memory study: 3 s event-related bursts; emotional task used 5 mA and 25 Hz.
- Acute-stress RCT: 15 min left cymba conchae stimulation before and during PASAT; sham at ear lobe.
- Cortisol/stress study: 30 min taVNS or sham, with a mental arithmetic stress test starting 10 min into stimulation.
- Insomnia RCT: 4 weeks of real or sham taVNS, with baseline BF connectivity used as a response biomarker.
- Military-exercise field study: 4-minute taVNS with individually adjusted intensity (12-26 mA) during a 24-hour training period with total sleep deprivation.
- Stroke biomarker trial: twice-daily taVNS for five days or until discharge in LVO AIS.
- Autonomic-function study: bilateral stimulation for 20 min at 25 Hz with 250 microsecond pulse width.
- The parameter spread is wide enough that "taVNS" is currently a family of interventions rather than one intervention.

## Open Contradictions

- Review-level optimism says taVNS may improve cognition in neuropsychiatric disease, but the newest healthy-volunteer stress RCT is cognitively null, the healthy-adult signal remains narrow, and the most robust new signal may actually be state regulation rather than enhancement.
- Memory effects may be real, but sensory stimulation alone may explain part of the signal.
- Autonomic modulation appears more reproducible than behavioral cognition.
- Insomnia and stroke biomarker data strengthen the responder/state-regulation story, but they also widen the gap between disease-context physiology and healthy enhancement.
- The military-exercise field study makes the branch look more like sleep-loss rescue than broad enhancement, which is useful but narrower than the label usually implies.
- The field still needs a quantitative meta-analysis with protocol harmonization before strong claims are justified, which is exactly what the 2026 protocol is trying to address.
