# Medicine and Cognition Research Summary

This wiki has converged on a few stable research clusters: [[cbt-i-and-cognition]] and related [[sleep-and-circadian-interventions-for-cognition]] pages for sleep timing and insomnia; [[bright-light-therapy-and-cognition]] and [[circadian-disruption-and-cognition]] for circadian alignment; [[orexin-hypocretin-axis-interventions-2026-scan]] for wakefulness, sleep pressure, and orexin-axis state restoration; [[incretin-therapies-brain-health]] and [[intranasal-insulin-and-cognition]] for metabolic-brain signaling; [[energy-substrate-comparator-map]] for creatine, ketones, lactate, glucose stability, and caffeine under one high-load battery; [[cerebrovascular-reserve-interventions-2026-scan]] for exercise, nitrate, and breath-linked reserve claims; [[repetitive-transcranial-magnetic-stimulation-and-cognition]], [[transcranial-direct-current-stimulation-and-cognition]], [[transcranial-alternating-current-stimulation-and-cognition]], and [[transcutaneous-auricular-vagus-nerve-stimulation]] for neuromodulation; and [[cerebrolysin]], [[peptide-neuroprotection]], [[semax]], [[selank]], [[dihexa]], [[bpc-157]], [[thymosin-beta-4]], and [[mots-c]] for peptide / translational neuroprotection. The glutamatergic-plasticity branch now sits alongside those clusters in [[ampakines-and-glutamatergic-plasticity-2026-scan]]. For the newest Cerebrolysin-focused scan, see [[cerebrolysin-2026-scan]], for the newest MOTS-c scan see [[mots-c-2026-scan]], and for the newest orexin scan see [[orexin-hypocretin-axis-interventions-2026-scan]].

Relevant raw PubMed notes for that peptide/translational cluster: `raw/articles/pubmed-24738258-neuroprotective-peptides-age.md`, `raw/articles/pubmed-41460391-cerebrolysin-age-induced-memory-decline.md`, and `raw/articles/pubmed-40816274-thymosin-beta4-ad-candidate.md`.

The strongest practical signal in the current graph is not a single "cognition booster" but a set of intervention domains with different evidence profiles. Sleep and circadian interventions are the most operationally mature, especially when the goal is better performance or less cognitive drag from sleep loss. Neuromodulation is attractive but protocol-sensitive and still heterogeneous. Metabolic-brain therapies remain promising but mixed, while peptide and photobiomodulation topics are best treated as exploratory or hypothesis-generating unless a specific source line is strong. The newest addition is the standardized-herbal branch: ginkgo EGb 761 now looks like the anchor candidate, with bacopa, ashwagandha, [[ginseng]], curcumin, and broader Chinese herbal medicine sitting behind it as more phenotype-specific or less reproducible options. The PDE4/PDE4D lane now has a dedicated [[roflumilast]] page, a separate [[bpn14770]] page, and a refreshed [[pde-camp-creb-cognition-2026-scan]], which separates roflumilast's low-dose human signals from the fragile-X PDE4D result. The PDE9/PDE10/cGMP branch now has its own negative-translator scan in [[pde9-pde10-cgmp-cognition-2026-scan]]. The energy-substrate branch now has its own comparison layer in [[energy-substrate-comparator-map]], which keeps creatine, ketones, lactate, glucose stability, and caffeine on the same strain-state battery instead of letting them drift across separate pages. For the peptide slice specifically, see [[peptide-neuroprotection]] and [[neurocognitive-interventions-under-study]]. MOTS-c now sits in the same low-confidence metabolic bridge lane: interesting human biomarker and trial activity, but no cognition endpoint yet.

For a one-table view of the full tradeoff space, see [[cognition-intervention-master-table]].

For the preclinical-versus-human translation lens specifically, see [[translational-gap-table]].

A useful way to read the wiki is by question rather than by modality: what improves sleep, what supports attention and alertness, what plausibly affects neuroinflammation, and what has only anecdotal or early translational support. That framing reduces overclaiming and keeps the graph useful for comparison across [[adjunctive-interventions-for-cognition]], [[noninvasive-brain-stimulation-for-cognition]], and the other synthesis pages.
