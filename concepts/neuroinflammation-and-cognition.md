# Neuroinflammation and Cognition

Neuroinflammation is best treated as a causal amplifier of cognitive decline rather than as a mere biomarker. Microglial activation, inflammatory lipid handling, complement signaling, and gut-brain immune pathways can all alter synaptic function and network stability, especially in Alzheimer’s disease and related neurodegenerative states.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41679362-microglial-metabolic-reprogramming-neurological-disease-therapy.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41929953-microbiome-alzheimers-disease-systematic-review.md]

The raw anchors worth opening are `raw/articles/pubmed-41679362-microglial-metabolic-reprogramming-neurological-disease-therapy.md`, `raw/articles/pubmed-41720758-amygdala-neuroinflammatory-disorders-dti.md`, and `raw/articles/pubmed-41929953-microbiome-alzheimers-disease-systematic-review.md`.

The most useful current framing is that immune-metabolic state modifies cognition through multiple channels: synapse pruning, vascular dysfunction, BBB stress, and network-level changes in regions such as the amygdala. That makes neuroinflammation a plausible bridge between [[incretin-therapies-brain-health]], [[transcutaneous-auricular-vagus-nerve-stimulation]], [[circadian-disruption-and-cognition]], [[gut-microbiome-and-cognition]], and [[adjunctive-interventions-for-cognition]].^[raw/articles/pubmed-41720758-amygdala-neuroinflammatory-disorders-dti.md]

For the wiki, the key caution is that broad anti-inflammatory claims are usually too strong. The literature supports a role for inflammation in cognitive decline, but disease-modifying benefit from generic anti-inflammatory treatment in established dementia remains uncertain. This is why the page should stay medium-confidence and linked to specific mechanistic or intervention pages rather than becoming a catch-all biomarker sink. For navigation, it also belongs with [[medicine-cognition-summary]].
