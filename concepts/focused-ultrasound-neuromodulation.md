# Focused Ultrasound Neuromodulation

Transcranial focused ultrasound (tFUS) is an emerging non-invasive stimulation technique that can modulate specific brain regions with acoustic energy. The current review literature frames it as promising across sensory, motor, cognitive, and emotional domains, but still early in clinical translation.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41152970-transcranial-focused-ultrasound-neuromodulation.md]

The raw anchors are `raw/articles/pubmed-41152970-transcranial-focused-ultrasound-neuromodulation.md` and `raw/articles/pubmed-41836667-neuromodulation-post-stroke-cognitive-impairment.md`.

For cognition, the main story is potential rather than proof. Reviews of post-stroke cognitive impairment explicitly describe transcranial ultrasound stimulation as novel and still lacking strong scientific evidence, which makes the modality high-interest but still experimental.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41836667-neuromodulation-post-stroke-cognitive-impairment.md]

A new 2026 hippocampal low-intensity TUS registry is the clearest current example of this branch moving into a cognition-plus-glymphatic protocol: it is randomized, triple-blind, and tests sleep quality and delayed recall while keeping glymphatic function as a secondary outcome. A separate 2026 mild-AD pilot RCT adds the first direct randomized human efficacy/safety signal for repeated clinic-based TUS, but it is still far too small to move the branch out of the experimental zone.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct07298876-hippocampal-tus-glymphatic-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41046632-transcranial-ultrasound-stimulation-ad-rct.md] That still does not prove efficacy, but it moves the ultrasound branch from generic plausibility toward a specific mechanistic hypothesis.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct07298876-hippocampal-tus-glymphatic-cognition.md]

The operational picture is now clearer: the newest safety consensus assumes no contraindications, compromised thermoregulation, vascular vulnerabilities, or administered ultrasound contrast agents, and it defines explicit nonsignificant-risk thresholds for mechanical and thermal exposure. The reporting consensus is equally demanding, requiring detailed reporting of the transducer/drive system, drive settings, free-field acoustic parameters, pulse timing, in situ exposure estimates, and intensity parameters. The engineering literature makes the delivery burden harder to ignore: hydrophone work requires calibration, uncertainty handling, and reporting discipline, while skull-model papers show that beam propagation changes with patient-specific cranial structure, placement, and frequency. A recent parameter review further notes that published human studies are still heterogeneous and were administered in clinical settings by trained health professionals, with multimodal navigation still needed for standardization. That is why an at-home or DIY protocol is not supported by the current evidence base, even though a wearable prototype now exists. The 2026 mild-AD pilot RCT strengthens the clinic-based case for safety and feasibility, but it also reinforces that current human evidence depends on supervised sessions, imaging surveillance, and small samples rather than consumer self-administration.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41072763-itrusst-biophysical-safety-transcranial-ultrasound-stimulation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-38410648-itrusst-standardised-reporting-transcranial-ultrasound-stimulation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-36215339-hydrophone-measurements-for-biomedical-ultrasound-applications.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-28777075-numerical-evaluation-of-the-skull-for-human-neuromodulation-with-transcranial-focused-ultrasound.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40470501-transcranial-ultrasound-stimulation-parameters-neurological-diseases.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39449176-wearable-steerable-transcranial-low-intensity-focused-ultrasound-system.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-31401074-safety-of-transcranial-focused-ultrasound-stimulation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41046632-transcranial-ultrasound-stimulation-ad-rct.md]
