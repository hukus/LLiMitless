# Repetitive Transcranial Magnetic Stimulation and Cognition

Repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation (rTMS) is the most clinically mature noninvasive stimulation modality in this wiki slice. The recent meta-analyses consistently position it as a serious cognition-relevant intervention for neurodegenerative disease and other cognitive disorders, with the clearest signals in multi-session protocols and in combination with other non-pharmacological therapies.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40676541-rtms-cognitive-impairment-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41192010-rtms-non-pharmacological-interventions-cognitive-disorders.md]

The 2025-2026 protocol-level literature makes the intervention look less monolithic and more state-dependent. A large 2025 meta-analysis across AD and PD found cognitive and anxiety benefits, with DLPFC comparing favorably to non-DLPFC for MoCA, while the 2025 dose-response meta-analysis argued that total pulse count is not linear for all endpoints: MoCA, HAMA, and ADL looked bell-shaped, while MMSE continued to trend upward across the studied range. That is the core reason this page now treats rTMS as a protocol family rather than a single device effect.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40676541-rtms-cognitive-impairment-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41191186-rtms-cognitive-emotional-symptoms-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]

Combination and target-selection questions also strengthened. A 2025 add-on meta-analysis found that rTMS combined with tDCS, Tai Chi, or cognitive training improved global cognition immediately and retained a smaller signal at 4-8 weeks, with the rTMS+tDCS subgroup looking especially strong in the short term. Meanwhile, the AD precuneus protocol literature is moving toward larger-coil, induction-plus-maintenance designs, and the healthy-adult review still finds only small, heterogeneous enhancement effects after outlier handling.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41192010-rtms-non-pharmacological-interventions-cognitive-disorders.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40065917-rtms-precuneus-alzheimers-study-design.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39866659-rtms-neuroenhancement-healthy-adults.md]

The stroke cognition meta-analysis adds a useful external comparator: high-intensity rTMS and iTBS appear to be the more effective protocol families when cognition is the endpoint. That does not prove the same recipe will transfer to AD or healthy adults, but it does reinforce the broader pattern that dose architecture matters as much as target selection.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40769285-rtms-stroke-cognitive-impairment.md]

For state-dependence and sequencing, see [[cbt-i-and-neuromodulation-sequence-effects-on-cognition]]. The current read is that rTMS during active sleep loss is a different experiment from rTMS after insomnia has been stabilized.

The strongest current protocol read is that rTMS behaves like a family of interventions with different operating windows rather than a single effect. High-frequency DLPFC protocols remain the default in PSCI and MCI, the AD precuneus program is explicitly moving toward a larger-coil induction-plus-maintenance design, and healthy-adult effects remain small enough that montage choice and outlier handling can flip the interpretation.^[raw/articles/pmid-38113027-psci-hf-rtms.md]^[raw/articles/pmid-37759936-mci-rtms.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40065917-rtms-precuneus-alzheimers-study-design.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39866659-rtms-neuroenhancement-healthy-adults.md]

## Protocol Families

| Family | Current read | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| High-frequency DLPFC | The most common cognition protocol in PSCI and MCI, and still the default starting point for disease-cohort work | It gives the strongest baseline comparator and explains why many meta-analyses cluster around the same target |
| Precuneus / network-hub AD | A newer AD-specific design that uses a larger coil and a two-stage induction-plus-maintenance frame | It implies that target choice may be disease-network specific, not just "more frontal stimulation" |
| Dose-window protocols | The 2025 dose-response work suggests intermediate cumulative pulse loads can outperform escalation for several endpoints | Pulse count is a meaningful optimization variable rather than just a trial-design detail |
| iTBS / high-intensity comparator | Stroke cognition data suggest these faster families can be competitive when cognition is the endpoint | Useful as a time-efficient comparator when designing future protocols |
| Adjunctive rTMS | Add-on rTMS with cognitive training, Tai Chi, or tDCS looks stronger than stand-alone enhancement claims | Combination context is now part of the signal, not a side note |

The exact target and protocol still matter a lot. Evidence across the newer meta-analyses suggests that left DLPFC, precuneus, and other network-relevant targets can produce improvements in global cognition or domain-specific measures, but effect sizes vary and are sensitive to dose, number of sessions, and patient population. That is why rTMS should be treated as a protocol-dependent intervention, not a single uniform therapy.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41191186-rtms-cognitive-emotional-symptoms-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40386876-rtms-with-aducanumab-lecanemab-donanemab.md]

The 2025 dose-response review is the sharpest protocol clue in the stack. Across AD and PD, it reports an average of 14 sessions and 23,300 pulses, with cognitive benefit in AD peaking in the mid-20k to high-20k pulse range instead of climbing linearly with dose. That makes cumulative pulse load a real optimization variable, not just a bookkeeping detail.^[raw/articles/frontiers-2025-rtms-dose-response-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]
