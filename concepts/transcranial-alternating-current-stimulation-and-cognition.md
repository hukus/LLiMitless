# Transcranial Alternating Current Stimulation and Cognition

Transcranial alternating current stimulation (tACS) is the most mechanistically specific of the three classic noninvasive stimulation methods in this cluster because it aims to entrain oscillations rather than simply bias excitability. That makes it especially interesting for memory and working-memory networks, where rhythm timing may matter as much as raw activation. The latest meta-analytic split now looks more concrete: theta-band stimulation carries the clearest working-memory signal, offline maintenance-oriented protocols look better than a generic all-purpose claim, gamma-band 40 Hz protocols are the active memory branch in AD/MCI, and a 2026 older-cognitive-impairment meta-analysis shows a memory/overall-cognition benefit but no attention effect.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39438426-tacs-working-memory-meta-2025.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41034500-tacs-older-adults-meta-2025.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41507453-tacs-cognitive-impairment-meta.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40283048-40hz-tacs-dementia-cognitive-exercises.md]

The key raw note here is `raw/articles/pubmed-41034500-tacs-cognitive-function-older-adults.md`, with the broader umbrella context in `raw/articles/pubmed-39910547-nibs-cognitive-impairment-umbrella-review.md`.

The evidence base is still small compared with rTMS and tDCS. Recent meta-analytic work in older adults suggests a signal worth tracking, but the field is still in the exploratory phase and the most important questions are about target, frequency band, and whether tACS works better as an add-on than as a stand-alone intervention. A 2025 healthy-adult theta meta-analysis shows a modest uncorrected working-memory signal that shrinks toward null after publication-bias correction, while the 2026 impairment meta-analysis suggests the treatment question may be clearer than the enhancement question.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40933163-theta-tacs-working-memory-healthy-adults.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41507453-tacs-cognitive-impairment-meta.md]

Timing matters here too, but the signal is still about sleep-state context rather than chronotype. A tiny 2023 insomnia trial reported that bilateral frontal 0.75 Hz tACS improved sleep quality, arousals, recall memory, sleep duration, sleep efficiency, and daytime sleepiness on the stimulation night, which keeps the branch anchored to sleep physiology rather than to a generic enhancement claim.^[raw/articles/pubmed-35321778-tacs-insomnia-time-of-day.md]

For the chronotype/timing scan, see [[chronotype-specific-response-across-sleep-stimulation-interventions]].

The 2026 memory-mapping paper pushes the branch further toward a region-specific interpretation: theta and gamma effects vary by cortical site, with some prefrontal and temporal regions showing negative or nonlinear associations rather than a clean positive effect. That makes tACS look less like a single frequency story and more like a target-frequency-mapping problem. The newer phase-lagged study adds another axis: the same network pair can behave differently at 45° versus 180° lag, so inter-network phase is part of the dose, not just a technical detail.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41784599-tacs-long-term-memory-mapping.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40097468-phase-lagged-tacs-executive-default-mode-working-memory.md]

A 2025 bifrontal tACS RCT adds a durability note: the active condition did not separate immediately, but it did at 4 weeks on verbal working memory and episodic memory, with a matching theta-power change. That makes the branch look more plasticity-sensitive than purely acute.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41461804-bifrontal-tacs-long-term-working-memory-episodic-memory.md]
