# Circadian Disruption and Cognition

Circadian disruption is one of the most reproducible ways to worsen cognition without touching a classic neurodegenerative pathway directly. When sleep timing, light exposure, and behavioral schedules drift out of sync, attention, reaction time, working memory, and broader neurobehavioral performance usually suffer.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41090408-circadian-disruption-astronauts-neurocognitive-consequences.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41139761-shift-work-cognitive-motor-performance-nurses.md]

Relevant raw PubMed notes: `raw/articles/pubmed-41090408-circadian-disruption-astronauts-neurocognitive-consequences.md|astronaut review`, `raw/articles/pubmed-41139761-shift-work-cognitive-motor-performance-nurses.md|shift-work meta-analysis`, and `raw/articles/pubmed-41277818-post-stroke-circadian-rhythm-disruption-prognosis.md|post-stroke review`.

The intervention side of the story is more cautious. [[sleep-banking-and-sleep-extension]] appears to help preserve performance under later sleep restriction, and shift-work sleep programs can improve sleep outcomes, but neither line yet proves durable disease prevention. The most defensible wiki framing is therefore “circadian stabilization improves neurobehavioral performance; long-term cognitive protection remains unproven.”^[raw/articles/pubmed-41892386-sleep-banking-cognitive-motor-impairments-sleep-restriction.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40984126-sleep-extension-cognitive-performance-military-aged-adults.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40890717-sleep-improvement-programs-shift-work-nurses.md]
