# Hyperbaric Oxygen Therapy and Neurocognitive Recovery

Hyperbaric oxygen therapy (HBOT) remains one of the more debated adjunctive interventions in brain-injury rehabilitation. Recent reviews and meta-analyses keep the topic alive by focusing on traumatic brain injury and neurocognitive deficits, while broader post-concussion literature emphasizes that symptom persistence can be prolonged and that treatment strategies remain heterogeneous. The newest quantitative review is positive but small, the older systematic review still called the evidence limited, and the 2026 comprehensive review again described mixed outcomes rather than a settled answer.^[raw/articles/pubmed-35213282-hbot-cognitive-impairments-tbi-systematic-review.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41979527-hyperbaric-oxygen-therapy-traumatic-brain-injury.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41180753-hbot-neurocognitive-deficits-traumatic-brain-injury.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41661006-post-concussion-syndrome-rehabilitation.md]

For the strict-bias read, see [[hbot-evidence-quality-review]].

The current picture is not “HBOT works” or “HBOT does not work,” but rather that the evidence is highly context-dependent and likely sensitive to population, protocol, and outcome choice. Since 2024, the evidence has become more retraction-aware: the 2020 severe-TBI randomized trial is no longer valid, both sham-controlled military trials were null overall, and the newest positive meta-analysis still mixes small randomized studies with retrospective cohorts. The latest 2025 review was positive overall, but its randomized subset was much less convincing for executive function and information processing speed, and the 2026 comprehensive review did not overturn that comparator problem.^[raw/articles/pubmed-38217421-hbot-severe-tbi-retraction-notice.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-26403017-hbot-tbi-sham-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-24052094-hbot-persistent-postconcussion-symptoms.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41180753-hbot-neurocognitive-deficits-traumatic-brain-injury.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41979527-hyperbaric-oxygen-therapy-traumatic-brain-injury.md]

The best current interpretation is that HBOT may still have context-specific recovery value in some post-injury phenotypes, but the cleaner blinded evidence does not support treating it as a reliable general cognition enhancer.^[raw/articles/pubmed-26403017-hbot-tbi-sham-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-24052094-hbot-persistent-postconcussion-symptoms.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41180753-hbot-neurocognitive-deficits-traumatic-brain-injury.md]

That keeps it in the same comparison bucket as [[transcranial-photobiomodulation]] and [[focused-ultrasound-neuromodulation]] rather than in the category of standard cognitive therapies. It is also grouped in [[adjunctive-interventions-for-cognition]] alongside incretin therapies, [[transcutaneous-auricular-vagus-nerve-stimulation]], [[creatine-cognitive-support]], and [[bright-light-therapy-and-cognition]]. For navigation, it also belongs with [[adjunctive-interventions-for-cognition]], [[most-promising-methods-for-cognition]], [[medicine-cognition-summary]], and [[hbot-evidence-quality-review]]. The HOT-POCS registry remains relevant because it is the cleanest placebo-gas question, but as of the latest update it still shows no posted results.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct05643482-hbot-post-concussion-syndrome.md]
