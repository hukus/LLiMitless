# Mitochondria-Derived Peptides

Mitochondria-derived peptides, or MDPs, are a small family of mitochondrial genome-encoded signaling peptides that now matter to this wiki for more than abstract mechanism. They connect endogenous aging biology, metabolic signaling, and a narrow but increasingly serious set of brain-protection hypotheses. The family most often discussed in the literature includes humanin, MOTS-c, and SHLP1-6; elamipretide/SS-31 is not endogenous, but it belongs in the same translational neighborhood because it targets mitochondrial membranes and now has real drug-level and trial-level status.^[raw/articles/pubmed-35499074-mdp-aging-healthspan-review.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40715951-mdp-neurodegeneration-review.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40294492-elamipretide-mitochondrial-mechanism-review.md]

The family split in this wiki is straightforward:

| Member | Current wiki status | Brain/cognition relevance |
|---|---|---|
| Humanin | endogenous MDP, entity page warranted | strongest cognition-linked signal, still mostly preclinical/genetic |
| MOTS-c | endogenous MDP, existing entity page | metabolism-first bridge, cognition still indirect |
| SHLPs / SHLP2 | review-level family members, with SHLP2 now emerging as a distinct translational sub-branch | metabolic, retinal, and PD biology; cognition still unproven |
| Elamipretide / SS-31 | exogenous cardiolipin-binding therapeutic, entity page warranted | preclinical memory signal only; approved for Barth syndrome and now entering healthy-aging pilot testing |

Humanin is the family member with the clearest brain-facing evidence. It has a mouse cognition signal, a human cognitive-aging association, an APOE4-resilience variant, and a TBI preclinical paper. That is enough to keep it on the brain-health map, but not enough to call it a validated cognition intervention.^[raw/articles/pubmed-30242290-humanin-age-related-cognitive-decline.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-38520065-humanin-p3s-apoe4-brain-pathology.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-37926362-humanin-tbi-related-cognitive-impairment.md]

MOTS-c remains the metabolism-first branch: useful for exercise, biomarker, and energy-substrate discussions, but not yet a direct nootropic lane. The newest MOTS-c update adds pancreatic-islet senescence / type 2 diabetes biology and a recruiting Phase 2a prediabetes trial, but the trial endpoints are insulin sensitivity and safety rather than cognition. Gray-market safety caution remains live because a 2026 sports-medicine peptide review emphasizes scarce rigorous human safety data for unapproved peptides.^[raw/articles/pubmed-25738459-mots-c-metabolic-homeostasis.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-36677050-mots-c-prevents-metabolic-disorders.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-36761202-mots-c-therapeutic-exploitation.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-12411631-mots-c-pancreatic-islet-senescence-diabetes.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct07505745-mots-c-prediabetes-record-2026-04-01.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41966639-peptide-therapies-sports-medicine-review.md]

SHLP2 is the branch that moved furthest beyond review-only status after 2023-2024. The energy-homeostasis paper tied SHLP2 to hypothalamic neurons and metabolic control, and the 2024 Parkinson's paper linked a SHLP2 variant to reduced PD risk. That makes SHLP2 a real translational sub-branch, but still not a cognition intervention.^[raw/articles/pubmed-37468558-shlp2-energy-homeostasis.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-38167865-shlp2-parkinson-protective-factor.md]

Elamipretide is the translational outlier because it is now an approved drug. FDA accelerated approval for Barth syndrome moves it out of the "research peptide" bucket, but the approved indication is muscle-strength rescue in a rare mitochondrial disease, not cognition enhancement.^[raw/articles/fda-forzinity-elamipretide-approval.md]

At the family level, the class now looks more serious than it did in 2024 because it has three different kinds of traction at once:

- `Brain-facing biology`: Humanin is now a durable cognition-resilience and injury-recovery node.
- `Active human translation`: MOTS-c has a recruiting metabolic Phase 2a study, and elamipretide has a healthy-aging pilot with cognitive secondary endpoints.
- `Regulatory reality`: elamipretide is already an approved mitochondrial drug, which means the family is no longer purely speculative.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct07505745-mots-c-prediabetes-record-2026-04-01.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct07275424-elamipretide-shape.md]^[raw/articles/fda-forzinity-elamipretide-approval.md]

SHLP6 adds a family-level preclinical anchor: a 2025 zebrafish toxicity paper found neuroprotection-adjacent effects under copper stress, including improved locomotion and acetylcholinesterase readouts. That is still far from human cognition evidence, but it keeps SHLPs from being a pure abstraction.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40292418-shlp6-cu-induced-oxidative-stress-neurodegeneration.md]

A 2026 approval review keeps the elamipretide efficacy story calibrated: the randomized crossover program did not significantly improve 6-minute walk or fatigue, with longer-term benefits mainly in the open-label extension.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41260682-elamipretide-first-approval-review.md]

Open questions:

- Do humanin levels or variants function as a durable cognitive-resilience biomarker, or are they mostly a correlative age signal?
- Does the APOE4/P3S result generalize outside the centenarian-enriched setting?
- Does SHLP2 ever connect to cognition, or does it stay confined to metabolic, retinal, and Parkinson's disease biology? SHLP6 now suggests at least one specific preclinical neuroprotection branch.
- Can elamipretide's mitochondrial membrane effects ever translate into human CNS benefit, or are the brain papers limited to preclinical inflammatory models?
- Does the healthy-aging elamipretide pilot generate any meaningful cognitive signal, or only safety and biomarker movement?
