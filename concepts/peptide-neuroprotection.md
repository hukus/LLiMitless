# Peptide Neuroprotection

Peptide neuroprotection is an umbrella concept for peptide-based agents proposed to support neuronal resilience, plasticity, recovery, or repair. The literature spans older regulatory peptides such as [[semax]], [[selank]], [[cortexin]], [[kortagen]], and [[pinealon]], peptide bioregulator discussions, and newer or re-highlighted work on [[cerebrolysin]] in memory decline, dementia prevention, and post-injury recovery.^[raw/articles/pubmed-24738258-neuroprotective-peptides-age.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-30383932-semax-transthyretin-neuroprotection.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39435777-cerebrolysin-mci-dementia-prevention.md]

Pinealon now sits somewhere between the older preclinical peptide cluster and a weak human-exposure layer: the literature includes an older comparative human geroprotective study, review-level memory/TBI claims, a newer human induced-neuron mechanistic paper, and older cortex/skin short-peptide mechanistic papers, but still no clean modern cognition RCT.^[raw/articles/pubmed-28539017-comparative-analysis-geroprotective-methods-pinealon-vezugen.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-33396470-edr-peptide-mechanism-alzheimers-disease.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39518916-short-peptides-induced-neurons-age-related-changes.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-24909721-pinealon-serotonin-expression-brain-cortex.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-22803085-bioregulatory-tripeptides-skin-cells.md]

Relevant raw PubMed notes: `raw/articles/pubmed-24738258-neuroprotective-peptides-age.md`, `raw/articles/pubmed-30383932-semax-transthyretin-neuroprotection.md`, and `raw/articles/pubmed-41460391-cerebrolysin-age-induced-memory-decline.md`.

The evidence pattern is uneven: mechanistic and preclinical claims are abundant, while strong randomized evidence for healthy cognitive enhancement remains sparse. Cerebrolysin is the main partial exception inside the cluster because it has blinded AD trials, very-low-certainty vascular-dementia Cochrane support, a recent aMCI comparative study, a blinded SAH pilot with a neutral MoCA readout, and 2025 stroke-recovery studies; the direct healthy-human signal is only an old oral healthy-elderly EEG/memory study. The 2023 acute-stroke Cochrane review and the 2024 anaphylaxis case report keep the safety/endpoint read conservative. Cortexin has its own older ischemia/recovery branch, but neither Cerebrolysin nor Cortexin makes the whole peptide class a validated enhancer.^[raw/articles/pubmed-11552768-cerebrolysin-alz-28-week-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-20500802-cerebrolysin-dose-finding-ad-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-6844361-cerebrolysin-vascular-dementia-cochrane-2019.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39435777-cerebrolysin-mci-dementia-prevention.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-33143640-cerebrolysin-sah-pilot.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-10961443-oral-cerebrolysin-healthy-elderly-alpha-memory.md]^[raw/articles/cochrane-2023-cerebrolysin-acute-ischaemic-stroke.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39055722-cerebrolysin-anaphylaxis-case-report.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-25403301-cortexin-brain-ischemia-open-clinical-trial.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-30335070-cortexin-chronic-cerebral-ischemia-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-20517210-cortexin-alcohol-encephalopathy-polyneuropathy.md]

The newest Cerebrolysin preclinical additions fit the neuroprotection story cleanly but should not be overpromoted: one 2025 study links vascular-risk recognition-memory rescue to dendritic/spine remodeling in hypertensive female rats, and another links ketamine-state recognition/anxiety rescue to mitochondrial and CREB/PGC-1alpha changes in mice. The 2025 psychiatry scoping review finds psychiatric literature sparse, so those animal findings remain mechanism hypotheses rather than human psychiatric cognition evidence.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39826813-cerebrolysin-hypertensive-rat-memory-spines.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41204270-cerebrolysin-ketamine-cognitive-impairment-creb-pgc1a.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40722733-cerebrolysin-psychiatry-scoping-review.md]
