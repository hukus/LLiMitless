# Transcranial Random Noise Stimulation and Cognition

Transcranial random noise stimulation (tRNS) is a form of transcranial electric stimulation that passes current at random frequencies rather than using the fixed-frequency framing of tACS or the direct-current framing of tDCS. The mechanism usually invoked is stochastic resonance, but the current human literature is still too small to treat that as a broad cognition-enhancement platform. The better read is that tRNS has a few narrow task- and phenotype-specific signals, plus several nulls that prevent overgeneralization.

The branch is now broader than it looked in the first pass. A 2024 healthy-adult randomized trial found an online DLPFC working-memory benefit during dual n-back. A 2025 crossover in anxious healthy men found a left-DLPFC effect on negative-emotion attention bias. A 2026 schizophrenia crossover found planning and working-memory gains with bilateral DLPFC tRNS. At the same time, a 2025 numerical-cognition RCT and a preregistered motion-processing report both came back null, which keeps the branch from drifting into broad-enhancement language.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39402554-trns-working-memory-healthy-individuals.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41387754-trns-attention-bias-negative-emotional-stimuli.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-12949120-trns-schizophrenia-bilateral-prefrontal.md]^[raw/articles/nature-s41598-025-21890-x-trns-numerical-acuity.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40669128-trns-motion-processing-null-registered-report.md]

The newest broad human source is still the 2026 systematic review in psychiatric disorders. It included 22 studies and 642 participants, with the clearest signals in ADHD, dyslexia, and some schizophrenia outcomes, while depression remained mixed. The review also reported mostly mild and transient adverse effects.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41582468-trns-psychiatric-systematic-review.md]

## Key Facts

- Native exposure is random-frequency transcranial current, so frequency spectrum and montage are part of the dose.
- The clearest positive human signals are not broad healthy-adult enhancement claims.
- Healthy working memory, anxious-attention bias, and schizophrenia are currently the strongest positive branches.
- Numerical estimation and motion processing are the clearest null boundary cases.
- The 2025 perceptual-motor paper adds a performance-adjacent positive signal, but it still sits below the executive-function evidence.

## Open Questions

- Whether any healthy-adult cognitive benefit survives outside online, task-specific working memory.
- Whether DLPFC is the most relevant cognitive target or just the best-studied one.
- Whether tRNS is acting through stochastic resonance, network excitability, or a mix of both.
- Whether the psychiatric signals generalize beyond small, protocol-specific studies.
