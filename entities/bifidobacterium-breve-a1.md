# Bifidobacterium Breve A1

Bifidobacterium breve A1, also identified as MCC1274 in the main trial, is a strain-specific probiotic cognition lead. It should not be collapsed into generic Bifidobacterium, yogurt, synbiotics, multistrain probiotics, or the broader [[gut-microbiome-and-cognition]] branch.

Medicinal signal: `low-moderate / suspected-MCI-specific`. The strongest single trial is a 2020 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study in 80 older adults with suspected mild cognitive impairment. Daily B. breve A1 at 2 x 10^10 CFU for 16 weeks improved RBANS total score by 11.3 points versus placebo, with immediate memory, visuospatial/constructional, and delayed-memory improvements; 79 participants completed the study and no adverse events were reported. A 2026 corrigendum corrected a JMCIS table value, so the page should track the result but avoid treating it as fully settled replication.^[raw/articles/pubmed-32623402-bifidobacterium-breve-mci-cognition.md]

Evidence boundary: this is not healthy-adult enhancement evidence. Recent probiotic meta-analyses are mixed: one broad 2025 RCT synthesis found small low-certainty gains on MMSE, MoCA, and categorical fluency, while a 2024 MCI/AD-specific meta-analysis stayed non-significant across reported cognitive domains. B. breve A1 is therefore a product-specific memory lead inside a noisy class, not proof that any probiotic improves cognition.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41291638-probiotic-supplements-cognitive-function-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40034358-probiotics-mci-ad-meta-analysis.md]

Product and protocol gates:

- Strain identity: B. breve A1 / MCC1274 must be explicit.
- Dose: the positive RCT used 2 x 10^10 CFU/day for 16 weeks.
- Population: older adults with suspected MCI, not young healthy adults or acute brain fog.
- Endpoint: RBANS memory-heavy signal, not broad IQ, processing speed, or fatigue relief.
- Quality controls: viable count, storage/cold-chain assumptions, expiry date, and lot-level identity matter.
- Safety screen: immunocompromise, central lines, severe illness, antibiotic timing, GI intolerance, and clinician oversight in frail or medically complex users.
