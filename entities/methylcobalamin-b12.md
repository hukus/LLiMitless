# Methylcobalamin / Vitamin B12 Forms

Methylcobalamin, also called methyl-B12 or mecobalamin, is a vitamin B12 form involved in one-carbon metabolism through methionine synthase. This page is the form-specific route for methylcobalamin brain-fog, methylation, and B12-deficiency claims. It should not be collapsed with [[hydroxy-adenosyl-cyano-b12]], B12 injections, multivitamins, [[folinic-acid-leucovorin]], [[l-methylfolate-5-mthf]], [[s-adenosyl-l-methionine]], or generic [[folate-b-vitamins]] evidence without status and form accounting.^[raw/articles/ods-2025-vitamin-b12-health-professional-fact-sheet.md]^[raw/articles/ods-2026-vitamin-b12-form-comparison.md]

Medicinal cognition signal: `low/status-correction only`. B12 deficiency can cause neurological and cognitive symptoms, and deficiency treatment is medical care rather than nootropic experimentation. Outside overt deficiency or advanced neurological disease, a 2021 meta-analysis of 16 RCTs found no evidence that B12 alone or B12/B-complex supplementation improved cognitive subdomains, depressive symptoms, or robust fatigue outcomes. This keeps methylcobalamin in the lab/status lane, not the broad healthy-adult enhancement lane.^[raw/articles/ods-2025-vitamin-b12-health-professional-fact-sheet.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-33809274-b12-cognition-fatigue-meta.md]

MCI / homocysteine signal: `low and combination-bound`. A 2025 methyl-nutrients network meta-analysis concluded that folate plus vitamin B12 was the best methyl-nutrient option for cognition and one-carbon markers in older adults with MCI, but this does not isolate methylcobalamin. A 2020 RCT more directly relevant to methylcobalamin used methylcobalamin 500 mcg plus folic acid 400 mcg daily for 24 months in 279 older MCI patients with elevated homocysteine. Homocysteine dropped, but the primary cognitive decline outcome and secondary cognitive outcomes were not significantly different at 24 months; 12-month executive-function and depression hints did not persist.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40249981-methyl-nutrients-mci-nma.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-31787369-methylcobalamin-folic-acid-mci-rct.md]

Anecdote signal: `weak-moderate, deficiency-skewed, and bidirectional`. The captured B12-deficiency thread reports high-dose methylcobalamin relieving migraines and brain fog while also causing marked demotivation. Comments discuss folate balance, magnesium, potassium, salt, and adjustment periods. This is useful for status/cofactor tracking and adverse-event monitoring, but it cannot upgrade efficacy.^[raw/articles/reddit-2025-b12-methylcobalamin-brain-fog-demotivation-thread.md]

Safety and interpretation signal: `status-gated`. ODS notes B12's roles in central nervous system development, myelination, red blood cells, DNA synthesis, methionine synthase, and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase. It highlights serum B12, methylmalonic acid, homocysteine, deficiency causes, metformin or acid-suppression exposure, vegan diets, gastrointestinal disease or surgery, pernicious anemia, and neurological irreversibility if deficiency is untreated. B12 has no established tolerable upper intake level because of low toxicity potential, but diagnosis, route, dose, and cause of deficiency still matter.^[raw/articles/ods-2025-vitamin-b12-health-professional-fact-sheet.md]

Form boundary: `now separated`. [[hydroxy-adenosyl-cyano-b12]] tracks hydroxycobalamin, adenosylcobalamin, and cyanocobalamin as non-methyl B12 form options. ODS and form-comparison reviews keep this boundary conservative: form choice can matter for tolerability, route, and status-correction logistics, but it is not cognition proof by itself. MTHFR/B12 forums produce visible hydroxy/adenosyl-as-gentler anecdotes, but those reports stay lab/status and stack-confounded.^[raw/articles/ods-2026-vitamin-b12-form-comparison.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-4692085-coenzyme-b12-forms-not-superior.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-28223907-b12-forms-bioavailability-review.md]^[raw/articles/reddit-2024-2026-hydroxy-adenosyl-cyano-b12-brainfog-threads.md]

## Signal Split

| Layer | Signal | Readout |
|---|---|---|
| Deficiency treatment | `real medical-status signal` | B12 deficiency can produce neurological and cognitive symptoms; cause and route matter |
| Non-deficient adult RCT meta-analysis | `negative counterweight` | B12 alone or B-complex supplementation did not improve cognitive subdomains in people without overt deficiency or advanced neurological disease |
| MCI methyl-nutrients NMA | `low/supportive combination` | Folate plus B12 looked best for older adults with MCI, homocysteine, and B12/folate markers |
| Methylcobalamin plus folic acid RCT | `low/mostly null` | Homocysteine improved, but 24-month primary and secondary cognitive outcomes were null |
| Healthy-adult methylcobalamin cognition | `unproven` | No clean methylcobalamin-alone healthy-adult cognition endpoint captured |

## Practical Interpretation

Methylcobalamin belongs in this wiki because B12 deficiency is a real cognitive/neurological status problem and because methyl-B12 is a common brain-fog self-experiment. It should not be ranked as a broad nootropic. The best current route is `status correction and attribution control`: confirm B12, MMA, homocysteine, folate, kidney function, medications, diet, and deficiency cause before interpreting subjective cognitive change.

Any confidence upgrade needs methylcobalamin-alone or form-separated trials with objective cognition endpoints, baseline B12/MMA/homocysteine/folate status, route and dose transparency, and separation from folic acid, 5-MTHF, B6, SAMe, TMG, choline, iron, thyroid, sleep, and long-COVID/autonomic confounds.
