# Atomoxetine

Atomoxetine is a prescription nonstimulant ADHD medication and selective norepinephrine reuptake inhibitor. In this wiki it sits in the [[nonstimulant-noradrenergic-attention-agent-watchlist]] beside [[bupropion]], [[viloxazine]], and [[reboxetine]], with [[guanfacine]] nearby as a different alpha-2A prefrontal-control comparator.

## Bottom Line

Brain-fog / cognition signal: `low formal, moderate mixed anecdotal`. The best healthy-volunteer signal is response inhibition: a Science 2006 pharmacology study found that noradrenaline reuptake inhibition with atomoxetine improved stop-signal response inhibition while not improving probabilistic learning. That is a real but narrow prefrontal-control signal, not broad healthy-adult enhancement.^[raw/articles/pubmed-16469930-atomoxetine-response-inhibition-healthy-volunteers.md]

Clinical-state signal: ADHD treatment, not a nootropic benchmark. The label establishes atomoxetine as a prescription ADHD medication, but ADHD symptom reduction and healthy-adult cognitive enhancement are different claims.^[raw/articles/dailymed-2026-strattera-label.md]

Practical route: prescription/safety-first. DailyMed safety gates include suicidality monitoring, rare severe liver injury, BP/HR increases, cardiovascular caution, orthostasis/syncope, urinary retention or hesitation, priapism warnings, bipolar screening, CYP2D6 metabolism, and high-exposure contexts in poor metabolizers or with strong CYP2D6 inhibitors.^[raw/articles/dailymed-2026-strattera-label.md]

## Current Read

Atomoxetine is a useful comparator for norepinephrine-linked response inhibition and prefrontal control. It is not a general smart drug in the current corpus. The best way to route it is as a state-bound nonstimulant attention agent: potentially relevant when impulsivity, ADHD-like executive dysfunction, or stimulant avoidance is the problem, but too safety- and tolerability-gated for healthy-adult enhancement ranking.
