# Blueberry Anthocyanins

Blueberry anthocyanins are the cognition-relevant branch of blueberry products: wild blueberry juice, freeze-dried blueberry powder, whole blueberries, blueberry extracts, and mixed grape-blueberry polyphenol products. The wiki treats this as a product-specific anthocyanin / berry-polyphenol branch, not as proof that every berry, juice, powder, capsule, bilberry extract, aronia extract, grape-blueberry blend, or isolated anthocyanin supplement is interchangeable.

Medicinal cognition signal: `low-moderate/product-specific`. A recent anthocyanin RCT meta-analysis reports a positive family-level global-cognition signal, and the existing dietary-flavonoid meta-analysis keeps berries visible as one of the more plausible food-derived branches. The direct blueberry trial layer is also repeatedly positive but small and endpoint-selective: early wild-blueberry juice improved paired-associate learning and word-list recall in 9 older adults with memory changes; 24 g/day freeze-dried blueberry powder improved selected executive-function measures in healthy older adults; 24-week blueberry supplementation in subjective cognitive impairment improved cognitive symptoms and memory discrimination; and the 2023 wild-blueberry powder trial improved immediate recall, task-switch accuracy, flow-mediated dilation, and systolic blood pressure. Counterweights are important: the MCI fMRI trial showed neural activation without clear working-memory enhancement, the mixed grape-blueberry Memophenol trial is not blueberry alone, and a 1-week fresh/powder blueberry cross-over trial found no consistent responder pattern across cognitive endpoints.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41351717-anthocyanin-cognition-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-9787524-dietary-flavonoids-human-cognition-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/acs-2010-blueberry-supplementation-memory-older-adults.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-28283823-dietary-blueberry-older-adults-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-29458842-fish-oil-blueberry-subjective-cognitive-impairment-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-10315404-wild-blueberry-polyphenols-cognition-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-28221821-blueberry-mci-fmri-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-37063535-memophenol-grape-blueberry-mci-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-38542806-blueberry-interindividual-response-rct.md]

Anecdote signal: `weak-moderate salience, weak efficacy`. The captured /r/Nootropics thread had high visible attention for a food-based nootropic topic and treated blueberries as a memory/focus base stack, but the material is mostly study-sharing, food-health lore, and product-potency discussion. It has no blinded denominator or objective cognitive readout.^[raw/articles/reddit-2019-blueberries-memory-bdnf-thread.md]

Safety and product-quality signal: `low-moderate caution`. Food-dose blueberries have a much lower risk profile than gray-market nootropics, and the captured extract trial reported no significant adverse reactions in the abstract. The main practical issues are product transportability, juice sugar load, background diet, extract potency, food-versus-capsule equivalence, and the possibility that vascular/metabolic changes are being mistaken for a direct nootropic effect.^[raw/articles/pubmed-37063535-memophenol-grape-blueberry-mci-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-38542806-blueberry-interindividual-response-rct.md]

## Signal Split

| Layer | Signal | Readout |
|---|---|---|
| Anthocyanin family meta-analysis | `low-moderate` | Broad positive RCT-level family signal, but not product-interchangeability proof |
| Early memory-complaint juice study | `low/preliminary` | Very small 12-week wild-blueberry juice memory signal |
| Healthy older-adult powder RCTs | `low-moderate/product-specific` | 24-26 g/day freeze-dried blueberry powder improved selected executive, recall, task-switching, vascular, or blood-pressure endpoints |
| Subjective cognitive impairment | `low` | Blueberry improved symptoms and memory discrimination; fish-oil plus blueberry was not additive |
| MCI fMRI and Memophenol | `low/contested` | Neural activation and mixed grape-blueberry extract signals support plausibility, but are not clean blueberry-alone behavioral proof |
| Responder heterogeneity | `negative/variable` | One 1-week trial found no consistent cognitive-response pattern or metabolite predictor |
| Anecdote | `weak-moderate salience, weak efficacy` | Strong food-health / nootropics visibility, weak direct self-experiment evidence |
| Safety / quality | `low-moderate caution` | Food-dose path is comparatively benign; extracts, juices, dose, sugar, and product identity remain gating variables |

## Practical Interpretation

Blueberry anthocyanins now cross the standalone threshold, but only as a low-confidence, product-specific branch. The most defensible read is older-adult / subjective-memory / vascular-metabolic support with selected recall, executive, and task-switching endpoints. It should not outrank benchmark comparators in [[molecule-intake-triage-table]], and it should stay linked to [[polyphenol-nrf2-cognition-watchlist]] rather than being treated as a universal healthy-adult nootropic.

For future updates, keep these exposures separate: wild blueberry juice, freeze-dried wild blueberry powder, highbush whole blueberries, standardized anthocyanin dose, bilberry-style extracts, [[aronia-melanocarpa-extract]], mixed grape-blueberry products, and generic anthocyanin capsules. Product equivalence is the main unresolved issue, not whether the berry/polyphenol branch is worth tracking.
