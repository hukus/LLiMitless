# Methylsulfonylmethane / MSM

Methylsulfonylmethane, also called MSM, dimethyl sulfone, or DMSO2, is a small organosulfur supplement. In this wiki it is a low-confidence attribution-control page for sulfur, methylation, detox, joint-health, oxidative-stress, and brain-fog anecdotes. It should not be collapsed with [[s-adenosyl-l-methionine]], [[l-methionine]], DMSO, [[n-acetylcysteine]], [[glynac]], [[glutathione-products]], [[taurine]], or sulfonamide drugs.^[raw/articles/pubchem-2026-methylsulfonylmethane-identity.md]

Medicinal cognition signal: `very low`. No captured human trial tests MSM for objective cognition or healthy-adult brain-fog relief. The formal human literature is mainly joint pain, inflammation, exercise recovery, oxidative-stress, and safety context. A half-marathon RCT in healthy runners found no statistically significant time-by-treatment benefit for oxidative-stress, muscle-damage, or pain outcomes, with clinically but not statistically significant pain reductions and no cognitive endpoints.^[raw/articles/pmc-5521097-msm-half-marathon-rct.md]

Brain-exposure signal: `real but not efficacy`. A 2001 MRS study detected MSM in the brains of MSM-exposed normal volunteers and memory-loss patients after 1-3 g/day, with brain concentrations in the millimolar range and no reported adverse clinical or neurochemical effects in that small exposed group. This supports blood-brain-barrier passage and MRS detectability, not cognitive benefit.^[raw/articles/sciencedirect-2001-methylsulfonylmethane-human-brain-mrs.md]

Methylation / sulfur signal: `mechanism only`. A PubMed-indexed HepaRG cell study reports that MSM can serve as a methyl-group donor for DNA methylation in human liver cells. That makes MSM relevant to one-carbon and methylation lore, but the result is cell-model evidence, not human brain evidence and not a proof of "methylation support" for cognition.^[raw/articles/pubmed-36469606-msm-dna-methyl-donor-heparg.md]

Safety / evidence-quality signal: `low-moderate caution`. A 2017 review frames MSM as generally well tolerated in common supplement ranges and summarizes anti-inflammatory / antioxidant uses, but it is not a cognition review and has ingredient-industry relevance. NCCIH is more conservative for osteoarthritis: it says only a small amount of research exists, no conclusion can be reached about usefulness, and MSM safety is uncertain because little research has been done, with possible allergic reactions, digestive upset, and rash.^[raw/articles/pmc-2017-methylsulfonylmethane-applications-safety-review.md]^[raw/articles/nccih-2026-dmso-msm-osteoarthritis-safety.md]

Anecdote signal: `weak-moderate and bidirectional`. Reddit and supplement-community discussion includes clearer focus, less anxiety, less fog, or better energy reports, but also brain fog, anxiety, insomnia, headache, histamine/sulfur-intolerance framing, and wired/tired reactions. MSM is often stacked with vitamin C, collagen, glucosamine/chondroitin, methyl-B vitamins, NAC, glutathione products, molybdenum, binders, or detox protocols, so attribution is weak.^[raw/articles/reddit-2020-2026-msm-brainfog-sulfur-threads.md]

## Practical Interpretation

MSM deserves a standalone page because it is common, buyable, chemically adjacent to methylation/sulfur lore, and capable of reaching the brain. It is not a cognition lead. Its useful role is boundary control: when a brain-fog report mentions sulfur, methyl donors, NAC/GSH, detox, joint pain, or methylation, MSM should be tracked separately from SAMe, methionine, NAC, GlyNAC, glutathione, taurine, and B vitamins.

Any future confidence upgrade would require MSM-alone trials with objective cognition endpoints, product identity, dose and timing, baseline inflammation/oxidative-stress or sulfur-handling markers, medication/supplement co-use, sleep tracking, and adverse-event capture for anxiety, insomnia, headache, GI state, rash/allergy, and fog.
