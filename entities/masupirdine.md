# Masupirdine

Masupirdine, also known as SUVN-502, is a 5-HT6 receptor antagonist tested as adjunctive therapy in moderate Alzheimer's disease. It is a useful example of a cognition-negative primary trial with a later post hoc behavioral hypothesis.

Medicinal cognition signal: `negative for cognition`. In a 26-week phase 2 trial, masupirdine 50 mg/day or 100 mg/day was added to donepezil and memantine in moderate Alzheimer's disease. The primary ADAS-Cog11 endpoint was not significant at either dose, and the secondary efficacy outcomes were also negative.^[raw/articles/pubmed-35662833-masupirdine-suvn-502-moderate-ad-cognition.md]

Behavioral hypothesis signal: `post hoc only`. A later analysis reported agitation/aggression and psychosis subgroup signals, but that result is neuropsychiatric-symptom hypothesis generation, not evidence that masupirdine improves cognition.^[raw/articles/pubmed-36168659-masupirdine-posthoc-agitation-psychosis.md]

## Safety And Interpretation Caveats

- The phase 2 cognition miss should dominate any cognition routing.
- Post hoc behavioral signals can justify future symptom-targeted trials, but they should not be counted as memory or executive-function evidence.
- Adjunctive donepezil plus memantine exposure makes attribution and ceiling effects relevant, but it does not convert a negative primary endpoint into a positive class signal.
