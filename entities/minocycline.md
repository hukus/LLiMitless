# Minocycline

Minocycline is a tetracycline antibiotic with anti-inflammatory, microglial, and neuroimmune hypotheses layered on top of its antimicrobial use. In the wiki it belongs near [[neuroinflammation-and-cognition]], [[neuroimmune-brain-fog-chemical-guide]], and [[pde-camp-creb-cognition-2026-scan]], not in the casual supplement/nootropic lane.

## Bottom Line

Brain-fog clearance signal: `low but notable`. The strongest direct source is an uncontrolled ME / long-COVID pilot in which minocycline was associated with broad symptom improvement, including brain fog, and the corrected long-COVID table moved brain fog from 8/18 before treatment to 3/18 after treatment. Because there was no placebo arm, no blinding, and symptom ratings were highly vulnerable to regression and selection effects, this is a hypothesis-generating signal rather than proof.^[raw/articles/sciencedirect-2024-minocycline-me-long-covid-pilot.md]^[raw/articles/sciencedirect-2025-minocycline-me-long-covid-corrigendum.md]

General cognition signal: `mixed-to-negative`. Healthy-volunteer spatial-memory work found a task-specific boundary/landmark split rather than broad improvement, while small-vessel disease, mild Alzheimer disease, postoperative cognition, and schizophrenia studies provide important counterweights against treating minocycline as a general cognitive enhancer.^[raw/articles/pubmed-32839527-minocycline-spatial-memory.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-38629936-minerva-svd-minocycline.md]^[raw/articles/jamanetwork-2020-minocycline-mild-ad-rct.md]^[raw/articles/researchgate-2023-minocycline-postoperative-cognition-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-27919523-minocycline-schizophrenia-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/johnshopkins-2019-minocycline-schizophrenia-large-rct.md]

Practical route: keep minocycline as a medically supervised antibiotic/neuroimmune hypothesis. Its brain-fog signal is more direct than most chemicals in this category, but its safety, resistance, microbiome, vestibular, autoimmune, pregnancy, intracranial-hypertension, and hypersensitivity issues make it a poor self-experiment anchor.^[raw/articles/dailymed-2026-minocycline-label.md]

## Brain Fog And Recovery Evidence

The 2024 pilot used 100 mg twice on day 1 and then 100 mg daily for 41 days in 55 ME patients, including 19 long-COVID patients. Four discontinued early because of nausea or dizziness. Among 51 completers, 41 improved by at least two performance-status points, and 16/18 long-COVID completers were rated favorable. The paper reported significant improvements in fatigue, post-exertional malaise, unrefreshing sleep, brain fog, disequilibrium, orthostatic intolerance, and neuropathic pain.^[raw/articles/sciencedirect-2024-minocycline-me-long-covid-pilot.md]

The 2025 corrigendum is important because it gives a cleaner long-COVID symptom table: brain fog was recorded in 8/18 long-COVID completers before treatment and 3/18 after treatment. Fatigue and post-exertional malaise also fell sharply in the corrected table. This strengthens the direct brain-fog relevance but does not solve the uncontrolled-design problem.^[raw/articles/sciencedirect-2025-minocycline-me-long-covid-corrigendum.md]

## Counterweights

In healthy men, 150 mg twice daily for three days did not produce a simple enhancement pattern. Boundary-based spatial memory was marginally better, but landmark-based and overall performance were worse, and right caudate activity decreased. Treat that as target modulation, not broad clarity.^[raw/articles/pubmed-32839527-minocycline-spatial-memory.md]

The MINERVA small-vessel-disease trial did not find meaningful effects on microglial PET signal, blood-brain-barrier permeability, or serum inflammatory markers over three months. A 544-participant mild-Alzheimer trial found no cognitive or functional slowing over 24 months, with poorer tolerability at 400 mg/day. A postoperative-cognition RCT did not reduce postoperative cognitive dysfunction. Schizophrenia studies are also mixed: small trials and symptom signals exist, but meta-analysis and a larger 200-person RCT did not show reliable neurocognitive benefit.^[raw/articles/pubmed-38629936-minerva-svd-minocycline.md]^[raw/articles/jamanetwork-2020-minocycline-mild-ad-rct.md]^[raw/articles/researchgate-2023-minocycline-postoperative-cognition-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-31416745-minocycline-schizophrenia-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-27919523-minocycline-schizophrenia-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/johnshopkins-2019-minocycline-schizophrenia-large-rct.md]

## Safety And Status

DailyMed frames minocycline as a prescription tetracycline antibiotic, not a nootropic. Key safety gates include hypersensitivity, vestibular dizziness or vertigo, intracranial hypertension, autoimmune reactions including lupus-like syndrome and hepatitis, pigmentation, photosensitivity, GI effects, antimicrobial resistance, pregnancy/fetal-tooth-bone concerns, and drug-interaction context.^[raw/articles/dailymed-2026-minocycline-label.md]

## Signal Judgment

Minocycline deserves inclusion because it has one of the few direct long-COVID/ME brain-fog signals in this neuroimmune chemical set. It remains low-confidence and risk-heavy because the direct signal is uncontrolled, broader cognition trials are not supportive, and antibiotic exposure carries risks that are qualitatively different from supplement or legacy-xanthine candidates.
