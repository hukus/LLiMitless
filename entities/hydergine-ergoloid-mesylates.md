# Hydergine / Ergoloid Mesylates

Hydergine is the historical brand name for ergoloid mesylates / dihydroergotoxine-type ergot-alkaloid mixtures. It should not be collapsed into nicergoline, ergotamine migraine products, or generic "vasodilator nootropic" lore. PubChem and DailyMed describe it as a mixture involving dihydroergocornine, dihydroergocristine, and alpha/beta dihydroergocryptine mesylates.^[raw/articles/pubchem-2026-ergoloid-mesylates-identity.md]^[raw/articles/dailymed-2014-ergoloid-mesylates-label.md]

## Bottom Line

Medicinal signal: `low/legacy clinical-state`. The strongest formal source is the 2001 Cochrane review in dementia-like syndromes: 19 trials met inclusion criteria, global ratings and comprehensive rating scales favored Hydergine, but most trials predated modern dementia diagnostic standards and uncertainty remained. This is not healthy-adult enhancement evidence.^[raw/articles/pubmed-11405961-hydergine-dementia-cochrane.md]

Anecdotal signal: `weak-moderate historical smart-drug salience`, with one newer stimulant-crash self-report. The modern anecdote is interesting for hypothesis generation but stack-confounded, single-user, and involved a self-reported dose above the DailyMed label's 1 mg three-times-daily regimen.^[raw/articles/reddit-2019-hydergine-nicergoline-thread.md]^[raw/articles/reddit-2025-ergoloid-mesylates-methylphenidate-crash-thread.md]^[raw/articles/dailymed-2014-ergoloid-mesylates-label.md]

Practical route: keep [[hydergine-ergoloid-mesylates]] as a legacy clinical-state comparator below modern better-specified branches such as [[roflumilast]], [[sodium-benzoate]], [[citicoline]], and [[cholinergic-state-restoration-and-cognition]].

## Evidence

The Cochrane review is positive but dated. It reports 19 eligible randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials in dementia or dementia-consistent symptoms; 12 trials with global ratings favored Hydergine and nine trials with comprehensive ratings also favored it. The reviewers still stressed diagnostic-era limitations, unavailable data for many published results, and uncertainty about efficacy in dementia.^[raw/articles/pubmed-11405961-hydergine-dementia-cochrane.md]

The old multi-infarct dementia trial reinforces the vascular/clinical-state context. It anchors Hydergine in impairment and dementia rather than in healthy-adult focus or memory enhancement.^[raw/articles/pubmed-2680286-hydergine-multi-infarct-dementia-rct.md]

DailyMed is useful because it narrows the claim. The label states that a proportion of people over 60 with idiopathic mental-capacity decline may experience symptomatic relief, but also says the mechanism for mental effects is not clearly established and that there is no conclusive evidence it particularly affects cerebral arteriosclerosis or cerebrovascular insufficiency.^[raw/articles/dailymed-2014-ergoloid-mesylates-label.md]

## Status And Safety

MedlinePlus says ergoloid mesylates have been discontinued in the United States and that its monograph is no longer updated because the drug is no longer available in the U.S. market. This makes sourcing and country/formulation status part of any modern nootropic interpretation.^[raw/articles/medlineplus-2026-ergoloid-mesylates-discontinued.md]

DailyMed contraindicates ergoloid mesylates in hypersensitivity and acute or chronic psychosis. The listed adverse reactions are transient nausea and gastric disturbances. The label dose is 1 mg three times daily, with gradual onset over 3-4 weeks, which is far from rapid acute nootropic logic.^[raw/articles/dailymed-2014-ergoloid-mesylates-label.md]

## Signal Judgment

Hydergine deserves a standalone page because it is a historically important nootropic and dementia-drug name with a real Cochrane-era evidence packet. It does not deserve a recommendation upgrade: the formal evidence is old clinical-state work, the label itself is cautious about mechanism and predictors, and modern anecdotes are sparse or stack-bound.
