# Folate / B Vitamins

This page covers the cognition-relevant folate/B-vitamin lane: folate or folic acid (B9), vitamin B12 / cobalamins, vitamin B6, methylfolate/5-MTHF, and homocysteine-lowering combinations. It is tracked as a nutrient-status and one-carbon-metabolism branch, not as a generic energy or focus enhancer. It should not be collapsed with [[dha-epa]], [[vitamin-d3]], multivitamins, prenatal folate, methylation-genotype protocols, B12 injections for confirmed deficiency, or folic-acid+DHA stacks without attribution.

Medicinal cognition signal: `low/contested and homocysteine-status gated`. The strongest clean signal is VITACOG: folic acid 0.8 mg/day plus B12 0.5 mg/day plus B6 20 mg/day for 2 years slowed whole-brain atrophy in MCI, especially when baseline homocysteine was high, and the cognitive-outcome paper found benefit in elevated-homocysteine subgroups. A 2024 MCI meta-analysis also reports positive vitamin effects, with folic acid prominent, and the 2026 nutrient NMA reports folic acid plus DHA memory support. This is enough for a standalone entity, but the route is `MCI / high homocysteine / deficiency or insufficiency`, not broad enhancement.^[raw/articles/pmc-2935890-vitacog-b-vitamins-mci-brain-atrophy.md]^[raw/articles/oxford-2012-vitacog-b-vitamins-mci-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-11139751-vitamins-pufas-mci-meta.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41764841-nutritional-supplements-cognition-nma.md]

The counterweights are equally important. A DARE-assessed 2012 meta-analysis concluded that B12, B6, or folic acid supplementation did not improve cognitive function in older adults with or without cognitive impairment. A 2021 B12/B-complex RCT meta-analysis found no evidence of benefit on any cognitive subdomain, depressive symptoms, or general fatigue in people without overt B12 deficiency or advanced neurological disorders. ODS's B12 fact sheet similarly emphasizes that RCTs generally do not show cognitive improvement from B12 alone or with folate/B6 when deficiency treatment is not the question.^[raw/articles/ncbi-bookshelf-2012-homocysteine-lowering-b-vitamins-cognition-meta.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-33809274-b12-cognition-fatigue-meta.md]^[raw/articles/ods-2025-vitamin-b12-health-professional-fact-sheet.md]

Combination attribution is fragile. A 2024 MCI trial reported cognitive and oxidative/mitochondrial biomarker benefits from folic acid, DHA, and their combination, with the combination outperforming either alone. However, an earlier Journal of Alzheimer's Disease folic-acid-plus-DHA paper from the same broad claim area was retracted for ethics approval and registry issues. The wiki should keep FA, DHA, FA+DHA, B12, B6, and homocysteine-lowering stacks separate until trial lineage, registry status, and independent replication are clear.^[raw/articles/karger-2024-folic-acid-dha-mci-cognition-rct.md]^[raw/articles/jad-2023-folic-acid-dha-retraction.md]

Anecdote signal: `weak-moderate, deficiency-skewed, and adverse-prone`. Community reports often describe B12, folate, or B-complexes improving brain fog, migraines, mood, or baseline energy when deficiency is suspected or confirmed. The same communities report demotivation, wired anxiety, panic, insomnia, rumination, irritability, histamine-like reactions, and "too methylated" experiences, especially with high-dose methylcobalamin, methylfolate, or methylated B-complexes. Anecdote should prompt lab-status and dose/form tracking; it cannot upgrade efficacy.^[raw/articles/reddit-2025-b12-methylcobalamin-brain-fog-demotivation-thread.md]^[raw/articles/reddit-2025-methylfolate-mental-side-effects-thread.md]

Safety and status signal: `moderate lab-guided caution`. Folate status, B12 status, methylmalonic acid, homocysteine, kidney function, medication exposure, and form/dose all matter. High folic acid can mask B12-deficiency anemia while neurological injury progresses, and ODS notes concern around low B12 plus high folic acid in older adults. B12 has low toxicity and no established UL, but deficiency cause matters. B6 is not benign at high dose: chronic high-dose pyridoxine can cause sensory neuropathy, and EFSA's adult UL is much lower than the US UL. Confirmed deficiency treatment is medical care, not nootropic experimentation.^[raw/articles/ods-2025-folate-health-professional-fact-sheet.md]^[raw/articles/ods-2025-vitamin-b12-health-professional-fact-sheet.md]^[raw/articles/ods-2023-vitamin-b6-health-professional-fact-sheet.md]

## Signal Split

| Layer | Signal | Readout |
|---|---|---|
| VITACOG atrophy RCT | `low-moderate/phenotype-gated` | B9+B12+B6 slowed brain atrophy in MCI, strongest when homocysteine was high |
| VITACOG cognitive outcomes | `supportive subgroup` | Executive function stabilized and high-homocysteine participants showed MMSE, delayed-recall, and category-fluency benefits |
| 2024 MCI vitamin/PUFA meta-analysis | `supportive but low evidence` | Vitamins improved global cognition, memory, and attention; folic acid appeared prominent, but evidence intensity was low |
| 2026 nutrient NMA | `low/supportive` | Folic acid plus DHA improved memory in the older/MCI nutrient network |
| Broad homocysteine-lowering meta-analysis | `negative counterweight` | B12/B6/folic acid did not reliably improve cognition across older adults |
| 2021 B12/B-complex meta-analysis | `negative in non-deficient people` | No cognitive, depressive-symptom, or robust fatigue benefit without overt B12 deficiency or advanced neurological disease |
| FA+DHA lineage | `source-integrity caution` | 2024 trial is positive, but a related earlier JAD FA+DHA paper was retracted for ethics/registry concerns |
| Anecdote | `weak-moderate bidirectional` | Deficiency-correction brain-fog reports coexist with methylfolate/B12 anxiety, demotivation, and overstimulation reports |
| Safety / status | `moderate caution` | B12 masking, B6 neuropathy risk, homocysteine nonspecificity, kidney function, and medication effects gate interpretation |

## Practical Interpretation

Folate/B vitamins belong in the molecule atlas because the homocysteine/MCI branch has real RCT and biomarker support, and because B12/folate deficiency can cause cognitive or neurological symptoms. They should not be ranked as broad cognitive enhancers. The best read is `status correction and elevated-homocysteine MCI support`, with broad B12/B-complex supplementation in non-deficient people looking null.

Any confidence upgrade requires trials that pre-register endpoints, stratify by folate, B12, MMA, homocysteine, kidney function, baseline cognition, and DHA status, and clearly separate folic acid, 5-MTHF, B12 form, B6 dose, and DHA co-supplementation. Anecdote should be used for adverse-event and status-screening questions, not recommendation strength.
