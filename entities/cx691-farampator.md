# CX691 / Farampator

CX691, also called farampator, is a low-impact AMPAkine preclinical rescue candidate. It is relevant because it is a newer named molecule in the AMPAkine branch, but it has no human cognition evidence.

Medicinal cognition signal: `preclinical-only`. A 2025 preclinical paper reported that CX691 penetrated the blood-brain barrier, partially offset AMPAR desensitization, reduced amphetamine- and methamphetamine-driven locomotor activity in rats, preserved antipsychotic-like activity under chronic amphetamine, lacked catalepsy at supratherapeutic doses, and improved eight-arm radial-maze performance.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40245529-cx691-farampator-antipsychotic-like-low-impact-ampakine.md] That is useful schizophrenia-adjacent rescue biology, not a human cognition result.

Interpretation signal: `company-linked translational hypothesis`. CX691 should remain a comparison-level / preclinical entity until a human safety, target-engagement, or cognition study appears. The strongest role today is to show that the AMPAkine field continues to pivot toward low-impact rescue designs rather than general enhancement.
