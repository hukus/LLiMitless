# GPLD1

GPLD1, glycosylphosphatidylinositol-specific phospholipase D1, is a liver-derived exercise-responsive enzyme in the exerkine / blood-factor cognition lane. It is one of the strongest mechanistic exercise-transfer factors in mice, but it is not a validated human cognitive enhancer.

Medicinal cognition signal: `preclinical-plus-human-correlation`. In the 2020 Science paper, plasma from exercised aged mice transferred neurogenesis and cognition benefits to sedentary aged mice. GPLD1 rose after exercise, correlated with improved cognition in aged mice, was higher in active older humans, and systemic GPLD1 elevation improved age-related regenerative and cognitive impairments in aged mice.^[raw/articles/pubmed-32646997-gpld1-exercise-neurogenesis-cognition.md]

Mechanistic update: `preclinical / vasculature-mediated`. The 2026 Cell paper identified brain vasculature and TNAP cleavage as the downstream path: cerebrovascular TNAP impaired BBB transport and cognition in young mice, while GPLD1 elevation or TNAP inhibition improved BBB function and cognition in aging and Alzheimer's disease mouse models.^[raw/articles/sciencedirect-2026-gpld1-tnap-memory-loss-vasculature.md]

## Safety And Translation Caveats

- Exercise is the validated upstream intervention; GPLD1 is still a mechanism and target hypothesis.
- GPLD1 itself did not need to enter the brain in the 2026 model, which makes the pathway interesting but also harder to translate into simple dosing claims.
- Human evidence is activity-linked correlation, not exogenous GPLD1 treatment.
- Any future TNAP or BBB-targeting translation needs explicit vascular, bone/mineral, inflammatory, and cognitive safety monitoring before it can be compared with ordinary exercise.
