# Exenatide

## Bottom Line

Exenatide is the main negative clinical cognition anchor in the GLP-1 branch. Medicinal signal is `low/negative` for MCI because weekly long-acting exenatide improved metabolic markers but did not prevent cognitive decline on ADAS-Cog11. Anecdotal cognition signal is `absent-to-weak` in the current corpus.

## What It Is

Exenatide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist. In this wiki it belongs with [[incretin-therapies-brain-health]], [[incretin-therapies-brain-health-2026-scan]], and [[negative-evidence-watchlist-for-cognition-interventions]] because it is an important counterweight to GLP-1 optimism: the metabolic effect can be real while the cognition endpoint stays null.

## Medicinal Research Signal

The captured proof-of-concept MCI trial tested slow-release exenatide 2 mg subcutaneously once weekly for 32 weeks. The trial was negative on ADAS-Cog11 even though glucose and weight improved. This directly blocks a simple inference from metabolic improvement to cognitive improvement.^[raw/articles/pubmed-38565814-exenatide-mci-proof-of-concept-negative.md]

The broader 2026 GLP-1RA T2DM meta-analysis reported small MMSE and MoCA gains at the class level, especially with longer exposure. Exenatide should still be kept in that class discussion, but the molecule-specific MCI trial makes it a negative-evidence comparator rather than a positive standalone lead.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41104525-glp1-ra-cognitive-function-t2dm-meta-analysis.md]

## Safety And Status

The relevant cognition-interpretation risk is metabolic confounding. The MCI trial shows that better glucose or weight markers should not be treated as proof of cognitive rescue. Route exenatide reports through [[safety-interaction-matrix-for-multi-modal-stacks]] when glucose, weight, appetite, GI tolerability, sleep, or trial-participation effects could explain perceived cognition.

June 2026 metabolic-peptide attribution gate: exenatide reports should stay negative-evidence or comparator material unless they capture exact formulation, prescription status, route, dose schedule, titration or stable-dose phase, indication, baseline MCI/AD/T2DM/metabolic phenotype, glucose and weight changes, appetite and under-eating risk, hydration/electrolytes, sleep and mood state, GI adverse events, hypoglycemia-relevant co-medications, diet/exercise/metformin/SGLT2/stimulant co-interventions, washout or dechallenge, and objective cognition endpoints.
