# Beta Alanine

Beta-alanine is a non-proteinogenic amino acid used mainly to raise carnosine in muscle and improve high-intensity exercise performance. In this wiki it is a carnosine-precursor and state-challenge candidate, not the same intervention as pure [[anserine-carnosine]], [[essence-of-chicken]], ordinary meat intake, or stimulant preworkout blends.

Medicinal cognition signal: `low/subgroup and state-challenge bound`. The most direct cognition RCT in this pass tested 2.4 g/day beta-alanine for 10 weeks in 100 older adults. Across all participants, MoCA did not differ from placebo. In the subgroup whose baseline MoCA was at or below normal, beta-alanine improved MoCA at weeks 5 and 10. That is a preliminary older-adult screening-score signal, not healthy-adult enhancement proof.^[raw/articles/pmc-9960300-beta-alanine-older-adults-cognition-rct.md]

The tactical evidence is a counterweight. A combat-soldier trial reported improved tactical performance but not cognitive function. A 2023 soldier review says cognitive evidence is inconclusive and may depend on whether a stressor is applied during testing. This places beta-alanine near fatigue-resilience and tactical-performance physiology, not near acute nootropic focus.^[raw/articles/pmc-3983672-beta-alanine-combat-soldiers-cognition-null.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-9961614-beta-alanine-soldier-cognition-resiliency-review.md]

Safety and dose signal: `moderate for common sports dosing, with paraesthesia burden`. The ISSN position stand says 4-6 g/day for at least 2-4 weeks raises muscle carnosine and supports short-duration high-intensity exercise performance; it also says beta-alanine appears safe in healthy populations at recommended doses, with tingling / paraesthesia as the main reported side effect and less burden from divided or sustained-release dosing.^[raw/articles/pubmed-26175657-beta-alanine-issn-position-stand.md]

Anecdote signal: `not separately upgraded here`. Captured carnosine threads discuss beta-alanine as a cheaper way to raise carnosine, but the direct cognition anecdote corpus for beta-alanine is thin and often preworkout/tingling/exercise-confounded. Do not borrow the stronger sports supplement visibility as cognition evidence.

## Signal Split

| Layer | Signal | Readout |
|---|---|---|
| Older-adult MoCA RCT | `low/subgroup` | Overall null; MoCA <=26 subgroup improved |
| Combat-soldier trial | `negative cognition counterweight` | Tactical performance improved, cognition did not |
| Soldier resiliency review | `hypothesis/state-bound` | Cognitive evidence inconclusive and stressor-dependent |
| ISSN position stand | `ergogenic/safety support` | Muscle carnosine and high-intensity exercise support; paraesthesia main side effect |
| Anecdote | `weak/confounded` | Mostly preworkout, stamina, and carnosine-loading discussion |

## Product And Protocol Gates

- Separate sustained-release beta-alanine from immediate-release powders, multi-ingredient preworkouts, carnosine capsules, anserine/carnosine formulas, and chicken extract.
- Record total daily dose, dose splitting, duration, paraesthesia, exercise training, sleep, caffeine/preworkout co-ingredients, baseline MoCA/cognition, and task stressor.
- Treat exercise, tactical performance, muscular fatigue, and cognitive endpoints separately.
- Do not infer central carnosine target engagement unless brain, CSF, or validated CNS proxy data are present.

## Practical Interpretation

[[beta-alanine]] deserves standalone tracking because it is common, safe enough at sports doses to be widely self-experimented with, and has a small older-adult cognition signal plus tactical nulls. It should remain a `state-challenge / carnosine-precursor / fatigue-resilience` candidate, not a broad brain-fog treatment or healthy-adult cognitive enhancer.
