# Mevidalen

Mevidalen, also known as LY3154207, is a dopamine D1 receptor positive allosteric modulator in the dopamine-state precision-pharmacology lane. It is a disease-context comparator, not a healthy-adult nootropic.

Medicinal cognition signal: `low / cognition-negative so far`. In the PRESENCE phase 2 Lewy body dementia trial, 344 participants with Parkinson disease dementia or dementia with Lewy bodies received mevidalen 10, 30, or 75 mg daily for 12 weeks. The primary continuity-of-attention and ADAS-Cog13 cognition endpoints were negative. Motor symptoms and clinical global impression improved at higher doses, which is useful for disease-state interpretation but should not be converted into a cognition claim.^[raw/articles/pubmed-34859493-mevidalen-lbd-phase-2.md]

Pipeline signal: `active but unproven`. PubChem identifies mevidalen as a human dopamine D1 receptor positive allosteric modulator with maximum phase II status. A 26-week Alzheimer's disease phase 2 registry remains active-not-recruiting in the current raw corpus, with cognition, daily activity, sleep, irritability, and anxiety endpoints.^[raw/articles/pubchem-mevidalen.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct06538116-mevidalen-ad.md]

## Safety And Protocol Caveats

- PRESENCE showed blood-pressure increases and more cardiovascular serious adverse events at 75 mg.
- Dopaminergic state change can affect motor function, arousal, sleep, blood pressure, and psychiatric state without improving cognition.
- Treat any future positive global-impression or motor readout as disease-state support unless a prespecified cognitive endpoint separates from placebo.
