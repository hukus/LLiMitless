# IDO / TDO Kynurenine Modulation

IDO1, IDO2, and TDO are upstream tryptophan-catabolism targets that can shift flux into the [[kynurenine]] pathway. This page exists to keep immune-metabolic target claims separate from [[5-htp-tryptophan]] supplement claims and from downstream metabolite pages such as [[kynurenic-acid]] and [[quinolinic-acid]].

Medicinal signal: `low / target-review only for cognition`. A 2024 kynurenine-target review names IDO1, IDO2, TDO, and KMO as promising inflammatory-disorder targets, while emphasizing that inhibition or activation may be beneficial depending on disease context. A 2026 gut-brain-axis review adds the microbiome and indole branches, but still frames interventions such as probiotics, fecal microbiota transplantation, metabolite administration, and enzyme inhibitors as translational strategies rather than cognition-proven treatments. That context-sensitivity is the key cognition lesson: upstream pathway suppression is not automatically good, and activation is not automatically bad.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39120289-kynurenines-target-inflammatory-disorders-review.md]^[raw/articles/springer-2026-tryptophan-metabolism-gut-brain-signaling-review.md]

The dementia and aging literature supports pathway relevance, not an IDO/TDO cognition intervention. The 2025 dementia meta-analysis found compartment-specific metabolite shifts with mostly inconclusive cognition correlations, while the normal-aging review showed age-related drift in tryptophan, kynurenine, and KYN/TRP.^[raw/articles/springer-2025-kynurenine-pathway-dysregulation-cognitive-impairment-dementia.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-38056721-kynurenine-pathway-normal-age-review.md]

## Signal Judgment

This is a routing page for future IDO/TDO inhibitors, activators, immunometabolic drugs, gut-microbiome tryptophan-metabolism papers, and biomarker enrichment logic. It should remain low-confidence until a named compound has controlled human cognition endpoints or a major safety/regulatory signal.
