# D-Serine

D-serine is an NMDA receptor co-agonist and mechanistic probe in the glutamatergic cognition lane. It should be kept separate from [[sarcosine]], [[sodium-benzoate]], [[luvadaxistat]], and [[iclepertin]] because its direct clinical cognition signal remains weak.

## Bottom Line

Medicinal signal: `low / mostly null for broad cognition`. The key captured multicenter add-on RCT in schizophrenia tested d-serine 2 g/day for 16 weeks. Both d-serine and placebo groups improved, but there were no significant between-group differences in negative symptoms or MATRICS cognition.^[raw/articles/pubmed-22795211-d-serine-schizophrenia-rct.md]

Anecdotal signal: `not captured`. No structured self-experimenter or patient-experience packet for d-serine cognition is present in the current wiki corpus.

Practical route: track d-serine as a mechanistic comparator and possible biomarker-enriched learning/remediation probe, not as a broad cognitive enhancer.

## Human Evidence

The multicenter randomized controlled trial used d-serine 2 g/day as add-on therapy in schizophrenia. The null MATRICS cognition result is the main direct cognition anchor in the current source set. That does not prove the NMDA co-agonist hypothesis is biologically irrelevant, but it does block broad enhancement claims.^[raw/articles/pubmed-22795211-d-serine-schizophrenia-rct.md]

## Signal Judgment

D-serine belongs in [[nmda-coagonist-glyt1-daao-cognition-2026-scan]] as a negative/weak comparator. The most plausible future route is phenotype- or biomarker-enriched protocol design, not consumer nootropic use.
