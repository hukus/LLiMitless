# Ginkgo

Ginkgo here means the standardized leaf-extract branch, especially `EGb 761`, not generic supplement branding. It is one of the clearest examples in the wiki of why product identity matters: the signal is strongest when the extract is fixed and the phenotype is impaired rather than healthy.

## Key Facts

- The 2016 subjective-memory-impairment pilot found that `EGb 761` improved task-switch cost and trended toward better response inhibition, but several other cognitive and imaging outcomes were mixed. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-27147264-ginkgo-egb761-smi-rct.md]
- A 2025 patient-subgroup meta-analysis in mild dementia pooled four randomized placebo-controlled trials and found benefits for cognition, global assessment, activities of daily living, and quality of life at 240 mg/day, with similar adverse-event frequency. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-39895346-ginkgo-egb761-mild-dementia-meta-analysis.md]
- A 2025 overview of systematic reviews found that most neurocognitive reviews favored `EGb 761` for cognition and behavioral symptoms, but the underlying review quality was poor and overlap across reviews was very high. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-40121884-ginkgo-egb761-overview-systematic-reviews.md]
- A 2025 FAERS analysis identified many real-world reports and a short median onset time, so the extract should not be treated as trivially low risk just because it is familiar. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-39476880-ginkgo-faers-safety.md]
- A 2026 pooled-data meta-analysis in dementia after imaging-confirmed cerebral infarction found significant benefits for cognition, activities of daily living, and global clinical impression at 240 mg/day for 22-24 weeks, with adverse-event rates similar to placebo. The trial set was homogeneous and sponsor-linked, so the signal is useful but not independent. ^[raw/articles/pmc-13023401-ginkgo-egb761-cerebral-infarction-meta-analysis.md]

## Interpretation

The current read is phenotype-specific rather than hype-friendly:

- `EGb 761` looks most defensible in mild dementia and related impairment phenotypes.
- The healthy-adult enhancement case is much weaker.
- Product standardization matters, but it does not remove the need for safety monitoring or review-quality skepticism.
