# Soy Isoflavones

Soy isoflavones are phytoestrogenic polyphenols from soy and related legumes, especially genistein, daidzein, glycitein, and daidzein-derived equol. This entity covers soy/isoflavone cognition claims, not ordinary dietary protein quality, soy lecithin, red-clover isoflavones, isolated genistein from non-soy botanicals, hormone therapy, or fermented-soy products as interchangeable exposures.

Medicinal cognition signal: `low-moderate for memory, contested and phenotype/product-sensitive`. The strongest current synthesis is a 2026 systematic review/meta-analysis of 37 studies, with 16 RCTs meta-analyzed. It found a modest positive composite-cognition effect and a memory effect, while executive function, psychomotor speed, and processing speed were not significant. That supports tracking the branch, but the effect is small and domain-specific.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41483829-isoflavone-cognition-meta-analysis.md]

The most recent trial signal is fermented-soy specific. A 2025 RCT in adults aged 65+ tested 12 weeks of non-probiotic fermented soy powder and found a small memory improvement versus placebo, with stronger post hoc global-cognition and memory findings in women aged 70+. Attrition, dyspepsia, palatability issues, and subgroup dependence keep the signal preliminary.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41010462-fermented-soy-cognition-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-12472887-fermented-soy-cognition-rct-fulltext.md]

The main counterweight is the WISH trial. In 350 healthy postmenopausal women, 25 g/day soy protein with 91 mg/day aglycone-weight isoflavones for 2.5 years did not improve the primary global-cognition endpoint, although a visual-memory factor improved secondarily. A later exposure analysis found no global-cognition association with urinary/plasma isoflavonoid change and raised a post hoc general-intelligence decrement signal. WISH prevents this page from becoming a broad cognitive-enhancement recommendation.^[raw/articles/pubmed-22665144-soy-isoflavones-cognition-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-3369523-wish-soy-isoflavones-cognition-rct-fulltext.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-4226524-wish-isoflavonoid-exposure-cognition-posthoc.md]

Anecdote signal: `weak and menopause-confounded`. Community material is mostly research-sharing, menopause symptom discussion, and hormone-safety argument rather than nootropic self-experimentation. The clearest cognitive-adjacent reports mention slightly less brain fog with soy milk in perimenopause contexts, but the reports are entangled with hot flashes, mood, sleep, joint pain, HRT access, dose uncertainty, and endocrine symptoms.^[raw/articles/reddit-2024-soy-perimenopause-brain-fog-thread.md]^[raw/articles/reddit-2020-soy-isoflavones-scientificnutrition-thread.md]

Safety/product signal: `moderate product-form and endocrine-context caution`. NCCIH says soy's cognitive effects remain uncertain because studies conflict. It flags soy allergy, GI effects, concentrated isoflavone extract exposure limits in research, tyramine-rich soy products with MAO inhibitors, medication-interaction caution, and uncertainty for pregnancy/breastfeeding above food amounts. Practical identity gates are whole soy foods vs soy protein isolate, fermented vs non-fermented products, aglycone-equivalent dose, genistein/daidzein/glycitein ratio, equol-producer status, and whether the product is a food, powder, extract, or multi-ingredient menopause supplement.^[raw/articles/nccih-2025-soy-usefulness-safety.md]

## Signal Snapshot

| Layer | Signal strength | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 meta-analysis | `low-moderate/memory-leaning` | Composite cognition and memory positive; speed/executive domains not significant |
| 2025 fermented-soy RCT | `low/preliminary` | Older-adult memory signal; women 70+ subgroup stronger; product not generic soy |
| WISH long-term RCT | `null primary with secondary visual-memory hint` | Strong counterweight for broad postmenopausal global-cognition claims |
| WISH exposure post hoc | `cautionary` | No global association; possible general-intelligence decrement needs confirmation |
| Anecdote | `weak/menopause-confounded` | Brain-fog reports exist but are not isolated nootropic experiences |
| Safety / QA | `moderate` | Food and supplement forms differ; phytoestrogen, allergy, GI, tyramine/MAOI, pregnancy, and product-form gates matter |

## Identity Gates

- Separate soy foods, soy protein isolate, isolated isoflavone capsules, fermented soy powder, tempeh/natto/miso, soy milk, soy lecithin, red-clover isoflavones, and non-soy genistein sources.
- Report dose as aglycone-equivalent isoflavones when possible, plus genistein, daidzein, glycitein, and equol context.
- Do not collapse fermented-soy peptide/aglycone matrix evidence into generic soy-protein or isolated-isoflavone evidence.
- Track sex, menopause status, age, baseline diet, equol-producer status, thyroid/iodine context, breast/endometrial history, MAOI use, and soy allergy.

## Practical Ranking

Soy isoflavones deserve inclusion because the formal literature repeatedly points to a small memory-leaning signal and because fermented-soy products have new older-adult data. They should rank below cleaner or stronger candidates because WISH is a long, adequately powered primary-endpoint null, the most attractive new trial is small and product-specific, and community cognition anecdotes are weak.
