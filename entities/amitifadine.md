# Amitifadine

Amitifadine / EB-1010 / DOV 21,947 is an older serotonin-preferring triple reuptake inhibitor studied for major depressive disorder. It is useful as a mixed-signal comparator for [[ansofaxine-toludesvenlafaxine]], [[liafensine]], [[gsk372475]], and [[triple-reuptake-inhibitor-cognition-watchlist]].

## Current Read

Medicinal cognition signal: `very low / discontinued mixed antidepressant signal`. A 2012 proof-of-concept MDD study found a statistically significant MADRS improvement versus placebo and a cognition-adjacent anhedonia-factor signal that included concentration difficulty and lassitude. That is mood-state evidence, not objective cognition evidence.^[raw/articles/sciencedirect-2012-amitifadine-mdd-rct.md]

The counterweight is the TRIADE phase 2b/3a topline result. Euthymics reported that amitifadine 50 mg and 100 mg did not significantly differ from placebo on the primary MADRS endpoint in MDD patients who had failed one first-line antidepressant course.^[raw/articles/biospace-2013-amitifadine-triade-topline.md]

That makes amitifadine useful mainly as a class-discipline page: early triple-reuptake mood signal plus later failed development should keep "SNDRI" from being treated as automatically procognitive.

## Interpretation Gates

- Treat the 2012 positive proof-of-concept and 2013 failed TRIADE result together.
- Do not convert anhedonia-factor movement into objective cognition proof.
- Watch the usual monoamine-reuptake burdens: insomnia, activation, anxiety, BP/HR, appetite, nausea, serotonergic/dopaminergic stacking, and medication transitions.
- Compare against [[ansofaxine-toludesvenlafaxine]], [[liafensine]], [[gsk372475]], [[centanafadine]], and [[bupropion]] before making class claims.

Current verdict: historical mixed antidepressant TRI; no healthy cognition upgrade.
