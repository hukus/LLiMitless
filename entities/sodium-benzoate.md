# Sodium Benzoate

Sodium benzoate is the practical outlier in the NMDA co-agonist / DAAO cognition branch. It is not a broad nootropic, but it has a more reproducible human cognition signal than many glutamatergic candidates that sound more advanced mechanistically.

## Medicinal Signal

- Formal signal: `low-moderate`, phenotype-sensitive.
- A 2024 neuropsychiatric meta-analysis found a global cognition effect around SMD 0.40, with stronger signals in women, doses above 500 mg/day, early-phase AD, chronic schizophrenia, and MDD.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39315325-sodium-benzoate-neuropsychiatric-meta-analysis.md]
- A 2026 pooled analysis of three aMCI trials, n=133, found ADAS-cog improvement versus placebo at weeks 16 and 24, with a stronger female subgroup and IADL signal.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41858175-sodium-benzoate-mci-pooled-trials.md]
- Sarcosine-plus-benzoate data suggest combination logic in schizophrenia, but the branch should not be flattened into "NMDA boost works."^[raw/articles/pubmed-26691576-sarcosine-plus-benzoate-schizophrenia-rct.md]

## Interpretation

Sodium benzoate belongs in [[nmda-coagonist-glyt1-daao-cognition-2026-scan]] because it prevents the whole glutamatergic branch from being reduced to iclepertin's failure. It is still a disease-context, phenotype-sensitive candidate. The strongest current read is early AD / aMCI and women, not rested healthy adults.

## Safety And Product Caveats

- Dose, sodium burden, duration, and source identity matter.
- Food-additive familiarity should not be treated as cognition-trial safety at gram-scale or long duration.
- The branch needs independent replication and clearer responder rules before any upgrade.
