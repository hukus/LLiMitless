# Curcumin

Curcumin here means standardized curcumin supplementation and formulations that are intended to solve the bioavailability problem, not generic turmeric powder. The current cognition story is mixed but no longer flatly null: a 2025 meta-analysis found a modest pooled benefit, while the older Alzheimer disease RCT stayed negative and highlighted the formulation bottleneck.

## Key Facts

- The 2025 updated meta-analysis pooled 9 RCTs with 501 participants and found improved global cognitive function versus placebo, with the strongest signal in older and Asian cohorts and at longer durations. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-40308636-curcumin-cognitive-function-meta-analysis.md]
- The older AD RCT remained negative, which keeps curcumin from reading as a clean, universal nootropic and makes dose, formulation, and duration central to interpretation. ^[raw/articles/pubmed-23107780-curcumin-ad-rct.md]
- The practical question is therefore not whether curcumin is "traditional" but whether a given formulation actually achieves enough exposure to test cognition credibly.

## Interpretation

Curcumin now belongs in the "mixed but worth watching" bucket rather than the "discarded" bucket.

- It has a real meta-analytic signal.
- It still lacks a consistent, large, replication-grade human effect.
- It remains highly formulation-sensitive, so product identity matters as much as ingredient naming.
