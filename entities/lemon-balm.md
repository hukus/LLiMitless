# Lemon Balm / Melissa officinalis

Lemon balm is a Melissa officinalis leaf extract / herb used in calm, anxiety, sleep, and mild cognition lore. The useful wiki interpretation is stress-state and product-specific: Zensera, older standardized extracts, dried leaf, tea/tincture products, essential oil, and Boswellia+Melissa combination tablets should not be treated as the same intervention.

## Medicinal Evidence

The older healthy-adult acute RCTs are mixed rather than uniformly positive. In the 2002 balanced-crossover study, 20 healthy young participants received 300, 600, and 900 mg standardized extract. The 600 mg dose improved Accuracy of Attention, but the same study reported time- and dose-specific reductions in Secondary Memory and Working Memory, early calmness at the lowest dose, and reduced alertness at the highest dose.^[raw/articles/pubmed-12062586-lemon-balm-acute-cognition-mood-rct.md]

The 2003 dried-leaf study also supports product-specific interpretation. After in vitro cholinergic receptor-binding screening, 20 healthy young participants received 600, 1000, and 1600 mg encapsulated dried leaf. The 1600 mg dose produced the most favorable profile, including improved memory and increased calmness, but lower doses had more unfavorable speed/performance effects. The abstract explicitly notes that different preparations from the same plant can have different properties.^[raw/articles/pubmed-12888775-lemon-balm-cholinergic-acute-cognition-rct.md]

The 2004 lab-stress study is the cleanest stress-state bridge. In 18 healthy volunteers, 600 mg ameliorated negative mood effects of a defined stressor, increasing calmness and reducing alertness. The 300 mg dose improved mathematical-processing speed without reducing accuracy. This fits the calm-focus lane better than a broad memory-enhancement claim.^[raw/articles/pubmed-15272110-lemon-balm-lab-stress-rct.md]

The newest formal anchor is product-specific Zensera. The 2026 study tested 300 mg Zensera against placebo in 106 young healthy adults with moderate subjective stress during a cognitive-overload day. It did not move the primary overall calmness outcome, but it improved hardest executive-function performance at 5 hours and helped restore transient calmness after cognitive-demand blocks. That supports "stress-buffering under load," not a general nootropic ranking upgrade.^[raw/articles/pmc-12954045-lemon-balm-zensera-cognitive-overload-rct.md]

The older-adult memory combination trial is useful but not isolating. A Boswellia serrata plus Melissa officinalis tablet improved WMS-R total and several memory subscales over 1 month in older adults, but lemon balm cannot be credited independently because the active product combined two botanicals.^[raw/articles/pubmed-29306113-boswellia-melissa-memory-rct.md]

## Safety And Product Gates

Liver-specific risk looks relatively favorable: LiverTox states that lemon balm extracts have not been linked to serum aminotransferase elevations during treatment or clinically apparent acute liver injury, and assigns likelihood score E for liver injury. The same source still notes few prospective lab-reporting studies and mild nonspecific adverse effects such as headache, dizziness, and bloating.^[raw/articles/ncbi-bookshelf-2024-livertox-lemon-balm.md]

The practical risk is functional, not only hepatic. Several controlled studies show reduced alertness or calming effects, which can be useful before sleep or during stress but counterproductive for vigilance, driving, or high-speed work. Product identity is also central: tea, essential oil, dried leaf, rosmarinic-acid-rich extracts, Zensera, and multi-herb formulas are not interchangeable.

## Practical Interpretation

[[lemon-balm]] earns standalone coverage as a `low-moderate` stress-state botanical. The strongest use-case hypothesis is anxiety/stress-mediated performance preservation under cognitive load. It should not be described as a broad memory enhancer: the evidence includes task-specific positives, primary-outcome nulls, dose/preparation heterogeneity, and alertness or memory-domain decrements.
