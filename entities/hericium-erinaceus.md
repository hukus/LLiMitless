# Hericium Erinaceus

Hericium erinaceus, commonly sold as lion's mane mushroom, is a food-derived cognition candidate with an unusually product-dependent evidence trail. Whole-mushroom powder, mycelium, erinacine-A-enriched mycelia, and commercial extracts should not be treated as interchangeable.

Medicinal signal: `low-moderate`, product-dependent. The human record includes a positive 2009 MCI trial with 3 g/day powdered Yamabushitake for 16 weeks, a positive 2020 early-AD pilot using erinacine-A-enriched mycelia for 49 weeks, a null 2022 healthy college-age adult trial using 10 g/day for four weeks around fatiguing exercise, a 2024 young-adult pilot with an acute Stroop signal, and a 2026 preprint reporting attention / working-memory and wellbeing signals with sponsor sensitivity.^[raw/articles/pubmed-18844328-hericium-erinaceus-mci-rct.md]^[raw/articles/frontiers-2020-hericium-erinacine-a-early-ad.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-36582308-hericium-erinaceus-null-cognition-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-38004235-hericium-erinaceus-young-adults-pilot.md]^[raw/articles/medrxiv-26350781-hericium-erinaceus-lions-mane-cognition-pilot.md]

## Signal Split

| Layer | Signal | Readout |
|---|---|---|
| MCI / early AD | `low-moderate` medicinal | Small positive trials, different product identities |
| Healthy young adults | `low/negative` | Null chronic study plus one small acute Stroop signal |
| Mechanism | `preclinical/mechanistic` | NGF, hericenones, erinacines, anti-inflammatory framing |
| Anecdote | `weak` captured | High market salience, but no dedicated community dataset captured in this wiki slice |

## Practical Interpretation

Hericium belongs with [[food-derived-cognition-leads-2026-scan]] and [[supplement-identity-evidence-map]], not as a generic mushroom claim. It is more promising than many food-derived leads, but the correct gate is product identity: fruiting body versus mycelium, erinacine enrichment, dose, duration, and baseline cognitive status.
