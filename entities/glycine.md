# Glycine

Glycine is the simplest proteinogenic amino acid and the only achiral one. In this wiki it is a standalone molecule, not a synonym for [[glynac]], magnesium glycinate, collagen, [[sarcosine]], [[dimethylglycine-dmg]], [[betaine-tmg]], or [[d-serine]]. It belongs near the SAMe-adjacent one-carbon branch chemically, but its most useful cognition-relevant evidence is sleep-state and attribution control rather than methyl-donor efficacy.^[raw/articles/pubchem-2026-glycine-identity.md]

Medicinal cognition signal: `low and sleep-state bound`. The best healthy-adult anchor is a small sleep-restriction study where 3 g glycine before bedtime reduced next-day fatigue and improved psychomotor-vigilance reaction time after partial sleep restriction, while simple reaction, memory recognition, and number-addition outcomes were not significant. This is a fatigue/vigilance rescue clue, not a broad memory or healthy-enhancement result.^[raw/articles/pubmed-22529837-glycine-sleep-restriction-performance.md]

The direct healthy-control cognition counterweight is stronger than the nootropic claim: acute high-dose glycine at 0.8 g/kg did not significantly improve working memory, declarative memory, attention, or perceptual processing in a randomized repeated-measures study. This keeps glycine below [[sarcosine]], [[d-serine]], and GlyT1/DAAO route claims for direct NMDA-coagonist cognition until better healthy-adult trials appear.^[raw/articles/scu-2008-high-dose-glycine-healthy-cognition-null.md]

The broad 2024 human-adult systematic review found that glycine administration has been studied across healthy and diseased adults, with the nervous-system category looking most relevant. Longer-term glycine improved sleep in healthy populations, but the sleep studies were small and high risk of bias; psychiatric-population findings, especially schizophrenia, should not be translated into healthy-adult nootropic confidence.^[raw/articles/geroscience-2024-glycine-human-adults-systematic-review.md]

Anecdote signal: `moderate but bidirectional`. Reddit threads include strong sleep, calm, mood, motivation, and next-day energy reports, but also morning brain fog, lethargy, reduced stimulant benefit, intrusive thoughts, elevated mood, tachycardia/BP symptoms, and dissociation-like fog. The anecdote layer supports a responder/safety watch, not a cognition upgrade.^[raw/articles/reddit-2020-2026-glycine-sleep-brainfog-threads.md]

Safety signal: `low-moderate supplement caution`. VKM judged supplemental doses up to 650 mg/day unlikely to cause adverse health effects in adults, but common sleep-study doses around 3 g and high-dose cognition studies are outside that low-dose food-supplement risk characterization. Practical interpretation should track dose, timing, next-day sedation, paradoxical activation, renal/hepatic context, psychiatric history, and confusion with magnesium glycinate or [[glynac]].^[raw/articles/vkm-2016-glycine-food-supplement-risk-assessment.md]

## Signal Split

| Layer | Signal | Readout |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep-restriction healthy volunteers | `low/state-support` | 3 g before bed reduced fatigue and improved psychomotor-vigilance reaction time; memory and number-addition outcomes were not significant |
| Acute high-dose healthy-control cognition | `negative/null` | 0.8 g/kg did not improve working memory, declarative memory, attention, or perceptual processing |
| Human-adult systematic review | `low/sleep and healthspan hypothesis` | Longer-term healthy sleep findings were small/high-risk; disease-state nervous-system findings do not prove healthy enhancement |
| Anecdote | `moderate bidirectional` | Sleep and next-day energy positives coexist with brain fog, lethargy, activation, intrusive thoughts, and magnesium-glycinate confounding |
| Safety | `low-moderate` | Low supplemental doses were judged unlikely to cause adverse effects, but common sleep/nootropic doses exceed that assessed range |

## Practical Interpretation

Glycine is worth tracking because it is cheap, common, mechanistically plausible, and often used for sleep or brain-fog self-experimentation. It should not enter the top cognition tier. The strongest practical read is `sleep-mediated state support with bidirectional next-day effects`, plus an attribution-control role for [[glynac]], magnesium glycinate, collagen, [[sarcosine]], [[dimethylglycine-dmg]], and [[betaine-tmg]] discussions.

Any future confidence upgrade needs a randomized healthy-adult trial that tests ordinary bedtime doses, prespecifies vigilance and memory domains, measures sleep architecture or actigraphy, tracks next-day sedation/activation, and separates glycine from magnesium, NAC, collagen, and methyl-donor stacks.
