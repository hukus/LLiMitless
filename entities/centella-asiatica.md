# Centella asiatica / Gotu Kola

Centella asiatica, commonly called gotu kola or Asian pennywort, is a traditional-medicine herb with memory, calming, vascular, wound-healing, and longevity lore. The active-marker story usually centers on triterpenoid glycosides and aglycones such as asiaticoside, madecassoside, asiatic acid, and madecassic acid. It should not be collapsed into [[bacopa]] even though both can be called "Brahmi" in some traditions.^[raw/articles/ncbi-bookshelf-2024-livertox-centella-asiatica.md]

## Bottom Line

Medicinal signal: `low/product-specific and mood-leaning`. A small 2008 healthy-elderly RCT reported high-dose working-memory and mood signals, and a post-stroke vascular-cognitive-impairment study found global MoCA improvement comparable to folic acid with better delayed recall. The 2017 systematic review/meta-analysis is the main counterweight: five Centella-alone RCTs and six Centella-containing-product RCTs did not show significant cognitive-domain advantages over placebo, though acute alertness and anger improved. The 2026 ADDF read stays modest for the same reason.^[raw/articles/sciencedirect-2008-centella-healthy-elderly-cognition-mood-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-4908235-gotu-kola-poststroke-vci.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-5587720-centella-cognition-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/alzdiscovery-2026-centella-asiatica-cognitive-vitality.md]

Anecdotal signal: `weak-moderate`, mostly product-identity, mild calm/sleep, topical, triterpenoid, and liver-safety discussion rather than repeated objective cognition reports.^[raw/articles/reddit-2020-gotu-kola-experiences-thread.md]

Practical route: track [[centella-asiatica]] as a low-confidence standardized botanical, below better-supported product-specific botanicals such as [[polygala-bt11]], [[lemon-balm]], and [[nigella-sativa]].

## Human Evidence

The most direct healthy-person cognition source is the 2008 double-blind placebo-controlled study in 28 healthy elderly volunteers. Participants received 250, 500, or 750 mg/day Centella extract for 2 months. The abstract reports high-dose working-memory improvement, increased N100 event-related-potential amplitude, and self-rated mood improvement. This is enough to keep Centella visible, but the sample is too small and too old for robust enhancer confidence.^[raw/articles/sciencedirect-2008-centella-healthy-elderly-cognition-mood-rct.md]

The 2016 post-stroke VCI study tested 750 or 1000 mg/day gotu kola extract against folic acid 3 mg/day for 6 weeks in 48 completers. All groups improved on MoCA-Ina; gotu kola was not superior to folic acid for global cognition, although delayed memory recall improved more. This is a recovery/vascular-cognitive context, not healthy enhancement.^[raw/articles/pmc-4908235-gotu-kola-poststroke-vci.md]

The 2017 meta-analysis is the ranking brake. It found no significant cognitive-function-domain differences for Centella versus placebo, while acute mood-related outcomes favored alertness and anger. Its limitations list is exactly the wiki's caution list: dose regimen, plant preparation, standardization, and product variation.^[raw/articles/pmc-5587720-centella-cognition-meta-analysis.md]

## Product Identity And Mechanism

Centella is not a single purified molecule in most use. LiverTox lists more than 100 constituents and highlights triterpenoid glycosides/aglycones. The ECa 233 phase-I PK study reinforces this: madecassoside and asiaticoside parent compounds were rarely absorbed, instead biotransforming into madecassic acid and asiatic acid. That means claims should name the extract, marker profile, dose, and route whenever possible.^[raw/articles/ncbi-bookshelf-2024-livertox-centella-asiatica.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-33767223-centella-eca233-pk-healthy-volunteers.md]

Preclinical mouse work supports a plausible aging/AD-metabolome lane, including aged Tg2576 and wild-type mice treated with Centella water extract. This is useful for mechanism hypotheses, not for upgrading human confidence.^[raw/articles/sage-2024-centella-aged-tg2576-metabolome.md]

## Safety

LiverTox is the main safety guardrail. It describes Centella as generally safe and well tolerated in small trials, with mild nonspecific adverse effects such as headache, dizziness, bloating, diarrhea, and nausea. But it also assigns Centella a likelihood score C for probable rare clinically apparent liver injury, including jaundiced hepatocellular cases with 3-8 week latency and a rechallenge-positive case. Rechallenge should be avoided after suspected liver injury.^[raw/articles/ncbi-bookshelf-2024-livertox-centella-asiatica.md]

This makes Centella different from a benign culinary-herb assumption. Users with liver disease, abnormal liver enzymes, weight-loss product stacks, alcohol-heavy use, or multi-herb formulas need extra caution before interpreting subjective calm or memory effects.

## Signal Judgment

Centella belongs in the molecule graph because it has human cognition trials, a meta-analysis, a current ADDF review, exposure/PK work, mechanistic preclinical support, and recognizable community use. It does not belong in the top tier: the controlled evidence is small, mixed, and product-dependent, and the strongest formal synthesis says cognitive-domain benefit is not significant versus placebo.
