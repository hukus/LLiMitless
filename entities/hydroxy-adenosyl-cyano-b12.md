# Hydroxy / Adenosyl / Cyano-B12 Forms

Hydroxycobalamin, adenosylcobalamin, and cyanocobalamin are vitamin B12 forms that often appear beside [[methylcobalamin-b12]], [[l-methylfolate-5-mthf]], [[folinic-acid-leucovorin]], [[s-adenosyl-l-methionine]], [[betaine-tmg]], and the [[one-carbon-methyl-donor-cognition-watchlist]]. This page exists as the non-methyl-B12 form boundary: it tracks hydroxo-B12, adenosyl-B12 / 5-deoxyadenosylcobalamin, and cyano-B12 claims without letting them automatically inherit methylcobalamin or SAMe evidence.

Official nutrient signal: `status correction, not nootropic proof`. ODS lists cyanocobalamin, adenosylcobalamin, methylcobalamin, and hydroxycobalamin as B12 forms found in supplements and states that available evidence does not show important absorption or bioavailability differences among the forms. B12 deficiency can cause neurological and cognitive symptoms, but B12-form choice is not itself a healthy-adult cognition endpoint.^[raw/articles/ods-2026-vitamin-b12-form-comparison.md]

Form-comparison signal: `contested and purpose-dependent`. One review argues against the broad marketing claim that coenzyme forms are globally superior to cyanocobalamin. A separate review argues that form-specific utilization may matter in some contexts. Together they support a cautious read: B12 form can be worth tracking for route, tolerability, deficiency treatment logistics, and individual response, but "methyl", "hydroxy", "adenosyl", "cyano", "active", or "natural" labels do not establish cognitive efficacy.^[raw/articles/pmc-4692085-coenzyme-b12-forms-not-superior.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-28223907-b12-forms-bioavailability-review.md]

Cognition counterweight: `negative outside deficiency`. A 2021 systematic review and meta-analysis of 16 RCTs in adults without overt B12 deficiency or advanced neurological disease found no evidence that B12 alone or B12/B-complex supplementation improved cognitive subdomains, depressive symptoms, or robust fatigue outcomes. That counterweight applies to form-specific claims unless a trial separates forms, baseline status, route, and objective cognition.^[raw/articles/pubmed-33809274-b12-cognition-fatigue-meta.md]

Anecdote signal: `weak-moderate, form-sensitive, and bidirectional`. MTHFR and B12-deficiency forums often frame hydroxycobalamin or adenosylcobalamin as gentler alternatives when methylcobalamin, methylfolate, SAMe, TMG, or methylated B-complexes are blamed for anxiety, insomnia, rumination, or brain fog. The same thread cluster also contains null reports and continued adverse reports after form switching. The useful signal is triage, not efficacy upgrading.^[raw/articles/reddit-2024-2026-hydroxy-adenosyl-cyano-b12-brainfog-threads.md]

## Practical Interpretation

Use this page when a brain-fog report names hydroxo-B12, adeno-B12, cyano-B12, or "non-methyl B12". The current read is `low-confidence status and tolerability triage`. Confirm B12, methylmalonic acid, homocysteine, folate, kidney function, CBC/iron, metformin or acid-suppression exposure, vegan diet, gastrointestinal disease or surgery, pernicious anemia, pregnancy context, route, dose, and co-supplement changes before interpreting subjective cognitive change.

Any confidence upgrade needs a form-separated trial with objective cognition endpoints, baseline B12/MMA/homocysteine/folate status, route and dose transparency, deficiency-cause accounting, and explicit separation from [[methylcobalamin-b12]], folic acid, [[l-methylfolate-5-mthf]], [[folinic-acid-leucovorin]], B6/P5P, riboflavin, SAMe, TMG, choline, iron, thyroid, sleep, and long-COVID/autonomic confounds.
