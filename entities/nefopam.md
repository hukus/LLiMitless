# Nefopam

Nefopam is a non-opioid central analgesic with serotonin, norepinephrine, and dopamine reuptake-inhibition relevance. It is included here as a risk-first SNDRI-adjacent comparator, not as a cognition treatment.

## Current Read

Medicinal cognition signal: `risk-first / pain-state only`. Nefopam's clinical identity is analgesia. Pain relief can secondarily improve concentration or brain fog in pain-state users, but the captured formal evidence is analgesic, not cognitive.^[raw/articles/pubmed-1980-nefopam-review.md]

Safety signal: `high relevance`. An open-access safety case discussion reports serious neuropsychiatric and cardiovascular side effects, including confusion, hallucinations, convulsions, and delirium, and says nefopam has a prominent anticholinergic effect.^[raw/articles/pmc-4273008-nefopam-status-epilepticus.md]

Abuse/salience signal: an indexed case-series abstract reports three patients who abused nefopam and describes it as inhibiting serotonin, norepinephrine, and dopamine reuptake. That makes it relevant to the [[triple-reuptake-inhibitor-cognition-watchlist]], but mostly as a cautionary comparator.^[raw/articles/sage-2002-nefopam-abuse.md]

## Interpretation Gates

- Do not rank nefopam as a nootropic because it touches SERT/NET/DAT.
- Separate pain relief from direct cognitive improvement.
- Watch seizure risk, confusion/delirium, hallucinations, anticholinergic burden, tachycardia/cardiovascular effects, abuse, serotonergic stacking, and medication interactions.
