# Tinospora Cordifolia

Tinospora cordifolia is an Ayurvedic herb also known as Guduchi or Giloy. It is often framed as a rasayana, immune-support, anti-inflammatory, or rejuvenative herb, but its cognition evidence is far weaker than its traditional and immune-market visibility.

## Bottom Line

Medicinal cognition signal: `preclinical/mechanism-only`. A 2025 metabolomics and network-pharmacology paper mapped Tinospora metabolites to Alzheimer's-relevant target hypotheses including BACE1, MAO-B, neurotransmitter synapse pathways, mTOR, MAPK, and PI3K-Akt signaling, but this is not a human cognition trial.^[raw/articles/nature-2025-tinospora-ad-network-metabolomics.md]

Safety signal: `risk-first`. LiverTox and a multicenter Indian case series document clinically apparent liver injury temporally associated with Giloy/Tinospora use, including autoimmune-like features and acute hepatitis presentations.^[raw/articles/ncbi-bookshelf-2025-livertox-tinospora.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-9134809-giloy-induced-liver-injury-india.md]

## Interpretation

Tinospora should not be ranked as a healthy-adult nootropic. If it belongs in a brain-fog map, it belongs as an immune/inflammation hypothesis with strong safety gates: baseline liver disease, autoimmune history, concurrent hepatotoxic products, product identity, duration, dose, and liver-function monitoring.

## Signal Judgment

Tinospora is a safety-first ethnomedicine node. The current research packet supports mechanism exploration and liver-risk monitoring, not cognitive enhancement. Any future human cognition trial would need verified species, extract chemistry, liver-safety monitoring, autoimmune screening, and a clear phenotype.
