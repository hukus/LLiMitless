# Grape Extract

Standardized grape extract is one of the cleaner recent healthy-older-adult botanical cognition signals in the wiki. The key is product identity: this is not evidence that any grape-derived supplement or food matrix is interchangeable with the tested extract.

## Medicinal Signal

- Formal signal: `low-moderate`; strongest among newer standardized botanical candidates, still replication-limited.
- A randomized placebo-controlled trial of 250 mg/day standardized grape extract in healthy older adults reported improvements across immediate and delayed memory, visuospatial ability, language, attention, and global cognitive batteries.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39335927-grape-extract-cognition-rct.md]
- The broader 2025 natural-extract NMA and 2026 plant-active-substance NMA keep grape/raisin-style polyphenol products visible, but rankings should not be merged across products without marker and dose normalization. Use [[grape-food-products]] for raisins, Concord grape juice, whole grapes, and similar food matrices.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40213691-natural-extracts-healthy-adults-network-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-12864429-plant-active-substances-healthy-older-adults-nma.md]

## Current Read

Grape extract belongs in [[standardized-herbal-extracts-for-cognition]] as a product-specific lead. It should not be collapsed into generic polyphenols, resveratrol, grape seed, grape-blueberry blends, or [[grape-food-products]] until product identity is normalized.
