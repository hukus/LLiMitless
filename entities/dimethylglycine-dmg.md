# Dimethylglycine / DMG

N,N-dimethylglycine (DMG) is a glycine-family molecule with two N-methyl groups, chemically adjacent to [[betaine-tmg]] / trimethylglycine and [[sarcosine]] / N-methylglycine. In supplement use it is marketed for energy, immune function, autism, seizures, athletic performance, chronic fatigue, and methylation support, but this wiki treats it as a low-confidence one-carbon / methylation-adjacent watch item rather than a cognition enhancer.^[raw/articles/pubchem-2026-dimethylglycine-identity.md]^[raw/articles/mskcc-2026-dimethylglycine-herb-monograph.md]

The cognition read is `low and mostly negative`. The most direct fatigue/cognition-adjacent controlled trial is the progressive-MS pilot: one year of DMG 125 mg/day did not improve disability, fatigue, cognition, or gait versus placebo. Pediatric autism and mitochondrial oxygen-consumption trials are also small and do not establish efficacy. The best current role for DMG is attribution control inside [[one-carbon-methyl-donor-cognition-watchlist]], not ranking beside stronger candidates.^[raw/articles/pmc-8142643-dmg-progressive-ms-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-11305684-dmg-autism-rct.md]^[raw/articles/emconsulte-2003-dmg-cox-deficiency-vo2-rct.md]

## Practical Interpretation

- Best fit: a low-priority marker in methylation, NR/NMN, homocysteine, or energy-stack attribution.
- Weak fit: healthy-adult memory, attention, executive function, or brain-fog self-treatment claims.
- Main confound: DMG is often implied rather than tested, and it travels with [[betaine-tmg]], methylfolate, B12 forms, glycine, [[s-adenosyl-l-methionine]], [[nadh]], NR/NMN, and mitochondrial stacks.
- Safety interpretation: use [[molecule-intake-triage-table]] and status testing logic before treating DMG as benign. Its formal trial packet is small, and consumer-product dose/form claims are not the same as clinical exposure.
