# Sembragiline

Sembragiline is a potent, selective, long-acting reversible MAO-B inhibitor developed for Alzheimer's disease. It is useful because it cleanly separates target engagement from clinical cognition efficacy.

## Current Read

Medicinal cognition signal: `negative/target-engaged`. PET work showed strong brain MAO-B inhibition after oral sembragiline, with estimated maximum inhibition around 80% to 90%, EC50 around 1 to 2 ng/mL, and near-maximal inhibition at 1 mg daily.^[raw/articles/pubmed-27633250-sembragiline-maob-pet-target-engagement.md]

The phase 2 MAyflOwer RoAD trial then tested 1 mg and 5 mg sembragiline for 52 weeks in 542 moderate Alzheimer's disease patients on background acetylcholinesterase inhibitor with or without memantine. The primary ADAS-Cog11 endpoint was not met, ADCS-ADL was not significant, and the main positive signal was neuropsychiatric-symptom improvement on BEHAVE-AD-FW at Week 52.^[raw/articles/pubmed-28550255-sembragiline-mayflower-road-ad-phase2.md]

## Interpretation

Sembragiline is a cautionary anchor for [[mao-b-propargylamine-cognition-watchlist]]: even strong MAO-B target engagement did not produce a clear AD cognitive endpoint. That matters when evaluating [[kds2010-tisolagiline]] or [[rasagiline]] biomarker-positive interpretations.

## Current Route

Route sembragiline through [[negative-evidence-watchlist-for-cognition-interventions]] and [[mao-b-propargylamine-cognition-watchlist]]. Its closest class comparators are [[ladostigil]], [[rasagiline]], [[selegiline]], and [[kds2010-tisolagiline]].
