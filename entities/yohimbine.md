# Yohimbine

Yohimbine is an alpha-2 adrenergic antagonist found in yohimbe bark and sold as yohimbine HCl or yohimbe supplements. It is common in fat-loss, fasted-cardio, libido, and stimulant-adjacent stacks. This page covers yohimbine/yohimbe as a cognition-relevant adrenergic risk comparator, not rauwolscine/alpha-yohimbine, [[p-synephrine]], higenamine, hordenine, octopamine, ephedrine, [[caffeine]], or multi-stimulant fat burners.

Medicinal cognition signal: `low/risk-first`. A 2015 randomized clinical trial in 20 healthy young volunteers tested cognition, psychomotor performance, and working memory, but the study is too small and peripheral to make yohimbine a robust enhancer. The stronger formal interpretation comes from the 2000 PET study in nine healthy humans: intravenous yohimbine produced adrenergic activation, symptom increases including a panic attack in one subject, blood-pressure increases, hyperventilation, and cerebral-blood-flow changes tied to anxiety/interoception. That makes yohimbine a stress/anxiety probe more than a clean nootropic.^[raw/articles/jpri-2015-yohimbine-cognition-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-10949101-yohimbine-cbf-humans.md]

Safety and product signal: `high burden`. NCCIH says there is very little research on yohimbe as a supplement and not enough evidence for effectiveness; it links yohimbine/yohimbe products to arrhythmia, blood-pressure problems, heart attacks, seizures, tachycardia, anxiety, high blood pressure, labeling variability, and medication interactions, especially MAO inhibitors and tricyclic antidepressants. Supplement products may not behave like prescription yohimbine, and yohimbine quantity can vary widely.^[raw/articles/nccih-2025-yohimbe-usefulness-safety.md]

Anecdote signal: `moderate alertness/focus/fat-loss salience, strong adverse counterweight, weak objective cognition`. Captured threads include morning brain-fog/focus reports and fat-loss/fasted-cardio use, but also fight-or-flight intensity, anxiety, panic-like reactions, nausea, dizziness, racing heart, sleep interference, and stimulant-medication stacking. This is exactly the kind of community salience that should trigger caution, not recommendation.^[raw/articles/reddit-2025-yohimbine-morning-energy-focus-thread.md]^[raw/articles/reddit-2023-yohimbine-fat-loss-mental-adverse-thread.md]

## Identity Gates

- Separate yohimbine HCl, yohimbe bark, rauwolscine/alpha-yohimbine, and multi-stimulant products.
- Do not equate fat-loss or libido use with cognition improvement.
- Track dose, fasted state, caffeine/preworkout use, stimulant medications, antidepressants, blood pressure, heart rhythm, panic/anxiety/PTSD vulnerability, bipolar/psychosis vulnerability, sleep timing, nausea, pregnancy/breastfeeding, and age under 18.
- Treat "focus" reports as arousal-state changes unless task accuracy, working memory, learning, or executive-control outcomes are measured.

## Practical Ranking

Yohimbine deserves standalone coverage because it is common, pharmacologically strong, and safety-relevant. It should not be ranked as a practical cognitive enhancer. The current route is `adrenergic stress-probe / risk-first stimulant-adjacent comparator`, with any short-term alertness report counterweighted by anxiety, cardiovascular, interaction, and product-label risks.
