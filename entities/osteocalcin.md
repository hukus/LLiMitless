# Osteocalcin

Osteocalcin is a bone-derived osteokine / endocrine factor in the bone-brain cognition lane. It is adjacent to exerkine biology, but it should be treated as a bone-brain endocrine comparator rather than a proven nootropic molecule.

Medicinal cognition signal: `low / association-plus-preclinical`. In women with and without HIV, undercarboxylated osteocalcin was associated with some cognitive domains, but the direction was mixed: executive-function associations were positive, attention/working-memory association was negative in the full sample, and neither osteocalcin nor sclerostin predicted cognitive change over time.^[raw/articles/pubmed-36094482-undercarboxylated-osteocalcin-cognition-women-hiv.md]

Mechanistic signal: `preclinical`. A 2017 mouse study found osteocalcin necessary for the memory benefit of young plasma in older mice and sufficient to improve hippocampal-dependent memory when delivered peripherally to aged mice. The same work identified Gpr158 in hippocampal CA3 neurons as a receptor mediating osteocalcin's memory effects.^[raw/articles/pubmed-28851741-gpr158-osteocalcin-cognition.md]

Review signal: `mechanism-rich, not clinical proof`. The 2025 osteocalcin/GPR158 review frames the pathway as relevant to neuronal survival, plasticity, mood/stress biology, and neurodegenerative disease hypotheses, but it does not add a human cognition intervention result.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40337551-osteocalcin-gpr158-bone-brain-review.md]

## Safety And Translation Caveats

- Osteocalcin should not be collapsed into generic exercise, vitamin K, bone-density, or testosterone claims.
- The human cognition record is biomarker association, not supplementation or peptide treatment.
- Bone, glucose, reproductive, mood, and cognition biology may interact; that makes phenotype control important before any intervention claim is ranked.
