# Lysolecithin / Lysophosphatidylcholine

Lysolecithin, also called lysophosphatidylcholine or LPC, is a choline-containing lipid source. It belongs in the wiki as a choline-bioavailability comparator, not as a proven cognitive enhancer.

Medicinal cognition signal: `negative for broad healthy-adult cognition`. The current captured trial randomized healthy adults aged 40-74 to 480 mg LPC lysolecithin or placebo for 8 weeks. Plasma choline and LPC18:2 increased slightly, but memory and subjective mental-function outcomes did not improve versus placebo.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39896163-lysolecithin-cognition-choline.md]

Biomarker signal: `present but non-translating`. The trial is useful because it separates choline exposure from cognition: moving plasma choline/LPC markers was not enough to move the primary memory endpoint. That makes [[lysolecithin]] a cautionary comparator for [[alpha-gpc]], [[citicoline]], phosphatidylcholine, and generic choline claims.

Safety signal: `short-term tolerability only`. The raw note reports no adverse-event imbalance, inflammatory LPC-fraction change, or reactive-oxygen-metabolite signal over 8 weeks. This supports limited short-term tolerability in that small sample, not long-term safety or broad formulation equivalence.
