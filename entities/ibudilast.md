# Ibudilast

Ibudilast is a small-molecule phosphodiesterase inhibitor and glial-modulating drug used historically in Japan for asthma and studied for neuroinflammatory and neurodegenerative conditions. In this wiki it is a [[neuroinflammation-and-cognition]] and [[neuroimmune-brain-fog-chemical-guide]] candidate, adjacent to [[pentoxifylline]] and [[propentofylline]].

## Bottom Line

Brain-fog clearance signal: `protocol-only plus anecdotes`. RECLAIM has completed ibudilast and pentoxifylline long-COVID drug arms against placebo, with brain fog listed among the symptom targets, but official results were still being analyzed on the captured trial page. That makes ibudilast directly relevant but not yet efficacy-positive.^[raw/articles/reclaim-2026-ibudilast-pentoxifylline-long-covid.md]

Neuroimmune signal: `low-moderate translational`. Reviews describe ibudilast as a nonselective PDE inhibitor with glial attenuation, neuroinflammation suppression, and TLR4-related hypotheses. The strongest human seriousness marker is a progressive-MS trial in which ibudilast slowed brain atrophy relative to placebo, while GI and headache side effects were common enough to matter.^[raw/articles/pubmed-19929708-ibudilast-review.md]^[raw/articles/nih-2018-ibudilast-progressive-ms-brain-atrophy.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-9737612-ibudilast-neurodegenerative-review.md]

Practical route: watch for RECLAIM results before upgrading. Ibudilast has a cleaner glial/PDE rationale than many community candidates, but current brain-fog evidence is not yet a controlled outcome result.

## Formal Evidence

The 2009 review frames ibudilast as a nonselective PDE inhibitor with microglial attenuation and potential relevance to multiple sclerosis, neuropathic pain, and opioid-use biology. The later neurodegenerative review adds neuroinflammation, mitochondrial, autophagy, and oxidative-stress mechanisms, but also makes clear that clinical translation remains incomplete.^[raw/articles/pubmed-19929708-ibudilast-review.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-9737612-ibudilast-neurodegenerative-review.md]

The progressive-MS signal is not a brain-fog result, but it matters for category seriousness: in a trial of more than 250 participants, ibudilast slowed brain shrinkage versus placebo. That supports CNS disease-modification plausibility more than subjective clarity claims.^[raw/articles/nih-2018-ibudilast-progressive-ms-brain-atrophy.md]

An Alzheimer repurposing analysis identified ibudilast as a candidate through PDE inhibition and TLR4 antagonism, but it is a modeling/repurposing argument rather than a cognitive-efficacy trial.^[raw/articles/pubmed-35411386-ibudilast-alzheimers-repurposing.md]

## Long-COVID Status

RECLAIM is the key live source for brain-fog relevance. The trial page lists long-COVID symptoms including fatigue, tachycardia, brain fog, insomnia or sleep disturbance, GI issues, anxiety, and depression. It says ibudilast and pentoxifylline arms are completed, recruitment is closed, and findings are still being analyzed.^[raw/articles/reclaim-2026-ibudilast-pentoxifylline-long-covid.md]

## Safety And Status

Ibudilast is not a routine U.S. consumer nootropic. The captured clinical and anecdotal material repeatedly points to GI symptoms, headache, sleep disruption, and neuropsychiatric tolerability as practical gates. Sourcing and medical supervision matter because dose, product identity, and drug-interaction context are not handled by supplement-like assumptions.

## Signal Judgment

Ibudilast is a high-priority watch item in the neuroimmune brain-fog category because it combines CNS clinical exposure, plausible glial/PDE mechanisms, and a completed long-COVID trial arm. It should not yet be ranked as a proven brain-fog clearer; the controlled long-COVID data are still pending in the current source set.
