# Homocysteine / S-Adenosylhomocysteine (SAH)

Homocysteine is an endogenous sulfur amino-acid metabolite. S-adenosylhomocysteine / SAH is the downstream SAMe-adjacent metabolite formed after methyl transfer. This page is not a supplement recommendation; it is the biomarker and pathway gate for [[s-adenosyl-l-methionine]], [[l-methionine]], [[betaine-tmg]], [[folate-b-vitamins]], [[l-methylfolate-5-mthf]], [[methylcobalamin-b12]], [[hydroxy-adenosyl-cyano-b12]], [[vitamin-b6-p5p]], [[riboflavin-b2]], [[niacin-nicotinamide-b3]], [[choline-donor-cognition-signal-map]], and [[creatine]] claims.^[raw/articles/pubchem-2026-homocysteine-identity.md]^[raw/articles/pubchem-2026-s-adenosylhomocysteine-identity.md]

Medicinal cognition signal: `low, biomarker-gated, and contested`. Elevated homocysteine can mark a one-carbon or nutrient-status phenotype where intervention may matter, but homocysteine lowering is not itself cognitive improvement. VITACOG is the strongest positive anchor: folic acid, B12, and B6 slowed brain atrophy in MCI, especially when baseline homocysteine was high, and cognitive-outcome reporting found benefits in elevated-homocysteine subgroups. The broad counterweight is a DARE-assessed RCT meta-analysis concluding that B12, B6, or folic-acid homocysteine-lowering treatment did not reliably improve cognition across older adults with or without cognitive impairment.^[raw/articles/pmc-2935890-vitacog-b-vitamins-mci-brain-atrophy.md]^[raw/articles/oxford-2012-vitacog-b-vitamins-mci-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/ncbi-bookshelf-2012-homocysteine-lowering-b-vitamins-cognition-meta.md]

Current update: a 2025 methyl-nutrient network meta-analysis in older adults with MCI reports folate plus B12 as the best-ranked methyl-nutrient option for slowing cognitive decline while reducing serum homocysteine and increasing folate/B12 markers. That supports the status-correction lane, not a general healthy-adult nootropic claim.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40249981-methyl-nutrients-mci-nma.md]

SAMe / methionine boundary: acute methionine loading in healthy humans can move plasma methionine, SAMe, SAH, folate, and total homocysteine markers. This is why [[l-methionine]] and [[s-adenosyl-l-methionine]] need homocysteine/SAH context before anecdotal "methylation" effects are interpreted. It does not prove that raising SAMe or perturbing methionine improves cognition.^[raw/articles/pubmed-8774264-methionine-loading-same-homocysteine.md]

TMG / betaine boundary: [[betaine-tmg]] has real homocysteine-lowering evidence in healthy adults and prescription rare-disease use for homocystinuria, but the endpoint is biochemical. Betaine/TMG should be routed as a remethylation and lab-status lever unless objective cognition endpoints are present.^[raw/articles/pubmed-23997720-betaine-homocysteine-meta.md]

Anecdote signal: `moderate class salience, low confidence`. MTHFR, COMT, methylfolate, B12, SAMe, TMG, choline, niacinamide, and "overmethylation" communities often use homocysteine as a narrative anchor for brain fog, focus, anxiety, insomnia, and mood shifts. These reports are useful for deciding which labs, forms, and stop conditions to record, but they should not upgrade efficacy without baseline and achieved homocysteine, MMA/B12/folate/B6/B2 status, kidney context, medication context, and dechallenge/rechallenge quality.^[raw/articles/reddit-2022-2026-same-tmg-methyl-donor-brainfog-threads.md]

## Signal Split

| Layer | Signal | Readout |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | `pathway chemical / biomarker` | Homocysteine and SAH are endogenous one-carbon metabolites, not nootropic actives |
| Methionine loading | `biomarker-positive` | Acute loading moves SAMe, SAH, folate, methionine, and homocysteine markers |
| VITACOG atrophy | `low-moderate, phenotype-gated` | B9+B12+B6 slowed MCI brain atrophy most clearly with high baseline homocysteine |
| VITACOG cognitive outcomes | `supportive subgroup` | High-homocysteine participants showed selected cognitive/clinical benefits |
| Broad homocysteine-lowering meta-analysis | `negative counterweight` | B-vitamin homocysteine lowering did not reliably improve cognition across unselected older adults |
| 2025 methyl-nutrient MCI NMA | `supportive but combination-bound` | Folate plus B12 ranked best for MCI cognition and one-carbon markers |
| Betaine/TMG | `biomarker-only for cognition` | Lowers homocysteine; no healthy-adult cognition endpoint captured |
| Anecdote | `moderate class salience` | Brain-fog and focus claims are visible but heavily lab/form/stack/confounder dependent |

## Interpretation Gates

- Treat homocysteine and SAH as biomarkers or pathway readouts, not cognition outcomes.
- Record B12, methylmalonic acid, folate, B6/PLP, riboflavin/B2 status, kidney function, thyroid context, alcohol, smoking, diet/protein intake, pregnancy context, and relevant medications before attributing effects.
- Keep high-homocysteine MCI evidence separate from healthy-adult enhancement.
- Separate folic acid, folinic acid, 5-MTHF, methylcobalamin, hydroxy/adenosyl/cyano-B12, P5P, riboflavin, betaine/TMG, choline, creatine, SAMe, and L-methionine.
- Treat "overmethylation" and "undermethylation" as forum models unless labs and reproducible symptom timing support the interpretation.
- Do not use homocysteine lowering to rank a compound above direct cognition-positive interventions unless behavior changes are measured.

## Practical Interpretation

Homocysteine deserves a standalone page because it is the main safety and attribution hinge for the SAMe-adjacent cluster. The current route is `status biomarker with possible MCI/high-homocysteine relevance`, not a healthy-adult cognition target. If a methyl-donor report lacks baseline homocysteine, B12/MMA, folate, B6/B2, kidney, diet, and medication context, it should remain anecdote-level.
