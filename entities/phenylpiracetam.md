# Phenylpiracetam

Phenylpiracetam, also called fonturacetam, phenotropil, actitropil, and carphedon, is a racetam derivative with stimulant-like properties and a strong community reputation. The current human evidence clusters around asthenia, fatigue, and chronic cerebral-ischemia / recovery contexts rather than clean healthy-adult enhancement.

The June 2026 read is `very-high anecdotal salience, phenotype-bound formal evidence`. Phenylpiracetam remains one of the loudest racetam-style community signals in the wiki, but the best newer human package points to asthenia / fatigue / recovery, not rested baseline cognition.^[raw/articles/troof-nootropic-ratings-2022.md]^[raw/articles/troof-nootropics-ratings-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/darktka-2017-nootropics-survey-on-r-nootropics.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40047835-fonturacetam-asthenia-meta-analysis.md]

## Overview

The wiki should treat phenylpiracetam as a state-rescue compound first and a general nootropic only very cautiously. The newest human package is mostly Russian-language and mostly phenotype-bound:

- 2023 open-label reactive-asthenia program in young adults.
- 2023 observational chronic-cerebral-ischemia follow-up.
- 2025 meta-analysis pooling 11 studies / 549 patients with asthenia.
- 2025 WADA prohibited-list entry naming `Fonturacetam [4-phenylpiracetam (carphedon)]`.

The healthy-adult enhancement bridge is still thin. The strongest practical interpretation is "fatigue or load relief in selected clinical or rescue states," not universal memory enhancement.

## Key Facts

- Common studied dose in the recent Russian clinical literature: 200 mg/day, usually split.
- Main measured outcomes: asthenia, fatigue, sleep, mood, MoCA, quality of life, and general neurologic function.
- The evidence looks more actoprotective / rescue-oriented than baseline-enhancement oriented.
- WADA sports visibility is real and current.
- The top-level [[anecdote-signal-ledger]] now treats phenylpiracetam as the clearest `very-high` structured-community heat example; that status is useful for triage and safety audits, not efficacy ranking.

## Safety And Sourcing

- The current practical safety issue is less "clean toxicity syndrome" and more tolerance, cycling, and sourcing uncertainty.
- WADA visibility makes the branch especially relevant for athletes and other regulated-performance contexts.
- The recent clinical literature is centered on fatigue or recovery phenotypes, so any "works for everyone" claim is too broad.
- June 2026 racetam/nootropic gate: phenylpiracetam reports should not update rankings unless they capture exact identity across phenylpiracetam/fonturacetam/phenotropil/actitropil/carphedon labels, source and COA or prescription/clinical status, dose and timing, acute versus repeated use, tolerance/cycling pattern, baseline fatigue/asthenia/sleep-loss/recovery state, sleep and stimulant history, caffeine/nicotine/[[bromantane]]/modafinil-class/stimulant/racetam/choline co-use, WADA or regulated-performance context, adverse events, washout and dechallenge/rechallenge, and objective work-output, endurance, attention, memory, or fatigue-scale endpoints. ## What Changed Since 2024

- The 2025 meta-analysis gave the branch a denser Russian-language human evidence package, but it still points to asthenia rather than universal enhancement.
- The 2025 WADA list now makes the sports-visibility problem explicit by naming fonturacetam directly.
- The current evidence set supports a narrower state-rescue interpretation than the nootropic folklore usually implies.
