# Pyritinol / Encephabol

Pyritinol, also called pyrithioxine / pyrithioxin / piritinol and marketed historically as Encephabol-style products, is a disulfide derivative of pyridoxine-like structures. The wiki treats it as a legacy drug/nootropic candidate, not as ordinary vitamin B6 and not as a modern supplement with a clean safety packet.^[raw/articles/pubchem-2026-pyritinol-identity.md]

## Medicinal Evidence

The healthiest direct-cognition signal is small and old. In the 1990 normal-volunteer study, 12 healthy male volunteers received 600 mg pyritinol, 1200 mg pyritinol, or placebo for 3 days in a randomized double-blind crossover design. Critical flicker fusion and choice reaction time improved significantly; memory tests and subjective drug effects did not. That makes pyritinol an old arousal/psychomotor-processing candidate, not a broad memory enhancer.^[raw/articles/pubmed-2135070-pyritinol-healthy-volunteers.md]

The dementia evidence is also old but real enough to track. A 1980 placebo-controlled trial randomized 40 moderately advanced dementia patients to pyritinol 800 mg/day or placebo for 3 months and reported greater behavioral-scale improvement with pyritinol, with laboratory tests remaining within normal limits. A 1989 double-blind crossover trial in 26 mild-to-moderate SDAT patients reported cognitive improvement and a more normalized rCBF activation pattern during pyritinol treatment.^[raw/articles/pubmed-7001490-pyritinol-encephabol-dementia-placebo-trial.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-2687355-pyritinol-sdat-trial.md]

The mechanism bridge is mostly historical physiology. In 87 patients with organic brain disorders, pyritinol-HCl 900-1000 mg/day reportedly improved previously disturbed cerebral glucose metabolism but did not clearly improve disturbed cerebral blood flow or oxygen consumption. This supports the legacy "brain metabolism" story without proving modern cognitive enhancement.^[raw/articles/pubmed-577444-pyritinol-brain-glucose-metabolism-dementia.md]

## Safety And Product Gates

The safety floor is not vitamin-like. The strongest captured adverse source is a case report in which a 23-year-old man had three episodes of acute pancreatitis after pyritinol intake, and a controlled single-dose rechallenge triggered a fourth episode. The authors considered the timing and immunologic testing consistent with an immune-mediated drug reaction.^[raw/articles/pubmed-9679051-pyritinol-pancreatitis-case.md]

That case does not quantify incidence, but it changes routing. Pyritinol should be interpreted as a legacy drug with rare serious idiosyncratic-risk potential, not as a harmless B-vitamin derivative. The old dementia trial's normal lab tests do not rule out rare immune-mediated reactions.^[raw/articles/pubmed-7001490-pyritinol-encephabol-dementia-placebo-trial.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-9679051-pyritinol-pancreatitis-case.md]

## Practical Interpretation

[[pyritinol]] earns standalone coverage because it has direct human cognition and dementia-era clinical evidence plus a real safety signal. The medicinal rating is `low-moderate` only for old, endpoint-specific arousal/processing-speed and clinical-state evidence. It should not be ranked with modern high-confidence comparators, and anecdotal sourcing enthusiasm should not upgrade the medicinal signal.
