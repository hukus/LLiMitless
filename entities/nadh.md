# NADH

NADH is reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide, also labeled beta-NADH, DPNH, coenzyme-I, nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide reduced, or ENADA in stabilized oral-product contexts. It belongs in the redox / mitochondrial cofactor lane of [[redox-mitochondrial-cofactor-cognition-watchlist]], but it should stay separate from NAD+, [[nicotinamide-riboside]], [[nicotinamide-mononucleotide]], niacin, nicotinamide, IV NAD+, direct NAD+ lozenges, and broad "NAD booster" blends.^[raw/articles/pubchem-2026-nadh-identity.md]

The current cognition read is `low/illness-state and contested`. NADH is relevant to brain-fog mapping mostly because chronic-fatigue / ME-CFS trials and CoQ10 plus NADH studies touch fatigue and perceived cognitive fatigue, while older dementia studies are small and mixed. There is no captured healthy-adult cognition RCT; the 2025 healthy-young-adult trial is an endurance / physiology proxy without cognitive endpoints.^[raw/articles/pubmed-10071523-nadh-cfs-crossover.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-20447621-nadh-cfs-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-8399248-coq10-nadh-mecfs-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-15134388-nadh-ad-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-11459000-nadh-dementia-null.md]^[raw/articles/ejmed-2025-nadh-healthy-endurance-rct.md]

## Evidence Snapshot

| Layer | Signal | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | Reduced NADH / beta-NADH / ENADA redox cofactor | Keep product form, dose, route, and stabilization explicit |
| CFS pilot | 10 mg/day stabilized oral NADH produced 8/26 favorable responses versus 2/26 placebo in a 1999 crossover trial | Positive illness-state symptom signal, not objective cognition proof |
| CFS randomized trial | 20 mg/day oral NADH was mostly not different from placebo, with anxiety and stress-test heart-rate changes | Major downgrade for broad CFS or cognition claims |
| CoQ10 plus NADH | 200 mg CoQ10 plus 20 mg NADH improved fatigue measures and mitochondrial/redox biomarkers in CFS / ME-CFS studies | Relevant to cognitive-fatigue routing, but combination attribution prevents NADH-alone claims |
| Dementia | One small AD RCT reported MDRS advantage; a dementia open-label study found no cognitive effect | Contested disease-state signal; not transportable to healthy adults |
| Healthy adults | 40 mg/day sublingual NADH improved endurance proxies in 16 healthy young men | Physiology proxy only; no cognition endpoint |

## Practical Interpretation

- Best fit: low-confidence mitochondrial/redox candidate for ME/CFS-like cognitive fatigue, post-viral fatigue, or redox-cofactor responder hypotheses.
- Not a broad enhancer: the controlled packet does not show healthy-adult memory, attention, executive-function, or processing-speed improvement.
- Attribution problem: CoQ10 plus NADH studies should route through both [[nadh]] and [[coenzyme-q10]], but they cannot be used as NADH-alone evidence.
- NAD naming problem: keep NADH separate from [[nicotinamide-riboside]], [[nicotinamide-mononucleotide]], NAD+ IVs, and generic NAD+ booster stacks.

## Safety And Use Boundaries

- Treat insomnia, activation, anxiety, tachycardia-like symptoms, crash/fatigue, and paradoxical fog as stop-rule-relevant signals in self-report contexts.
- Do not borrow NAD+ precursor logic from [[nicotinamide-riboside]] or [[nicotinamide-mononucleotide]]; reduced NADH has a different identity and evidence packet.
- Do not rank NADH inside [[top-buyable-cognition-chemicals]] unless the use case is specifically illness-state cognitive fatigue and the caveats remain visible.
