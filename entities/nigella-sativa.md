# Nigella sativa / Black Seed

Nigella sativa, also called black seed, black cumin, or black caraway, is a seed/oil botanical whose cognition relevance comes from small seed-capsule human trials plus a much larger preclinical and traditional-medicine literature around thymoquinone and related constituents. The wiki treats it as a product-specific botanical candidate, not as interchangeable with isolated thymoquinone, culinary spice exposure, generic "anti-inflammatory" supplements, or black-seed-oil pain/sleep anecdotes.

## Medicinal Evidence

The cleanest cognition anchor is the 2013 healthy-elderly trial. Forty healthy elderly male volunteers were randomized to 500 mg Nigella sativa seed capsules twice daily or placebo for 9 weeks. The abstract reports significant active-placebo differences for logical memory I/II, digit span, 30-minute delayed recall on Rey-Osterrieth, letter-cancellation time, Trail Making Test A/B time, and Stroop part C. Cardiac, liver, and kidney biochemical markers did not significantly change during the study.^[raw/articles/pubmed-23707331-nigella-sativa-healthy-elderly-cognition.md]

That signal is useful but fragile. The sample was small, male-only, older, and from a single research stream. It does not establish dose-response, durability, product-marker transferability, or whether the same seed-capsule preparation maps to commercial oils, extracts, thymoquinone-enriched products, or culinary intake.

The adolescent trial adds a second human signal but also shows why confidence stays low. Forty-eight healthy adolescent males were randomized to 500 mg Nigella sativa seed or placebo once daily for 4 weeks. The abstract reports selected between-group CVLT-II benefits for immediate trial 5, long-term free recall, and long-term cued recall, but mood and trait-anxiety findings were partly within-group only. The result is memory/mood-adjacent support, not broad enhancer proof.^[raw/articles/pubmed-24412554-nigella-sativa-adolescent-cognition-mood.md]

Mechanistically, Nigella sativa has enough learning-memory literature to deserve more than a passing mention. The 2016 review links black seed and thymoquinone to animal and cellular evidence involving oxidative stress, anti-inflammatory pathways, beta-amyloid neurotoxicity, possible acetylcholinesterase inhibition, scopolamine/diabetes/hypoperfusion models, and anxiety-performance interactions. This explains plausibility, but the medicinal confidence ceiling remains set by the small human trials.^[raw/articles/pubmed-27022403-nigella-sativa-learning-memory-review.md]

## Safety And Product Gates

LiverTox is reassuring on liver injury: black cumin seed has not been associated with serum enzyme elevations during therapy or with clinically apparent liver injury, and it receives likelihood score E. It also lists ordinary side effects such as abdominal discomfort, bloating, dysgeusia, diarrhea, and headache, with rare rash and hypersensitivity reactions.^[raw/articles/ncbi-bookshelf-2023-livertox-black-cumin-seed.md]

The broader safety review keeps the product gate alive. Different preparations have different toxicity profiles; chloroform and petroleum ether extracts were more cytotoxic than other extracts, chronic fixed-oil exposure was described as slightly toxic in animal work, and human reports include allergic contact dermatitis. The same review notes possible CYP2D6/CYP3A4 relevance, so medication users should not treat black seed oil as a pharmacologically inert food.^[raw/articles/pubmed-37551386-nigella-sativa-safety-overview.md]

## Practical Interpretation

[[nigella-sativa]] earns standalone coverage because it has two small human cognition-adjacent RCTs plus plausible preclinical mechanism. Its medicinal rating is `low-moderate` only for the old/small seed-capsule trial lane, and `low` overall after product-form and replication penalties. Anecdote supports symptom salience, not cognitive efficacy.
