# LM22A-4

LM22A-4 is a small-molecule BDNF loop-domain mimetic / TrkB partial-agonist candidate. It belongs in the wiki as a preclinical TrkB comparator, not as a validated cognitive enhancer.

Medicinal cognition signal: `preclinical-only`. The strongest cognition-relevant captured source is a Rett syndrome mouse paper where 4 weeks of systemic LM22A-4 improved hippocampal-dependent object-location memory and restored LTP in female Mecp2 heterozygous mice. Earlier discovery work reported TrkB-dependent neuroprotection and motor-learning improvement after rodent TBI, and a 2024 Rett model paper adds dendritic-spine and atypical-behavior rescue. None of this is human cognition efficacy.^[raw/articles/pubmed-20407211-lm22a4-bdnf-mimetics-trkb.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-28679669-lm22a4-rett-object-location-memory.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-38785269-lm22a4-rett-spine-behavior.md]

Mechanism signal: `plausible but target-specificity-gated`. LM22A-4 is part of the small-molecule TrkB agonist literature that later caution papers treat carefully. The current wiki should keep it separate from [[7-8-dihydroxyflavone]], [[r13]], [[deoxygedunin]], and [[zeb85]] because each lane has different chemistry, delivery, and specificity problems.^[raw/articles/pmc-8398766-small-molecule-trkb-agonist-limitations.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-37488840-trkb-agonist-caution-review.md]

Human translation signal: `absent in captured registry search`. The captured 2026 ClinicalTrials.gov search did not surface a named LM22A-4 cognition program. That keeps LM22A-4 in [[translational-gap-table]], not in a practical nootropic tier.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-trkb-bdnf-mimetic-registry-search-2026.md]

## Practical Interpretation

LM22A-4 is relevant because it gives the TrkB branch a disease-model object-memory anchor. It should stay preclinical until human exposure, dose, safety, and cognition endpoints exist. Use [[target-specificity-gate-for-plasticity-molecules]] before upgrading any claim.
