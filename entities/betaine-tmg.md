# Betaine / Trimethylglycine (TMG)

Betaine, also called trimethylglycine or TMG, is a choline-derived methyl donor. In prescription form as CYSTADANE, betaine anhydrous is a methylating agent used to decrease elevated [[homocysteine]] in homocystinuria. In supplement communities, TMG is often discussed as a "methylation support" chemical beside [[s-adenosyl-l-methionine]], [[folate-b-vitamins]], [[choline-donor-cognition-signal-map]], and [[creatine]].

Medicinal cognition signal: `very low; biomarker/status only`. The formal signal is homocysteine remethylation, not cognition. A 2013 meta-analysis of five randomized placebo-controlled trials in healthy adults found that at least 4 g/day betaine for 6-24 weeks lowered plasma homocysteine by a pooled 1.23 micromol/L. The analysis did not test cognitive endpoints.^[raw/articles/pubmed-23997720-betaine-homocysteine-meta.md]

Medical-status signal: `real but rare-disease specific`. DailyMed's CYSTADANE label says betaine acts as a methyl-group donor in remethylating homocysteine to methionine and is indicated for homocystinuria due to CBS deficiency, MTHFR deficiency, or cobalamin cofactor metabolism defects. The label reports biochemical homocysteine reduction and physician-reported clinical improvements in some homocystinuria cases, but many patients also used B6, B12, folate, and diet changes, so cognition attribution is not clean.^[raw/articles/dailymed-2026-cystadane-betaine-label.md]

Mechanism boundary: ODS describes choline as a source of methyl groups and notes that folate deficiency increases the need for dietary choline because choline becomes the primary methyl donor. That makes betaine/TMG part of the one-carbon map, but it does not make every choline, betaine, or methylfolate anecdote interchangeable.^[raw/articles/ods-2026-choline-health-professional-fact-sheet.md]

Anecdote signal: `weak-moderate and bidirectional`. Forum reports include brain-fog improvement, calmer fullness, homocysteine-lowering rationale, and methylation-protocol narratives, but also nausea, diarrhea, bile/GI concerns, depersonalization-like feelings, memory problems, depression, insomnia, and "overmethylation" concern.^[raw/articles/reddit-2022-2026-same-tmg-methyl-donor-brainfog-threads.md]

Safety signal: `lab-gated caution`. The CYSTADANE label highlights hypermethioninemia and cerebral edema risk in CBS-deficient homocystinuria, with methionine monitoring and a target below 1,000 micromol/L. Common or reported adverse effects include nausea, GI distress, diarrhea, bad taste, odor, agitation, depression, irritability, sleep disturbance, vomiting, skin odor abnormalities, and rare severe cerebral edema in the indicated population.^[raw/articles/dailymed-2026-cystadane-betaine-label.md]

## Practical Interpretation

Betaine/TMG belongs in the wiki because it is a real one-carbon pathway chemical with a prescription rare-disease label and healthy-adult homocysteine-lowering evidence. It should not be promoted as a cognition enhancer. The current route is `lab/status hypothesis`: homocysteine, folate, B12, MMA, kidney function, diet, choline intake, and medication context must be known before subjective brain-fog claims are interpretable.
