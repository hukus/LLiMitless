# Cortexin

Cortexin is a peptide bioregulator / polypeptide preparation that appears in the older neuroprotective-peptide literature alongside [[semax]], [[cerebrolysin]], [[kortagen]], and [[pinealon]]. In that family, it is usually framed as a potential support for neuronal resilience and recovery rather than as a standard nootropic. The branch now deserves more attention than a mere legacy name because it has a real, if regional and heterogeneous, human recovery literature.

The current wiki sweep gives Cortexin more human history than the old stub suggested. There is an open clinical trial in brain ischemia, a multicenter randomized study in chronic cerebral ischemia, and an open randomized study in chronic alcohol encephalopathy / polyneuropathy. Those studies are still mostly regional and recovery-oriented, but they make Cortexin a real human-exposure node rather than just a review citation.^[raw/articles/pubmed-25403301-cortexin-brain-ischemia-open-clinical-trial.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-30335070-cortexin-chronic-cerebral-ischemia-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-20517210-cortexin-alcohol-encephalopathy-polyneuropathy.md]

The 2026 ischemia-reperfusion paper is preclinical/mechanistic support, not a cognition result. That keeps Cortexin in the translational lane: more grounded than Kortagen, less clinically grounded than [[cerebrolysin]].^[raw/articles/pubmed-40783844-cortexin-ischemia-reperfusion-2026.md]

## Frontier Read

Cortexin is not an upcoming Western development peptide, but it is a more serious recovery-state peptide branch than its old coverage implied. The practical read is `historical human exposure plus modern mechanistic persistence`, not healthy-adult enhancement. That is enough to keep Cortexin in the active peptide map rather than burying it inside family comparisons.
