# Aronia melanocarpa Extract

Aronia melanocarpa extract, also called chokeberry extract or AME, is a standardized anthocyanin-rich berry extract with two human cognition RCTs. The current signal is narrow but replicated across two different age bands: psychomotor speed and reaction-time measures improved, while memory and executive function were mostly unchanged.

## Key Facts

- A 2020 parallel RCT in 101 healthy middle-aged overweight adults tested 90 mg/day and 150 mg/day AME for 24 weeks. The 90 mg/day arm improved psychomotor speed versus placebo; attention, cognitive flexibility, BDNF, and most vascular outcomes did not change. The effect therefore looks closer to throughput support than to a memory or executive-function effect.^[raw/articles/pubmed-32824483-aronia-melanocarpa-extract-cognition-rct.md]
- A 2024 crossover RCT in 35 healthy young adults used 180 mg anthocyanins/day for 1 week. Movement time improved on the five-choice reaction time task and serum BDNF increased, but memory and executive function did not change.^[raw/articles/pubmed-38656355-aronia-melanocarpa-extract-cognition-rct.md]
- The two trials used different populations, durations, and endpoint batteries, yet both landed in the same narrow speed/reaction-time lane. That is why this extract is more than a single-study curiosity, even though the effect is still small and domain-limited.

## Interpretation

Aronia looks more like a candidate for acute or short-term psychomotor support than a universal nootropic. It is more reproducible than an undefined berry supplement because the extract is named and standardized, and the cross-study pattern is consistent enough to keep it as a standalone candidate. The remaining limitation is scope: the effect has not yet generalized into broad memory or executive-function gains.
