# Yokukansan

Yokukansan is a Japanese Kampo formula also known as Yi-Gan San. It is commonly discussed for neuropsychiatric symptoms in dementia, irritability, insomnia, and agitation-like states. For this wiki it is a formula-level traditional medicine with a behavioral-symptom signal, not a cognitive enhancer.

## Bottom Line

Medicinal cognition signal: `negative/low for cognition`, `low-moderate for dementia behavioral symptoms`. The best captured meta-analysis found yokukansan reduced behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia overall, but MMSE scores did not differ from placebo or usual-care controls.^[raw/articles/sage-2016-yokukansan-bpsd-updated-meta-analysis.md]

That means yokukansan belongs near agitation, irritability, sleep, and caregiver-burden discussions, not near healthy-adult cognition or brain-fog enhancement rankings.

## Formula Identity

Yokukansan is usually described as a seven-crude-drug formula: Atractylodes lancea rhizome, Poria, Cnidium rhizome, Uncaria hook, Japanese Angelica root, Bupleurum root, and Glycyrrhiza/licorice. This is formula evidence, not proof for isolated Uncaria, Poria, Angelica, Bupleurum, or licorice.

## Formal Evidence

The 2016 updated meta-analysis included five RCTs with 381 patients with behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia. Yokukansan reduced BPSD total scores and some symptom subscales such as delusions, hallucinations, and agitation/aggression. However, in Alzheimer's-only analyses it was not superior for BPSD total or subscales, and MMSE did not improve versus placebo/usual care.^[raw/articles/sage-2016-yokukansan-bpsd-updated-meta-analysis.md]

This creates a strict routing rule: if a person reports "clearer thinking" on yokukansan, the first interpretation should be calmer sleep/behavioral-state modulation, not direct memory enhancement.

## Safety And Monitoring

The licorice/Glycyrrhiza component creates a hypokalemia and pseudoaldosteronism gate. In a retrospective cohort of 665 patients, 8.3% developed hypokalemia below 3.0 mEq/L, with older age, lower albumin, and daily dose at least 7.5 g flagged in the abstract.^[raw/articles/pubmed-33132320-yokukansan-hypokalemia-cohort.md]

Practical safety questions include baseline potassium, blood pressure, edema, muscle weakness, diuretics/laxatives, kidney or cardiac disease, age/frailty, and other licorice-containing products.

## Signal Judgment

Yokukansan should stay in the fixed-formula watchlist as a negative-cognition comparator: human RCT synthesis supports behavioral symptoms more than cognition, and the licorice safety burden is real. It is useful precisely because it prevents the wiki from treating every traditional neuropsychiatric formula as a memory enhancer.
