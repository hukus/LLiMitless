# Berberine / Dihydroberberine

Berberine is a specific cationic alkaloid sold as a supplement for glucose, lipid, weight, and metabolic-health claims. Dihydroberberine is a reduced berberine-related compound marketed as a higher-bioavailability form. In this wiki they belong in the chemical/metabolic branch, not as a traditional-medicine page, and they should not be collapsed with goldenseal, barberry extracts, [[metformin]], [[myo-inositol]], or generic "blood sugar support."^[raw/articles/pubchem-2026-berberine-identity.md]^[raw/articles/pubchem-2026-dihydroberberine-identity.md]

The cognition read is `low/indirect and phenotype-gated`. Formal evidence supports metabolic syndrome components better than cognition endpoints, the 2026 obesity/MASLD RCT is a body-fat and liver-fat counterweight, and the only direct cognitive-function clue captured here is a chronic-schizophrenia trial with inflammation and illness-state confounding. The practical hypothesis is postprandial or metabolic-state brain fog, not broad healthy-adult cognitive enhancement.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40740996-berberine-metabolic-syndrome-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41543854-berberine-obesity-masld-rct.md]^[raw/articles/springer-2023-berberine-schizophrenia-cognition-rct.md]

## Evidence Snapshot

| Layer | Signal | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| Identity | Berberine CID 2353; dihydroberberine CID 10217 | Separate molecule/form branch; do not borrow generic plant or extract claims |
| Metabolic syndrome meta-analysis | Placebo-controlled RCT meta-analysis reports improvements in fasting glucose, triglycerides, waist circumference, LDL, total cholesterol, BMI, and 2-hour OGTT, with no HDL or blood-pressure signal | Good metabolic-state rationale, not a cognition endpoint |
| 2026 obesity/MASLD RCT | 337 diabetes-free adults; berberine 1 g/day for 6 months did not reduce visceral adipose tissue or liver fat versus placebo | Counterweight to broad weight-loss and body-fat claims |
| Dihydroberberine PK pilot | n=5 healthy male crossover; 100/200 mg dihydroberberine produced greater plasma berberine exposure than 500 mg berberine, without short-term glucose/insulin differences | Bioavailability clue only; not efficacy, not cognition |
| Schizophrenia cognition trial | Chronic-schizophrenia RCT reported better negative-symptom and trail-making measures plus inflammatory-marker reductions | Disease-state anti-inflammatory cognition clue; not healthy-adult enhancement |
| Official/safety context | NCCIH cautions that social-media weight-loss claims outrun rigorous evidence; MSKCC lists GI effects and medication/pregnancy cautions | Keeps the branch risk- and interaction-gated |
| Anecdote | Repeated reports around post-meal clarity, cravings, CGM/glucose, fatigue, low-blood-sugar-like symptoms, GI effects, and no effect | Useful hypothesis layer, not dose or efficacy proof |

## Practical Interpretation

Berberine is most plausible when the "brain fog" phenotype looks metabolic: post-meal sleepiness, carbohydrate-sensitive energy crashes, PCOS/metabolic-syndrome context, high fasting glucose/A1c, high triglycerides, or CGM-visible excursions. It is a weak fit for a rested, metabolically healthy adult seeking memory, IQ, or general executive-function enhancement.

Dihydroberberine belongs inside the same branch but needs its own product label. The current human bridge is pharmacokinetic and tiny, so "more bioavailable" should not be translated into "more cognitive" or "more effective for glucose" without an efficacy study in the target phenotype.^[raw/articles/pubmed-35010998-dihydroberberine-pk-glycemia-pilot.md]

## Safety And Use Boundaries

- Check medication context first: diabetes drugs, sulfonylureas, CYP2D6/CYP2C9/CYP3A4 substrates, bosutinib, tacrolimus, cyclosporin, transplant immunosuppression, and pregnancy/breastfeeding are not minor details.^[raw/articles/mskcc-2026-berberine-safety-interactions.md]
- Track fasting glucose/A1c or CGM, meal timing, dose timing, GI symptoms, sleep, BP/HR if relevant, and next-day cognitive tasks before interpreting "clearer thinking."
- Do not use social-media weight-loss framing as evidence of cognition benefit; NCCIH's public guidance is useful precisely because it separates internet popularity from rigorous clinical evidence.^[raw/articles/nccih-2026-berberine-weight-loss-safety-context.md]
- Keep the cognition claim below [[creatine]], caffeine, [[ketone-ester]], and stronger state-support comparators until a direct healthy-adult or metabolic-phenotype cognition trial exists.
