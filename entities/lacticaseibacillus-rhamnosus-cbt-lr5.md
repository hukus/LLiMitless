# Lacticaseibacillus Rhamnosus CBT-LR5

Lacticaseibacillus rhamnosus CBT-LR5, also described as LR5 / KCTC 12202BP in the trial material, is a Korean strain-specific probiotic lead for suspected mild cognitive impairment. It belongs beside [[bifidobacterium-breve-a1]] and [[lacticaseibacillus-rhamnosus-gg]], not inside a generic probiotic bucket.

Medicinal signal: `low / tiny suspected-MCI pilot`. A 2025 randomized, double-blind, parallel-group non-comparative study enrolled only 20 older adults with suspected MCI. Both arms received 1 x 10^10 CFU/day LR5, but with different skim-milk carrier doses. After 12 weeks, the low-dose carrier arm improved MOCA-K naming and delayed recall; the high-dose arm did not show the same signal. That is enough for a watchlist page and not enough for an efficacy claim.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40005019-cbt-lr5-mci-cognition.md]

Interpretation: the awkward dose/carrier pattern is the central caution. The trial does not cleanly separate LR5 from skim milk, matrix effects, practice effects, or small-sample noise. Broader probiotic syntheses remain low-certainty or null in MCI/AD domains, so CBT-LR5 should be read as a product-development signal rather than a class-level result.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41291638-probiotic-supplements-cognitive-function-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40034358-probiotics-mci-ad-meta-analysis.md]

Product and protocol gates:

- Strain identity: CBT-LR5 / LR5 / KCTC 12202BP must be visible.
- Matrix identity: the tested product included skim milk; dairy allergy and lactose intolerance matter.
- Dose: trial arms used 1 x 10^10 CFU/day, with different skim-milk amounts.
- Population: older adults with suspected MCI, not healthy adult enhancement.
- Endpoint: MOCA-K subdomains only, not global cognition replication.
- Replication need: a larger placebo-controlled dose-finding trial with clean matrix controls.
