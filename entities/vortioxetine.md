# Vortioxetine

Vortioxetine is a prescription antidepressant with cognition-relevant post-COVID evidence because it was tested in a randomized placebo-controlled post-COVID condition trial. In this wiki it belongs in the illness-state lane with [[post-viral-brain-fog-chemical-watchlist]], not in broad healthy-adult nootropic rankings.

## Bottom Line

Post-COVID cognition signal: `low-moderate but contested`. The 149-participant randomized trial did not show a significant overall cognitive benefit on Digit Symbol Substitution Test change, but baseline CRP moderated response and the above-mean-CRP subgroup favored vortioxetine. Depressive symptoms and health-related quality of life also improved versus placebo.^[raw/articles/brain-2023-vortioxetine-post-covid-condition-rct.md]

Healthy-adult enhancement signal: `not established`. The captured evidence is post-COVID condition with mood, quality-of-life, and inflammation-state context.

Practical route: medical-supervision-first. Vortioxetine carries antidepressant-label gates: suicidal-thought monitoring in young patients, serotonin syndrome, bleeding risk with NSAIDs/anticoagulants/antiplatelets, mania/hypomania screening, angle-closure glaucoma, hyponatremia/SIADH, sexual dysfunction, pregnancy/newborn issues, and CYP2D6/CYP-inducer dose considerations.^[raw/articles/dailymed-2026-trintellix-vortioxetine-label.md]

## Formal Evidence

The post-COVID trial used flexible dosing from 5-20 mg/day for eight weeks. The cleanest read is not "vortioxetine improves long-COVID brain fog"; it is "overall cognitive endpoint miss, inflammation-moderated cognitive signal, and mood/quality-of-life benefit." That makes CRP or inflammatory phenotype the key next-test variable.^[raw/articles/brain-2023-vortioxetine-post-covid-condition-rct.md]

## Signal Judgment

Vortioxetine is one of the few post-COVID brain-fog candidates with a real placebo-controlled trial, but it remains phenotype-bound and contested. It should be tracked as an inflammation/mood-enriched post-COVID candidate, not a general pro-cognitive drug.
