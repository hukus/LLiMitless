# Cathepsin B

## Bottom Line

Cathepsin B is a mixed lysosomal/inflammatory mediator, not a practical cognitive enhancer. Medicinal cognition signal is `mechanism-only` in the current corpus: review literature links cathepsin B to neurodegeneration, TBI, inflammation, behavioral deficits, and possible exercise-mediated cognition effects, but the human mediator story is heterogeneous and no direct cognition intervention is indexed. Anecdotal signal is `absent`.

## What It Is

Cathepsin B is a lysosomal cysteine protease with roles in inflammation, cell death, neurodegeneration, and exercise-biology hypotheses. In this wiki it belongs with [[exerkine-myokine-cognition-2026-scan]], [[irisin]], [[clusterin]], [[pf4]], and [[neuroinflammation-and-cognition]] as a compartment- and disease-state-sensitive mechanism node.

## Medicinal Research Signal

The captured review maps cathepsin B across AD, traumatic brain injury, ischemia, and related brain disorders. It argues that lysosomal leakage and cytosolic cathepsin B can contribute to cell death, inflammation, cognitive and behavioral deficits, while also noting that exercise biology sometimes treats cathepsin B as a plausible mediator of cognitive benefit. This bidirectional-looking biology is exactly why the page should stay mechanism-first.^[raw/articles/pmc-7261628-cathepsin-b-in-neurodegeneration-of-alzheimers-disease-traumatic-brain-injury-and-related-brain-disorders.md]

No direct human cathepsin B cognition intervention is indexed. The correct interpretation is mediator ambiguity, not target validation.

## Safety And Status

Cathepsin B is not a consumer intervention target. Its proximity to inflammation, lysosomal leakage, cell death, and injury biology means any future translational program would need tight compartment, disease-stage, and safety monitoring.

Route cathepsin B claims through [[safety-interaction-matrix-for-multi-modal-stacks]] when exercise-mediator, neurodegeneration, TBI, inflammation, or lysosomal-leakage mechanisms are being translated into cognition claims.
