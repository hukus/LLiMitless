# Melatonin

Melatonin is an endogenous circadian hormone and dietary-supplement ingredient used mainly for sleep timing. In this wiki it should be handled as a sleep/circadian-state molecule, not a direct acute nootropic. It should not be collapsed with tryptophan, 5-HTP, magnesium sleep stacks, antihistamine sleep aids, Epithalon/Epithalamin melatonin-modulation claims, or DHA/EPA/vitamin E/tryptophan/melatonin multi-nutrient combinations.

Medicinal cognition signal: `low/contested and sleep-circadian mediated`. The 2026 nutrient NMA reports a global-cognition signal for melatonin and for a DHA/EPA/vitamin E/tryptophan/melatonin stack, but that source is domain- and phenotype-sensitive rather than broad enhancement evidence. Two 2025 RCT meta-analyses in cognitively impaired adults lean positive on MMSE/global cognition: one found total sleep time and MMSE improvements in older adults with MCI or dementia, and the other found a pooled MMSE improvement but no ADAS-Cog improvement. The best interpretation is a possible cognitive-impairment/sleep-normalization signal, especially in MCI-like or sleep-disturbed phenotypes. It is not proof that healthy users get sharper cognition from melatonin.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41764841-nutritional-supplements-cognition-nma.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41240058-melatonin-sleep-cognition-cognitive-impairment-meta.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-12581400-melatonin-cognition-cognitive-impairment-meta.md]

The counterweight is real. A 2014 prolonged-release melatonin AD trial reported better MMSE/IADL and sleep-efficiency, especially in the insomnia subgroup, but mean ADAS-Cog did not differ and the trial stopped early. A 2016 Alzheimer RCT meta-analysis found a small total-sleep-time signal but no MMSE or ADAS-Cog cognitive benefit. This makes the medicinal claim contested and endpoint-specific: MMSE/global cognition may move in some impaired sleepers, while more disease-specific cognitive batteries and Alzheimer-only pooling are less convincing.^[raw/articles/pubmed-24971004-prolonged-release-melatonin-ad-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-27645169-melatonin-alzheimers-meta-analysis-null.md]

Anecdote signal: `weak-moderate and bidirectional`. Community reports include a sleep-mediated positive pattern: users who sleep better or reset timing sometimes report feeling refreshed, clearer, or more functional the next day. The adverse pattern is just as important: users report next-day grogginess, brain fog, sluggishness, depressed/irritable mornings, vivid dreams, and "hangover" effects, often with high dose, slow-release products, late timing, or unknown product content. Anecdotes should guide dose/timing/product-quality questions, not upgrade efficacy.^[raw/articles/reddit-2023-melatonin-refreshed-sleep-thread.md]^[raw/articles/reddit-2024-melatonin-grogginess-brain-fog-thread.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-5263083-melatonin-supplement-content-variability.md]

Safety and product-quality signal: `moderate caution`. NCCIH says short-term use appears safe for most people, but long-term safety is unclear; side effects can include headache, dizziness, nausea, and sleepiness. Clinician supervision is especially relevant with epilepsy medicines, blood thinners, pregnancy/breastfeeding, older age, dementia, and pediatric use. US supplement regulation and product variability are central: one analytical study found many products far above or below labeled dose and some with serotonin. Product form, actual dose, timing, release profile, light exposure, and morning alertness should be tracked before interpreting either benefits or harms.^[raw/articles/nccih-2026-melatonin-usefulness-safety.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-5263083-melatonin-supplement-content-variability.md]

## Signal Split

| Layer | Signal | Readout |
|---|---|---|
| 2026 nutrient NMA | `low/supportive` | Melatonin improved global cognition in an older/MCI nutrient network, but attribution is phenotype-sensitive |
| 2025 Age and Ageing meta-analysis | `low/supportive` | 10 RCTs, sleep time +12.4 minutes and MMSE +1.8 points; authors call for larger rigorous trials |
| 2025 multidimensional meta-analysis | `low/endpoint-specific` | MMSE improved, ADAS-Cog did not; strongest subgroup was MCI and 13-24 week evening dosing |
| 2014 prolonged-release AD RCT | `supportive but exploratory` | 2 mg PRM improved MMSE/IADL and sleep efficiency; ADAS-Cog mean difference was null and recruitment stopped early |
| 2016 AD meta-analysis | `negative cognition counterweight` | Total sleep time improved slightly; MMSE and ADAS-Cog did not |
| Anecdote | `weak-moderate bidirectional` | Refreshed/clearer next-day reports coexist with grogginess, brain fog, vivid dreams, and hangover reports |
| Safety / quality | `moderate caution` | Short-term adult safety is usually mild, but long-term safety, older adults, dementia, children, interactions, and product-label accuracy are gating variables |

## Practical Interpretation

Melatonin belongs in the molecule atlas because recent meta-analyses now show enough cognitive-impairment signal to justify a standalone page. It should sit below cleaner cognition candidates and be routed through [[sleep-and-circadian-interventions-for-cognition]], [[phase-locked-triad-for-cognition-interventions]], and [[nutrient-supplement-cognition-signal-map]]. The defensible claim is "possible cognition support when sleep/circadian timing is a limiting factor in older or cognitively impaired populations," not "melatonin is a focus enhancer."

A confidence upgrade requires larger blinded RCTs that separate MCI from AD/dementia, prespecify cognitive batteries beyond MMSE, measure sleep and circadian markers, normalize dose/release form/timing, and report next-day sedation. For self-experiments, actual product quality and morning function should be treated as first-class outcomes.
