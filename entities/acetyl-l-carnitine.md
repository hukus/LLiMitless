# Acetyl-L-Carnitine

Acetyl-L-carnitine, usually abbreviated ALCAR or LAC, is the acetylated carnitine ester used in supplement, mitochondrial-support, depression, neuropathy, and cognitive-decline contexts. For this wiki it should sit in the mitochondrial / cholinergic / disease-state restoration lane beside [[amino-acid-mitochondrial-stack-watchlist]], not in the same category as acute prescription stimulants or broad healthy-adult nootropics.

Healthy enhancement signal: `weak/insufficient`. The cleanest healthy-person gate is the 2017 Cochrane review of L-carnitine and derivatives for people without cognitive impairment. It found only two eligible short trials in healthy young adults, no evidence of benefit on reaction time, vigilance, immediate memory, or delayed recall after three days in the usable trial, and very-low-quality evidence overall.^[raw/articles/pmc-6464592-l-carnitine-cognitive-enhancement-cochrane.md]

MCI / early-AD signal: `low-moderate but contested`. A DARE critical abstract of the 2003 MCI / mild-AD meta-analysis reports a small pooled advantage for ALCAR across many scales and trials, including memory and attention/performance categories. The same DARE record flags heterogeneity, broad outcomes, unclear validity assessment, incomplete method reporting, and heavy sponsor involvement, so this is inclusion-grade but not recommendation-grade.^[raw/articles/ncbi-bookshelf-2003-alcar-mci-ad-meta-analysis-dare.md]

Dementia signal: `low/contested`. Cochrane's dementia review included 16 double-blind placebo-controlled trials and found some nominal CGI/MMSE signals, but no evidence of benefit across cognition, severity, function, or continuous CGI outcome areas, concluding that routine dementia use was not supported.^[raw/articles/cochrane-2003-acetyl-l-carnitine-dementia.md]

Metabolic encephalopathy signal: `low-moderate disease-state`. In severe hepatic encephalopathy, a 61-person double-blind RCT using ALCAR 2 g twice daily for 90 days reported improvements in memory, executive/attention tests, ammonia, and EEG versus placebo. This supports toxin-state / gut-liver-brain restoration logic, not normal-productivity enhancement.^[raw/articles/pubmed-21870121-alcar-hepatic-encephalopathy-rct.md]

Mechanistic signal: `plausible but non-decisive`. The 2024 neuropsychopharmacology review frames LAC as a mitochondrial metabolite and acetyl donor linked to histone acetylation, glutamatergic plasticity, inflammation, insulin resistance, depression phenotypes, and AD hypotheses. That strengthens phenotype selection but cannot substitute for controlled cognitive outcomes.^[raw/articles/springer-2024-acetyl-l-carnitine-neuropsychopharmacology-review.md]

Safety / sourcing signal: `moderate caution`. NIH ODS notes that healthy people normally synthesize sufficient carnitine, that acetyl-L-carnitine bioavailability is not well studied, and that around 3 g/day of supplemental carnitine can cause GI symptoms and fishy body odor; seizure-disorder, uremia, medication, and unresolved TMAO/cardiovascular questions should be routed through safety before ranking.^[raw/articles/ods-2023-carnitine-health-professional.md]

## Practical Interpretation

ALCAR belongs in [[molecule-intake-triage-table]] as `state-restoration / disease-context / mitochondrial-support`, not broad enhancement. The most defensible research questions are phenotype-specific: MCI/early-AD subgroups, hepatic encephalopathy and other toxin/metabolic cognitive states, depression-linked low-LAC biology, fatigue states, and whether any healthy-adult effect survives longer, better-controlled testing. For a healthy user, the wiki should treat ALCAR as a low-confidence experiment with explicit stop rules rather than as a proven focus compound.
