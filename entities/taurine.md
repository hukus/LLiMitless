# Taurine

Taurine is a sulfur-containing amino acid abundant in animal foods, tissues, supplements, and energy drinks. For cognitive improvement it is a common stack ingredient, but its independent signal is mostly a negative or caffeine-confounded story rather than a clean nootropic win.

Medicinal cognition signal: `low/mostly negative`. A 2025 meta-analysis of 7 RCTs / 9 intervention trials and 402 participants found insufficient evidence that taurine improves cognitive function. Taurine alone or with exercise did not significantly improve cognitive scores overall, and taurine added to therapeutic drugs did not outperform therapeutic drugs alone overall.^[raw/articles/tandfonline-2025-taurine-cognition-meta-analysis.md]

Healthy acute cognition signal: `low/inconsistent`. The 2026 systematic review focused on sulfur-containing amino acids and cognition found 8 RCTs with 244 healthy participants, all testing taurine. Acute single doses, usually 1 to 3 g and up to about 50 mg/kg, produced small and inconsistent results, with most cognitive outcomes showing no effect. Caffeine-combination trials looked more positive but did not isolate taurine.^[raw/articles/pmc-12939648-taurine-sulphur-amino-acids-cognition-review.md]

Caffeine / fatigue-context signal: `low combination-only`. In 20 elite male boxers, caffeine 6 mg/kg plus taurine 3 g improved post-fatigue physical and some cognitive reaction-time measures more than placebo or single-agent conditions. This is a sports-fatigue combination result, not evidence that taurine alone improves rested cognition.^[raw/articles/mdpi-2022-caffeine-taurine-elite-boxers-cognition-rct.md]

Energy-drink attribution signal: `negative gate`. A 2012 review of energy-drink ingredients concluded that evidence did not substantiate claims that ingredients other than caffeine meaningfully contribute to physical or cognitive performance, apart from weak glucose and guarana evidence. Treat taurine in energy drinks as a product-identity confound unless isolated.^[raw/articles/pubmed-23206286-energy-drinks-caffeine-taurine-review.md]

Biomarker / dementia plausibility: `hypothesis only`. Cognitive Vitality summarizes observational and preclinical interest, including higher blood taurine associated with lower dementia risk, but says intervention trials have not shown human cognitive benefits so far. Its safety readout is generally benign at common trial doses, with medication, alcohol, caffeine, and energy-drink cardiovascular caveats.^[raw/articles/alzdiscovery-2025-taurine-cognitive-vitality.md]

Safety-context caveat: `specific caution, not general proof`. A 2025 Nature study found that taurine from a bone-marrow tumor niche can fuel aggressive myeloid leukemia biology through taurine-transporter and metabolic pathways. This does not prove that supplemental taurine causes leukemia in healthy people, but it is relevant for high-dose use and active myeloid-malignancy risk discussions.^[raw/articles/nature-2025-taurine-leukemia-metabolism.md]

## Signal Split

| Layer | Signal | Readout |
|---|---|---|
| Broad cognition RCT meta-analysis | `low/mostly negative` | 2025 meta-analysis does not support efficacy |
| Healthy acute cognition | `low/inconsistent` | 2026 review found small, inconsistent acute taurine results |
| Caffeine / sports-fatigue combination | `low combination-only` | Positive boxer crossover signal depends on caffeine plus taurine |
| Energy-drink attribution | `negative gate` | Most energy-drink cognitive effects should be attributed to caffeine unless isolated |
| Sleep / calm anecdote | `moderate` | Repeated reports of sleep quality, physical calm, and caffeine smoothing |

| Safety | `moderate caution` | Usually well tolerated in trials, but watch medication/caffeine/alcohol interactions, energy-drink cardiovascular context, high-dose use, and myeloid-cancer context |

## Practical Interpretation

Taurine should be routed through [[molecule-intake-triage-table]] as a `calm / sleep / caffeine-combination / negative-cognition-control` candidate. It is relevant to an ultimate molecules wiki because it is common, studied, and frequently stacked, but its independent cognitive-enhancement signal is weak. The strongest use-case hypothesis is not "taurine improves cognition"; it is "taurine may alter arousal, sleep continuity, caffeine tolerability, or fatigue-state performance in a subset, while objective cognition remains mostly unproven."
