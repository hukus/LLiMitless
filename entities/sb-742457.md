# SB-742457

SB-742457 is an early 5-HT6 receptor antagonist Alzheimer's disease candidate. It is the best example in this branch of an early global-impression hint that did not become a durable cognition program.

Medicinal cognition signal: `mixed early / negative later`. The phase 2 study in mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease tested 5 mg, 15 mg, and 35 mg once daily for 24 weeks. The 35 mg arm showed a nominal global-impression signal, but ADAS-Cog was not clearly positive, so the study was never a stable cognition win.^[raw/articles/pubmed-20043816-sb742457-phase2-ad.md]

Replication signal: `negative`. Two later randomized trials tested SB-742457 in mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease, including monotherapy and adjunctive donepezil designs. Neither study met overall success criteria; some adjunctive timepoint-specific signals were not enough to rescue the program.^[raw/articles/pubmed-29854923-sb742457-two-rcts-ad.md]

## Safety And Interpretation Caveats

- Treat early global-impression movement as hypothesis generation unless durable cognitive endpoints replicate.
- The later RCT failures dominate the branch interpretation even if isolated timepoints looked better than placebo.
- SB-742457 is useful as a trial-design warning: a plausible serotonergic mechanism plus a phase 2 hint can still fail when endpoint durability is required.
