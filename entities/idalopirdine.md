# Idalopirdine

Idalopirdine is a 5-HT6 receptor antagonist that became one of the class-defining failed Alzheimer's disease cognition programs. It belongs in the wiki as a negative serotonergic comparator, not as a live cognitive-enhancement candidate.

Medicinal cognition signal: `negative`. The LADDER phase 2 study tested idalopirdine 90 mg/day, equivalent to 30 mg three times daily, as adjunctive therapy to donepezil in moderate Alzheimer's disease. The primary ADAS-Cog endpoint did not separate from placebo, so the early program already lacked a clean cognition win.^[raw/articles/pubmed-25297016-idalopirdine-ladder-moderate-ad.md]

Replication signal: `negative / class-defining`. The later phase 3 program randomized 2525 participants across three trials of idalopirdine added to cholinesterase inhibitors in mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease. The program did not reduce cognitive decline over 24 weeks, and the source conclusion does not support idalopirdine use for Alzheimer's disease.^[raw/articles/pubmed-29318278-idalopirdine-three-rcts-ad.md]

## Safety And Interpretation Caveats

- The failure is mainly efficacy translation, not a simple intolerability story.
- Cholinesterase-inhibitor adjunct design does not make idalopirdine a cholinergic enhancer; the active drug hypothesis was 5-HT6 antagonism.
- Early symptomatic or subgroup optimism should be routed through the phase 3 miss before any new serotonergic cognition claim is upgraded.
