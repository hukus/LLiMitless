# VU0467319 / VU319

## Bottom Line

VU0467319, also called VU319, is a muscarinic M1 positive allosteric modulator with healthy-volunteer target-engagement evidence but no cognition-efficacy proof. Medicinal signal is `target-engagement only`; anecdotal signal is absent in the current corpus.

## What It Is

VU0467319 is an investigational positive allosteric modulator of the M1 muscarinic acetylcholine receptor. The goal is to stimulate cholinergic cognition-relevant circuits with fewer dose-limiting effects than older orthosteric agonists such as [[xanomeline]]. It belongs with [[muscarinic-agent-cognition-watchlist]] and [[cholinergic-and-nicotinic-agents-2026-scan]] as a mechanism lead.

## Medicinal Research Signal

The captured phase 1 study tested single ascending doses in healthy volunteers plus a food-effect arm. VU319 was given to 52 healthy adults aged 18-55 years, with single-dose cohorts from 60 to 600 mg and a 120 mg food-effect study. Tolerability was good with no dose-limiting side effects, and exploratory cognitive and event-related-potential readouts suggested CNS target engagement at higher doses.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40598606-vu0467319-m1-pam-healthy-participants.md]

This is useful human pharmacology, not efficacy. The correct wiki interpretation is that receptor-selective muscarinic modulation has entered healthy-volunteer testing, but cognition benefit still needs a trial designed and powered for behavior.

## Safety And Status

The first-in-human readout was tolerability-positive and did not show dose-limiting side effects across the tested range, but this was short, single-dose, and small. It cannot establish chronic safety or practical cognitive utility.
