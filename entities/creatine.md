# Creatine

Creatine is the wiki's main low-cost energy-buffer molecule for cognition. The evidence supports a `moderate, context-dependent` medicinal signal: most credible under sleep loss, high cognitive load, aging / lower reserve, or selected female hormonal-transition phenotypes, and weaker for young, rested, already-replete healthy adults.

June 2026 routing read: `formal strain-state lead with weak-moderate anecdote`. Creatine should be kept visible beside the strongest nootropic anecdote signals for the opposite reason from phenylpiracetam, methylene blue, or nicotine: its forum signal is mixed and not especially clean, but the formal human evidence is unusually practical for sleep loss, older/lower-reserve contexts, and selected hormonal-transition phenotypes. Treat it as a baseline energy-buffer comparator, not as a universal daily enhancer.

Medicinal cognition signal: `moderate for strain-state support; low-moderate for baseline enhancement`. A 2024 adult meta-analysis reported small domain-specific gains for memory, attention time, and processing-speed time, while global cognition and executive function were not clearly positive.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39070254-creatine-cognition-adults-meta-analysis.md] A separate healthy-memory meta-analysis found a small memory signal, strongest in older adults.^[raw/articles/pubmed-35984306-creatine-memory-healthy-individuals.md]

High-demand signal: `moderate`. The cleanest acute healthy-adult result is the sleep-deprivation crossover line: a single high oral dose of 0.35 g/kg partially rescued cognitive performance and processing speed during sleep loss and moved cerebral phosphocreatine / ATP measures; a 2026 lower-dose 0.2 g/kg crossover trial still reduced deterioration in logic, numerical, language-processing-speed, and PVT outcomes during 21 hours awake, though with a smaller effect and the same strain-state limits.^[raw/articles/pubmed-38418482-creatine-sleep-deprivation-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/mdpi-2026-single-dose-creatine-sleep-deprivation-cognition.md] A 2025 re-analysis strengthened the mechanism story by showing sleep-loss hemispheric energy asymmetry that creatine partly balanced, while a separate biomarker pilot found serum creatine rose after 24 hours of sleep deprivation.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40520504-creatine-sleep-deprivation-hemispheric-asymmetry.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40988912-sleep-deprivation-circulating-creatine-pilot.md]

Older-adult and phenotype signal: `low-moderate, still underpowered`. A 2025 older-adult systematic review found five of six studies positive, mainly in memory and attention, but only two were double-blind intervention studies and overall quality was mixed.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40971619-creatine-cognition-older-adults-systematic-review.md] The 2026 CONCRET-MENOPA RCT adds a narrow formulation/phenotype signal: medium-dose creatine hydrochloride outperformed placebo on reaction time and frontal brain creatine in perimenopausal and menopausal women, but this does not generalize to every creatine form or population.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40854087-creatine-menopause-cognition-rct.md]

Safety signal: `comparatively favorable`. The strongest captured aggregate safety review across 685 clinical trials and adverse-event databases did not find an overall excess side-effect signal versus placebo, and the 2026 lower-dose sleep-deprivation trial reported no GI distress or other acute physical adverse symptoms; practical tolerability still includes GI discomfort, hydration context, cramping reports, kidney-disease caution, and heat/exercise-state interpretation.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40198156-creatine-safety-side-effects-adverse-event-reports.md]^[raw/articles/mdpi-2026-single-dose-creatine-sleep-deprivation-cognition.md]

Registry watch: older-adult MCI / resistance-training and heat-stress cognition trials remain useful because they test the two most plausible responder hypotheses: lower reserve and high environmental demand.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct06948149-creatine-resistance-training-mci.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct07472426-creatine-heat-exercise-cognition.md]
