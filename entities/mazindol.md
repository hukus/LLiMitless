# Mazindol

Mazindol is an older anorectic / wakefulness-adjacent compound now best treated as a narcolepsy-development comparator. It sits near [[tesofensine]] because both are monoamine-reuptake anorectic stimulants, but mazindol's most relevant modern signal is sleep-disorder wakefulness, not healthy-adult cognition.

## Current Read

Medicinal cognition signal: `low / state-bound`. The old narcolepsy literature and modern mazindol ER program support wakefulness-state relevance. They do not establish broad cognitive enhancement in rested healthy adults.

The old PubMed narcolepsy summary reports sustained improvement in narcolepsy, with 6 mg mazindol described as having the same effect on narcolepsy as d-amphetamine 50 mg and fewer side effects. This is a sleep-disorder comparator, not a cognition endpoint.^[raw/articles/pubmed-525256-mazindol-narcolepsy.md]

The 2023 SLEEP abstract for mazindol ER / NLS-2 described it as a partial orexin 2 receptor agonist and triple monoamine reuptake inhibitor. In a 67-participant four-week phase 2 trial, mazindol ER improved Epworth Sleepiness Scale versus placebo and reduced cataplexy episodes in the narcolepsy type 1 subgroup, with no severe or serious adverse events reported in the abstract.^[raw/articles/sleep-2023-mazindol-er-narcolepsy-phase2.md]
