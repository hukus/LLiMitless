# Ulotaront

Ulotaront, also known as SEP-363856, is a TAAR1 agonist with additional 5-HT1A agonism. It belongs in the non-D2 dopamine-state / psychiatric-symptom comparator lane rather than the cognitive-enhancement lane.

Medicinal cognition signal: `low / no direct cognition win`. The early schizophrenia package was encouraging for symptoms: a sponsor-authored review described a 4-week phase 2 PANSS total signal versus placebo with effect size 0.45 and continued open-label tolerability. Later phase 3 DIAMOND 1 and DIAMOND 2 trials did not separate from placebo on the primary PANSS endpoint.^[raw/articles/pmc-10465394-ulotaront-taar1-review.md]^[raw/articles/otsuka-us-diamond-ulotaront-phase3-failure.md]

The 2025 dose-response meta-analysis softens the flat-null story but does not change the cognition conclusion. Across three randomized trials and 1,144 participants, the 100 mg dose showed the largest PANSS total improvement with a small effect size, while CGI-S had no clear dose-response and anxiety-related adverse events were higher than placebo at 50 and 75 mg. This is a symptom-dose signal, not a validated cognitive signal.^[raw/articles/pmc-12421877-ulotaront-dose-response-meta-analysis.md]

Pipeline signal: `still active`. A 2026 phase 3 schizophrenia registry remains recruiting in the current raw corpus despite the DIAMOND failures, so ulotaront should stay on the watchlist rather than be treated as abandoned.^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct06894212-ulotaront-phase3-schizophrenia.md]

## Signal Judgment

Ulotaront is useful for testing whether non-D2 dopaminergic / serotonergic modulation can improve psychosis symptoms without classic D2 blockade. It is not evidence that TAAR1 agonism improves healthy-adult attention, working memory, or executive function.
