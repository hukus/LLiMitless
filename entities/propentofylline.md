# Propentofylline

Propentofylline is a legacy xanthine derivative also known as HWA 285 / Hextol. PubChem routes it under nootropic xanthine derivatives, but its meaningful human evidence is an old Alzheimer's disease / vascular dementia development program, not modern healthy-adult enhancement.^[raw/articles/pubchem-2026-propentofylline-identity.md]

## Bottom Line

Medicinal signal: `low/legacy dementia-only`. Propentofylline has more formal clinical evidence than many old smart-drug names, including positive 1990s dementia trials and trial-program summaries. But the Cochrane review found only limited usable data, only four of nine studies had detailed reports, unpublished randomized data were not released, tolerability data were thin, and DARE judged the effect sizes modest with doubtful clinical relevance.^[raw/articles/pubmed-9329701-propentofylline-dementia-clinical-trials.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-9298634-propentofylline-12mo-dementia-rct.md]^[raw/articles/cochrane-2003-propentofylline-dementia-limited-evidence.md]^[raw/articles/ncbi-bookshelf-1999-propentofylline-dementia-dare.md]

Anecdotal signal: `not captured` in the current source set. This page should not borrow general xanthine, caffeine, or racetam anecdotes.

Practical route: track [[propentofylline]] as a historical dementia / glial-adenosine / PDE comparator near [[legacy-vasoactive-racetam-nootropic-watchlist]], [[pde-camp-creb-cognition-2026-scan]], and [[negative-evidence-watchlist-for-cognition-interventions]], not as a consumer nootropic.

## Human Evidence

The positive clinical-program summary reported four double-blind placebo-controlled randomized studies including 901 mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease patients and 359 mild-to-moderate vascular dementia patients, with propentofylline 300 mg three times daily one hour before food for up to 12 months. The abstract reports statistically significant and clinically relevant improvements over placebo in cognition, global function, and activities of daily living, with mostly minor transient digestive and nervous-system adverse events.^[raw/articles/pubmed-9329701-propentofylline-dementia-clinical-trials.md]

The 12-month multinational RCT in 260 outpatients reported statistically significant differences favoring propentofylline for global measures, cognitive measures, MMSE, and activities of daily living in the total dementia population. Patient self-ratings did not show significant treatment differences, and subgroup signals differed between vascular dementia and Alzheimer's disease.^[raw/articles/pubmed-9298634-propentofylline-12mo-dementia-rct.md]

The 1994 190-patient trial is a crucial counterweight. GBS and MMSE favored propentofylline, but the predefined primary efficacy definition required improvement on the GBS plus at least two of eight psychometric assessments. Psychometric assessments and the cognitive difficulties scale failed to demonstrate therapeutic benefit, so efficacy could not be established according to the predefined primary measures.^[raw/articles/pubmed-7972348-propentofylline-dementia-rct.md]

The 2003 Cochrane review and the 1999 DARE record keep the total rating low. Cochrane reported limited evidence of benefit but emphasized unavailable unpublished data on another 1200 randomized-trial patients. DARE described the improvements as statistically significant but modest, with very doubtful clinical relevance, and noted adverse events in 40% of propentofylline participants versus 22% of placebo participants in the reviewed data.^[raw/articles/cochrane-2003-propentofylline-dementia-limited-evidence.md]^[raw/articles/ncbi-bookshelf-1999-propentofylline-dementia-dare.md]

## Mechanism And Dosing Context

Propentofylline is framed as a xanthine derivative that inhibits adenosine uptake and phosphodiesterase, with glial, microglial, free-radical, glutamate, calcium, and cAMP/PKA hypotheses in the older literature. Trial dosing commonly used 300 mg three times daily, one hour before meals, and phase-I material flagged food interactions.^[raw/articles/pubchem-2026-propentofylline-identity.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-9329701-propentofylline-dementia-clinical-trials.md]

## Safety And Status

Clinical trial abstracts often describe propentofylline as well tolerated, but the DARE adverse-event imbalance and PubChem hazard flags keep safety from being dismissed. PubChem's H302/H312/H332/H373 notifications are handling hazards, while trial adverse events were mainly digestive and nervous-system complaints.^[raw/articles/pubchem-2026-propentofylline-identity.md]^[raw/articles/ncbi-bookshelf-1999-propentofylline-dementia-dare.md]

The program's bigger practical issue is not acute toxicity proof; it is that the old dementia-development package did not translate into a modern, transparent, replicated cognition drug with accessible full data.

## Signal Judgment

Propentofylline deserves standalone coverage because it is repeatedly cited in old dementia/nootropic literature and has a larger formal program than most fringe molecules. It still stays low-confidence for cognitive improvement: the signal is old, dementia-bound, partly sponsor-controlled, limited by unavailable unpublished data, and not transportable to healthy-adult enhancement.
