# Sibutramine

Sibutramine is a withdrawn monoamine-reuptake anorectic. In this wiki it is a safety-first comparator for [[tesofensine]], [[mazindol]], and other stimulant-adjacent appetite / wakefulness compounds, not a cognition candidate.

## Current Read

Medicinal cognition signal: `deprioritize / risk-first`. The relevant formal evidence is obesity and cardiovascular safety, not healthy-adult cognition. If sibutramine appears in a nootropic, weight-loss, or "energy" context, the first interpretation should be product-risk and cardiovascular-risk triage rather than cognitive-enhancement hypothesis.

SCOUT is the key class-warning anchor. It enrolled 10,744 overweight or obese older adults with cardiovascular disease, type 2 diabetes, or both. The composite outcome of nonfatal myocardial infarction, nonfatal stroke, resuscitation after cardiac arrest, or cardiovascular death occurred in 11.4% of the sibutramine group versus 10.0% of placebo, with HR 1.16.^[raw/articles/pubmed-20818901-sibutramine-scout-cv-outcomes.md]

FDA's hidden-ingredient notice adds the practical sourcing layer: FDA warned consumers in 2015 after finding sibutramine in a weight-loss product, stating that sibutramine was removed from the U.S. market in October 2010 for safety reasons and can substantially increase blood pressure and/or pulse rate.^[raw/articles/fda-2015-sibutramine-hidden-ingredient-withdrawn.md]
