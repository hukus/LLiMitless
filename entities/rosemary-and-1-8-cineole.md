# Rosemary / 1,8-Cineole

Rosemary is a Lamiaceae botanical whose cognition evidence splits across inhaled aroma / 1,8-cineole exposure, low-dose dried leaf powder, and rosemary water. The useful unit is route plus product, not "rosemary" as a single nootropic.

## Signal Snapshot

| Evidence layer | Direction | Signal strength | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aroma / 1,8-cineole | positive correlation, small study | `low` | In 20 healthy volunteers, absorbed 1,8-cineole after rosemary aroma correlated with better task speed and accuracy.^[raw/articles/pmc-3736918-rosemary-cineole-cognition-aroma.md] |

## Medicinal Evidence

The strongest mechanistic human bridge is the 1,8-cineole aroma study. It did not randomize participants to rosemary versus non-rosemary efficacy arms; instead, it varied exposure time and measured serum 1,8-cineole. Higher blood 1,8-cineole correlated with better speed and accuracy on serial subtraction and visual-information-processing tasks. This supports plausibility but remains an acute small-sample correlation with sensory/expectancy constraints.^[raw/articles/pmc-3736918-rosemary-cineole-cognition-aroma.md]

The dried-leaf powder study shows why dose matters. In a crossover trial in older adults, 750 mg improved speed of memory, but 6000 mg impaired speed of memory and other deleterious findings appeared less consistently. That is a direct warning against assuming more rosemary equals more cognition.^[raw/articles/sage-2012-rosemary-powder-elderly-cognition.md]

Rosemary water adds a third route. A randomized acute study in 80 healthy adults found small beneficial cognitive effects after 250 mL rosemary extract/hydrolat water versus mineral water, with near-infrared spectroscopy used to track cerebrovascular response. This is promising but still product-specific and acute.^[raw/articles/pubmed-30318972-rosemary-water-acute-cognition.md]
