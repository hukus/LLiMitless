# Selegiline

Selegiline, also called deprenyl, is a prescription MAO-B inhibitor with a state-restoration and disease-context cognition story. It is now the historical/lore anchor for [[mao-b-propargylamine-cognition-watchlist]], but it should not be treated as a casual nootropic because interaction, tyramine, dose-selectivity, and washout constraints dominate practical interpretation.

## Medicinal Signal

- Formal signal: `low`; disease-context and short-lived.
- The AD meta-analysis found statistically significant short-term cognitive differences at 4-6 and 8-17 weeks, but the effect disappeared later and was judged unlikely to be clinically important.^[raw/articles/pubmed-11813282-selegiline-alzheimers-meta-analysis.md]
- The early Parkinson disease trial was tiny and disease-specific, not a healthy-adult enhancement trial.^[raw/articles/pubmed-8665548-selegiline-cognition-early-pd.md]
- A 2026 MAO-B network meta-analysis found no significant global-cognition advantage for selegiline versus placebo in PD and no significant domain-specific benefits across the MAO-B inhibitor class.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41524120-maob-inhibitors-pd-cognition-network-meta.md]
- KDS2010/tisolagiline preclinical work explicitly uses selegiline as the older irreversible comparator: prolonged selegiline did not rescue memory in APP/PS1 mice in that model, while reversible KDS2010 did. This is a mechanistic caution, not a direct human selegiline verdict.^[raw/articles/pubmed-30906861-kds2010-reversible-maob-ad-mice.md]
- The current U.S. label keeps selegiline firmly in prescription MAOI territory and warns that higher doses lose MAO-B selectivity with interaction and tyramine concerns.^[raw/articles/dailymed-selegiline-hydrochloride-tablet.md]
