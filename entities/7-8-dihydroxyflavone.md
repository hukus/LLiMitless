# 7,8-Dihydroxyflavone

7,8-Dihydroxyflavone, often shortened to 7,8-DHF, is the main small-molecule TrkB / BDNF-mimetic lead in the wiki. It is relevant because it has repeated animal cognition and plasticity signals plus nootropic-market salience. It is not a validated human cognition enhancer.

Medicinal cognition signal: `preclinical-only`. The strongest cognition-relevant evidence is rodent memory consolidation, traumatic-brain-injury recovery, and NHE6/SLC9A6-linked synaptic-plasticity rescue. A targeted registry search found no named human cognition program for 7,8-DHF in the captured 2026 snapshot.^[raw/articles/pubmed-24070857-78-dhf-memory-consolidation-rodents.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-25661191-78-dhf-tbi-trkb.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39341363-78-dhf-nhe6-plasticity.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-trkb-bdnf-mimetic-registry-search-2026.md]

Mechanism signal: `plausible but contested`. Older review literature frames 7,8-DHF as an orally bioactive, blood-brain-barrier-penetrant TrkB agonist / BDNF mimetic.^[raw/articles/pubmed-26740873-78-dhf-trkb-review.md] Later caution papers keep confidence low because small-molecule TrkB agonist claims can be assay-sensitive, and some benefits may reflect broader neuroprotection, antioxidant activity, or non-TrkB pathways rather than clean receptor agonism.^[raw/articles/pubmed-37488840-trkb-agonist-caution-review.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-8398766-small-molecule-trkb-agonist-limitations.md]

Safety and translation signal: `low`. The captured corpus has no human cognition trial or robust human safety package. User reports discuss oral versus sublingual use, product-derivative confusion with 4-DMA-7,8-DHF, tolerance, sleep disruption, crashes, and possible adverse symptoms; these are useful caveats but not controlled safety data.
