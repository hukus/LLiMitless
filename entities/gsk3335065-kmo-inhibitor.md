# GSK3335065 KMO Inhibitor

GSK3335065 is the human target-engagement and safety anchor for kynurenine-3-monooxygenase (KMO) inhibition. It belongs in the cognitive-improvement molecule atlas because KMO is a plausible neuroinflammation / neurodegeneration target, but the current compound-level readout is safety-first and cognition-unproven.

Medicinal signal: `target-engagement only / risk-first for cognition`. In the first-in-human randomized placebo-controlled study, healthy male volunteers received ascending single IV bolus doses. One subject showed tryptophan-pathway metabolite changes consistent with partial KMO inhibition, but the same subject had broad complex ventricular tachycardia. The study was terminated early and development was discontinued.^[raw/articles/pubmed-34327739-gsk3335065-kmo-first-in-human.md]

KMO remains biologically interesting because it is a branch-point enzyme in the kynurenine pathway and has been framed as a drug target for neurodegenerative and neuroinflammatory disease. That target review layer does not rescue GSK3335065 as a cognition candidate.^[raw/articles/pubmed-26589832-kynurenine-3-monooxygenase-review-structure-mechanism-inhibitors.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39120289-kynurenines-target-inflammatory-disorders-review.md]

## Interpretation

GSK3335065 is a cautionary comparator for biomarker-positive / program-stopped drug development. It shows why the wiki should separate "pathway engagement" from "cognitive improvement." It also keeps the KMO branch from being reduced to supplement-style optimism.
