# Wild Green Oat Extract

Wild green oat extract is the wiki's product-specific branch for Avena sativa green-oat herb extracts such as Neuravena and Cognitaven. It should not be merged with ordinary oats, oat beta-glucan foods, oat straw tea, milky oat tinctures, oat seed extracts, or generic "nervine" herbal preparations. The active unit is a defined green-oat extract with dose, extraction method, marker chemistry, and task timing.

Medicinal cognition signal: `low/contested but product-specific`. The positive layer is real enough for standalone tracking: acute Neuravena studies report faster timed-task performance, selected delayed-recall / executive-task improvements, EEG or ERP shifts, and protection from task-related tiredness; the 2020 Cognitaven trial reported acute and four-week gains on Corsi Blocks and multitasking under a laboratory stressor. The counterweight is also important: 1500 mg/day wild green-oat extract for 12 weeks in healthy older adults did not improve cognition, even though a companion vascular study found improved brachial and cerebrovascular responsiveness. The best read is acute or stressor-state attention / processing-speed support, not durable memory enhancement.^[raw/articles/pubmed-26618715-neuravena-acute-middle-aged-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-8517335-green-oat-herb-extract-cognition-eeg-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-7352613-cognitaven-green-oat-stressor-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-3367260-wild-green-oat-chronic-cognition-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-23221935-neuravena-vascular-responsiveness.md]

Mechanistically, the branch is often framed around electrophysiology, attention-resource allocation, MAO-B / PDE-4 inhibition, and vascular responsiveness. That makes it adjacent to the PDE/CREB and fatigue-state parts of the graph, but it should not be treated as a small-molecule PDE4 comparator like [[roflumilast]]. The evidence is product-specific botanical evidence with sponsor/manufacturer involvement in several sources, not a validated target-engagement-to-cognition chain.^[raw/articles/pubmed-21563962-neuravena-eeg-spectral-frequencies.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-8517335-green-oat-herb-extract-cognition-eeg-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-23221935-neuravena-vascular-responsiveness.md]

Anecdote signal: `weak/bidirectional`. The nootropics thread is mostly a product-identity discussion: oat straw, milky oat, oat seed tincture, Neuravena-style extracts, and normal oats are repeatedly blurred. The herbalism thread points the other direction, with oatstraw tea described as calming, sedating, and motivation-lowering by some users. Those reports are not standardized-extract evidence, but they matter because community language can collapse "green oat extract" into calming oatstraw preparations.^[raw/articles/reddit-2023-oat-straw-cognitive-health-thread.md]^[raw/articles/reddit-2023-oatstraw-tranquilizer-thread.md]

Safety and product-quality signal: `low-moderate caution`. The controlled trials were in selected healthy adults with exclusions for factors such as relevant medication use, high blood pressure, pregnancy/lactation, nicotine use, high caffeine intake, or health conditions. That makes short-term tolerability in trial settings more reassuring than broad stack safety. Product identity, dose, botanical part, extraction solvent, caffeine/stimulant co-use, sleep timing, and sedation/calming response should be tracked before treating a green-oat product as a focus tool.

## Signal Split

| Layer | Signal | Readout |
|---|---|---|
| Acute Neuravena middle-aged crossover | `low/product-specific` | 800 mg improved global timed-task speed, delayed-recall errors, Peg and Ball performance, and second-exposure Corsi span |
| Acute Neuravena EEG/ERP study | `low/product-specific` | 800 mg improved speed without accuracy loss and shifted N2/ERN/P3 components in a small healthy sample |
| Cognitaven stressor trial | `low-moderate/product-specific` | 430 mg and 1290 mg over four weeks improved Corsi Blocks and multitasking under stressor conditions |
| Chronic older-adult Neuravena cognition trial | `negative/contested` | 1500 mg/day for 12 weeks did not improve measured cognition in healthy older adults |
| Vascular physiology companion | `supportive/physiology-only` | 1500 mg/day improved brachial FMD and cerebrovascular responsiveness without proving cognition |
| Anecdote | `weak/bidirectional` | Nootropics discussion is product-identity focused; herbalism reports can be calming or sedating |
| Safety / quality | `low-moderate caution` | Trial populations were screened; product part, extract type, dose, stimulation/sedation response, and stack context remain gating variables |

## Practical Interpretation

Wild green oat extract now clears a low-confidence standalone threshold because there are multiple product-specific human cognition and neurophysiology anchors. It should still sit below cleaner comparators in [[molecule-intake-triage-table]]. The most defensible route is acute attention / speed / stressor-state support, especially for defined Neuravena or Cognitaven products. Chronic daily use for durable cognitive enhancement remains unproven because the older 12-week cognition trial was null.

Future updates must keep these exposures separate: Neuravena, Cognitaven, generic green-oat extract, oat straw tea, milky oat tincture, oat seed extract, and ordinary oats. Do not merge this page with [[guarana]], [[sage-salvia-extract]], [[caffeine]], or [[cocoa-flavanols]] just because all can appear in plant-product cognition discussions.
