# Benzphetamine

Benzphetamine is a prescription sympathomimetic anorectic and stimulant-related weight-loss drug. In this wiki it is a risk-first comparator for [[phentermine]], [[diethylpropion]], [[phendimetrazine]], and broader "diet-pill focus" claims.

## Current Read

Medicinal cognition signal: `risk-first / absent`. The captured source packet is label-level anorectic safety and status, not a cognition-efficacy packet. No healthy-adult cognitive-enhancement trial or strong benzphetamine-specific brain-fog anecdote corpus is captured here.^[raw/articles/dailymed-2026-sympathomimetic-anorectic-labels.md]

The main reason to keep a standalone page is abuse and generalization control. The label cluster explicitly flags that amphetamines and related stimulant drugs have been extensively abused and says abuse potential should be considered before including benzphetamine in weight reduction.^[raw/articles/dailymed-2026-sympathomimetic-anorectic-labels.md]

## Label And Safety Layer

The shared anorectic-label packet for benzphetamine, phendimetrazine, and diethylpropion emphasizes monotherapy or avoidance of combinations with other anorectic products, primary pulmonary hypertension, valvular-heart-disease concern, cardiovascular stimulation, hypertension caution, alcohol / CNS-active-drug caution, abuse/dependence, tolerance, and withdrawal after prolonged high-dose use.^[raw/articles/dailymed-2026-sympathomimetic-anorectic-labels.md]

That safety layer dominates any informal focus claim. If benzphetamine appears in nootropic or brain-fog discussions, the first question should be whether the report is stimulant masking, appetite-state change, caloric restriction, sleep disruption, or dependence/tolerance dynamics.

## Interpretation Gates

- Do not treat benzphetamine as interchangeable with [[d-amphetamine]], [[methylphenidate]], [[lisdexamfetamine]], or [[phentermine]] for cognition.
- Do not infer nootropic value from weight loss, appetite suppression, mood lift, or drive.
- Watch BP/HR, insomnia, anxiety/agitation, tolerance, abuse/dependence, withdrawal, alcohol, MAOIs, other stimulants, cardiovascular history, pulmonary hypertension / valvular-disease concern, and product/status context.
