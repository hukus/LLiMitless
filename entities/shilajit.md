# Shilajit / Fulvic Acid Matrix

Shilajit is a traditional phytomineral exudate marketed for energy, stamina, testosterone, mitochondrial support, and cognitive clarity. It is not a single molecule: most claims involve a variable mineral-organic matrix, often discussed through fulvic acid, humic substances, minerals, and dibenzo-alpha-pyrone-like constituents.^[raw/articles/pubmed-22482077-shilajit-procognitive-review.md]^[raw/articles/bmc-2025-shilajit-inorganic-anions-variability.md]

## Bottom Line

Medicinal cognition signal: `preclinical/mechanistic-only for cognition; risk-first`. The main cognition-relevant formal source is a 2012 procognitive review focused on fulvic acid, tau self-aggregation, and Alzheimer's-disease hypotheses. The review itself calls for more basic and clinical investigation, and the captured human trials are endocrine or physical-fatigue studies, not memory/focus RCTs.^[raw/articles/pubmed-22482077-shilajit-procognitive-review.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-26395129-shilajit-testosterone-healthy-volunteers.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-6364418-shilajit-fatigue-strength-rct.md]

Anecdotal signal: `moderate-strong salience / low confidence`. The captured Reddit thread has clear brain-fog, focus, energy, and stamina claims, but it also has no-effect, chest pain, gas, aggression, heartburn, dose uncertainty, and heavy-metal / certificate-of-analysis concerns. Anecdote does not upgrade the medicinal cognition rating.^[raw/articles/reddit-2022-shilajit-brain-fog-thread.md]

Practical route: keep [[shilajit]] as a risk-first product-quality dossier below product-normalized botanicals such as [[polygala-bt11]], [[lemon-balm]], [[centella-asiatica]], and [[nigella-sativa]].

## Human Evidence

The best captured human evidence is not cognition-directed. A purified-Shilajit placebo-controlled trial in healthy men aged 45-55 used 250 mg twice daily for 90 days and reported increases in total testosterone, free testosterone, and DHEAS versus placebo. That supports an endocrine exposure lane, not a nootropic endpoint.^[raw/articles/pubmed-26395129-shilajit-testosterone-healthy-volunteers.md]

An 8-week study in 63 active men tested 250 or 500 mg/day Shilajit versus placebo. The main signal was physical: high-dose Shilajit helped preserve maximal voluntary isometric contraction after fatigue. This may explain stamina/energy anecdotes better than it explains direct cognition.^[raw/articles/pmc-6364418-shilajit-fatigue-strength-rct.md]

No captured human source provides a clean randomized memory, attention, executive-function, or brain-fog endpoint. Until that changes, the medicinal cognition rating stays below low-confidence standardized botanicals that at least have direct cognitive tests.

## Product Identity And Contamination Gate

The product-identity problem is central. A 2025 BMC Chemistry anion study shows source variability across Himalayan Shilajit, and the 2025 thallium study found commercial supplements with higher thallium than raw samples. That means "Shilajit" claims are not transportable unless the product, extraction, standardization, and contaminant testing are named.^[raw/articles/bmc-2025-shilajit-inorganic-anions-variability.md]^[raw/articles/bmc-2025-shilajit-thallium-supplement-risk.md]

FDA's general supplement page is the regulatory floor: dietary supplements are not pre-approved for safety and effectiveness. FDA's 2025 Ayurvedic heavy-metal warning and its 2021 fulvic-product lead/arsenic warning are not Shilajit efficacy evidence, but they are directly relevant to mineral/fulvic product safety assumptions.^[raw/articles/fda-dietary-supplement-consumer-safety.md]^[raw/articles/fda-2025-ayurvedic-heavy-metal-poisoning-warning.md]^[raw/articles/fda-2021-fulvic-care-lead-arsenic-warning.md]

Minimum interpretability gate: batch COA for lead, arsenic, mercury, cadmium, and ideally thallium; product identity; dose mass; fulvic/humic or marker reporting; third-party lab name; and country/source. Without those, subjective effects and adverse effects are hard to classify.

## Safety

Contamination is the largest practical safety issue, but not the only one. The captured anaphylaxis case report describes a 52-year-old woman with urticaria, dizziness, nausea, sweating, abdominal pain, and loss of consciousness after Shilajit ingestion. This is a case report, not a rate estimate, but it prevents a benign-natural-product assumption.^[raw/articles/pubmed-30863761-shilajit-anaphylaxis-case.md]

Because Shilajit is often stacked with testosterone, workout, mitochondrial, and mineral supplements, adverse-event attribution can be poor. Endocrine claims should stay separate from cognition claims.

## Signal Judgment

Shilajit deserves standalone coverage because it is common in cognitive-improvement communities and has enough human exposure, mechanistic, analytical, regulatory, and anecdotal sources to prevent a shallow watchlist entry. It does not deserve an efficacy upgrade: cognition remains hypothesis-level, and product-quality risk comes before dose or ranking.
