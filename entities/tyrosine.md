# Tyrosine

Tyrosine is a catecholamine precursor: it sits upstream of dopamine and norepinephrine and is often marketed as L-tyrosine or N-acetyl-L-tyrosine. For cognitive improvement, its best-supported route is acute state support under stress, cold, sleep loss, noise, or high working-memory demand, not daily rested-baseline enhancement.

Medicinal signal: `low-moderate` for stress-state cognition. A 2015 review concluded that tyrosine can enhance cognition particularly in short-term stressful or cognitively demanding situations when dopamine / norepinephrine function is intact but temporarily depleted. A separate military rapid evidence assessment found 10 RCTs and 4 controlled clinical trials, with a weak recommendation in favor of cognitive-stress benefit but insufficient evidence for confident broad recommendations.^[raw/articles/pubmed-26424423-tyrosine-stress-cognition-review.md]^[raw/articles/milmed-2015-tyrosine-stress-performance-rapid-assessment.md]

Direct acute trial signal: `low`. In a 16-person healthy-young-adult trial under 90 dB noise stress, 100 mg/kg tyrosine improved two stress-sensitive cognitive tasks one hour after dosing. This is the right use-case shape: short-term stress rescue, high dose, and task specificity.^[raw/articles/pubmed-8293316-tyrosine-cognition-stress.md]

Phenotype signal: `low`. A 2020 healthy-adult study found baseline-dependent effects on working-memory gating but not updating, reinforcing the wiki's rule that tyrosine response depends on state and baseline cognitive-control profile.^[raw/articles/pmc-7266860-tyrosine-working-memory-gating.md]

Anecdote signal: `moderate`. Forum reports often describe acute energy, focus, stress resilience, or stimulant-smoothing effects, but adverse/tolerance threads also report insomnia, irritability, hyperarousal, and thyroid-like concerns. Treat daily long-term use as a separate risk hypothesis, not a continuation of the acute-stress literature.^[raw/articles/reddit-2021-l-tyrosine-report-thread.md]^[raw/articles/reddit-2022-l-tyrosine-adverse-long-term-thread.md]

## Signal Split

| Layer | Signal | Readout |
|---|---|---|
| Acute stress / cognitive demand | `low-moderate` medicinal | Review and rapid assessment support stress-state benefit, with weak recommendation strength |
| Direct noise-stress trial | `low` medicinal | Small healthy-adult trial, 100 mg/kg, task-specific improvement |
| Rested healthy baseline | `weak/unclear` | Evidence does not support universal daily enhancement |
| Working-memory control phenotype | `low` mechanistic | Baseline-dependent gating but not updating signal |
| Anecdote | `moderate` | Acute focus/energy reports plus long-term adverse/tolerance reports |
| Safety / stop-rule | `moderate caution` | Watch insomnia, palpitations, irritability, heat intolerance, medication interactions, thyroid-like symptoms, and stimulant/caffeine stacking |

## Practical Interpretation

Tyrosine should be ranked as a state-challenge tool beside [[state-challenge-matrix-for-cognition-interventions]] and [[amino-acid-mitochondrial-stack-watchlist]]. It is more plausible before an acute stressor than as a continuous productivity supplement. The right research question is not "does tyrosine make people smarter?" but "which depleted-catecholamine states and baseline working-memory phenotypes respond without overstimulation?"
