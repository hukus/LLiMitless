# Leucovorin, cerebral folate deficiency, FRAA, and autism review

- Source type: PubMed abstract / systematic review and meta-analysis
- PMID: 34834493
- Journal: Journal of Personalized Medicine
- Published: 2021-11-03
- Checked: 2026-05-16 via PubMed E-utilities

## Summary

Rossignol and Frye reviewed cerebral folate deficiency, folate-receptor-alpha autoantibodies, and d,l-leucovorin / folinic acid treatment in autism spectrum disorder. The abstract reports a pooled prevalence of ASD in people with cerebral folate deficiency of 44%, cerebral folate deficiency in ASD of 38% with heterogeneity, and folate-receptor-alpha autoantibodies in ASD of 71%.

For individuals with ASD and cerebral folate deficiency, the abstract reports improvements with d,l-leucovorin in overall ASD symptoms and multiple neurological or behavioral domains. It states that 21 studies treated individuals with ASD using d,l-leucovorin, including four placebo-controlled studies and three prospective controlled studies. The authors concluded that leucovorin significantly improved communication with medium-to-large effect sizes in individual studies.

Adverse effects across studies were generally mild in the abstract, with reported aggression, excitement/agitation, headache, insomnia, and increased tantrums.

## Interpretation Boundaries

The review supports keeping folinic acid visible as a pediatric ASD / cerebral-folate-deficiency / folate-receptor-autoantibody branch. It does not provide healthy-adult cognition evidence.

The review also needs conflict-aware handling. PubMed lists research funding for one author to study leucovorin in ASD and an uncompensated scientific-advisory-board relationship to a commercial lab performing folate-receptor-alpha autoantibody tests. The review is useful as a map, but confidence should not rest on it alone.
