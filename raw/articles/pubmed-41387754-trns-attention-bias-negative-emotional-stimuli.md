# Reducing attention bias toward negative emotional stimuli with transcranial random noise stimulation: a randomized, double-blind, sham-controlled, crossover study

- PMID: 41387754
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41387754/
- Ingested: 2026-04-30

## Summary

Randomized, double-blind, crossover, sham-controlled study in healthy right-handed men with moderate or high anxiety. The final analyzed sample was 29. Left DLPFC tRNS reduced attention bias toward negative emotional stimuli relative to sham, with corrected p = 0.047 and Cohen's d = 0.72. There was no significant difference between tRNS and tDCS, and baseline-bias correlations did not survive permutation testing.

## Notes

This is a genuine positive tRNS result, but it is an emotion-attention phenotype rather than a general cognition enhancement signal. It is most relevant as a comparator for anxiety-linked attention bias and left-DLPFC targeting.
