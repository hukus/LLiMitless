# FDA: How much caffeine is too much?

- Source type: FDA consumer safety update.
- Date checked: 2026-05-03.

The FDA states that for most adults, 400 mg caffeine per day is an amount not generally associated with negative effects, but sensitivity varies by body weight, medications, medical conditions, pregnancy/lactation status, and metabolism.

FDA lists potential excessive-caffeine symptoms including increased heart rate, heart palpitations, high blood pressure, insomnia or sleep disruptions, anxiety, jitters, upset stomach, nausea, and headache. It also notes that energy drinks may contain caffeine from sources such as guarana and that labels may list total caffeine from all sources.

The FDA separately warns against pure and highly concentrated caffeine products because rapid consumption around 1,200 mg can produce toxic effects such as seizures, and pure caffeine powders/liquids can be hard to measure safely.

Use in the wiki: caffeine-safety governor for guarana, energy drinks, and multiingredient stimulant products.
