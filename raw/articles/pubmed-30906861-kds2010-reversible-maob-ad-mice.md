# KDS2010 reversible MAO-B inhibitor in Alzheimer's disease mouse model

- PMID: 30906861
- PMCID: PMC6426469
- DOI: 10.1126/sciadv.aav0316
- Journal: Science Advances (2019)

## Captured takeaways

- The paper frames MAO-B as an Alzheimer's disease target via astrocytic GABA in reactive astrocytes.
- It argues that irreversible MAO-B inhibitors such as selegiline produced short-term cognition effects but disappointing long-term clinical results.
- In APP/PS1 mice, prolonged selegiline did not reduce astrocytic GABA or rescue memory because of compensatory D-amino acid oxidase activity.
- KDS2010 was described as a potent, selective, reversible MAO-B inhibitor with IC50 7.6 nM and 12,500-fold selectivity over MAO-A.
- Long-term KDS2010 treatment reduced astrocytic GABA and astrogliosis, enhanced synaptic transmission, and rescued learning and memory impairments in APP/PS1 mice.
- Interpretation for the wiki: KDS2010/tisolagiline is mechanistically interesting but remains preclinical for cognition until human trial results appear.
