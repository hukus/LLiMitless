# Methylcobalamin plus folic acid in older MCI with elevated homocysteine

- Source type: PubMed abstract / randomized placebo-controlled trial
- PMID: 31787369
- Journal: Clinical Nutrition
- Published online: 2019-11-20; issue date 2020-08
- Checked: 2026-05-16 via PubMed E-utilities

## Summary

Kwok et al. randomized 279 older outpatients with mild cognitive impairment and serum homocysteine at least 10.0 micromol/L to methylcobalamin 500 mcg plus folic acid 400 mcg daily or placebo for 24 months. The supplement lowered homocysteine substantially, from a mean of 13.9 micromol/L at baseline to 9.3 micromol/L at month 24. At 24 months, there was no significant group difference in the primary outcome, CDR sum of boxes, or secondary cognitive outcomes. At 12 months, executive function and depressive symptoms improved in the supplement group, but these effects were not significant at 24 months; aspirin use showed a negative interaction with B supplementation for cognitive functioning.

## Use In Wiki

- Directly relevant to methylcobalamin plus folic acid in an MCI / high-homocysteine state.
- Supports the status-gated interpretation: biomarker movement does not guarantee durable cognitive improvement.
- Useful counterweight to methyl-B12 brain-fog anecdotes and to broad B-vitamin nootropic claims.

## Caveats

- Combination trial; it does not isolate methylcobalamin from folic acid.
- Older MCI plus elevated-homocysteine population limits transfer to healthy adults.
- The 12-month executive-function and mood hints were not durable at 24 months.
