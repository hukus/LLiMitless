# FDA 2026 reminder on NAD+ ingredients for sterile compounding

FDA reminded compounders to produce sterile drugs only from components suitable for sterile compounding. The agency said it was aware of compounders using food-grade nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) sold by repackagers to make intravenous products.

FDA stated that food-grade ingredients are not suitable for sterile-drug compounding without appropriate processing because of contamination risk from microbes and endotoxins. The agency reported adverse-event reports after NAD+ injectable drugs, including severe chills, shaking, vomiting, and fatigue, with some cases requiring medical treatment. FDA said these reactions were consistent with excessive endotoxin levels.

Interpretation for the wiki: direct NAD+ injection and infusion claims require a sterile-compounding and ingredient-suitability gate. Product route, supplier, certificate of analysis, sterile processing, clinic protocol, and adverse-event reporting are part of the evidence packet, not administrative details.
