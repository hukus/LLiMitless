# Effects of Transcranial Pulsed Electromagnetic Field Stimulation on Quality of Life in Parkinson's Disease

Source type: PubMed-indexed randomized controlled trial.

Core design:
- Published 2018 in European Journal of Neurology.
- N=97 participants with idiopathic Parkinson's disease.
- Double-blind study randomized active T-PEMF (n=49) versus placebo treatment (n=48).
- Home treatment: one daily 30-minute session for eight consecutive weeks.
- Main endpoint was PDQ-39 quality of life.
- Adverse events and compliance were tracked.

Main findings:
- Active T-PEMF improved clinical-effect-size readouts for mobility and activities of daily living compared with placebo.
- No between-group differences were found for the remaining PDQ-39 dimensions.
- No between-group difference in adverse events was found.
- Compliance was 97.9%.

Wiki takeaway:
- Disease-context home T-PEMF can be studied with placebo control and adverse-event tracking.
- This is not a healthy-adult cognition or brain-fog enhancement result.
- It helps define practical protocol and safety-monitoring expectations for clinical T-PEMF studies.

Limitations:
- Parkinson's disease motor/quality-of-life context, not healthy cognition.
- Cognitive endpoints were not the central claim.
- Home use was within a formal clinical-trial device protocol, not consumer self-experimentation.
