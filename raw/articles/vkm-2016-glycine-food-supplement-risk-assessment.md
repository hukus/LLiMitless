# VKM 2016 glycine food-supplement risk assessment note

The Norwegian Scientific Committee for Food Safety (VKM) assessed glycine as an "other substance" in food supplements for the Norwegian Food Safety Authority. The 2016 report characterized risk for specified supplemental doses of 20, 50, 100, 300, 500, and 650 mg/day.

VKM concluded that those specified supplemental doses were unlikely to cause adverse health effects in adults, adolescents, and children aged 10 to under 14. Children under 10 were outside scope. The comparison value used in the report was 20 mg/kg/day, equivalent to 1.4 g/day for a 70 kg adult, derived from a rat NOAEL with uncertainty factor.

The report also notes that glycine is synthesized endogenously, is a constituent of proteins, functions as a neurotransmitter, and can have both stimulatory and depressant roles in the brain. Mean dietary glycine intake from NHANES III was cited around 3.2 g/day, with higher intakes in young men.

Wiki use: this is a safety-context anchor, but it should not be overextended. The assessed supplement doses were lower than the 3 g bedtime doses in sleep studies and far lower than acute 0.8 g/kg high-dose cognition testing. Practical interpretation should track dose, timing, sedation/grogginess, renal/hepatic context, sleep state, psychiatric activation, and product-form confusion.
