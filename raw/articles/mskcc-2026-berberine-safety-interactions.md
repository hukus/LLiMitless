# MSKCC 2026 berberine safety and interaction note

Memorial Sloan Kettering's About Herbs monograph treats berberine as an alkaloid constituent found in several plant species and marketed for glucose and lipid metabolism. Its consumer and professional sections describe limited human evidence for cholesterol, triglyceride, and blood-sugar effects, but also emphasize that better trials are needed and that lifestyle or appropriate medical therapy should not be replaced.

The monograph lists mild adverse reactions such as appetite loss/anorexia, upset stomach, diarrhea, constipation, and rash. It also flags interaction concerns for bosutinib, tacrolimus, cyclosporin, sulfonylureas, and drugs metabolized by CYP2D6, CYP2C9, or CYP3A4. Pregnancy and breastfeeding are cautioned against because infant jaundice-related concerns are listed.

Interpretation for the wiki: berberine is not a low-friction "ordinary supplement" for cognition self-experiments. It has glucose/lipid plausibility, but safety routing needs medication review, pregnancy/lactation exclusion, diabetes-drug/hypoglycemia caution, CYP substrate awareness, GI tolerance, and transplant/immunosuppressant caution.
