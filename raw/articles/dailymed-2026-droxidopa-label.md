# DailyMed droxidopa label

## Captured takeaways

- DailyMed labels droxidopa for orthostatic dizziness, lightheadedness, or near-blackout feelings in adult symptomatic neurogenic orthostatic hypotension.
- The label states that effectiveness beyond 2 weeks has not been established and that continued effectiveness should be assessed periodically.
- Droxidopa carries a boxed warning for supine hypertension, requiring supine blood-pressure monitoring before and during treatment and more often during dose increases.
- The label recommends the last daily dose at least 3 hours before bedtime and head-of-bed elevation to reduce supine-hypertension risk.
- Warnings include potential worsening of ischemic heart disease, arrhythmias, and congestive heart failure, plus postmarketing hyperpyrexia/confusion resembling neuroleptic malignant syndrome during dosage changes or levodopa reduction.
- Common adverse reactions in trials included headache, dizziness, nausea, and hypertension.
- Interpretation for the wiki: droxidopa is a pressor/norepinephrine-prodrug comparator for severe orthostatic physiology, not a cognition treatment. Brain-fog relevance is indirect and safety-gated.
