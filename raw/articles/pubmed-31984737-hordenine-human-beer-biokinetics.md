# Hordenine human absorption, biokinetics, and metabolism after beer

2020 Journal of Agricultural and Food Chemistry pilot study in four volunteers consuming beer containing hordenine at 0.075 mg/kg body weight. The paper frames hordenine as a natural barley constituent and biased dopamine D2 receptor agonist, then measures plasma and urine kinetics.

Free hordenine peaked at low nanomolar plasma concentrations after 0-60 minutes. The authors report plasma half-lives of about 53-66 minutes for free hordenine and conclude that observed plasma levels seemed too low to provoke direct D2 receptor interaction related to food reward, though additive or synergistic effects with alcohol or N-methyltyramine could occur.

Use in the wiki: human pharmacokinetic counterweight for hordenine hype. It supports biological exposure, but not strong direct central dopaminergic/nootropic action from ordinary dietary levels.
