# DailyMed rasagiline tablet label

- Label source: DailyMed.
- Label update shown on page: 2025-11-14.
- Initial U.S. approval: 2006.
- Product class: monoamine oxidase B inhibitor / MAOI.

## Captured takeaways

- Rasagiline tablets are indicated for treatment of Parkinson disease.
- Dosing in the label is 1 mg once daily as monotherapy or adjunct without levodopa; adjunct to levodopa starts at 0.5 mg once daily and may increase to 1 mg daily.
- Patients using ciprofloxacin or other CYP1A2 inhibitors should not exceed 0.5 mg once daily.
- Mild hepatic impairment caps dosing at 0.5 mg once daily; moderate or severe hepatic impairment is not recommended / should not use.
- Contraindications include meperidine, tramadol, methadone, propoxyphene, dextromethorphan, St. John's wort, cyclobenzaprine, and other selective or nonselective MAO inhibitors.
- Key warnings include hypertension, serotonin syndrome with antidepressants, falling asleep during activities of daily living, hypotension/orthostatic hypotension, dyskinesia, hallucinations or psychotic-like behavior, impulse-control/compulsive behaviors, and withdrawal-emergent hyperpyrexia/confusion.
- DailyMed warns not to exceed recommended doses because of hypertension risk and states that very high-tyramine foods can cause severe hypertension even though ordinary dietary restriction is not framed the same way as nonselective MAOIs.
- Interpretation for the wiki: rasagiline is a prescription Parkinson drug and cannot be treated like a casual dopamine nootropic, even when cognition/anhedonia forums frame it as a clean MAO-B option.
