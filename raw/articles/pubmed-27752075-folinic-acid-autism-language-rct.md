# Folinic acid in children with autism and language impairment

- Source type: PubMed abstract / randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial
- PMID: 27752075
- Journal: Molecular Psychiatry
- Published online: 2016-10-18; issue date 2018-02
- Trial registration: NCT01602016
- Checked: 2026-05-16 via PubMed E-utilities

## Summary

Frye et al. randomized 48 children with non-syndromic autism spectrum disorder and language impairment to high-dose folinic acid or placebo for 12 weeks. The folinic-acid dose was 2 mg/kg/day, capped at 50 mg/day. Participants were also subtyped by glutathione and folate-receptor-alpha autoantibody status.

The abstract reports greater improvement in verbal communication in the folinic-acid group than placebo, with a standardized-point effect of 5.7 and Cohen's d of 0.70. Folate-receptor-alpha autoantibody positivity predicted larger response; the reported effect in that subgroup was 7.3 standardized points and Cohen's d of 0.91. The abstract also reports improvements in Vineland, ABC, ASQ, and BASC subscales and no significant adverse-effect difference between groups.

## Interpretation Boundaries

This is pediatric, autism/language-impairment evidence, not a healthy-adult cognition trial. It supports a phenotype-gated CNS folate-transport hypothesis, especially when folate-receptor-alpha autoantibodies are present. It does not prove broad nootropic value.

The PubMed record includes conflict-of-interest language: two authors were inventors on a patent related to detecting the autoantibodies, and two authors had scientific-advisory-board ties to a company involved in the testing ecosystem. This does not negate the result, but it keeps the branch sponsor/conflict-aware.
