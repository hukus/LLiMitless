# Oxytocin, vasopressin, and their crosstalk in sexually-dimorphic psychiatric conditions

- PMID: 41862144
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41862144/
- Publication date: 2026-06

## Summary

Review of oxytocin and vasopressin crosstalk in sexually-dimorphic psychiatric conditions. The title reinforces the branch's context-dependence framing: sex, diagnosis, and social phenotype likely matter more than any broad nootropic interpretation.

## Notes

This is a mechanistic and translational review, not human efficacy evidence for cognition. It is still useful because it keeps the branch anchored to sex-specific social-behavior biology rather than broad enhancer claims.
