# Drug Holidays May Attenuate Beneficial Effects of Treatment on Emotion Regulation and Recognition Among Children with ADHD: A Single-Center, Prospective Study

- PMID: 39629771
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39629771/
- Ingested: 2026-04-29

## Summary

Single-center naturalistic prospective cohort study in children with ADHD treated with methylphenidate. Outcomes were assessed pre-treatment, after treatment, and after an 8-week summer drug holiday.

MPH reduced irritability, improved clinician-rated severity, improved reading-the-mind-in-the-eyes performance, and increased planning as a cognitive emotion-regulation strategy. After the holiday, irritability returned to baseline and the planning boost disappeared, while RMET performance stayed improved.

## Notes

- This is one of the few recent human sources directly showing that a stimulant holiday can attenuate some treatment gains.
- It supports a narrower view of cycling: holidays may be useful for adverse effects or growth concerns, but they are not automatically an enhancement strategy.
