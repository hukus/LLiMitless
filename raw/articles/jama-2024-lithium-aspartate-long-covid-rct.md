# Lithium aspartate for long-COVID fatigue and cognitive dysfunction RCT

2024 JAMA Network Open randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of lithium aspartate for neurologic post-COVID condition fatigue and cognitive dysfunction.

The trial enrolled 52 participants with bothersome fatigue or cognitive dysfunction after COVID-19. Participants received lithium aspartate 10-15 mg/day or placebo for three weeks, followed by open-label lithium. The main outcome combined Fatigue Severity Scale-7 and Brain Fog Severity Scale changes. The low-dose randomized phase showed no significant intergroup benefit on the primary outcome or secondary cognitive/symptom measures.

The same report also included a small open-label dose-finding extension. Among three completers, lithium aspartate 40-45 mg/day was associated with numerically larger fatigue and cognitive-dysfunction score reductions than 15 mg/day in two people with higher measured serum lithium levels. This is hypothesis-generating only because it was open-label and very small.

Interpretation for the wiki: lithium aspartate is a useful example where strong initial clinician anecdote led to a controlled test, and the low-dose supplement-like regimen was negative. Higher-dose signals should remain RCT-watch and medically supervised because lithium salt identity, elemental dose, serum level, renal/thyroid monitoring, and interactions matter.
