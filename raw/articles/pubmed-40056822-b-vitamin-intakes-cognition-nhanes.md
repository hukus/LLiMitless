# B-vitamin intakes and cognition in NHANES older adults

PubMed 40056822: quantile g-computation analysis of B-vitamin intakes and cognitive function in older adults in the United States.

Key capture:

- Population: 2,716 adults aged 60 years or older in NHANES 2011-2014.
- Exposure: dietary intake mixture of B vitamins.
- Main result shape: higher combined B-vitamin intake was associated with lower odds of low cognitive performance, and niacin had the largest model weight among the B vitamins in the reported mixture analysis.
- Reported niacin-specific association: the highest niacin-intake quartile was associated with lower odds of cognitive-function deficiency versus the lowest quartile in adjusted analysis.
- This is still cross-sectional nutrition epidemiology. It does not isolate supplemental niacin, nicotinamide, NAD precursors, SAMe-adjacent methylation effects, or a causal cognition mechanism.

Interpretation for the wiki:

This source reinforces dietary/status visibility for [[niacin-nicotinamide-b3]] and the broader [[nutrient-supplement-cognition-signal-map]]. It should not upgrade B3 above low confidence, because dietary B-vitamin intake can be a proxy for many non-B3 variables.
