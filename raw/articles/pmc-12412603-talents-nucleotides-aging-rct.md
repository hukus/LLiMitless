# TALENTs Nucleotides Anti-Aging RCT

- Article: Nucleotides as an Anti-Aging Supplementation in Older Adults: A Randomized Controlled Trial (TALENTs study).
- Journal: Advanced Science, 2025.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202417728
- PMCID: PMC12412603.
- PMID: 40433895.
- Registry: ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05243108.
- Date checked: 2026-05-03.

## Summary

The TALENTs study is an adjacent randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled older-adult nucleotide trial, not the same cognition-focused RiboDiet / RiboCARE Neuro study. It enrolled community-living adults aged 60-70 years in Chengdu, China. Participants were assigned to exogenous nucleotides or placebo for 19 weeks; the intervention was 1.2 g/day nucleotides in capsule form.

Primary outcomes were biological-aging markers rather than cognitive performance: median DNA methylation age and leukocyte telomere length. The trial reported a greater reduction in median DNA methylation age versus placebo after 19 weeks, no significant telomere-length difference, and improved insulin sensitivity by HOMA-IR. The abstract reports no severe adverse events or significant safety-indicator changes.

The methods note that the nucleotide composition was modeled after human breast milk and used 5'-AMP, 5'-CMP, 5'-GMPNa2, and 5'-UMPNa2 in a specified ratio. The paper also lists broad geriatric assessment and questionnaire outcomes, including MoCA, PSQI, fatigue, and psychological-distress measures, but the captured public abstract and summary emphasize epigenetic age, insulin sensitivity, body composition, and safety rather than a clear cognition efficacy claim.

Safety interpretation remains cautious. The full text discusses routine blood, liver, kidney, and tumor-marker monitoring, and notes no serious adverse events. It also notes a transient uric-acid increase at the week-11 midpoint that returned to baseline by week 19, so purine / uric-acid context should be monitored rather than dismissed.
