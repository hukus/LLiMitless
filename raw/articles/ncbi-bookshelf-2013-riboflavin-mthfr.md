## Summary

The chapter frames methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase (MTHFR) as a flavoenzyme that catalyzes conversion of 5,10-methylenetetrahydrofolate to 5-methyltetrahydrofolate, a methyl donor used to convert homocysteine to methionine.

Riboflavin is transformed into FMN and FAD. The chapter's figure connects FMN/FAD with folate, B12, and vitamin B6 enzymes involved in homocysteine remethylation and transsulfuration.

The chapter notes that the common MTHFR 677C-to-T variant reduces enzyme activity and that the mutant enzyme has lower affinity for its flavin cofactor. It also summarizes evidence that plasma homocysteine is inversely related to riboflavin in people carrying the T allele.
