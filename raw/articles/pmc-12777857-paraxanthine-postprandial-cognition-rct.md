# Acute paraxanthine and postprandial cognitive performance

2025 open-access randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover study of paraxanthine in 24 young healthy adults. Participants received 200 mg or 300 mg paraxanthine or placebo after an overnight fast and 30 minutes before a mixed meal, with subjective energy/focus/productivity/satiety and cognitive tests including N-back, Go/No-Go, and Serial 7s.

The abstract frames paraxanthine as a caffeine metabolite and tests whether it can improve energy, focus, satiety, and postprandial cognition in the fed state. The paper is directly relevant to the stimulant-adjacent branch, but it is still small, acute, product/context specific, and industry-linked.

Use in the wiki: direct human cognition and subjective-energy source for paraxanthine, especially postprandial context. It should be kept separate from caffeine and from multi-ingredient energy drinks.
