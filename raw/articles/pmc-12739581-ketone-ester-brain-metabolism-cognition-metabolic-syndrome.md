# Effects of oral Ketone Ester on brain metabolism and cognition: a randomized controlled trial

- PMCID: PMC12739581
- DOI: 10.1002/alz70859_097825
- Published: December 2025
- Source type: Alzheimer's & Dementia supplement abstract / randomized controlled trial report

## Summary

Fifty cognitively intact participants over age 55 with metabolic syndrome were randomized 1:1 to receive either 25 g oral ketone ester or isocaloric dextrose placebo three times daily for four weeks. The primary outcome was brain beta-hydroxybutyrate by magnetic resonance spectroscopy. Secondary outcomes included other brain metabolites, Preclinical Alzheimer Cognitive Composite scores, and NIH Toolbox performance.

Ketone ester increased brain beta-hydroxybutyrate, reduced brain glutamate, and increased serum and urine beta-hydroxybutyrate, confirming target engagement. Logical memory improved in both groups, consistent with practice effects, but trends favored ketone ester; within-group improvements in delayed verbal recall and digit-symbol substitution were reported only with ketone ester. Mild gastrointestinal adverse events were the only noted harms.

## Interpretation

This is proof-of-concept target-engagement evidence in a metabolic-syndrome phenotype, not a sleep-loss study and not a durable efficacy readout.
