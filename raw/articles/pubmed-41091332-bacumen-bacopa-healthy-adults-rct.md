# Bacumen Bacopa healthy-adult cognition stress fatigue RCT

2025 Clinical Drug Investigation randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial of Bacumen, a Bacopa monnieri extract, in adults with self-reported memory and attention problems.

The study randomized 101 volunteers aged 40-70 to Bacumen 300 mg/day or placebo for 12 weeks; 87 completed the trial. Primary cognitive outcomes covered verbal learning, attention, and working memory. The abstract reports no between-group differences for those primary cognitive outcomes: verbal learning p = 0.391, attention p = 0.713, and working memory p = 0.610.

Secondary outcomes favored Bacopa for overall self-reported stress reactivity, plus fatigue and stress after a cognitively demanding computer task. Blood BDNF, malondialdehyde, and acetylcholinesterase activity did not differ between groups. Supplementation was generally tolerated, but adverse reactions were more frequent in the Bacopa group, mainly digestive complaints and headaches.

Interpretation for the wiki: this is an important freshness update because it weakens broad healthy-adult cognition claims for Bacopa while preserving a possible stress/fatigue-state benefit. It should not erase older memory-positive trials, but it should prevent the Bacopa branch from being upgraded as a general nootropic.
