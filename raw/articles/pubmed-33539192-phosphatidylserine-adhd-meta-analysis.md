# Phosphatidylserine for pediatric ADHD: systematic review and meta-analysis

## Bibliographic snapshot

- PubMed record for Bruton et al., Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine, 2021.
- PubMed ID: 33539192.
- PMCID: PMC9208377.
- DOI: 10.1089/acm.2020.0432.

## Study design

- Systematic review and meta-analysis of phosphatidylserine supplementation for pediatric ADHD symptoms.
- Databases searched: Medline, Cochrane Library, and ClinicalTrials.gov through August 2020.
- Included studies: four in narrative review with n = 344; three randomized clinical trials in meta-analysis with n = 216.

## Results captured

- Dose range in the meta-analysis: 200-300 mg/day phosphatidylserine.
- Inattention improved versus placebo: effect size 0.36, 95% CI 0.07 to 0.64, p = 0.01.
- Overall ADHD symptoms were not statistically significant: effect size 0.76, 95% CI -0.07 to 1.60, p = 0.07.
- Hyperactivity / impulsivity was not statistically significant: effect size 0.24, 95% CI -0.04 to 0.53, p = 0.09.
- Authors concluded preliminary evidence suggests PS may reduce inattention in children with ADHD, but evidence quality is low and more research is needed.

## Wiki interpretation

- This supports an attention/inattention hypothesis, but it is pediatric ADHD evidence, not healthy-adult nootropic proof.
- It helps explain why ADHD-focused anecdotes exist.
- It should be routed as phenotype-bound and low confidence, with age/population and endpoint boundaries explicit.
