# LiverTox: Black Cumin Seed

NCBI Bookshelf LiverTox monograph, updated 2023-04-27, Bookshelf ID NBK591552, PMID 37184195.

Identity: black cumin is also called black seed and Nigella sativa. LiverTox lists other names including black caraway, small fennel, fennel flower, Roman coriander, Nigella, and Nigella sativa. Thymoquinone is identified as a volatile-oil constituent and suspected major bioactive component.

Clinical/safety summary: LiverTox says black cumin is well tolerated, generally recognized as safe as a spice, and has not been associated with serum enzyme elevations during therapy or implicated in clinically apparent liver injury. It assigns likelihood score E for hepatotoxicity, meaning unlikely cause of clinically apparent liver injury.

Practical adverse effects: typical supplement doses are described as 300-1000 mg one to two times daily. Side effects can include abdominal discomfort, bloating, dysgeusia, diarrhea, and headache, generally transient and mild. Rare potentially severe events include rash and hypersensitivity reactions.

Regulatory/product interpretation: black cumin extracts are available as over-the-counter dietary supplements, but LiverTox notes that concentrated thymoquinone oils and black cumin extracts are not FDA-approved therapies for hyperlipidemia, diabetes, hypertension, or other medical conditions.
