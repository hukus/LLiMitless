# Acorus tatarinowii Schott: A review of its botany, traditional uses, phytochemistry, and pharmacology

- Published: 2023
- Evidence layer: review / safety and phytochemistry context

## Summary

This review covers Acorus tatarinowii, a rhizome used in traditional formulas such as Kai-Xin-San. It identifies alpha-asarone and beta-asarone as important volatile-oil constituents and flags safety uncertainty around asarone exposure. The review states that toxicological studies have reported hepatoma, mutagenic, genotoxic, and teratogenic concerns for alpha-asarone and beta-asarone, that beta-asarone may be more toxic than alpha-asarone, and that long-term intake of high-asarone Acorus products has been associated with persistent vomiting in human consumers.

The review also notes European limits for beta-asarone in food and beverages and calls for further dose-dependent in vivo safety work. For wiki purposes, this makes Acorus identity, extraction, beta-asarone content, and jurisdictional quality control central to any KXS-style claim.

## Wiki Use

- Use as a safety and product-identity gate for [[kai-xin-san]] and any Acorus-containing formula.
- Do not ignore beta-asarone risk when a product claims "traditional memory formula" status.
