# Heart rate variability coefficient of variation during sleep as a digital biomarker that reflects behavior and varies by age and sex

- PMID: 41309064
- Published: 2026-01-01 (epub 2025-11-27)
- Study type: large wearable-dataset biomarker analysis
- Key finding: nocturnal HRV-CV over 7 days was reliably estimated with at least five nights; higher HRV-CV was associated with greater alcohol consumption, lower physical activity, shorter and less consistent sleep, and higher BMI.
- Why it matters: HRV is not just recovery noise; it can act as a behavior-sensitive state marker that belongs in the scorecard as a covariate or enrichment screen.
