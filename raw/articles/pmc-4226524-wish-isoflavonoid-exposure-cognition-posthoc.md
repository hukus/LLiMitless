# WISH isoflavonoid exposure and cognition post hoc analysis

2014 post hoc analysis of the Women's Isoflavone Soy Health trial examined urinary and plasma isoflavonoid exposure in 300 healthy postmenopausal women over roughly 2.7 years. Change in overnight urinary isoflavonoid excretion was not significantly associated with change in global cognition, supporting the main WISH null result.

Secondary exposure analyses found an inverse association between increasing isoflavonoid exposure and a general-intelligence factor, while verbal and visual episodic-memory factors were not significantly associated. This is not proof of cognitive harm, but it is a source-integrity counterweight against treating higher isoflavone exposure as automatically better.
