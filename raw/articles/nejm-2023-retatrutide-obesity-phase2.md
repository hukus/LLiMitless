# NEJM 2023 retatrutide obesity Phase 2 trial

Primary publication: Triple-Hormone-Receptor Agonist Retatrutide for Obesity, published in the New England Journal of Medicine in 2023.

## Captured takeaways

- The Phase 2 trial enrolled adults with obesity or overweight and no type 2 diabetes, testing weekly retatrutide across multiple doses for 48 weeks.
- The primary and secondary efficacy readouts were weight and cardiometabolic outcomes, not cognitive outcomes.
- Weight loss was dose-dependent and reached about 24% at 48 weeks in the 12 mg group.
- Retatrutide was associated with improvements in cardiometabolic measures including blood pressure, HbA1c, fasting glucose, fasting insulin, and lipid measures.
- Safety was dominated by gastrointestinal events. These were mostly mild to moderate, more common at higher doses, concentrated during escalation, and partially mitigated by a lower starting dose.
- Fatigue appeared in the adverse-event table in the low single-digit to about 10% range across dose groups, with about 7% across retatrutide groups versus about 4% on placebo.
- No clinically significant hypoglycemia was reported in the obesity trial.
- Interpretation for the wiki: Phase 2 gives a formal fatigue-adverse-event anchor, but it does not provide a cognitive-efficacy signal.
