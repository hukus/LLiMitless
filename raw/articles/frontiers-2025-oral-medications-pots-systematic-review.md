# Frontiers 2025 oral medications for POTS systematic review

## Captured takeaways

- This systematic review searched the oral-medication POTS literature through 2024-04-06, including PASC-associated POTS.
- The review frames POTS as dysautonomia with symptoms including lightheadedness, tachycardia, fatigue, and cognitive impairment, while noting renewed interest after COVID-19.
- Thirty-two studies met inclusion criteria: randomized trials, observational studies, and systematic reviews.
- The most frequently studied medications were beta-blockers, ivabradine, and midodrine.
- In the review's synthesis, ivabradine and midodrine had the highest symptomatic-improvement rates, while beta-blockers had the largest heart-rate-variability reduction.
- The authors conclude that ivabradine, midodrine, and beta-blockers appear reasonable front-line pharmacologic choices, including for PASC-associated POTS, while calling for longer-term RCTs and subtype-specific evidence.
- The review also notes that no medication is FDA-approved specifically for POTS.
- Interpretation for the wiki: use this as the broad class router for autonomic brain-fog chemicals, but keep cognition confidence low because medication studies usually track symptoms and hemodynamics rather than objective cognition.
