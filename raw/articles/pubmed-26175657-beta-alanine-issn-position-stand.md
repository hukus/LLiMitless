# International Society of Sports Nutrition position stand: Beta-Alanine

- PMID: 26175657
- PMCID: PMC4501114
- DOI: 10.1186/s12970-015-0090-y
- Published: 2015-07-15
- Ingested: 2026-05-11

## Summary

ISSN position stand reviewing beta-alanine mechanisms, performance evidence, and safety. The position stand concludes that four weeks of 4-6 g/day beta-alanine increases muscle carnosine concentrations, acts as an intracellular pH buffer, and can improve short-duration high-intensity exercise performance.

For safety, beta-alanine appears safe in healthy populations at recommended doses, with paraesthesia / tingling as the main reported side effect. Divided lower doses or sustained-release formulations can reduce paraesthesia. The position stand says more research is needed on longer-duration endurance, strength, and broader health effects related to carnosine.

## Relevance

This is the main safety and dose-context source for [[beta-alanine]]. It supports ergogenic use and carnosine-loading logic, not direct cognitive enhancement.

## Caveats

- Sports-nutrition position stand, not a cognition-specific systematic review.
- Long-term safety and special-population data remain incomplete.
- Does not show that beta-alanine meaningfully raises human brain carnosine enough to improve cognition.
