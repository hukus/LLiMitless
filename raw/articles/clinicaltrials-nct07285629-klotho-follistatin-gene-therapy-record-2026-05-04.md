# NCT07285629 Klotho and follistatin gene therapy current registry snapshot

- Trial ID: NCT07285629
- Source: https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07285629
- Ingested: 2026-05-04

## Summary

Current ClinicalTrials.gov record for an early phase 1, open-label, single-group, non-placebo-controlled pilot study of nonviral plasmid-delivered Klotho plus follistatin gene therapy in healthy adults aged 50 to 80. Estimated enrollment is 30. The study start date is listed as 2025-12-16, with primary completion and study completion estimated for 2026-06.

The intervention is subcutaneous injection of plasmid-delivered follistatin and Klotho gene therapy into abdominal fat deposits. The registry states that the investigational product will be administered outside the United States at a site not under FDA jurisdiction, while the U.S. site performs non-treatment pre/post assessments such as cognitive assessments and blood collection.

Primary outcomes are serum alpha-Klotho concentration, serum follistatin concentration, and treatment-emergent adverse events assessed with PRO-CTCAE over pre-treatment to three-month post-treatment windows. Secondary outcomes include WHOQOL-BREF, NIH Toolbox-style cognitive tests for pattern comparison processing speed, picture sequence memory, flanker inhibitory control and attention, dimensional change card sort, and picture vocabulary, plus epigenetic age, physical performance, hs-CRP, calcium/phosphate/renal markers, PF4, fibrinogen, HbA1c, homocysteine, IGF-1, and CMP safety labs.

## Signal Use

- Medicinal signal: active translational lead only; no posted results and no placebo control.
- Cognition signal: secondary cognitive endpoints are specific enough for future surveillance, but the design cannot by itself establish efficacy.
- Safety/regulatory signal: high-friction intervention layer because this is a combination gene-therapy protocol, open-label, small, and partly outside U.S. FDA jurisdiction.
- Attribution caveat: Klotho and follistatin are combined, so any future outcome cannot be treated as Klotho-specific without additional evidence.
