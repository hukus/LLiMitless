# Severe Illness Associated with Eating Mushroom-Containing Chocolate Products - United States, January-October 2024

- Agency / publication: CDC MMWR
- Published: 2026-04-09
- Source type: outbreak investigation report
- URL: https://www.cdc.gov/mmwr/volumes/75/wr/mm7513a2.htm
- Ingested: 2026-05-03

## Summary

CDC MMWR report on severe illness associated with Diamond Shruumz and other mushroom-containing chocolate products in the United States during January-October 2024. The investigation identified 180 cases in 34 states. Among cases with available outcome data, 73 people were hospitalized, 38 required ICU admission, 29 required endotracheal intubation, and two deaths were reported in the MMWR summary.

Eating Diamond Shruumz chocolate bars was associated with higher odds of hospitalization, ICU admission, seizures, and intubation compared with other mushroom-containing chocolate products, and larger amounts eaten were associated with increased severe outcomes. Product testing identified varying substances, including muscimol, psilocin, kavalactones, acetylpsilocin, pregabalin, and ibotenic acid in a raw ingredient.

## Notes

- This is the strongest current public-health evidence for the product-adulteration and dose-ambiguity risk around "mushroom microdosing" edibles.
- Muscimol was present in some products, but not all symptoms were explainable by muscimol alone.
- Use this source to avoid treating Amanita/muscimol product anecdotes as clean single-molecule evidence.
