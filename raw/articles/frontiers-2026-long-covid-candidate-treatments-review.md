# Long-COVID candidate treatments expert and patient priority review

2026 Frontiers in Medicine narrative review of candidate long-COVID treatments prioritized by people with lived experience and clinicians. It summarizes evidence quality, possible mechanisms, safety, feasibility, and acceptability across treatments.

The review is useful for this wiki because it explicitly grades several high-salience chemical candidates as low or very-low evidence. In its summary table, low-dose naltrexone had low-quality non-randomized evidence with possible small benefit; antihistamines had very-low-quality evidence; nicotine had very-low-quality evidence from one four-person case series and unclear efficacy; guanfacine had very-low-quality evidence from one case series; nattokinase and metformin had no treatment-effect studies in the reviewed set.

The nicotine section states that no robust long-COVID nicotine clinical trials had reported efficacy and that the published evidence was limited to the small case study using 7.5 mg/day patches. The same review highlights rare serious adverse events and dependency/side-effect concerns for nicotine, while describing it as relatively inexpensive and accessible.

Interpretation for the wiki: this source is a current external evidence-quality cross-check for the post-viral brain-fog watchlist. It helps prevent community-salient ideas from being upgraded past their evidence tier.
