# Springer 2023 berberine schizophrenia cognition RCT note

This Chinese Medicine article reports a 3-month randomized clinical trial of berberine versus placebo in adults with chronic schizophrenia. The abstract describes per-protocol analysis of 106 patients, with lower scores on negative-symptom and trail-making measures in the berberine group by month 3, along with reductions in inflammatory markers including IL-1 beta, IL-6, and TNF-alpha.

The study is useful because it includes cognitive-function measures, especially TMT-A and TMT-B, but it is disease-state evidence in chronic schizophrenia and framed through neuroinflammation. It is not a healthy-adult cognition trial and should not be generalized to ordinary brain-fog use without phenotype matching.

Interpretation for the wiki: this source gives berberine a disease-state cognition clue, not a nootropic proof. The main routing value is to keep inflammation/metabolic phenotype and psychiatric illness state explicit when berberine anecdotes mention motivation, focus, or cognitive clarity.
