# Effects of Exercise and Intensive Vascular Risk Reduction on Cognitive Function in Older Adults

- Source: JAMA Neurology
- Published online: 2026-03-23
- DOI: 10.1001/jamaneurol.2026.0359
- ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT02913664
- Ingested: 2026-05-12

## Summary

Multicenter single-blind 2 x 2 factorial randomized clinical trial in 513 adults aged 60 to 85 years without dementia but with hypertension, family history of dementia, and/or subjective cognitive decline. Arms tested moderate-to-vigorous aerobic exercise training, intensive pharmacological vascular-risk reduction, both, or usual care over 24 months.

The primary endpoint was change in Preclinical Alzheimer Cognitive Composite at 24 months. Exercise did not significantly outperform no exercise, intensive vascular-risk reduction did not significantly outperform no intensive vascular-risk reduction, and secondary NIH Toolbox cognition outcomes showed similar null patterns.

## Cognitive-Relevance Extraction

- Evidence layer: randomized clinical trial, older at-risk adults.
- Direction: cognition-null despite a plausible exercise and vascular-risk-reserve rationale.
- Use in wiki: important counterweight for cerebrovascular-reserve and exercise claims. It does not negate acute exercise cognition effects, but it argues against assuming that long-duration exercise plus aggressive BP/LDL management produces measurable cognitive improvement over 24 months in already treated older at-risk cohorts.
- Boundary: clinical-risk / prevention context rather than healthy young enhancement.
