# Low-dose aripiprazole retrospective ME/CFS brain-fog study

2021 Journal of Translational Medicine retrospective Stanford clinical-practice study of 101 ME/CFS patients treated off-label with low-dose aripiprazole. ME/CFS was framed as involving fatigue, unrefreshing sleep, post-exertional malaise, and either cognitive dysfunction or orthostatic intolerance; the paper notes brain fog can include memory, attention, and information-processing difficulty.

The aripiprazole dose range was 0.2 to 2.0 mg/day, with a mean of 1.1 mg/day; therapy averaged 7.8 months. Seventy-five of 101 patients improved in at least one category: fatigue, brain fog, unrefreshing sleep, or PEM frequency. Among responders with complete scores, brain fog fell from 4.39 to 2.06 on a 0-10 symptom scale, while nonresponders had a nonsignificant change from 4.25 to 3.60. Fatigue, sleep, and PEM frequency also improved in responders. Twelve patients had no observable difference at maximum dose, and 14 worsened or stopped due to side effects.

Reported side effects beyond titration included headache, irritability/agitation, insomnia, extreme agitation, and variable body-weight changes. The authors concluded that aripiprazole warrants randomized testing.

Interpretation for the wiki: low-dose aripiprazole is a serious ME/CFS and post-viral brain-fog candidate, but the evidence is retrospective, uncontrolled, symptom-rated, and drug-risk heavy. It is not a healthy-adult enhancer.
