# NCCIH soy usefulness and safety fact sheet

NCCIH's April 2025 soy fact sheet treats soy as an adult health-purpose food/supplement category covering soy foods, soy protein, and isoflavone supplements. It says cognitive effects remain uncertain because studies have had conflicting results. It also separates soy-protein effects from isoflavone-supplement effects for other outcomes, reinforcing the product-form problem.

Safety notes: soy allergy requires labeling in the United States; soy protein supplements have been used safely for up to 16 weeks in research, and concentrated isoflavone extracts for up to 6 months. Common side effects include bloating, constipation, diarrhea, and nausea. NCCIH flags tyramine-rich soy products with MAO inhibitors, pregnancy/breastfeeding uncertainty above food amounts, and general medication-interaction caution.
