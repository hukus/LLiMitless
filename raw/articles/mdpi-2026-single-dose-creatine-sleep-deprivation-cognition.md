# Single-Dose Creatine Reduces Sleep Deprivation-Induced Deterioration in Cognitive Performance

- Source: Nutrients 2026;18(8):1192
- DOI: 10.3390/nu18081192
- Published: 2026-04-10
- Trial registration: DRKS00039845, retrospectively registered
- Ingested: 2026-05-12

## Summary

Double-blind randomized balanced crossover trial in 29 healthy adults tested a single lower dose of creatine monohydrate, 0.2 g/kg, versus placebo during 21 hours of total sleep deprivation. Participants completed cognitive testing at evening baseline and 3, 5.5, and 7.5 hours after supplementation. Creatine reduced sleep-deprivation-related deterioration on logical and numerical tasks, language-related processing speed, and the Psychomotor Vigilance Test. The authors reported effects up to about 12%, with a weaker effect than the earlier 0.35 g/kg sleep-deprivation study.

## Notes

- Sample: 29 healthy subjects, 17 female, 9 vegetarian, mean age about 27 years.
- Protocol: two supervised sleepless nights separated by 7 days; creatine or placebo was given around 21:00 after baseline testing.
- Battery: PVT, word memory, forward digit span, spatial 2-back, and language, logic, and numerical multiple-choice tasks.
- Sex signal: females showed larger apparent benefit in logic, PVT, and language/logic processing-speed measures.
- Tolerability: the paper reported no gastrointestinal distress or other adverse physical symptoms in this short acute protocol.
- Interpretation: this strengthens the creatine-as-strain-buffer branch by adding a lower-dose replication-style sleep-deprivation challenge, but it still does not show rested-baseline enhancement, long-term durability, or broad transfer.
- Caveats: small sample, short laboratory sleep-deprivation model, many task/time-point comparisons, and retrospective trial registration.
