# ACG 2022 online hormones versus stimulant DILI case with N-methylcyclazodone

## Captured takeaways

- The American Journal of Gastroenterology / ACG 2022 liver clinical-vignettes supplement includes abstract S2958, "Do Not Trust the Internet: Acute Liver Failure Secondary to DILI From Online Hormones Versus Stimulant Use in a Transgender Patient."
- The case involved a 27-year-old transgender woman with anxiety, depression, and ADHD who presented with jaundice, abdominal discomfort, acholic stools, and memory issues after consuming several drugs purchased from international websites.
- Exposures included unsupervised estradiol, intermittent cyproterone acetate, and N-methyl cyclazodone for ADHD. After jaundice began, the patient also used large water/laxative intake, milk thistle, and delta-8 THC extract.
- Labs included AST 485, ALT 668, total bilirubin 52.8, alkaline phosphatase 140, INR 3, and potassium 2.3; chronic liver disease workup was negative.
- Biopsy showed severe hepatocyte dropout, marked cholestasis, and mixed portal inflammatory infiltrate with eosinophils, interpreted as consistent with drug-induced liver injury.
- The course progressed to multiorgan complications and acute liver failure; transplant was considered the only viable recovery option, and the patient underwent orthotopic liver transplantation.
- Interpretation for the wiki: this is a multi-agent DILI case, not a clean N-methylcyclazodone-only causality proof. It still matters because N-methylcyclazodone appears in a severe online-drug DILI stack and should heighten the liver-risk gate for gray-market pemoline-adjacent stimulant use.
