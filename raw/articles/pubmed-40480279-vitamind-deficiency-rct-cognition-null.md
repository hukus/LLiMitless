# Impact of vitamin D supplementation on cognition in adults with mild to moderate vitamin D deficiency: VitaMIND randomized controlled trial

- PMID: 40480279
- DOI: 10.1016/j.jamda.2025.105711
- Published: 2025-08
- Source type: two-arm, parallel, 24-month randomized controlled trial
- Population: 620 adults age 50+ with mild to moderate vitamin D deficiency and early cognitive impairment
- Ingested: 2026-05-03

## Summary

VitaMIND tested daily vitamin D supplementation against placebo in a remote 24-month trial. The primary outcome was executive function on Trail Making Test Part B, with secondary cognition, function, and well-being measures. Vitamin D produced no significant benefit on the primary executive-function outcome, other cognitive outcomes, function, or well-being. Defined subgroup and per-protocol analyses were also null.

## Notes

- Important current counterweight because it directly enrolled people with mild to moderate deficiency and early cognitive impairment.
- This source lowers confidence in broad deficiency-correction claims, especially for mild/moderate deficiency rather than severe deficiency.
- The remote design is useful but also means endpoint fidelity and adherence/context should be considered.
