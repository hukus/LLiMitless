# Dietary niacin intake and cognition in NHANES older adults

PubMed 37576033: cross-sectional NHANES analysis of dietary niacin intake and cognitive function in older adults in the United States.

Key capture:

- Population: 2,523 participants aged 60 years or older from NHANES 2011-2014.
- Exposure: dietary niacin intake.
- Cognitive outcomes: Consortium to Establish a Registry for Alzheimer's Disease Word Learning and Delayed Recall tests, Animal Fluency Test, Digit Symbol Substitution Test, and a global cognitive z-score.
- Main result: higher dietary niacin intake was associated with lower odds of cognitive impairment. In adjusted models, the third and fourth niacin-intake quartiles had lower odds than the lowest quartile. The authors described an L-shaped association between niacin intake and cognitive impairment.
- The paper is observational and cross-sectional. It cannot determine whether niacin supplementation improves cognition, whether B3 is causal, or whether niacin is acting as a proxy for diet quality, protein intake, socioeconomic factors, health status, or other nutrients.

Interpretation for the wiki:

This is a status / dietary-pattern clue for [[niacin-nicotinamide-b3]], not evidence that high-dose niacin or niacinamide improves healthy-adult cognition. It supports keeping B3 visible in the nutrient branch while emphasizing confounding and dose/status gates.
