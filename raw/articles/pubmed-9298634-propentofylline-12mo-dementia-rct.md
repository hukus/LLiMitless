# PubMed 9298634: 12-month propentofylline dementia RCT

This 1997 Dementia and Geriatric Cognitive Disorders study is titled "A 12-month, randomized, placebo-controlled trial of propentofylline (HWA 285) in patients with dementia according to DSM III-R."

The abstract describes a multinational, randomized, 12-month, double-blind, parallel-group study in 260 outpatients with mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease or vascular dementia. Patients received 300 mg propentofylline three times daily one hour before meals or placebo.

Assessments covered global function, cognitive function, and activities of daily living from physician, psychologist, relative, and patient rater levels. At 12 months, the total population showed statistically significant differences favoring propentofylline for global measures, cognitive measures, MMSE, and activities of daily living. Patient self-rating scales did not show significant treatment differences.

Subgroup notes from the abstract: vascular dementia patients showed significant differences favoring propentofylline on several measures; Alzheimer's disease patients had treatment differences favoring propentofylline but statistical significance was reached for the SKT cognitive measure. Safety was described as good with respect to adverse events, vital signs, ECG, and labs.

Interpretation for the wiki: a positive old phase III-style dementia RCT, but still disease-context evidence and later systematic reviews emphasize limited available data and clinical-relevance concerns.
