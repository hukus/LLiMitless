# Quercetin LiverTox safety summary

- Source type: NCBI Bookshelf LiverTox monograph.
- Last update: 2020-03-28.
- Date checked: 2026-05-03.

LiverTox describes quercetin as a dietary flavonoid found in many foods and as an over-the-counter supplement often dosed at 500-1000 mg/day. It notes that quercetin has not been shown effective in clinical trials for any medical condition.

Safety readout: quercetin supplements are generally well tolerated and have not been linked to serum aminotransferase elevations or clinically apparent liver injury. Abdominal discomfort, nausea, and headache have been reported in some studies at rates similar to placebo. LiverTox gives quercetin a likelihood score of E, meaning unlikely to cause clinically apparent liver injury.

Use in the wiki: safety anchor for quercetin. The main caution is not hepatotoxicity; it is supplement dose, product quality, interaction context, and extrapolation beyond weak cognition data.
