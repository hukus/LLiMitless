# Resveratrol LiverTox safety summary

- Source type: NCBI Bookshelf LiverTox monograph.
- Last update: 2024-11-30.
- Date checked: 2026-05-03.

LiverTox describes resveratrol as a plant polyphenol supplement proposed for metabolic, cardiovascular, aging, and liver-related indications. Human trials have used a wide dose range, but commercial supplement purity and active form are often poorly defined, and oral bioavailability is limited.

Safety readout: clinically apparent liver injury has not been convincingly linked to resveratrol, and the LiverTox likelihood score is E, meaning an unlikely cause of clinically apparent liver injury. Rare serum-enzyme elevations have occurred, especially at high doses, but jaundiced liver injury is not established. Minor adverse effects can include gastrointestinal upset, nausea, headache, and fatigue. LiverTox also flags possible interactions with estrogens and anticoagulants.

Use in the wiki: safety anchor for supplement-dose caution. Resveratrol is not a major hepatotoxicity signal, but product quality, high-dose GI effects, bioavailability, and anticoagulant/estrogen interaction cautions matter.
