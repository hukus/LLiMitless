# Effects of xanomeline, a selective muscarinic receptor agonist, on cognitive function and behavioral symptoms in Alzheimer disease

- PMID: 9109749
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9109749/
- Companion paper: PMID 9876958
- Ingested: 2026-04-28

## Summary

This is the key older human cognition signal for the muscarinic branch. In a 343-patient randomized trial in mild-to-moderate AD, high-dose xanomeline improved ADAS-Cog and CIBIC+ versus placebo and also reduced behavioral symptoms such as delusions, hallucinations, agitation, and suspiciousness. The tradeoff was heavy cholinergic toxicity: GI adverse effects were dose-dependent, 52% of the high-dose group discontinued because of adverse events, and syncope occurred in the high-dose arm. The companion 1998 CNTB analysis reported a dose-response signal for reaction time and delayed verbal recall and suggested the CNTB was more sensitive than ADAS-cog during washout.
