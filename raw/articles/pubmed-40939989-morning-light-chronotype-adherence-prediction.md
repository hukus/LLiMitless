# Examining photoperiod, chronotype, and adherence as predictors of morning light treatment effects on depression symptoms: A study of five clinical trials

- PMID: 40939989
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40939989/
- Published: 2026-01
- Study type: secondary analysis / pooled clinical trials
- Sample: 86 participants across five morning-light trials
- Key finding: morning light treatment at home (60 min/day for 2-4 weeks) reduced depression symptoms across trials. Photoperiod and chronotype predicted baseline depression severity, but later bedtime was only a moderate and non-significant predictor of symptom improvement. Season, photoperiod, and objectively measured adherence were weak or non-significant predictors of symptom improvement.
- Why it matters: this softens the chronotype-personalization story for morning light. It supports measuring chronotype and baseline schedule, but does not justify assuming better symptom response from light availability matching alone.
