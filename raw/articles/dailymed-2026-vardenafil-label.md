# DailyMed vardenafil label safety context

DailyMed label check for vardenafil hydrochloride tablets and related vardenafil labels. This raw note is for safety, indication, and protocol boundaries, not cognition efficacy.

Checked URL:

- https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/search.cfm?query=vardenafil

Label-level practical points:

- Vardenafil is labeled for erectile dysfunction; it should not be treated as a cognition protocol.
- Typical ED use is an as-needed dose before sexual activity, with dose adjustment by efficacy and tolerability and no more than once-daily use in label context.
- Contraindication / major interaction gate: organic nitrates. Combining vardenafil with nitrates can produce dangerous hypotension.
- Current PDE5 labeling also treats soluble guanylate cyclase stimulators such as riociguat as a major contraindication because of blood-pressure risk.
- Alpha-blockers, antihypertensives, and alcohol can add hypotension or dizziness risk.
- CYP3A4 inhibitors can raise exposure; age, hepatic impairment, renal impairment, and cardiovascular disease affect risk interpretation.
- Vardenafil has an additional QT-prolongation caution compared with some PDE5 peers; congenital long-QT syndrome and class IA/III antiarrhythmics are important label gates.
- Key warnings include cardiovascular status before sexual activity, priapism, sudden vision loss, and sudden hearing loss.
- Common adverse reactions in label context include headache, flushing, rhinitis/nasal congestion, dyspepsia, and related vasodilatory or GI symptoms.

Wiki use: [[vardenafil]] should use this as a prescription-safety anchor. Keep vardenafil separate from [[tadalafil]] because half-life, QT cautions, dose timing, and the direct healthy-adult cognition study differ.
