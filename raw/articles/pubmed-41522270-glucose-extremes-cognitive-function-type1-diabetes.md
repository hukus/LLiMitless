# Glucose Extremes and Cognitive Function: A Review of the Neurological Impacts of Hypoglycemia and Hyperglycemia in Type 1 Diabetes

- PMID: 41522270
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41522270/
- Ingested: 2026-04-28

## Summary

Review of the neurological and cognitive effects of hypoglycemia and hyperglycemia in type 1 diabetes. Hypoglycemia is framed as an acute brain energy crisis that increases oxidative stress and neuronal vulnerability, while repeated episodes may cumulatively damage hippocampal and prefrontal systems. Hyperglycemia is framed as a vascular/perfusion problem with neurotransmitter dysregulation and structural brain effects such as reduced gray matter volume. The review also argues that CGM can better capture glycemic variability than A1C alone and may help reduce cognition-related harms from glucose swings.

## Notes

This strengthens glucose stability as a cognition-relevant state variable, especially in T1D and other dysglycemic contexts. It does not convert glucose control into a broad nootropic claim; it mainly justifies tracking glucose extremes when cognition is the endpoint.
