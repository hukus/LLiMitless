# Lisdexamfetamine and d-amphetamine in healthy-subject cognition

- PMID: 29511807
- DOI: 10.1007/s00213-018-4849-0
- Journal: Psychopharmacology (2018)
- Trial registration: NCT02668926

## Captured takeaways

- Randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind, crossover study in 24 healthy volunteers.
- Compared single high equimolar doses: d-amphetamine 40 mg, lisdexamfetamine 100 mg, and placebo.
- Social cognition was assessed with Facial Emotion Recognition Task, Multifaceted Empathy Test, and Sexual Arousal Task.
- Cognitive performance was assessed with Digit Symbol Substitution Test, Digit Span, Stop-Signal Task, and Mackworth Clock Test.
- Both amphetamines had small effects on social cognition and no effect on emotion recognition.
- D-amphetamine and lisdexamfetamine increased cognitive performance on go-accuracy and vigilance measures.
- Lisdexamfetamine increased processing speed on the DSST.
- Neither drug affected Digit Span.
- The authors concluded that single high equimolar doses enhanced certain aspects of cognitive performance in healthy non-sleep-deprived subjects and slightly altered social cognition.
- Interpretation for the wiki: this is direct healthy-adult evidence, but it is acute, small, high-dose, and safety-constrained rather than a durable enhancement or brain-fog treatment result.
