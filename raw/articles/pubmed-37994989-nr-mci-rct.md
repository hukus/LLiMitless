# A randomized placebo-controlled trial of nicotinamide riboside in older adults with mild cognitive impairment

- PMID: 37994989
- PMCID: PMC10828186
- DOI: 10.1007/s11357-023-00999-9
- Trial registry: NCT02942888
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37994989/
- Ingested: 2026-04-28

## Summary

2024 GeroScience placebo-controlled pilot in older adults with mild cognitive impairment. Twenty participants with MCI completed a 10-week double-blind randomized study of nicotinamide riboside (NR; NIAGEN) versus placebo, escalating to 1 g/day.

The trial achieved the biological exposure target: blood NAD+ rose about 2.6-fold in the NR arm, and adverse-event reporting was not different from placebo. Cognition did not separate. MoCA was the primary cognitive outcome, and MoCA plus other neurocognitive/psychometric measures remained stable through the study.

Secondary and exploratory signals moved in ways that keep NR biologically interesting but not cognition-proven: cerebral blood flow in default-mode-network regions changed on uncorrected analyses, walking speed improved only in placebo in a way the authors interpreted as possible practice effect, and methylation/epigenetic-age analyses showed exploratory movement.

Use in the wiki: biomarker-positive, cognition-null anchor for [[nicotinamide-riboside]]. The trial supports tolerability and NAD+ target engagement at 1 g/day over 10 weeks, but it does not support ranking NR as a demonstrated cognitive enhancer.

Conflict / product note: ChromaDex provided NR and placebo; one co-author was ChromaDex chief scientific advisor, owned stock, and was an inventor on NR-use intellectual property, while the paper says he was not involved in trial design or execution.
