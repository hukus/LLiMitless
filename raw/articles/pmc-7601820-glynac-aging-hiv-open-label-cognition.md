# GlyNAC aging-HIV open-label cognition trial

Open-label clinical trial in eight people with HIV and eight matched uninfected controls. People with HIV were studied at baseline, after 12 weeks of GlyNAC supplementation, and after 8 weeks of withdrawal; controls did not receive supplementation. The trial targeted premature-aging-like defects in HIV, not broad healthy enhancement.

Key cognition details: cognition was measured with MoCA, Trail Making Tests A and B, verbal fluency, and digit-symbol substitution. At baseline, people with HIV had lower cognition than controls: MoCA about 25% lower, Trails A 81% higher, Trails B 118% higher, DSST completion 30% lower, DSST accuracy 4% lower, and verbal fluency 30% lower. After 12 weeks of GlyNAC, reported improvements were MoCA 18%, Trails A 26%, Trails B 41%, DSST completion 19%, DSST accuracy 3%, and verbal fluency 20%. After supplementation, MoCA and Trails A remained significantly lower than controls, while other cognition differences were no longer significant or trended toward normalization.

Safety and protocol details: the study used oral glycine plus N-acetylcysteine capsules, monitored renal and liver function every 4 weeks, and excluded several comorbid or interacting contexts.

Use in wiki: supports a premature-aging / metabolic-redox responder hypothesis. It does not establish GlyNAC as a general nootropic because there was no randomized placebo arm and the population was disease-context.
