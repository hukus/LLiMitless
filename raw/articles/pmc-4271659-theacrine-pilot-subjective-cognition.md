# The effects of TeaCrine on subjective cognitive function in healthy humans

- PMCID: PMC4271659
- Venue: Journal of the International Society of Sports Nutrition supplement (2014)

## Captured takeaways

- Randomized, double-blinded, within-subject crossover pilot in 15 healthy participants.
- Participants ingested 200 mg TeaCrine / theacrine or placebo.
- The abstract-level source frames outcomes as subjective measures of cognitive function, psychometric indices, and hemodynamic indices.
- The paper also references an N=6 cohort showing moderate-to-large effect sizes for subjective energy, fatigue, concentration, anxiety, motivation to exercise, and libido over a 7-day assessment period at 200 mg.
- The authors state that future studies were needed to confirm neurotropic effects and explore objective cognitive and physical performance outcomes.
- Interpretation for the wiki: this closes the "no standalone theacrine source captured" gap, but the signal remains subjective and pilot-level rather than objective cognition proof.
