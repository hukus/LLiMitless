# Methylphenidate for post-COVID cognitive dysfunction case series

2024 Journal of Medical Cases case series from the Johns Hopkins Post-Acute COVID-19 Team clinic on methylphenidate for post-COVID cognitive dysfunction / brain fog.

The series described six patients with function-limiting post-COVID cognitive complaints, commonly involving memory, concentration, fatigue, confusion, and inability to return to work. Methylphenidate was used in routine care at 5-20 mg and patients were followed for 4-8 weeks. Three of six reported subjective improvement; objective pre/post testing favored treatment on Hopkins Verbal Learning Test immediate recall, delayed recall, and category-cued verbal fluency.

Tolerability was mixed but not alarming in this small sample: four of six reported no adverse effects, while two had gastric upset or increased fatigue and one discontinued because of adverse effects. The design was uncontrolled, unblinded, tiny, and vulnerable to practice effects and recovery trajectory.

Interpretation for the wiki: methylphenidate has a direct post-COVID brain-fog case-series signal, but it should be routed as a prescription stimulant / state-support candidate, not as evidence that stimulants reverse the underlying post-viral process.
