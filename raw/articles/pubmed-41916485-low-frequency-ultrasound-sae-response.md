# Risk of low-frequency ultrasound stimulation response to brain injury report

- PMID: 41916485
- DOI: 10.1016/j.brs.2026.103091
- Source: https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/outputs/letter/Risk-of-low-frequency-ultrasound-stimulation-Response/991022172972704721
- Ingested: 2026-05-12

## Summary

Brain Stimulation 2026 peer-reviewed letter responding to a reported serious adverse event in a low-intensity transcranial focused ultrasound neuromodulation study. The authors argue that the case is not a simple indictment of all low-intensity tFUS, but it is a concrete warning about low-frequency operation, high-output repurposed systems, incomplete parameter disclosure, mechanical-index exceedance, and narrowed cavitation/spatial safety margins. They note that initial reporting gave only 90 W free-field power and that later parameter release allowed comparison to ITRUSST limits. They summarize that ITRUSST nonsignificant-risk framing uses MI or MI_tc <= 1.9 plus conservative thermal constraints in the absence of contrast agents, while estimates in the SAE case ranged from MI 2.7 to 5.1.

The letter emphasizes frequency as a safety variable: mid-frequency 500-700 kHz tFUS has a strong safety record in controlled neuromodulation studies, whereas low-frequency 200-340 kHz exposure can narrow cavitation margins, broaden focal volumes, increase standing-wave risk, and raise concern when combined with vascular pathology, thrombolysis, microbubbles, or repurposed high-output hardware. It cites TRUMBI as a historical example in which 300 kHz transcranial ultrasound plus thrombolysis in acute ischemic stroke increased intracranial hemorrhage, while noting that TRUMBI was not a neuromodulation trial.

## Wiki Relevance

This is a high-salience safety boundary source. It strengthens the wiki rule that tFUS parameters cannot be copied from headlines, that low-frequency BBB-opening or ablation-capable hardware is not equivalent to purpose-built neuromodulation hardware, and that protocol safety depends on full acoustic parameterization, frequency, focal geometry, cavitation margin, participant risk state, and monitoring.
