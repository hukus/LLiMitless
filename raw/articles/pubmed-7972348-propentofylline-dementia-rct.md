# PubMed 7972348: Placebo-controlled propentofylline dementia trial

This 1994 Pharmacopsychiatry study is titled "Placebo-controlled trial of the xanthine derivative propentofylline in dementia."

The abstract describes a 3-month multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study in 190 patients with dementia. Propentofylline was given at 0.9 g/day to test whether it might improve cognition or slow disease progression.

Primary efficacy definition: improvement on the Gottfries-Brane-Steen scale and in at least two of eight psychometric assessments. The GBS total score improved more in the propentofylline group, and MMSE increased more than placebo. However, psychometric assessments and the cognitive difficulties scale failed to demonstrate therapeutic benefit after three months.

The authors concluded that, despite positive GBS findings, efficacy could not be established according to the predefined primary efficacy measures. They also noted that engaging subjects in cognitive tasks may itself enhance performance.

Interpretation for the wiki: this is an important mixed trial, because it prevents the propentofylline story from being summarized as simply positive.
