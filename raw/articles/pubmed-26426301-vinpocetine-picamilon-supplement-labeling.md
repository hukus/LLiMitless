# Vinpocetine and picamilon in US dietary supplements

2016 Drug Testing and Analysis validation/label-accuracy study, PMID 26426301, DOI 10.1002/dta.1853.

Scope: analytical testing of supplements sold in the United States that were labeled as containing vinpocetine or picamilon. The authors developed and validated an ultra-high performance liquid chromatography-photodiode-array method.

Regulatory context in abstract: vinpocetine and picamilon are drugs prescribed in many countries to treat cerebrovascular disorders. In the United States, neither had been approved by FDA, but both were available directly to consumers as dietary supplements.

Picamilon findings: of 31 picamilon supplements available from varied retailers, 30 contained picamilon. Quantities ranged from 2.7 mg to 721.5 mg per recommended daily serving.

Interpretation: major product-quality and dose-exposure warning. The study supports risk-first routing because consumers could not rely on supplement labels for accurate presence or quantity of picamilon, and labeled daily exposures could span trivial to prescription-level or above-prescription-style ranges.
