# Bupropion improves attention but does not affect impulsive behavior in healthy young adults

## Captured takeaways

- 2008 experimental psychopharmacology study in healthy adults, PMID 18489015.
- Thirty-three participants completed four 4-hour sessions with placebo, bupropion 150 mg, bupropion 300 mg, or d-amphetamine 20 mg.
- Cognitive testing started about 90 minutes after capsule administration.
- Outcomes included simple reaction time / lapses in attention, stop-signal response inhibition, delay/probability discounting, balloon analogue risk task, and mood questionnaires.
- Bupropion 150 mg decreased lapses on the simple reaction time task.
- Bupropion did not materially improve stop-signal, discounting, or risk-taking measures in this study.
- Bupropion 300 mg increased subjective arousal, which matters when interpreting any "focus" report as stimulation versus cleaner cognition.
- Use in wiki: a narrow healthy-adult attention anchor for [[bupropion]], not evidence for broad executive, memory, or healthy-adult enhancement.
