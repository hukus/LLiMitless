# Clinical Toxicology 2022 N-methyl-cyclazodone toxicity abstract

## Captured takeaways

- The CFSRE page presents a 2022 American Academy of Clinical Toxicology meeting abstract by Basile, Nolan, Krotulski, and Osterhoudt.
- The case involved a 38-year-old man who sought evaluation for uncontrollable body movements and palpitations after self-treating attention deficit disorder for five days with online-purchased N-methyl-cyclazodone powder.
- The patient reported consuming about 5 g total mixed in water. He also had prescriptions for fluoxetine and aripiprazole for bipolar disorder.
- Clinical features included inattention, restlessness, tremor, choreiform whole-body movements, tachycardia, elevated blood pressure, widened QRS, prolonged QTc, elevated creatine kinase, and mildly elevated ALT.
- Supportive care included IV crystalloid, lorazepam, sodium bicarbonate, and magnesium. Symptoms resolved by hospital day 3, with CK and ALT trending downward.
- Liquid-chromatography high-resolution mass-spectrometry testing confirmed cyclazodone in urine, described as an expected metabolite of N-methyl-cyclazodone. The sample did not identify pemoline or 4-methylaminorex.
- The discussion describes cyclazodone as a centrally acting dopaminergic stimulant with chemical similarity to pemoline and aminorex, online research-chemical market appearance in 2017, no current FDA approval, and limited documented toxic-effect history.
- Interpretation for the wiki: N-methyl-cyclazodone has a direct laboratory-confirmed toxicity case. It should not be treated as a cleaner methylphenidate or modafinil substitute on anecdote alone.
