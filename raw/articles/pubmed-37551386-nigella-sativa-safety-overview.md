# Nigella sativa black seed safety overview

2020 Asian Biomedicine narrative safety review, PMID 37551386, PMCID PMC10373399, DOI 10.1515/abm-2020-0020.

Scope: review of short- to long-term toxicological studies of Nigella sativa seed extracts and oils, searched across Web of Science, PubMed, Scopus, and Google Scholar.

Toxicology summary from abstract: LD50 values for administered Nigella sativa fixed oil ranged from 28.8 mL/kg to 3371 mg/kg in mice; aqueous, methanol, and chloroform extracts at 21 g/kg did not lead to mortality. Subacute evaluations indicated that aqueous, methanol, and chloroform extracts at doses as high as 6 g/kg did not produce toxicity. Chronic toxicity testing found fixed oil at 2 mL/kg slightly toxic. Chloroform and petroleum ether extracts were more cytotoxic than other extracts.

Human/practical safety notes: the review describes clinical-trial safety as generally favorable but not complete. It flags that more detailed safety studies are required, and its human section notes allergic contact dermatitis reports and potential CYP2D6/CYP3A4 interaction relevance for black seed exposure.

Interpretation: supports moderate product/safety caution rather than alarm. It is compatible with LiverTox's favorable hepatic read, but it also reinforces preparation, extract, route, and drug-interaction gates.
