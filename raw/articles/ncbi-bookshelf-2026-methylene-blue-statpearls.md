# NCBI Bookshelf 2026 - Methylene Blue StatPearls

## Citation

- Ostrovsky A, Afzal M. "Methylene Blue." StatPearls. Last updated 2026-01-02. NCBI Bookshelf.
- Source type: clinical reference / review.

## Extracted Claims

- FDA-approved use is treatment of acquired methemoglobinemia.
- The source describes methylene blue as a redox agent and alternative electron carrier; at the cellular level it can shuttle electrons from NADH and donate them to cytochrome c, bypassing parts of the electron transport chain.
- Off-label clinical contexts include vasoplegic shock, ifosfamide-induced encephalopathy, surgical staining, and investigational neurologic or psychiatric use; cognition enhancement is not an approved indication.
- Standard methemoglobinemia dosing is IV 1 mg/kg over 5 to 30 minutes, with one repeat dose if needed.
- The safety discussion flags serotonin syndrome, anaphylaxis, hemolytic anemia, cardiovascular effects including palpitations, tachycardia and hypertension, seizure-like phenomena, confusion, dizziness, visual disturbance, and interference with pulse oximetry and some urine tests.
- G6PD deficiency is a contraindication because of hemolysis and worsening anemia risk.
- The reference describes dose-dependent toxicity: single doses of 3 mg/kg or more have been associated with hypotension, wheezing, and reduced oxygenation; cumulative doses of 7 mg/kg or more can produce significant toxicity; severe overdose around 20 mg/kg or more has caused severe hemolysis, hyperbilirubinemia, and death.
- The source says concomitant serotonergic agents and opioids should be avoided; it also describes counseling to avoid serotonergic medications for at least 72 hours after the final dose.

## Cognition-Relevant Extraction

- This is a safety and mechanism anchor, not a cognition-efficacy source.
- It supports keeping nootropic or red-light synergy claims below the safety gate: serotonergic polypharmacy, G6PD deficiency, pregnancy, renal impairment, cardiovascular symptoms, and dose errors materially change risk.
- It strengthens the distinction between FDA-approved IV rescue therapy, off-label medical uses, and unapproved oral nootropic use.
