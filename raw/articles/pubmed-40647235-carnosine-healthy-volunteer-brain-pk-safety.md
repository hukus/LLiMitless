# Dietary Carnosine Supplementation in Healthy Human Volunteers: A Safety, Tolerability, Plasma and Brain Concentration Study

- PMID: 40647235
- PMCID: PMC12252343
- DOI: 10.3390/nu17132130
- Published: 2025-06-27
- Ingested: 2026-05-11

## Summary

Healthy-volunteer dose-escalation and pharmacokinetic study of oral carnosine. Sixteen participants underwent single-dose escalation; five had proton magnetic-resonance spectroscopy for brain carnosine concentration, and four completed a longer 4-week dosing phase.

Oral carnosine was reported safe and well tolerated up to 10 g. At 15 g, adverse events became common: 77% of participants had side effects, most often headache, nausea, and paraesthesia. Peak plasma concentrations occurred within the first hour and little circulating carnosine was detectable beyond 4 hours. Brain carnosine increased at 1 hour after a 10 g dose and returned to baseline by 5 hours. Long-term dosing at 5 g twice daily produced no adverse events in the tiny long-term subset.

## Relevance

This is the main human exposure / brain-target-engagement note for [[anserine-carnosine]]. It gives a plausible exposure bridge while adding a high-dose tolerability boundary.

## Caveats

- Not an efficacy trial.
- Very small sample, especially MRI and longer-dosing subsets.
- Acute brain concentration increase does not prove cognitive benefit.
- High-dose adverse events matter for self-experiment stop rules.
