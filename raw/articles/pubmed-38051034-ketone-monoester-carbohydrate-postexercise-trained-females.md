# A Ketone Monoester with Carbohydrate Improves Cognitive Measures Postexercise, but Not Performance in Trained Females

- PMID: 38051034
- Study type: placebo-controlled double-blind crossover trial
- Population: 12 trained females, age 23 +/- 3 years
- Intervention: 375 mg/kg ketone monoester with 6% carbohydrate solution across 3 boluses (50:25:25) versus carbohydrate alone during cycling exercise
- Exercise load: 6 x 5-min intervals at 40% to 65% Wmax plus 10-km time trial
- Cognitive tasks: PVT, task switching, incongruent flanker
- Main signal: postexercise PVT speed improved about 4%; flanker reaction time, speed, and correct responses improved about 14%, 15%, and 13% respectively with KME + CHO
- Main caveat: no cycling-performance benefit and no broad cognition effect across the full battery; context is exercise, not sleep loss

## Summary

This is a small but useful high-load positive signal for ketone monoester when combined with carbohydrate in trained females. It supports the idea that ketones can selectively help postexercise vigilance or executive control, but it does not turn ketones into a universal performance enhancer.
