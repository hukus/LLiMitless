# CHEST 2022 pyridostigmine ME/CFS iCPET randomized trial

## Captured takeaways

- Joseph et al. tested oral pyridostigmine in a single-center randomized double-blind placebo-controlled ME/CFS trial registered as NCT03674541.
- Forty-five participants with ME/CFS were randomized 1:1 to a single 60 mg pyridostigmine dose or placebo after an invasive cardiopulmonary exercise test; a second iCPET was performed 50 minutes later.
- The primary endpoint was between-group difference in peak exercise oxygen uptake after dosing.
- Twenty-three participants received pyridostigmine and 22 received placebo.
- Peak VO2 increased after pyridostigmine and decreased after placebo; the reported treatment effect was 53.6 mL/min with a confidence interval that barely excluded zero.
- Peak-versus-rest VO2, cardiac output, and right atrial pressure also favored pyridostigmine versus placebo.
- The authors interpreted the result as support for treatable neurovascular dysregulation and acute exercise intolerance in ME/CFS.
- Interpretation for the wiki: this is the strongest formal pyridostigmine cognition-adjacent signal, but it is an acute exercise physiology / ME/CFS dysautonomia study, not a direct brain-fog or healthy-adult cognitive-enhancement trial.
