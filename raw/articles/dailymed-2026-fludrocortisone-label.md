# DailyMed fludrocortisone acetate label

## Captured takeaways

- DailyMed's fludrocortisone acetate label frames the drug as a corticosteroid/mineralocorticoid, not a cognition drug.
- The label's adverse-reaction section says most adverse reactions are caused by mineralocorticoid activity: sodium and water retention, hypertension, edema, cardiac enlargement, congestive heart failure, potassium loss, and hypokalemic alkalosis.
- Elderly-use cautions include conditions that can be exacerbated by fludrocortisone, including hypertension, edema, hypokalemia, congestive heart failure, cataracts, glaucoma, increased intraocular pressure, renal insufficiency, and osteoporosis.
- The overdosage section emphasizes hypertension, edema, hypokalemia, excessive weight increase, and heart-size increase, and recommends regular monitoring of blood pressure and serum electrolytes.
- Interpretation for the wiki: fludrocortisone belongs in the hypovolemia/volume-expansion POTS bucket, but any brain-fog benefit is secondary to orthostatic physiology and tightly safety-gated by BP, edema, potassium, cardiac, ocular, renal, and steroid risks.
