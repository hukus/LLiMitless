# Acute Zensera lemon balm extract under cognitive overload

2026 randomized double-blind placebo-controlled study of Zensera, a Melissa officinalis / lemon balm extract, in 106 young healthy adults with moderate subjective stress. The study tested 300 mg extract against placebo during a cognitive-overload day with mood, cognition, heart-rate, and blood-pressure measures.

Design: acute parallel-group randomized placebo-controlled double-blind study, with measurements at baseline and 1, 3, and 5 hours after dosing. Participants were adults aged 18-40 with moderate subjective stress complaints. Cognitive tasks included verbal learning, task switching, attention-network testing, rapid visual information processing, and paced auditory serial addition.

Main result: the primary overall calmness outcome did not change. Zensera improved performance on the hardest executive-function trials at 5 hours, and regression linked harder attention-network performance with higher calmness and contentment at 5 hours. Zensera also helped restore transient calmness after cognitive-demand blocks, a pattern not seen with placebo.

The source matters because it is recent, healthy-adult, and stress-state specific. It supports lemon balm as a calm/stress and cognitive-overload candidate rather than a broad memory enhancer. It also shows why primary-outcome discipline matters: the most direct mood endpoint was null while harder-task and transient calmness signals were positive.

Relevance: fresh formal anchor for lemon balm in the calm-focus botanical lane.
