# Effects of a food supplement containing phosphatidylserine on cognitive function in Chinese older adults with mild cognitive impairment

## Bibliographic snapshot

- Journal of Affective Disorders, volume 369, 15 January 2025, pages 35-42.
- DOI: 10.1016/j.jad.2024.09.131.
- Open-access ScienceDirect article.

## Study design

- Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in Tianjin, China.
- Participants: 190 older adults with mild cognitive impairment, randomized 95 to intervention and 95 to placebo.
- Duration: 12 months.
- Intervention: two capsules/day. Each intervention capsule contained 144 mg alpha-linolenic acid (ALA), 31.5 mg phosphatidylserine, 3.6 mg Ginkgo total flavonoids, 0.48 mg vitamin B1, 0.48 mg vitamin B6, and 90 micrograms folic acid.
- Placebo capsules were identical but contained no active ingredients.
- Cognitive function, serum omega-3 PUFAs, and serum neurotransmitters were assessed at baseline and 12 months.

## Results captured

- Reported improvements versus placebo: arithmetic test beta 0.688 (95% CI 0.103-1.274), similarity test beta 1.070 (95% CI 0.472-1.667), and short-term memory beta 0.600 (95% CI 0.399-0.800).
- Intervention group also showed increases in serum ALA, DHA, EPA, acetylcholine, GABA, and 5-HT versus placebo.
- Mediation analysis suggested serum ALA may mediate short-term memory improvement, with average causal mediation effect 0.132 and mediation proportion 19.7%.

## Wiki interpretation

- This is a newer, larger, longer MCI trial, but it is not isolated phosphatidylserine.
- The active formula mixes PS, ALA, small Ginkgo flavonoid exposure, B vitamins, and folate, so it should update the phosphatidylserine branch cautiously as combination-formula evidence.
- It is relevant for older adults with MCI and short-term memory, not healthy adult enhancement.
- It reinforces the product-identity rule: formula outcomes cannot be assigned to PS alone.
