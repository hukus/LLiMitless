# Nigella sativa and active constituents in learning and memory

2016 Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine review, PMID 27022403, PMCID PMC4789020, DOI 10.1155/2016/6075679.

Scope: narrative review of Nigella sativa seed extract and isolated constituents such as thymoquinone in learning and memory. The abstract frames learning/memory loss as a dementia and brain-injury problem, then reviews black seed and constituent data as possible cognitive/neuroprotective agents.

Key claim: the review says evidence appears to support the hypothesis that Nigella sativa and/or its bioactive constituents can enhance learning and memory in health and disease in animals and humans.

Mechanism anchors captured by the review/reference list include antioxidant and anti-inflammatory activity, beta-amyloid neurotoxicity protection, acetylcholinesterase inhibition by thymoquinone-related compounds in vitro, scopolamine/diabetes/hypoperfusion animal models, and anxiety/mood-performance interactions.

Interpretation: mechanism support is real but mostly preclinical and review-level. It should be used to explain plausibility and responder hypotheses, not to raise medicinal confidence above the small human trial base.
