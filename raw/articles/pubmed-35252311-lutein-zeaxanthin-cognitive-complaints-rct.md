# Lutein and zeaxanthin supplementation in adults with self-reported cognitive complaints

- Source type: randomized double-blind placebo-controlled parallel trial.
- Publication: Frontiers in Nutrition, 2022.
- Date checked: 2026-05-11.

This trial randomized 90 adults aged 40-75 with self-reported mild cognitive complaints to 10 mg lutein plus 2 mg zeaxanthin once daily or placebo for 6 months. Outcomes included computerized cognitive tasks, cognitive-failure and executive-function questionnaires, mood, and quality-of-life measures.

The supplement arm improved visual episodic memory and visual learning more than placebo. Other cognitive tests and self-report questionnaires did not show statistically significant treatment advantages.

Use in the wiki: this supports a selective visual-learning / visual-memory signal for [[lutein-zeaxanthin]] in a complaint-enriched adult group. It does not support broad attention, mood, daily executive-function, or brain-fog claims.
