# Effects of NMN on Glucose and Lipid Metabolism in Adults

- Journal: Current Diabetes Reports
- DOI: 10.1007/s11892-024-01557-z
- Published: 2024-11-12
- Checked: 2026-05-03

## Summary

Open-access systematic review and meta-analysis of human randomized controlled trials of NMN supplementation and glucose/lipid metabolism. The review included 8 RCTs involving 342 middle-aged or older adults, mostly relatively healthy and mainly non-diabetic. NMN doses ranged from 250 mg/day to 2000 mg/day over 14 days to 12 weeks.

Five of the 8 RCTs reported increased blood NAD+ after NMN. Meta-analyses did not find significant benefit for fasting glucose, fasting insulin, HbA1c, HOMA-IR, or lipid profile. No trial reported major safety concerns, but the authors noted a tendency for more adverse-event reporting in NMN groups at 1000 mg/day or higher, regardless of relevance to treatment.

The review emphasized important uncertainties: oral NMN may be converted to nicotinamide before systemic circulation, tissue-level NAD effects remain unclear, human doses and durations differ sharply from rodent work, long-term high-dose safety in older adults is uncertain, and higher-dose nicotinamide-related concerns should be considered.

Use in the wiki: metabolic-null and safety-caution counterweight for [[nicotinamide-mononucleotide]]. It supports NAD+ target engagement but limits claims that NMN broadly improves metabolic health, let alone cognition.
