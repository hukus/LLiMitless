# CYSTADANE / betaine anhydrous DailyMed label

DailyMed label current in April 2026 for CYSTADANE, betaine anhydrous powder for oral solution. The label describes CYSTADANE as a methylating agent indicated for pediatric and adult patients with homocystinuria to decrease elevated homocysteine blood concentrations, including CBS deficiency, MTHFR deficiency, and cobalamin cofactor metabolism defects.

The adult and pediatric dosage for age 3 years and older is 6 g/day orally in divided doses of 3 g twice daily. For patients under 3 years old, the recommended starting dose is 100 mg/kg/day divided twice daily and increased gradually while monitoring plasma homocysteine.

The main warning is hypermethioninemia in CBS deficiency. The label says betaine can further increase plasma methionine due to remethylation of homocysteine to methionine, and cerebral edema has been reported in patients with hypermethioninemia. Plasma methionine should be monitored in CBS deficiency and kept below 1,000 micromol/L.

Common adverse reactions in the physician survey were nausea and gastrointestinal distress. Postmarketing adverse reactions include severe cerebral edema with hypermethioninemia, plus anorexia, agitation, depression, irritability, personality disorder, sleep disturbance, diarrhea, stomach discomfort, vomiting, skin odor abnormalities, and urinary incontinence.

Clinical pharmacology: CYSTADANE acts as a methyl-group donor in remethylating homocysteine to methionine. The label also notes that betaine is a choline metabolite and occurs in foods such as beets, spinach, cereals, and seafood. In MTHFR deficiency and cobalamin metabolism defects, it increased low plasma methionine and SAM concentrations.
