# ODS vitamin B12 form comparison note

- Publisher: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements
- Source type: official nutrient status, form, safety, and interaction overview
- Checked: 2026-05-18
- Relevant forms: cyanocobalamin, adenosylcobalamin, methylcobalamin, and hydroxycobalamin

## Summary

ODS describes vitamin B12 as a family of cobalamins required for central nervous system development, myelination, red blood cell formation, DNA synthesis, methionine synthase, and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase. Dietary supplements can contain cyanocobalamin, adenosylcobalamin, methylcobalamin, and hydroxycobalamin. ODS notes that evidence does not show important differences in absorption or bioavailability among these forms.

ODS also summarizes the cognition boundary: B12 deficiency can cause neurological and cognitive symptoms, but randomized trial evidence does not show cognitive improvement from B12 alone or B12 plus folate/B6 in older adults with or without dementia/MCI/AD when deficiency treatment is not the issue.

## Wiki Interpretation

- Use this as the official form-separation source for [[hydroxy-adenosyl-cyano-b12]] and [[methylcobalamin-b12]].
- Treat non-methyl B12 forms as status-correction and tolerability candidates, not as proven nootropics.
- Track serum B12, methylmalonic acid, homocysteine, folate, kidney function, metformin or acid-suppression exposure, vegan diet, gastrointestinal disease or surgery, pernicious anemia, route, and dose before interpreting brain-fog reports.
- Do not upgrade hydroxycobalamin, adenosylcobalamin, cyanocobalamin, or methylcobalamin by form preference alone.
