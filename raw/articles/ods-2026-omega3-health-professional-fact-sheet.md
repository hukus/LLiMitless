# NIH ODS omega-3 fatty acids health professional fact sheet

NIH Office of Dietary Supplements health professional fact sheet for omega-3 fatty acids.

Key identity details: the major omega-3s discussed are ALA, EPA, and DHA. DHA is especially high in the retina, brain, and sperm and is part of cell membranes. EPA and DHA are mainly obtained from fish/seafood or supplements; ALA conversion to EPA/DHA is limited.

Safety details: FDA has concluded that dietary supplements providing no more than 5 g/day EPA plus DHA are safe when used as recommended. EFSA similarly considered long-term combined EPA/DHA up to about 5 g/day safe. ODS notes common mild side effects such as unpleasant taste, bad breath, heartburn, nausea, GI discomfort, diarrhea, headache, and odoriferous sweat. Large trials using 4 g/day omega-3 for years found a small increase in atrial fibrillation risk in cardiovascular or high-risk populations.

Interaction details: fish oil may have antiplatelet effects at high doses and should be monitored with warfarin or similar anticoagulants, although many studies have not found clinically significant bleeding at 3-6 g/day.

Use in wiki: safety, dose ceiling, product identity, and interaction guardrails for DHA/EPA supplementation.
