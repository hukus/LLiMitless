# Acute guarana ingestion compared to low-dose caffeine for mental performance and vagal modulation

- Source type: double-blind placebo-controlled crossover study.
- Publication: Nutrients, 2024.
- Date checked: 2026-05-03.

This study tested 20 healthy adult volunteers in a crossover design: placebo, 100 mg caffeine, and 500 mg guarana. Independent NSF verification found the guarana product contained 130 mg caffeine rather than the 100 mg listed on the label. Cognitive tasks were Simon and 2N-back at baseline and 60 minutes post-ingestion; a subgroup of 11 also completed physical-fatigue testing.

The trial found no consistent cognitive benefit for guarana or low-dose caffeine. Simon-task response time and accuracy were unaffected by treatment. 2N-back response time improved across time regardless of treatment, and accuracy improved only in the placebo condition. Cognitive measures after physical fatigue were also not improved by guarana or caffeine. HRV did not support a vagal-modulation mechanism.

Use in the wiki: key current confidence governor. It directly tests guarana against low-dose caffeine and placebo and argues against upgrading guarana beyond low, caffeine-confounded, product-specific status.
