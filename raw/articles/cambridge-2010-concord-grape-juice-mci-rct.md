# Concord grape juice supplementation and memory in older adults with MCI-like memory decline

- Source type: randomized placebo-controlled double-blind trial.
- Publication: British Journal of Nutrition, 2010.
- DOI: 10.1017/S0007114509992364.
- Date checked: 2026-05-03.

This preliminary trial enrolled 12 older adults with memory decline but not dementia and randomized them to 12 weeks of 100% Concord grape juice or a matched placebo beverage. Dosing was body-weight based, approximately 6-9 ml/kg/day, split across meals. Welch Foods provided material and funding support, while authors reported no financial interest in the supporting company or study outcome.

The grape juice group improved significantly on a verbal-learning measure, with non-significant enhancement of verbal and spatial recall. There was no appreciable effect on depressive symptoms, weight, or waist circumference. Fasting glucose did not change, but fasting insulin was significantly elevated in the grape-juice group. Both juice and placebo were generally well tolerated, with no consistently reported adverse effect, though some participants noted urination from higher fluid intake or aversion to beverage taste over time.
