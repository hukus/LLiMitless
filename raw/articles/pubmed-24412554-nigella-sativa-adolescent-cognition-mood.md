# Nigella sativa seed, mood, anxiety, and cognition in healthy adolescent males

2014 Journal of Ethnopharmacology randomized controlled trial, PMID 24412554, DOI 10.1016/j.jep.2013.12.050.

Population/design: 48 healthy male adolescents aged 14-17 years at a Bangladesh boarding school, randomly split into placebo and active groups with n=24 per group. The active group received 500 mg Nigella sativa seed once daily for 4 weeks; placebo group received a 500 mg placebo capsule.

Endpoints: modified California Verbal Learning Test-II for cognition, Bond-Lader scale for mood, and State-Trait Anxiety Inventory for anxiety, measured before and after 4 weeks.

Results: no baseline parameter differed significantly between groups. After 4 weeks, the Nigella group showed within-group mood change, but between-group mood change was not statistically significant in the abstract. State anxiety did not differ within or between groups; trait anxiety changed within the Nigella group but not between groups. CVLT-II showed within-group improvements in selected immediate recall trials, short-term free recall, long-term free recall, and long-term cued recall, with between-group differences for trial 5, long-term free recall, and long-term cued recall.

Interpretation: useful adolescent memory/mood-adjacent signal, but not a clean broad cognition proof because several mood/anxiety findings are within-group only and the sample is small, all male, and socially/contextually specific.
