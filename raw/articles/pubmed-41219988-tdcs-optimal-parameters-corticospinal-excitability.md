# What are the optimal transcranial direct current stimulation parameters and design elements to modulate corticospinal excitability? A systematic review and longitudinal meta-analysis

- PMID: 41219988
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41219988/
- Published: 2025-11-11
- Ingested: 2026-04-28

## Summary

2025 systematic review and longitudinal meta-analysis of 84 cohorts (1,709 participants) on tDCS parameter effects on corticospinal excitability.

## Notes

- The strongest ontology value is parameter specificity, not cognition per se: polarity, duration, intensity, frequency, montage, and electrode design all changed the MEP signal.
- Anodal tDCS increased MEP size, with effects lasting up to 120 minutes post-stimulation.
- Consistent anodal effects were seen at durations >= 20 minutes and intensities >= 1.5 mA.
- Cathodal tDCS reduced MEP size for about 15 minutes, with significant effects at durations >= 9 minutes; intensity effects were inconclusive.
- Electrode montage and electrode size/shape mattered, which reinforces that `tDCS` is a field-configuration problem rather than a simple intensity label.
- Useful as a modern parameter-consensus anchor for the device ontology and delivery-validation pages.
