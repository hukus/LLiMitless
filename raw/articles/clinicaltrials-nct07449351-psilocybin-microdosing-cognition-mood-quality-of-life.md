# Psilocybin Microdosing on Cognition, Mood and Quality of Life

- ClinicalTrials.gov ID: NCT07449351
- Study title: Effects of Psilocybin Microdosing on Cognition, Mood and Quality of Life: A Pilot Study
- Status: Not yet recruiting
- Sponsor: Yale University
- Last update posted: 2026-03-04

## Summary

Yale-registered randomized, parallel-arm, double-blind pilot in healthy psychedelic non-users ages 21-40. The study will give 2.0 mg powdered psilocybin derived from Psilocybe cubensis three times weekly for four weeks versus matching placebo, with cognition, mood, subjective well-being, EMA, MRI/NODDI, and task batteries as endpoints. It is protocol evidence only, not efficacy evidence.

## Key protocol points

- Estimated enrollment: 20
- Population: healthy volunteers with no prior psychedelic use
- Primary purpose: basic science
- Intervention schedule: 2.0 mg psilocybin capsules three times weekly for 30 days
- Imaging / cognition readouts: CWMS fMRI, NIH Toolbox Cognition Battery, switching Stroop, Penn Conditional Exclusion, Flanker, Face Name Associated Memory Exam, NODDI, and EMA via MindLamp
- Key exclusions: mood or psychotic disorders, substance use disorder, MRI contraindications, recent concussion, pregnancy, and other neurologic or medical conditions that could compromise performance
- Study start estimate: 2026-04-01
- Primary completion estimate: 2028-04-01

## Interpretation

This is a protocol lead, not outcome evidence. It matters because it shows the field is still actively testing the "healthy microdosing improves cognition and mood" claim in a controlled setting, despite the newest pooled human evidence running negative for broad cognition.
