# L-Carnitine for Cognitive Enhancement in People Without Cognitive Impairment

- PMID: 28349514
- PMCID: PMC6464592
- PubMed Central: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6464592/
- DOI: 10.1002/14651858.CD009374.pub3
- Ingested: 2026-05-03

## Summary

2017 Cochrane review assessing L-carnitine and derivatives, including acetyl-L-carnitine and propionyl-L-carnitine, for cognitive enhancement in people without cognitive impairment. The review required randomized or quasi-randomized trials and excluded MCI, dementia, psychiatric disorders, and other cognitive-impairment conditions.

Only two eligible RCTs were found. One was a crossover trial with 18 participants and no usable cognitive data; the other randomized 400 young adults across four treatments, with extractable cognition data for about 200 participants in the L-carnitine and placebo arms. The review found no evidence of benefit for reaction time, vigilance, immediate memory, or delayed recall after three days of treatment, and rated the evidence very low quality because of poor reporting, short duration, and small usable samples.

## Interpretation

Use this as the healthy-adult gate for carnitine-family cognition claims. Acetyl-L-carnitine may have disease-context and mitochondrial plausibility, but healthy enhancement requires separate proof. The right label is not "proved ineffective"; it is "not supported by adequate healthy-adult evidence."
