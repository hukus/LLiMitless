# Effects of vitamin B12 supplementation on cognitive function, depressive symptoms, and fatigue

- PMID: 33809274
- PMCID: PMC8000524
- DOI: 10.3390/nu13030923
- Published: 2021-03-12
- Source type: systematic review, meta-analysis, and meta-regression of RCTs
- Population: adults without overt B12 deficiency or advanced neurological disorders
- Ingested: 2026-05-03

## Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 16 RCTs with 6,276 participants testing vitamin B12 alone or B12 plus folic acid with or without B6. The review found no evidence for an effect of B12 alone or B-complex supplementation on any cognitive subdomain, no overall effect on depressive symptoms, and too little high-quality fatigue evidence for robust conclusions.

## Notes

- This is a high-quality counterweight to community B12 energy/focus claims in non-deficient people.
- It does not negate treating overt B12 deficiency; it constrains supplementation claims when deficiency or advanced neurological disease is absent.
- Useful for keeping anecdotal B12 brain-fog reports in the deficiency/status lane.
