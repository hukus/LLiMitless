# Cognitive functions and underlying parameters of human brain physiology are associated with chronotype

- PMID: 34344864
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34344864/
- Published: 2021-08
- Study type: human chronotype and brain-stimulation interaction
- Key finding: motor learning and cognitive performance (working memory and attention) were better at the circadian-preferred than the non-preferred time. The study also found stimulation-by-chronotype-by-time-of-day interactions for tDCS after-effects and cortical excitability.

## Notes

- Participants were tested at circadian-preferred and non-preferred times, with fixed sessions at 8:00 and 19:00.
- tDCS sessions were delivered in the morning and evening in randomized order.
- This is the cleanest direct evidence in the current sweep that chronotype should be treated as a response moderator for tDCS timing.
