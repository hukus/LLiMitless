# DailyMed 2026 pyridostigmine bromide tablet label

## Captured takeaways

- DailyMed identifies pyridostigmine bromide tablets as human prescription drug labeling, updated February 2026.
- The label describes pyridostigmine bromide as an orally active cholinesterase inhibitor that permits freer transmission of nerve impulses across the neuromuscular junction.
- The labeled use is treatment of myasthenia gravis.
- The label contraindicates use in mechanical intestinal or urinary obstruction and calls for particular caution in patients with bronchial asthma.
- The warning section emphasizes that pyridostigmine overdosage can cause cholinergic crisis with increasing muscle weakness and possible respiratory-muscle involvement.
- The label notes renal excretion and says lower doses may be required in renal disease, with titration to effect.
- Common overdose-related muscarinic adverse effects include nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, abdominal cramps, increased peristalsis, increased salivation, increased bronchial secretions, miosis, and diaphoresis.
- Nicotinic adverse effects include muscle cramps, fasciculation, and weakness.
- Interpretation for the wiki: pyridostigmine is a prescription cholinergic drug with clear medical safety gates; it should not be treated like a benign choline donor or central memory enhancer.
