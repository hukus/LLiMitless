# Folate as adjunct therapy to SSRI/SNRI for major depressive disorder

- Source type: PubMed abstract / systematic review and meta-analysis
- PMID: 34450256
- Journal: Complementary Therapies in Medicine
- Published: 2021-09
- Checked: 2026-05-15 via PubMed E-utilities

## Summary

Altaf et al. reviewed folate adjunct therapy, including L-methylfolate or folic acid, for major depressive disorder in patients receiving SSRI or SNRI therapy. Six randomized controlled trials met inclusion criteria. Meta-analysis found lower Hamilton Depression Rating Scale scores with adjunctive L-methylfolate or folic acid, improved combined depression scale scores, improved response rate, and a borderline remission-rate improvement. The authors concluded that adjunctive L-methylfolate or folic acid improves depression scale scores, response, and remission rates.

## Use In Wiki

- Supports a formal depression-adjunct folate signal that includes L-methylfolate.
- Keeps the evidence layer mood-state / clinical-depression focused rather than direct cognition focused.
- Useful as a counterweight to purely anecdotal methylfolate / MTHFR forum narratives.

## Caveats

- The meta-analysis pools L-methylfolate and folic acid, so it is not fully form-isolated.
- Outcomes are depression scales, response, and remission, not objective cognition in healthy adults.
- Adjunctive antidepressant context limits transfer to unsupervised methylation stacks.
