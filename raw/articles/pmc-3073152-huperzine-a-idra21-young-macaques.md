# Huperzine A and IDRA-21 on visual recognition memory in young macaques

Open-access macaque study comparing the cholinesterase inhibitor huperzine A and AMPAkine IDRA-21 on visual recognition memory.

Key extracted points:

- The article frames nootropic agents as acting through mechanisms including amplification of cholinergic or glutamatergic excitatory neurotransmission.
- It describes IDRA-21 as an AMPAkine and positive AMPA receptor modulator that increases excitatory synaptic strength by attenuating rapid AMPA receptor desensitization.
- The paper notes earlier rodent and primate work, including oral IDRA-21 dose ranges in rhesus monkeys and a prior report of delayed matching-to-sample improvement.
- In the young macaque visual recognition task, IDRA-21 treatment improved performance, with the largest reported improvement at the longest delay condition.
- Interpretation for the wiki: this reinforces a nonhuman-primate memory signal for IDRA-21, but it remains an animal-cognition source and should be ranked below any controlled human cognition trial.
