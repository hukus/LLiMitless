# The acute and chronic cognitive effects of a sage extract in healthy humans

- Source type: randomized double-blind placebo-controlled parallel-group trial.
- Publication: Nutrients, 2021.
- Date checked: 2026-05-03.

This trial randomized healthy adults aged 30-60 years to 600 mg/day Cognivia, a proprietary combination of Salvia officinalis polyphenol-rich extract and Salvia lavandulaefolia terpenoid essential oil, or placebo. Cognition was assessed acutely at 120 and 240 minutes and again after 29 days.

The active product showed consistent benefits across working-memory and accuracy task outcomes, especially Corsi Blocks, Numeric Working Memory, and Name to Face Recall. The authors noted that the pattern differed somewhat from prior single-Salvia studies and called for direct follow-up comparing single and combined extracts.

The full-text safety section reported that participants guessed active treatment above chance, which is a blinding caveat. Adverse events possibly related to study treatment were more numerous in the sage group, especially headache/migraine reports, although all were mild or moderate except two severe headache reports and symptoms resolved during the study.

Use in the wiki: current product-specific Cognivia anchor for sage. Treat as low-confidence but entity-worthy; do not generalize to all sage tea, raw leaf, essential oil, or Salvia divinorum.
