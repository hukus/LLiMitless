# Effect of tesofensine on bodyweight loss, body composition, and quality of life in obese patients

- PMID: 18950853
- DOI: 10.1016/S0140-6736(08)61525-1
- Journal: The Lancet (2008)
- Trial registration: NCT00394667

## Captured takeaways

- Phase 2 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 203 adults with obesity in five Danish obesity management centers.
- Participants received energy restriction plus tesofensine 0.25 mg, 0.5 mg, 1.0 mg, or placebo once daily for 24 weeks.
- The primary endpoint was percent change in body weight.
- Completion was 161 of 203 participants.
- Mean weight loss after 24 weeks was 2.0% with diet plus placebo, versus 4.5%, 9.2%, and 10.6% with tesofensine 0.25 mg, 0.5 mg, and 1.0 mg.
- Common adverse events included dry mouth, nausea, constipation / hard stools, diarrhea, and insomnia.
- The 0.25 mg and 0.5 mg doses did not significantly increase systolic or diastolic blood pressure versus placebo, but heart rate increased by 7.4 bpm in the 0.5 mg group.
- The authors concluded that 0.5 mg might produce about twice the weight loss of then-approved drugs, but efficacy and safety needed phase 3 confirmation.
- Interpretation for the wiki: strong appetite/weight signal, but not a cognition endpoint; HR/insomnia and appetite suppression are major confounds for brain-fog anecdotes.
