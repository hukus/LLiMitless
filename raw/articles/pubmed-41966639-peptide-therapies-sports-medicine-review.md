# Safety and Efficacy of Approved and Unapproved Peptide Therapies for Musculoskeletal Injuries and Athletic Performance

- PMID: 41966639
- DOI: 10.1007/s40279-026-02437-0
- Journal: Sports Medicine, 2026
- Source type: narrative review of approved and unapproved peptide therapies
- Ingested: 2026-05-04

## Summary

2026 Sports Medicine review covering approved and unapproved peptides marketed for musculoskeletal injury recovery and athletic performance, including MOTS-C, BPC-157, CJC-1295, ipamorelin, SS-31/elamipretide, tesamorelin, thymosin beta-4, and TB-500. The abstract frames gray-market unapproved peptides as operating largely outside regulatory oversight, notes that many have favorable animal-model outcomes but scarce rigorous human safety data, warns of potential serious harm, and explicitly discusses placebo and social-media amplification as part of peptide efficacy perception.

## Signal Use

- Medicinal / formal signal: review-level safety/regulatory context for unapproved peptide therapies, not cognition evidence.
- MOTS-c implication: supports class-level caution that metabolic or tissue-repair animal data do not equal human safety/efficacy.
- Anecdotal signal: social-media amplification is treated as an evidence-quality problem, not as efficacy confirmation.
