# Spearmint extract improves working memory in age-associated memory impairment

- Source type: randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial.
- Publication: Journal of Alternative and Complementary Medicine, 2018.
- Date checked: 2026-05-11.

This study tested a proprietary spearmint extract high in polyphenols including rosmarinic acid in adults with age-associated memory impairment. Ninety participants, mean age about 59 years and 67% female, were randomized to 900 mg/day, 600 mg/day, or placebo for 90 days.

Cognition was assessed with the Cognitive Drug Research system at baseline, day 45, and day 90. The 900 mg/day group improved quality of working memory by 15% and spatial working-memory accuracy by 9% versus placebo. The 900 mg/day group also reported better ability to fall asleep than placebo.

The result is useful for the wiki because it supports a product-specific spearmint-extract cognition signal, but the population was not unrestricted healthy young adults. It belongs in an older-adult / memory-complaint lane and should not be generalized to spearmint tea, peppermint oil, or generic rosmarinic acid.

Use in the wiki: spearmint extract earns a low-confidence entity page beside sage, lemon balm, and rosemary, with product-identity gates.
