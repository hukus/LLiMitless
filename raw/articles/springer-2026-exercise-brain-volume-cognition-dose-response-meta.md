# Dose-response effects and mechanistic pathways linking physical exercise to brain volume and cognition

- Source: European Review of Aging and Physical Activity
- Published: 2026-01-05
- DOI: 10.1186/s11556-025-00398-3
- PROSPERO: CRD42024525635
- Ingested: 2026-05-12

## Summary

Preregistered systematic review and multilevel meta-analysis of 59 randomized controlled trials with 5,099 participants. The review examined whether physical exercise changes brain volume, whether dose-response moderators matter, and whether brain-volume change is linked to cognitive outcomes.

Physical exercise had a small but significant effect on relative brain-volume change versus control. The effect was stronger with older age, longer intervention duration, higher compliance, and sessions around 40 to 60 minutes. Post-hoc analyses suggested the effect more likely reflected preservation of brain volume than large increases. Relative brain-volume change was significantly associated with cognitive improvement, and mediation analyses linked motor performance, muscle function, and cardiorespiratory fitness to cognition through brain-volume change.

## Cognitive-Relevance Extraction

- Evidence layer: systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs.
- Direction: exercise has a small structural-brain/reserve signal that correlates with cognition, strongest in aging and high-compliance contexts.
- Effect details: overall brain-volume effect Hedges g 0.10; exercise-induced relative brain-volume change associated with cognitive function beta 0.20.
- Use in wiki: supports exercise as a reserve-preservation and aging-resilience intervention, not a fast nootropic. It also complements the acute-exercise cognition literature by explaining why longer structural effects may depend on duration, adherence, and age.
- Boundary: structural preservation and cognitive association are not the same as guaranteed healthy-adult enhancement.
