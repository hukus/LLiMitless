# PubMed 41748275 - Perineuronal Nets: Old Structures, New Functions

## Citation

- Tewari BP, Chaunsali L, Sontheimer H. "Perineuronal Nets: Old Structures, New Functions." The Neuroscientist. 2026 Feb 26. DOI: 10.1177/10738584261421180.
- Source type: peer-reviewed review.

## Extracted Claims

- Perineuronal nets are morphologically distinct extracellular-matrix aggregates, especially around fast-spiking GABAergic neurons.
- The review frames PNNs as regulators of synapse stabilization, developmental timing, and sensory-map plasticity.
- The disease discussion highlights PNN disruption or remodeling in epilepsy, glioma, and Alzheimer disease, so PNN changes can be disease-associated rather than simply enhancement-associated.
- Mechanistic themes include altered neuronal firing through membrane-capacitance effects, electrostatic filtering of ions, and links to astrocytic clearance of glutamate and potassium at tripartite synapses.
- The review treats PNNs as potentially targetable, but the targetability claim is mechanistic and disease-contextual, not evidence for human cognitive enhancement.

## Cognition-Relevant Extraction

- Cognitive relevance is indirect: PNNs gate plasticity and circuit stability, and the keyword set includes plasticity, social memory, and Alzheimer disease.
- Human enhancement evidence: none in this source.
- Safety interpretation for the wiki: interventions that destabilize or digest PNN structure should be treated as circuit-level manipulation, not a supplement-like cognitive enhancer.
