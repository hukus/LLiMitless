# PubMed 15374306: Pramiracetam and scopolamine-induced amnesia in healthy volunteers

This 1994 clinical trial evaluated pramiracetam for anti-amnesic effects in a scopolamine challenge model.

Design: two groups of 12 healthy male volunteers, ages 18-42 and 55-65, were randomly assigned to pramiracetam 600 mg twice daily or placebo for 10 days. On day 11 subjects received intramuscular scopolamine hydrobromide 0.5 mg.

Testing: before scopolamine and 1, 3, and 6 hours after scopolamine, subjects completed simple and choice visual reaction time tests, digit symbol substitution, and Rey 15-word short- and long-term verbal memory tests.

Results from the abstract: scopolamine impaired episodic memory and selective attention, while visuo-motor and incidental learning measures were unaffected. Pramiracetam partially reduced the scopolamine-induced amnesic effects in both young and old subjects compared with placebo.

Interpretation for the wiki: this is a real human cognition pharmacology signal, but it is anticholinergic-challenge rescue rather than everyday healthy enhancement.
