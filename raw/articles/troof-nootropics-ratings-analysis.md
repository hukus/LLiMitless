# What I learned gathering thousands of nootropic ratings

- Source type: blog analysis of recommender-system ratings
- Published: 2022-03-23
- Data/code: linked from the post

## Summary

Troof analyzed 36,163 nootropic ratings from 2,802 people, combining 29,387 new ratings with the original SlateStarCodex survey data. The analysis used the SSC 0-10 scale and a random-intercept multilevel Bayesian model to smooth user-level rating differences.

The post is useful as a broad community-signal map, not as causal evidence. It is open-label, self-selected, and vulnerable to expectation effects, but it is still a strong triage source for deciding which compounds deserve controlled follow-up.

## Key signals

- Lifestyle items such as sleep and sport ranked very highly.
- Semax, Cerebrolysin, and BPC-157 landed in the "uncommon-but-great" region of the plots.
- Tianeptine also scored strongly.
- Phenylpiracetam stood out among racetams.
- Phenibut still carried meaningful tolerance and addiction concerns.

## Caveats

- No random allocation.
- No blinding.
- Self-report and selection effects are large.
- Results are best used to reduce search space, not to estimate true effect size.
