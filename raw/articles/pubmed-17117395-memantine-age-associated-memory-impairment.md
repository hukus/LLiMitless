# A double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of memantine in age-associated memory impairment

PubMed-indexed randomized placebo-controlled trial of memantine in 60 adults aged 50-79 with age-associated memory impairment. Memantine was titrated to 20 mg and tested over 90 days with computerized cognitive batteries.

The abstract reports that the primary cognitive effects in this population appeared to be on attention and information processing speed rather than memory, with no adverse-event difference versus placebo. For the wiki, this is a narrow impairment-context signal, not evidence that memantine is a broad healthy-adult enhancer.

Relevance: memantine is common in nootropic communities because of NMDA-antagonist / tolerance-reset claims, but the formal human signal is clinical-state and endpoint-limited.
