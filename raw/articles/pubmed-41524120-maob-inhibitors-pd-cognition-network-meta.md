# Effects of MAO-B inhibitors on cognition in Parkinson disease: systematic network meta-analysis

- PMID: 41524120
- DOI: 10.1002/mdc3.70520
- Journal: Movement Disorders Clinical Practice (ahead of print, 2026)
- Search window: inception through 2025-05-30.

## Captured takeaways

- The review evaluated cognitive outcomes in Parkinson disease patients treated with selegiline, rasagiline, or safinamide.
- Thirteen randomized controlled trials were included.
- Network meta-analysis found only rasagiline significantly improved global cognition versus placebo, with SMD 0.863 and 95% CI 0.064 to 1.663.
- Selegiline and safinamide did not show statistically significant differences versus placebo for global cognition.
- None of the MAO-B inhibitors showed significant effects on specific cognitive subdomains: attention, executive function, memory, language, or visuospatial abilities.
- The authors concluded cognitive impacts of MAO-B inhibitors appear limited beyond motor-symptom management and called for larger, longer domain-specific studies.
- Interpretation for the wiki: the most current class-level synthesis supports a cautious rasagiline-only PD global-cognition signal and argues against upgrading the whole MAO-B class into a healthy-adult nootropic tier.
