# A randomized, controlled clinical trial of plasma exchange with albumin replacement for Alzheimer's disease: Primary results of the AMBAR Study

- PMID: 32715623
- PMCID: PMC7984263
- Source: Alzheimers and Dementia 2020
- Ingested: 2026-04-29

## Summary

Large phase 2b/3 randomized trial of plasma exchange with albumin replacement in mild to moderate Alzheimer's disease. The combined treated group slowed decline on ADCS-ADL and showed a trend toward benefit on ADAS-Cog, with stronger signals in the moderate-AD subgroup and no change in mild AD.

## Notes

- 347 patients were randomized 1:1:1:1 into three active arms and one sham control arm.
- Treatment combined 6 weeks of weekly conventional plasma exchange with a 12-month maintenance phase of monthly low-volume plasma exchange.
- The co-primary endpoint on ADCS-ADL was positive, and ADAS-Cog narrowly missed conventional significance in the combined treated group.
- In the pre-specified moderate-AD subgroup, both ADCS-ADL and ADAS-Cog looked better than placebo; the mild-AD subgroup did not show changes.
- Global measures, including CDR-SB and ADCS-CGIC, also favored treatment.
- Safety burden was real: about 10.6% of procedures had at least one adverse event, central access was riskier than peripheral access, and the paper reports two deaths during the study period without implying simple treatment causality.
