# Acupuncture: Effectiveness and Safety

- Publisher: National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health
- Evidence layer: official safety and effectiveness overview
- Checked: 2026-05-11

## Summary

NCCIH describes acupuncture as a technique in which practitioners insert fine needles into the skin; needles may be manipulated manually or stimulated electrically as electroacupuncture. The page notes that acupuncture is most commonly used in the United States for pain-related conditions.

For safety, NCCIH states that relatively few complications have been reported, but serious adverse effects can occur when acupuncture is not delivered properly, including infection, punctured organs, and central nervous system injury. It also notes that the U.S. FDA regulates acupuncture needles as medical devices and requires that they be sterile and labeled for single use only.
