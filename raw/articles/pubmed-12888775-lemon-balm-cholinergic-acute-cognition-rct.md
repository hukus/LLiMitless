# Melissa officinalis dried-leaf acute cognition RCT with cholinergic screening

PubMed-indexed 2003 randomized placebo-controlled double-blind balanced-crossover study of Melissa officinalis dried leaf selected after in vitro screening for cholinergic receptor-binding activity.

Design: 20 healthy young participants received 600 mg, 1000 mg, and 1600 mg encapsulated dried leaf or placebo at 7-day intervals. Cognition and mood were assessed predose and at 1, 3, and 6 hours postdose with the Cognitive Drug Research battery and Bond-Lader mood scales.

Results: the 1600 mg dose produced the most favorable profile, including improved memory performance and increased calmness at all postdose time points. Lower doses had more unfavorable effects, including decrements in timed-memory speed and rapid visual information-processing task performance.

Mechanism/product note: the selected extract displaced nicotinic and muscarinic receptor ligands in human cerebral cortex tissue but did not show cholinesterase inhibition. The abstract explicitly warns that different preparations from the same plant may have different properties depending on sample preparation.

Relevance: direct cognition RCT supporting product- and dose-specific interpretation for lemon balm.
