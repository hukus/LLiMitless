# Perforating corneal injury in rat and pentadecapeptide BPC 157

- PMID: 25912999
- DOI: 10.1016/j.exer.2015.04.016
- Journal: Exp Eye Res, 2015
- Source type: preclinical rat ophthalmology study
- Ingested: 2026-05-06

## Summary

Rat perforating-corneal-injury study testing topical BPC-157 immediately after injury and then every 8 hours up to 120 hours. The abstract reports faster closure of perforating corneal incisions, earlier negative fluorescein/Seidel tests, faster return of corneal transparency, and less pathologic neovascularization in the ng and microg treatment groups versus controls.

## Key points

- Model: 2 mm perforating corneal incision in rats.
- Intervention: topical BPC-157 at 2 pg/mL, 2 ng/mL, or 2 ug/mL.
- Signal: complete epithelial healing by 72 hours in the 2 ug group and by 96 hours in the 2 ng group.
- Additional signal: fewer aqueous cells and less limbal neovascular extension toward the injured area.
- Limit: preclinical topical-eye-drop model only; no human ocular trial and no cognition endpoint.

## Signal Use

- Ocular-health signal: stronger corneal-wound-healing support than a generic "repair peptide" claim.
- Mechanistic breadth: adds transparency and anti-neovascularization observations to the ocular lane.
- Use limit: cannot be generalized to human eye disease treatment, retina, or cognitive performance.
