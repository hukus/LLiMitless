# Effect of vitamin D on cognitive decline: results from two ancillary studies of the VITAL randomized trial

- Published: 2021
- Source type: randomized trial ancillary cognition studies
- Parent trial: VITAL vitamin D3 2,000 IU/day and fish oil factorial trial
- Population: adults age 60+ without cardiovascular disease or cancer at baseline
- Ingested: 2026-05-03

## Summary

Two VITAL ancillary studies tested vitamin D3 2,000 IU/day against placebo for cognitive decline. A phone-assessment substudy included 3,424 participants over 2.8 years, and an in-person substudy included 794 participants over 2.0 years. The pooled mean difference in annual global cognitive decline for vitamin D3 versus placebo was 0.01 with 95% CI -0.01 to 0.02 and p = 0.39. The study did not observe interaction with baseline 25(OH)D levels.

## Notes

- Major randomized counterweight for general older-adult cognition protection.
- This source argues against assuming vitamin D3 prevents cognitive decline in generally healthy older adults.
- The trial does not settle severe deficiency correction or selected clinical phenotypes, but it constrains broad claims.
