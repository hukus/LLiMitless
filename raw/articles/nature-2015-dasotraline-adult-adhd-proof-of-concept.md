# Dasotraline adult ADHD proof-of-concept trial

- Article: "Dasotraline for the Treatment of Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder: A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled, Proof-of-Concept Trial in Adults"
- Journal: Neuropsychopharmacology (2015)
- DOI: 10.1038/npp.2015.124

## Captured takeaways

- Multicenter randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in adult outpatients with ADHD at 30 U.S. sites from 2012 to 2013.
- Adults were randomized to once-daily evening dasotraline 4 mg/day, dasotraline 8 mg/day, or placebo for 4 weeks.
- Dasotraline is described as a potent dopamine and norepinephrine transporter inhibitor, with weaker serotonin-transporter inhibition.
- The primary endpoint was change in ADHD Rating Scale Version IV total score at week 4.
- The 8 mg/day arm significantly improved ADHD RS-IV total score versus placebo; the 4 mg/day arm was directionally positive but did not reach the primary comparison.
- CGI-S and selected WRAADDS attention/disorganization measures also moved in favor of dasotraline.
- A computerized cognitive assessment battery measured attention, working memory, and episodic memory, but the article reports no significant main effects for dasotraline on those cognitive measures.
- Frequent adverse events were insomnia, decreased appetite, nausea, and dry mouth. Discontinuation due to adverse events was 10.3% at 4 mg/day, 27.8% at 8 mg/day, and 1.8% with placebo.
- Pharmacokinetic notes include slow absorption, long half-life, and approximate steady state after 2 weeks, which makes sleep and accumulation central interpretation gates.
- Interpretation for the wiki: dasotraline is a useful ADHD/DNRI comparator and a cautionary example where symptom-scale signal did not translate into direct cognitive-battery benefit.
