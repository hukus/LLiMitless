# Soybean-derived phosphatidylserine improves memory function of elderly Japanese subjects with memory complaints

## Bibliographic snapshot

- PubMed / PMC record for Kato-Kataoka et al., Journal of Clinical Biochemistry and Nutrition, 2010.
- PubMed ID: 21103034.
- PMCID: PMC2966935.
- DOI: 10.3164/jcbn.10-62.

## Study design

- Double-blind randomized controlled study of soybean-derived phosphatidylserine made from soybean lecithin by enzymatic reaction with L-serine.
- Participants: 78 elderly Japanese people aged 50-69 years with mild cognitive impairment / memory complaints.
- Arms: Soy-PS 100 mg/day, Soy-PS 300 mg/day, or placebo.
- Duration: 6 months.

## Results captured

- The abstract reports no differences in blood markers or vital signs and no observed side effects caused by Soy-PS.
- Neuropsychological test scores increased similarly across all groups, including placebo.
- In the subgroup with relatively low baseline scores, memory scores in PS-treated groups increased against baseline while placebo remained unchanged.
- Reported memory improvements were mostly attributed to delayed verbal recall.

## Wiki interpretation

- This is more relevant to modern supplements than the older bovine cortex studies because it used soy-derived PS.
- The trial is subgroup-sensitive and not a clean broad win: overall practice/placebo improvement was visible, and the signal was concentrated in lower-baseline subjects.
- It supports an older-adult / memory-complaint branch, not rested healthy-adult enhancement.
- Form, dose, baseline cognition, and delayed verbal recall should be tracked before ranking phosphatidylserine against choline donors or omega-3 products.
