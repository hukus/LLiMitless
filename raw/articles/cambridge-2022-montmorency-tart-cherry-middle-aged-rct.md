# Polyphenol-rich Montmorency tart cherries and cognition in middle-aged adults

- Source type: randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial.
- Publication: British Journal of Nutrition, 2022.
- DOI: 10.1017/S0007114522000460.
- Date checked: 2026-05-03.

This 3-month parallel trial randomized middle-aged adults, mean age about 48 years, to 30 ml Montmorency tart-cherry concentrate twice daily or an isoenergetic placebo. The trial included 25 participants per arm and assessed computerized cognitive tasks, visual analogue mood ratings, near-infrared spectroscopy during the cognitive battery, subjective sleep and health questionnaires, and plasma metabolomics.

After 3 months, the Montmorency tart-cherry group had higher digit-vigilance accuracy, fewer false alarms, higher alertness ratings, and lower mental-fatigue ratings versus placebo. The article's discussion reports no influence on cerebral blood flow, sleep, or health measures, and frames the cognitive signal as sustained-attention / anti-fatigue rather than broad memory enhancement.

Funding and conflict notes in the article report support from Northumbria University and the Cherry Research Committee for a doctoral studentship, with external funders reported as having no role in study design, data collection, analysis, publication decision, or manuscript preparation; authors declared no conflicts.

Use in the wiki: direct tart-cherry cognition anchor. Treat as low-confidence and product-specific because the exposure is Montmorency concentrate, the effect is narrow, and replication remains limited.
