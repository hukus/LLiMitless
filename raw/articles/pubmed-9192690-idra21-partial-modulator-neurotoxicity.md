# IDRA-21 partial AMPA-desensitization modulator and neurotoxicity comparison

1997 PNAS paper comparing IDRA-21 and cyclothiazide in neonatal rat cerebellar granule neurons.

Key extracted points:

- The paper studied IDRA-21 and cyclothiazide as modulators that reduce rapid AMPA receptor desensitization.
- Both compounds increased AMPA receptor function, but IDRA-21 had lower intrinsic activity and produced shorter calcium transients than cyclothiazide in the model.
- Cyclothiazide plus AMPA was strongly neurotoxic across the tested concentration range, whereas IDRA-21 up to 100 uM was reported as devoid of neurotoxicity in that culture setup.
- The authors argued that cognition-relevant doses in animals were far below marginal neurotoxicity concentrations in cultured neurons.
- Interpretation for the wiki: this supports why IDRA-21 looked safer than cyclothiazide in early models, but it is not human safety evidence and does not remove seizure, excitotoxicity, ischemia, or gray-market sourcing concerns.
