# Unraveling the neurophysiological correlates of phase-specific enhancement of motor memory consolidation via slow-wave closed-loop targeted memory reactivation

- DOI: 10.1038/s41467-025-57602-2
- Journal: Nature Communications (2025)
- Published: 18 March 2025
- Design: within-subject, preregistered study in 31 healthy young adults

## Captured takeaways

- Slow-wave closed-loop targeted memory reactivation (CL-TMR) compared up-phase cueing, down-phase cueing, and a no-reactivation control during overnight sleep.
- Up-phase cueing produced greater overnight motor-performance improvement than down-phase cueing.
- Up-phase cueing also increased slow-oscillation amplitude and peak-nested sigma power.
- Task-related fMRI changes strengthened striato-motor and hippocampal network activity and segregation.
- The result is a stronger phase-specific behavioral signal than the older broad "closed-loop sleep cueing" framing, but it remains a narrow motor-learning result in young healthy adults.

## Interpretation for the wiki

This paper is the best recent direct evidence that CL-TMR phase matters for behavior, not just for sleep physiology. It supports the branch-level claim that timing is part of the dose, while also showing that the effect is still task-specific and not a general cognition win.
