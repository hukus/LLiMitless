# Histidine-containing dipeptide supplementation improves delayed recall: a systematic review and meta-analysis

- PMID: 38013229
- DOI: 10.1093/nutrit/nuad135
- Published: 2024-10-01
- Ingested: 2026-05-11

## Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials of histidine-containing dipeptides, including carnosine, anserine, beta-alanine, and related interventions, on human cognitive performance. The search covered databases from 1965 through 2022, screened 2,653 abstracts, assessed 94 full texts, and meta-analyzed 10 articles.

The pooled positive result was specific: Wechsler Memory Scale delayed recall improved with histidine-containing dipeptide treatment, with weighted mean difference 1.5 points, 95% CI 0.6 to 2.5, p < 0.01. Other endpoints did not clearly improve, including ADAS-Cog, MMSE, WAIS Digit Span Backward, WAIS Digit Span Forward, and WMS immediate recall.

## Relevance

This is the main class-level anchor for [[anserine-carnosine]], [[beta-alanine]], [[essence-of-chicken]], and the broader histidine-dipeptide branch. It supports a low-to-moderate delayed-recall signal but does not support broad attention, working memory, or global cognition claims.

## Caveats

- Trial interventions were heterogeneous.
- The strongest pooled result is delayed recall, not broad cognition.
- The authors call for longer studies, early-impairment cohorts, and better carnosine-dose standardization.
- Results should not be transferred automatically to ordinary meat intake, preworkout beta-alanine, or every chicken-extract product.
