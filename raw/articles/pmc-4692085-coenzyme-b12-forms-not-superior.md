# Coenzyme vitamin B12 forms are not globally superior to cyanocobalamin

- Citation anchor: Obeid et al., 2015, Molecular Nutrition & Food Research
- Source type: review / form-comparison argument
- Checked: 2026-05-18

## Summary

This review argues against the marketing claim that coenzyme B12 forms are intrinsically superior to cyanocobalamin for prevention or treatment. It discusses cyanocobalamin, hydroxocobalamin, methylcobalamin, and 5-deoxyadenosylcobalamin in the context of absorption, conversion, tissue distribution, and clinical use. Its practical position is that the appropriate B12 form depends on purpose, route, and clinical context rather than a universal "natural form" hierarchy.

For this wiki, the review is useful as a counterweight to methylation-forum claims that hydroxycobalamin, adenosylcobalamin, or methylcobalamin should automatically outperform cyanocobalamin. It also supports keeping methylcobalamin evidence separate from hydroxy/adenosyl/cyano-B12 anecdotes.

## Wiki Interpretation

- Use as a form-equivalence and marketing-caution source for [[hydroxy-adenosyl-cyano-b12]].
- Do not treat "active", "coenzyme", "methylated", "non-methyl", or "natural" B12 labels as cognition evidence.
- Product form may matter for tolerability, deficiency treatment logistics, rare clinical contexts, or individual response, but the form label is not a healthy-adult cognition endpoint.
