# NCCIH peppermint oil usefulness and safety

- Source type: NCCIH safety and evidence monograph.
- Last updated by source: May 2025.
- Date checked: 2026-05-11.

NCCIH frames peppermint oil primarily around digestive uses such as IBS, with much less evidence for other indications. It notes that peppermint oil is the concentrated essential oil from peppermint flowering parts and leaves, distinct from peppermint leaf or tea.

For safety, NCCIH says peppermint oil appears safe when taken orally or applied topically in commonly used doses and has been used safely in multiple clinical trials. Possible oral side effects include heartburn, nausea, abdominal pain, dry mouth, and rare allergic reactions. Enteric coating is often used to reduce heartburn. NCCIH also notes that aromatherapy inhalation is possibly safe, but reliable information is limited, and peppermint oil should not be applied to the faces of infants or young children because menthol inhalation can cause serious effects.
