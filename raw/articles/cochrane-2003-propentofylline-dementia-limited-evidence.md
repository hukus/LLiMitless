# Cochrane 2003: Propentofylline for dementia limited evidence summary

Cochrane's plain-language summary for CD002853 says propentofylline is a putatively neuroprotective agent that inhibits adenosine uptake and phosphodiesterase. It notes that randomized controlled trials existed, but data were available from only a limited number of studies.

The summary reports limited evidence that propentofylline may benefit cognitive and global function in people with Alzheimer's disease or vascular dementia, but emphasizes that Aventis, the manufacturing company, did not release information about unpublished studies, preventing a comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis.

Detailed review notes in the summary: only four of nine included studies had detailed reports. Significant effects favoring propentofylline were reported for cognition at 3, 6, and 12 months, including MMSE at 12 months; severity of dementia; activities of daily living; and global assessment at 3 months but not later. Tolerability data were minimal, and dropouts at 12 months favored placebo.

Authors' conclusion: evidence may suggest benefit, but the meta-analyses were far from satisfactory because unpublished information on another 1200 randomized-trial patients existed but was unavailable.

Interpretation for the wiki: propentofylline has an old dementia signal, but the data-release limitation is central and keeps confidence low.
