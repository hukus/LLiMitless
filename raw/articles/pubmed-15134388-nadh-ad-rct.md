# PubMed 15134388 stabilized oral NADH Alzheimer's randomized trial

This 2004 Drugs under Experimental and Clinical Research trial evaluated stabilized oral reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NADH) for cognitive functioning in Alzheimer's disease.

Design: randomized, placebo-controlled, matched-pairs, double-blind, 6-month study. Patients with probable Alzheimer's disease (n=26) received stabilized oral NADH 10 mg/day or placebo. Twelve subject pairs were matched for age and baseline Mattis Dementia Rating Scale (MDRS) and Mini Mental State Examination scores.

Result: after 6 months, NADH-treated subjects reportedly showed no evidence of progressive cognitive deterioration and had significantly higher total MDRS scores than placebo (p<0.05). PubMed snippet-level details also note better verbal fluency and visual-constructional performance in some secondary/subscale analyses, while attention, memory, and clinician dementia-severity ratings did not differ.

Interpretation for the wiki: this is a small older AD trial with a disease-state signal, not healthy-adult cognition proof. It must be read beside the negative open-label dementia study and the absence of modern replication.
