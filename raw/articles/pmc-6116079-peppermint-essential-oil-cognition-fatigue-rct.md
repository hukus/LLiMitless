# Peppermint essential oil cognitive-demand trial

- Source type: randomized double-blind placebo-controlled balanced crossover trial with in-vitro pre-screening.
- Publication: Nutrients, 2018.
- Date checked: 2026-05-11.

This study screened Mentha spicata and Mentha piperita essential oils for brain-relevant in-vitro properties, then selected a peppermint essential oil with nicotinic/GABA_A receptor-binding, acetylcholinesterase-inhibitory, and calcium-signaling properties for human testing.

Twenty-four healthy adults, mean age about 25 years, consumed single encapsulated doses of placebo, 50 uL peppermint essential oil, or 100 uL peppermint essential oil. Cognitive and mood assessments were collected pre-dose and at 1, 3, and 6 hours after dosing. The higher 100 uL dose improved Rapid Visual Information Processing accuracy at 1 and 3 hours. Both doses attenuated mental fatigue and improved Serial 3s performance at 3 hours.

The signal is acute, small, and route-specific. It concerns encapsulated peppermint essential oil, not inhaled peppermint scent, peppermint tea, or generic mint products.

Use in the wiki: peppermint essential oil deserves low-confidence acute cognitive-demand coverage, with strong route and safety separation from aromatherapy and culinary mint.
