# Frontiers 2025 pyridostigmine hand-grip strength in ME/CFS

## Captured takeaways

- Schlomer et al. studied a single 30 mg pyridostigmine dose in 20 patients with post-infectious ME/CFS.
- The design was within-subject rather than randomized: baseline hand-grip strength and orthostatic function were assessed without pyridostigmine, then repeated at a second visit with pyridostigmine.
- Sixteen of 20 patients improved hand-grip strength one hour after pyridostigmine.
- Without pyridostigmine, repeated hand-grip strength declined over one hour; after pyridostigmine, maximum and mean hand-grip strength increased, with second-session force about 1.5-fold higher than the no-pyridostigmine comparison.
- Passive standing testing was completed in 13 patients. Orthostatic heart-rate increase was lower one hour after pyridostigmine.
- The authors state that controlled clinical trials are needed for symptom improvement, clinical outcome, and side-effect assessment.
- The discussion explicitly points to the ongoing Brigham and Women's Hospital LIFT trial, NCT06366724.
- Interpretation for the wiki: useful objective short-term ME/CFS physiology signal, especially for exertional intolerance and orthostatic phenotype, but no direct cognitive endpoint and no healthy-adult enhancement inference.
