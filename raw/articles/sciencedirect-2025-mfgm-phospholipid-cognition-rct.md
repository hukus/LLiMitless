## Summary

Full-text source for the 2025 MFGM phospholipid RCT already represented by the PubMed raw note. The trial enrolled 263 adults aged 55-75 years with subjective memory complaints. Participants were randomized to powdered milk fortified with low-dose MFGM phospholipids (1.7 g phospholipids/day), powdered milk fortified with high-dose MFGM phospholipids (4.0 g phospholipids/day), or rice starch control for 16 weeks.

The primary cognitive outcome was RBANS, and the secondary cognitive outcome was COMPASS. Neither showed significant treatment effects. The high-dose group did show better-maintained subjective alertness during cognitive testing at endpoint on the Bond-Lader Visual Analogue Scales.

Biomarker changes were more interesting than cognitive outcomes. TNF-alpha was reduced in the high-dose group. GABA and serotonin increased in both MFGM groups. Cholesterol and LDL were reduced in both MFGM groups. Active B12 increased, and homocysteine tended to decrease in the high-dose group.

Interpretation: this is a useful negative/weak cognition anchor. The trial is large for a nutraceutical cognition study and directly targets older adults with subjective memory complaints, but primary and secondary cognitive batteries were null. The best current read is alertness during testing and cardiometabolic/inflammatory biomarker support, not demonstrated memory or global-cognition efficacy.

Product identity: the intervention used fortified powdered milk rather than a simple capsule. This means results should be mapped to the dairy matrix and phospholipid dose rather than to generic "MFGM," milk, butter, phosphatidylserine, phosphatidylcholine, sphingomyelin, or lecithin.
