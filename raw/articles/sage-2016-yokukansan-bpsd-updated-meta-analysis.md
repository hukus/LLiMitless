# Yokukansan in the Treatment of Behavioral and Psychological Symptoms of Dementia: An Updated Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials

- Published: 2016-08-01 online / 2016-09-06 issue
- Journal: Journal of Alzheimer's Disease
- DOI: 10.3233/JAD-160418
- Evidence layer: RCT meta-analysis in dementia behavioral symptoms

## Summary

This updated meta-analysis included five randomized controlled trials with 381 patients with behavioral and psychological symptoms of dementia. Compared with placebo plus usual care or usual care controls, yokukansan reduced BPSD total scores overall, with a standardized mean difference of -0.32 and no observed heterogeneity in the main analysis. Subscale benefits were reported for delusions, hallucinations, and agitation/aggression.

The cognition boundary is the important part for the wiki: MMSE scores did not differ between yokukansan and placebo/usual-care groups. The authors also reported no significant differences in all-cause discontinuation, discontinuation due to adverse events, or adverse-event incidence, but this should be paired with later hypokalemia/pseudoaldosteronism safety data because Yokukansan contains Glycyrrhiza/licorice.

## Wiki Use

- Use for [[yokukansan]] as a behavioral-symptom dementia signal, not a cognition-positive signal.
- Keep it separate from healthy-adult cognition and from formula-level memory candidates.
