# Oral minocycline therapy as first-line treatment in ME and long COVID pilot

2024 eNeurologicalSci open-label pilot study of oral minocycline in 55 patients with myalgic encephalomyelitis that emerged during the Corona era, including 19 long-COVID patients and 5 post-vaccination-sequelae patients. The regimen was 100 mg twice on day 1, then 100 mg daily for 41 days.

Four patients (7%) discontinued in the first few days because of acute nausea and/or dizziness. Among 51 completers, 41 (80%) had a performance-status improvement of at least 2 points. The response was stronger when disease duration was under 6 months: 27/30 (90%) responded, and shorter disease duration was associated with favorable response. In the long-COVID subgroup, 16/18 completers (89%) had favorable effects.

The paper reported significant improvement in fatigue, post-exertional malaise, unrefreshing sleep, brain fog, disequilibrium, orthostatic intolerance, and neuropathic pain. It is uncontrolled, single-clinician, symptom-rated, and vulnerable to selection/placebo/regression effects, so it is a hypothesis-generating brain-fog signal rather than proof.

Relevance: strongest formal brain-fog clearance source for [[minocycline]], but low-confidence because it is not randomized or blinded.
