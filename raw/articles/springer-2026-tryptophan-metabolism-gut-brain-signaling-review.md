# Molecular mechanisms and therapeutic potential of tryptophan metabolism in gut-brain signaling transduction

- Journal: Journal of Neuroinflammation
- Publication date: 2026-03-12
- Study type: open-access narrative review

## Captured takeaways

- The review frames tryptophan metabolism as a gut-brain-axis signaling interface spanning kynurenine, indole, and serotonin pathways.
- It emphasizes rate-limiting enzymes including IDO, TDO, KMO, KAT, tryptophanase, and tryptophan hydroxylase.
- It describes bidirectional gut-microbiome and host control over tryptophan metabolism, including microbial indole production and host IDO regulation.
- The review links disrupted tryptophan-metabolite patterns to IBS, IBD, depression, Alzheimer's disease, schizophrenia, and Parkinson's disease.
- The therapeutic strategies discussed include fecal microbiota transplantation, probiotics, metabolite administration, and enzyme inhibitors, but this is a translational review rather than a cognition-outcome trial.
- Interpretation for the wiki: useful gut-microbiome / IDO-TDO / enzyme-flux context for the kynurenine branch, not a direct cognition-efficacy source.
