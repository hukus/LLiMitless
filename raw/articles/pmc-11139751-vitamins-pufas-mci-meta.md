# Effects of vitamins and polyunsaturated fatty acids on cognitive function in older adults with mild cognitive impairment

- PMCID: PMC11139751
- DOI: 10.1007/s00394-024-03324-y
- Published: 2024-02-01
- Source type: systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
- Population: older adults with mild cognitive impairment
- Ingested: 2026-05-03

## Summary

Open-access meta-analysis of 16 RCTs in older adults with MCI. Vitamins affected global cognition, memory, and attention in pooled analyses, and PUFAs affected memory and attention. Single folic-acid supplementation had the largest reported global-cognition signal among vitamin comparisons, followed by folic acid plus B12 and folic acid plus DHA. The authors rated the evidence as low and called for further trials.

## Notes

- Current support for including the folate/B-vitamin lane in the molecule atlas.
- Low evidence intensity and mixed included interventions mean the result should not become a broad recommendation.
- The analysis partly overlaps with folic acid, B-vitamin, DHA, and vitamin D branches, so attribution remains difficult.
