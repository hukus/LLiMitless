# StatPearls Alpha-Lipoic Acid

- Source: NCBI Bookshelf / StatPearls.
- Last update: 2024-01-26.
- Ingested: 2026-05-03.

## Summary

StatPearls describes alpha-lipoic acid (ALA) as a mitochondrially synthesized caprylic-acid-derived antioxidant and cofactor in nutrient breakdown, also available as oral supplement or intravenous preparation. It is discussed mainly for oxidative-stress-associated disorders, diabetic neuropathy, metabolic syndrome, and antioxidant / anti-inflammatory mechanisms.

Mechanisms include reactive oxygen species scavenging, regeneration of antioxidants such as vitamins C and E, increased glutathione synthesis, and modulation of inflammatory signaling. Pharmacokinetic details are not well established.

Safety discussion says ALA is generally considered safe at common doses, with headache, heartburn, nausea, and vomiting as common adverse effects. It also cautions about liver disease, heavy alcohol use, diabetes / blood-glucose lowering, thyroid disorder, thiamine deficiency, rare intoxication, and severe overdose presentations including seizures, metabolic acidosis, and multiorgan complications.

## Interpretation

Use this as the mechanism and safety-gate source for alpha-lipoic acid. It supports redox/metabolic plausibility and neuropathy-context relevance, but it does not establish cognitive enhancement.
