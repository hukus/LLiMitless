# NCCIH: Sage usefulness and safety

- Source type: NCCIH consumer/professional safety summary.
- Last updated by source: April 2025.
- Date checked: 2026-05-03.

NCCIH distinguishes common sage (Salvia officinalis) and Spanish sage (Salvia lavandulaefolia) from the broader Salvia genus. It notes that sage is promoted for memory and other uses, but only small amounts of research exist, so definite conclusions cannot be reached.

For cognition, NCCIH states that a few studies suggest common sage, Spanish sage, or their combination may improve memory or other cognitive-test scores in healthy people, but the evidence base is small and Alzheimer's disease evidence is uncertain.

For safety, NCCIH says sage is likely safe in food amounts and larger amounts have been used safely for up to 8 weeks in studies. It cautions that some species, including common sage, contain thujone, which can be toxic in large amounts; therefore high doses or long-term use may be unsafe.
