# MAO-B inhibitors and cognitive functions in Parkinson disease review

- PMID: 36065929
- PMCID: PMC10286595
- DOI: 10.2174/1570159X20666220905102144
- Journal: Current Neuropharmacology (2023)

## Captured takeaways

- The review focuses on selegiline, rasagiline, and safinamide effects on cognition in Parkinson disease.
- It frames PD cognitive symptoms as stage-dependent and tied to dopaminergic plus non-dopaminergic transmitter systems.
- In early non-fluctuating PD, selegiline and rasagiline were described as improving some prefrontal executive functions in a pattern similar to other dopaminergic drugs.
- In more advanced fluctuating PD, some executive-function benefit may persist, but worsening prefrontal inhibitory control can also occur.
- High-dose safinamide was described as improving inhibitory control in fluctuating PD, plausibly through a dual dopaminergic and glutamatergic mechanism.
- The review is useful as a mechanism and study-map source, but it does not convert MAO-B inhibitors into healthy-adult cognition enhancers.
- Interpretation for the wiki: MAO-B cognition claims should be routed as Parkinson-state, executive-domain, dopamine/glutamate modulation hypotheses rather than broad brain-fog treatments.
