# ClinicalTrials.gov 2026: NCT00001933 nefiracetam in Alzheimer's disease

ClinicalTrials.gov lists NCT00001933, "Nefiracetam in the Treatment of Alzheimer's Disease" / "Nefiracetam Therapy of Alzheimer's Type Dementia", as a completed NIH/NINDS phase 2 interventional treatment study. The study started in July 1999, completed in January 2002, was last updated on March 4, 2008, and has no posted results in the registry as of the 2026-05-03 check.

The registry summary says the study was designed to test whether nefiracetam could safely improve memory, thinking, and activities of daily living in patients with mild-to-moderate intellectual impairment due to Alzheimer's disease. Enrollment is listed as 50. The trial used a 20-week double-blind placebo-controlled parallel-group design, with an optional continuation period.

Mechanistic framing in the registry: nefiracetam was described as enhancing nicotinic acetylcholine receptor activity and accelerating acetylcholine turnover/release, distinct from cholinesterase inhibitors. Planned efficacy assessment used standardized neuropsychological instruments in mild-to-moderate Alzheimer-type dementia.

Safety-relevant eligibility details included exclusion of severe renal, hepatic, cardiovascular, gastrointestinal, or hematological illness, centrally acting anticholinergics and selected CYP3A4-related agents, medical contraindications to cholinergics, known nefiracetam hypersensitivity, and male patients interested in conceiving children because of potential adverse effects on spermatogenesis.

Interpretation for the wiki: this registry establishes formal clinical interest and dosing-period context, but the absence of posted results prevents any efficacy upgrade.
