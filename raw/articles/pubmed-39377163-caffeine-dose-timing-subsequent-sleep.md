# Dose and timing effects of caffeine on subsequent sleep: a randomized clinical crossover trial

- PMID: 39377163
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39377163/
- Ingested: 2026-04-29

## Summary

Placebo-controlled, double-blind, randomized crossover trial in 23 healthy adult males with moderate habitual caffeine intake. Participants completed seven conditions separated by 48-hour washout: placebo plus 100 mg or 400 mg caffeine given 12, 8, or 4 hours before bedtime.

The 100 mg dose did not significantly change objective or subjective sleep versus placebo. The 400 mg dose worsened sleep when taken within 12 hours of bedtime, with stronger effects closer to bedtime. Subjective sleep quality was also worse at 4 hours pre-bedtime.

## Notes

- Useful for schedule logic because it shows dose and timing materially change the next sleep period.
- The study is more about sleep carryover than cognition, but that makes it directly relevant to anti-tolerance or periodization claims that ignore next-night sleep disruption.
