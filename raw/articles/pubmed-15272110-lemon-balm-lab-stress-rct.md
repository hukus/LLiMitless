# Melissa officinalis laboratory-induced stress RCT

PubMed-indexed 2004 double-blind placebo-controlled randomized balanced-crossover experiment in 18 healthy volunteers.

Design: participants received 300 mg Melissa officinalis extract, 600 mg extract, or placebo on separate days separated by a 7-day washout. Mood and cognitive performance were assessed before dosing and 1 hour after dosing during a 20-minute Defined Intensity Stressor Simulation battery.

Results: 600 mg ameliorated negative mood effects of the stressor, increasing self-rated calmness and reducing self-rated alertness. The 300 mg dose significantly increased mathematical-processing speed without reducing accuracy.

Relevance: formal support for lemon balm as a stress-buffering calm-focus candidate, with alertness reduction as a tradeoff.
