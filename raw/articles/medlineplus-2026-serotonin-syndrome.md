# Serotonin Syndrome

- Publisher: MedlinePlus Medical Encyclopedia
- Source type: official medical encyclopedia / safety overview
- Checked: 2026-05-03
- Ingested: 2026-05-03

## Summary

MedlinePlus describes serotonin syndrome as a potentially life-threatening reaction caused by too much serotonin. It is most often seen when two or more serotonergic medicines or drugs are taken together, and risk is higher when starting or increasing serotonergic therapy.

The page lists serotonergic drug combinations involving SSRIs, SNRIs, triptans, MAOIs, meperidine, fentanyl, dextromethorphan, and several recreational drugs. Symptoms can include agitation, abnormal eye movements, diarrhea, tachycardia, hypertension, hallucinations, hyperthermia, nausea/vomiting, overactive reflexes, tremor, and rapid blood-pressure changes. Treatment centers on stopping causative agents, observation, supportive care, benzodiazepines, and cyproheptadine in some cases.

## Interpretation For 5-HTP

The page is not a 5-HTP efficacy source. It is a general safety source for serotonergic stacking. In this wiki it supports keeping 5-HTP away from antidepressants, MAOIs, triptans, dextromethorphan, St. John's wort, methylene blue, and other serotonin-raising agents unless medical supervision is explicit.
