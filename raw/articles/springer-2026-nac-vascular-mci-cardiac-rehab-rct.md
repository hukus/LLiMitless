# N-acetylcysteine in vascular MCI during cardiac rehabilitation, 2026 RCT

Open-access Alzheimer's Research & Therapy early manuscript published 2026-04-27. Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial in 59 participants with possible vascular MCI enrolled in an exercise-based cardiac rehabilitation program.

Intervention: oral N-acetylcysteine 2400 mg/day for 24 weeks versus placebo. Primary endpoint was standardized executive-function composite; secondary domains included verbal memory, working memory, non-verbal memory, attention, and global cognition.

Result: executive function improved over time across the cohort, but NAC did not add benefit over placebo on the primary endpoint or other cognitive domains. Adverse events were comparable. This is a current negative controlled cognition anchor for NAC in a vascular-MCI plus rehabilitation context.

Use in wiki: route NAC as low / phenotype-specific rather than broad nootropic. The trial tested NAC on top of a strong behavioral/rehab background, so it is also useful for separating additive supplement effects from exercise-driven improvement.
