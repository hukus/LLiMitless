# Auricular Ultrasonic Vagus Nerve Stimulation: Effectiveness of Blinding and Occurrence of Adverse Effects in Healthy Volunteers

- PMID: 41008346
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41008346/
- Ingested: 2026-04-28

## Summary

Single-blind randomized study in healthy volunteers comparing true auricular ultrasonic vagus nerve stimulation with a sham device. The paper reports high James blinding-index values in both arms and only mild, transient skin sensations under the transducer, showing that carefully matched sham devices can work well.

## Notes

- Useful counterexample to the assumption that all neuromodulation is obviously unblindable.
- Helpful for the "matching matters" branch of the detector.
