# Cognitive Behavioral Therapy for Insomnia in People With Chronic Disease: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis

- PMID: 40982264
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40982264/
- JAMA Internal Medicine DOI: 10.1001/jamainternmed.2025.4610
- Published online: 2025-09-22

## Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of 67 randomized clinical trials (5232 participants) testing CBT-I in adults with chronic disease and insomnia.

The review found substantial improvements in sleep-focused outcomes: insomnia severity, sleep efficiency, and sleep onset latency all improved with moderate-to-large effects, and treatment acceptability was high.

## Notes

This is strong anchor evidence that CBT-I works well as an insomnia treatment in chronic disease populations, but it is still sleep-outcome evidence rather than direct nootropic evidence.

The result is useful for framing CBT-I as a state-stabilizing intervention that can plausibly improve daytime functioning indirectly through better sleep continuity and lower hyperarousal.
