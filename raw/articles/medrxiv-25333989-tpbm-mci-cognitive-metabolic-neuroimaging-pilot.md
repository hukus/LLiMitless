# A Multimodal Evaluation of Transcranial Photobiomodulation in Mild Cognitive Impairment: Cognitive, Metabolic, and Neuroimaging Outcomes of a Pilot Randomized Controlled Trial

- medRxiv preprint, DOI 10.1101/2025.08.19.25333989; version posted 2025-08-24.
- Single-blind, randomized, sham-controlled pilot trial (NCT05563298).
- Population: adults aged 50+ with mild cognitive impairment.
- Sample: 20 randomized participants, 10 active and 10 sham.
- Intervention: home-based tPBM using visually identical near-infrared devices aimed at default-mode-network hubs and the olfactory bulb.
- Active dose: pulsed 810 nm light for 20 minutes per session, 6 days per week for 6 weeks.
- Sham dose: light for 2 seconds per session.
- Outcomes included global cognition, episodic memory, processing speed, sleep quality, neuropsychiatric symptoms, blood lactate and pyruvate, lactate-to-pyruvate ratio, DMN functional connectivity, cerebral blood flow, MRI atrophy markers, and blood biomarkers of neurodegeneration and inflammation.
- Adherence was high in both arms, and no serious adverse events occurred.
- Mild adverse events were reported in both groups, including transient scalp or head discomfort, headache or migraine, dryness, low mood, and fatigue.
- Active tPBM improved MMSE and CVLT-II long-delay recognition versus sham.
- Active tPBM increased serum pyruvate and lactate, lowered the lactate-to-pyruvate ratio, increased DMN connectivity, and reduced plasma IL-6.
- The authors interpret the pattern as preliminary support for safety, feasibility, and a mitochondria-targeted anti-inflammatory mechanism in early cognitive decline.
