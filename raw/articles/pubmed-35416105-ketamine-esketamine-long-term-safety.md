# Long-term safety of ketamine and esketamine in treatment of depression

- PMID: 35416105
- DOI: 10.1080/14740338.2022.2066651
- Journal: Expert Opinion on Drug Safety, 2022.
- Review scope: long-term safety of racemic ketamine and esketamine as psychiatric therapies, drawing on experimental data and phase 3 esketamine trial programs.

## Captured takeaways

- The review describes ketamine as a rapid-acting antidepressant and notes FDA approval of esketamine for treatment-resistant depression in 2019.
- Common ketamine/esketamine adverse effects were summarized as generally transient and self-limited in clinical depression use, including dissociation, nausea, headache, elevated heart rate, and elevated blood pressure.
- The review flags lower urinary tract symptoms as an esketamine concern, while noting that severe bladder pathology was not reported in patients receiving ketamine/esketamine doses aligned with depression prescribing guidelines.
- High-dose ketamine exposure has a stronger literature trail for long-term cognitive impairment.
- In contrast, esketamine clinical trials generally found cognition stable or improved over time, suggesting no increased cognitive-impairment signal when used appropriately in clinical depression protocols.
- Interpretation for the wiki: this supports a state-bound cognitive-safety read for supervised depression treatment, not a healthy-adult cognition-enhancement claim. High-dose recreational or unsupervised exposure should not be merged with protocolized esketamine evidence, but neither should acute impairment be ignored.
