# Cognitive decline in the elderly: a double-blind, placebo-controlled multicenter study on efficacy of phosphatidylserine administration

## Bibliographic snapshot

- PubMed record for Cenacchi et al., Aging Clinical and Experimental Research, 1993.
- PubMed ID: 8323999.
- DOI: 10.1007/BF03324139.
- Trial type: double-blind, placebo-controlled, multicenter clinical trial.

## Study design

- The trial compared bovine cortex-derived phosphatidylserine (BC-PS) with placebo.
- Dose: 300 mg/day.
- Duration: 6 months.
- Participants: 494 elderly patients aged 65-93 years with moderate-to-severe cognitive decline by MMSE and Global Deterioration Scale criteria.
- Recruitment: 23 geriatric or general-medicine units in Northeastern Italy.
- Dropout count reported in the abstract: 69 patients during the 6-month trial.

## Outcomes

- Assessments occurred before therapy and after 3 and 6 months.
- Outcome instruments named in the abstract included the Plutchik Geriatric Rating Scale and Buschke Selective Reminding Test.
- PubMed abstract reports statistically significant improvements in behavioral and cognitive parameters in the phosphatidylserine group compared with placebo.
- Clinical evaluation and laboratory tests reportedly showed BC-PS was well tolerated.

## Wiki interpretation

- This is one of the largest phosphatidylserine cognition trials, but it used bovine cortex-derived PS, not the soy/sunflower forms now common in supplements.
- The population was impaired elderly patients, not healthy adults seeking cognitive enhancement.
- It supports a low-to-moderate older clinical cognition signal, not a broad healthy-adult nootropic claim.
- Transportability is limited by product source, age, impairment severity, outcome vintage, and the older clinical-trial context.
