# Rosemary aroma, 1,8-cineole, and cognitive performance

- Source type: small healthy-volunteer aroma exposure / pharmacokinetic correlation study.
- Publication: Therapeutic Advances in Psychopharmacology, 2012.
- Date checked: 2026-05-11.

Twenty healthy volunteers completed serial subtraction and rapid visual information processing tasks in a cubicle diffused with rosemary essential-oil aroma. Participants were assigned to different pre-test exposure durations to produce a range of absorbed 1,8-cineole levels, and blood samples were taken at the end of testing.

Serum 1,8-cineole concentration correlated with cognitive performance measures. Higher 1,8-cineole levels were linked to better speed and accuracy rather than a speed-accuracy tradeoff. Mood relationships were less consistent, with a negative correlation between contentment change and 1,8-cineole.

This is not a conventional placebo-controlled efficacy trial. It is useful because it connects an absorbed volatile constituent to task performance, but it should be treated as small, acute, and expectancy/sensory-confounded.

Use in the wiki: evidence anchor for a rosemary / 1,8-cineole aromatic branch, not a broad rosemary supplement recommendation.
