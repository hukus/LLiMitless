# LiverTox: Lemon Balm

NCBI Bookshelf LiverTox monograph for lemon balm, last updated 2024-02-10.

The monograph describes lemon balm as an extract from fresh or dried Melissa officinalis leaves, used traditionally for nervousness, anxiety, insomnia, and menstrual irregularities. It says lemon balm extracts are generally recognized as safe and have not been linked to serum aminotransferase elevations during treatment or clinically apparent acute liver injury.

Hepatotoxicity section: small trials report few minor nonspecific adverse effects such as headache, dizziness, and bloating, often similar to placebo. Despite wide supplement use, lemon balm extract has not been linked to published instances of clinically apparent liver injury. LiverTox assigns likelihood score E, meaning unlikely cause of clinically apparent liver injury.

Relevance: safety anchor for liver-specific risk. It does not remove sedation, interaction, product-quality, or multi-ingredient supplement caveats.
