# Nicotine patch post-COVID cholinergic hypothesis and case series

2023 Bioelectronic Medicine hypothesis paper and very small post-COVID case series arguing that nicotinic acetylcholine receptor disruption may contribute to post-COVID symptoms and that transdermal nicotine could transiently restore cholinergic neuromodulation.

The paper describes four nicotine-naive post-COVID patients treated with low-dose nicotine patches, usually 7.5 mg/24 h, with reported improvement in fatigue, concentration, retentiveness, dyspnea, smell/taste, sleep, mood, and weakness. One case included worsened GI symptoms after accidentally doubling the intended dose to 15 mg/24 h.

Interpretation for the wiki: this is very low-quality evidence and should be treated as hypothesis-plus-case-series only. It is still worth tracking because it explains why nicotine patch anecdotes cluster around brain fog, fatigue, smell/taste, and autonomic symptoms. It does not establish safety or efficacy in non-smokers, and dependency/cardiovascular/sleep risks remain central.
