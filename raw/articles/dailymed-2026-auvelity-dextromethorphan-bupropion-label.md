# DailyMed 2026 AUVELITY dextromethorphan-bupropion label

- DailyMed label checked 2026-05-15; page version 11.
- AUVELITY is dextromethorphan hydrobromide plus bupropion hydrochloride, extended-release multilayer tablets, indicated for major depressive disorder in adults.
- The label's most common adverse reactions at least 5% and more than twice placebo include dizziness, headache, diarrhea, somnolence, dry mouth, sexual dysfunction, and hyperhidrosis.
- Bupropion can cause seizure, with dose-related risk. The label contraindicates AUVELITY in seizure disorder, current/prior anorexia or bulimia, abrupt discontinuation of alcohol/benzodiazepines/barbiturates/antiepileptic drugs, MAOI use, and concomitant bupropion-containing products.
- AUVELITY can increase blood pressure and hypertension risk; blood pressure should be assessed before starting and monitored during treatment.
- The label warns about activation of mania/hypomania, psychosis and neuropsychiatric reactions, angle-closure glaucoma, dizziness/fall and machinery risk, and serotonin syndrome.
- Dextromethorphan drives the serotonin-syndrome warning; concomitant SSRIs, TCAs, other serotonergic drugs, MAOIs, linezolid, or IV methylene blue are especially relevant.
- Drug-interaction gates include MAOIs, serotonergic drugs, drugs lowering seizure threshold, strong CYP2D6 inhibitors, strong CYP2B6 inducers, CYP2D6 substrates, dopaminergic drugs, alcohol, digoxin, and CYP2B6 substrates.
- Interpretation for the wiki: AUVELITY is mechanistically relevant to NMDA/sigma-1 antidepressant discussions, but its cognition/brain-fog use case is bounded by antidepressant indication, seizure/BP/serotonergic risk, dizziness/somnolence, and strong stack-interaction hazards.
