# LiverTox: Guarana

- Source type: NCBI Bookshelf / LiverTox safety monograph.
- Last updated by source: 2023-01-28.
- Date checked: 2026-05-03.

LiverTox describes guarana as roasted and pulverized Paullinia cupana seed extract with caffeine and other xanthine alkaloids including theophylline and theobromine. It notes caffeine-like effects on the heart, vasculature, and central nervous system.

The source states that conventional doses have few side effects and that guarana has not been convincingly linked to clinically apparent liver injury. It gives guarana a likelihood score of E for liver injury, meaning unlikely. It also cautions that high doses can cause tremor, jitteriness, agitation, confusion, hypertension, and dehydration, and that combining multiple guarana/caffeine-containing products can produce caffeine toxicity.

Use in the wiki: safety and product-combination anchor. Liver risk is not the main issue; cumulative caffeine/xanthine exposure and multiingredient supplements are.
