# Merck holy basil safety summary

- Source type: Merck Manual consumer supplement monograph.
- Date checked: 2026-05-11.

Merck describes holy basil as an Ayurvedic herb and supplement made from leaves, stems, seeds, or essential oil. It states that the human evidence is limited and that many studies are small or low quality.

Merck says holy basil seems safe for most people when taken orally for up to 8 weeks, but long-term safety has not been studied. Reported side effects can include nausea or diarrhea. Merck cautions that holy basil may not be safe during pregnancy or when trying to become pregnant, that breastfeeding safety is unknown, that it might decrease thyroxine and worsen hypothyroidism, and that it could increase bleeding risk around surgery. It also flags possible interactions with anticoagulant/antiplatelet drugs, sedatives, and glucose-lowering medications.
