# S-Adenosylmethionine (SAMe) for Central Nervous System Health: A Systematic Review

2024 systematic review of SAMe in CNS-related signs. The review searched PubMed, CINAHL, and Web of Science for studies from January 1, 2004 to April 17, 2024 and included 36 studies, representing 1,799 participants outside systematic reviews.

CNS-related signs included depression, anxiety, mood, suicide attempt, ADHD, cognitive deficits, 22q11.2 deletion syndrome, creatine transporter deficiency, and schizophrenia. Most of the captured evidence is depression-centered rather than cognition-centered. The review reports that 24 of 36 studies found positive findings, mainly depressive-symptom improvement, with SAMe doses ranging from 200 to 3,200 mg/day and 800 mg/day as the most common dose.

The review highlights mixed results. It notes no benefit for ADHD symptoms in one study and that several studies found no significant difference versus placebo or conventional antidepressants. It also reports that 400-800 mg/day was used in anxiety studies, and that schizophrenia signals were mainly aggression, depressive symptoms, or quality of life rather than cognition.

Safety findings were mostly mild to moderate gastrointestinal complaints and headache, but adverse reports included anxiety, excitation, sleep disruption, behavioral changes, fluid retention, mania/hypomania, and psychomotor excitation. The review emphasizes limited long-term efficacy and safety data, variable oral formulation bioavailability, and caution outside depression/anxiety.

Wiki use: this is the broadest current CNS evidence anchor for [[s-adenosyl-l-methionine]]. It supports a `low mood-state / CNS-symptom` classification, not a direct nootropic classification.
