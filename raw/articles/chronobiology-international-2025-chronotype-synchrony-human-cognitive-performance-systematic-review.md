# Chronotype and synchrony effects in human cognitive performance: A systematic review

- Journal: Chronobiology International
- Published online: 2025-04-28
- Study type: systematic review
- Scope: 65 studies on chronotype, time of day, and cognitive performance in healthy adults

## Captured takeaways

- Most reviewed studies reported no main effect of chronotype on cognitive performance.
- Synchrony effects were common enough to matter: performance tended to improve when testing occurred at the chronotype-preferred time of day.
- The review reported synchrony effects in attention, inhibition, and memory, with stronger evidence in older adults than in younger adults.
- Exogenous factors such as task complexity and lighting conditions could modulate the synchrony effect.
- Interpretation for the wiki: chronotype is not a universal predictor of cognition, but it is a plausible moderator when a study compares optimal versus non-optimal time of day.
