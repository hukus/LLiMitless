# Neurochemical modulation of response inhibition and probabilistic learning in humans

## Captured takeaways

- Science 2006 healthy-volunteer study, PMID 16469930, testing noradrenaline and serotonin reuptake manipulation on prefrontal cognitive tasks.
- Participants completed stop-signal response-inhibition and probabilistic-learning tasks.
- Central noradrenaline reuptake inhibition with atomoxetine improved response inhibition on the stop-signal task.
- The noradrenergic manipulation did not improve probabilistic learning.
- The serotonergic comparator showed a separable pattern, supporting a double dissociation rather than a generic "more monoamine equals better cognition" story.
- Use in wiki: the cleanest formal healthy-adult anchor for [[atomoxetine]] is response inhibition, not broad memory, motivation, or general cognition.
