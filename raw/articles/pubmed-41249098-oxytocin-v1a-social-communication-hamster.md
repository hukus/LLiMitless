# Non-synaptically released oxytocin regulates social communication by acting on vasopressin V1a receptors

- PMID: 41249098
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41249098/
- Ingested: 2026-04-29

## Summary

This 2026 hamster study suggests that oxytocin can regulate social communication through vasopressin V1a receptors rather than only through canonical oxytocin receptors. The result matters for the branch because it makes the oxytocin/vasopressin boundary less clean and reinforces that receptor-level biology may matter more than the peptide label.

## Notes

- Source type: preclinical animal study.
- Main mechanistic point: non-synaptic oxytocin release can drive scent marking via V1a receptor activation.
- Relevance: supports receptor cross-talk and a non-canonical mechanism for social behavior.
- Caveat: hamster scent marking is a social-communication model, not a direct human cognition endpoint.
