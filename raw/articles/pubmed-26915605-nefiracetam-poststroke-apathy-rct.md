# PubMed 26915605: Nefiracetam post-stroke apathy randomized trial

This 2016 Journal of Stroke and Cerebrovascular Diseases article is titled "A Randomized, Placebo-Controlled, Double-Blind Efficacy Study of Nefiracetam to Treat Poststroke Apathy."

Design from the PubMed abstract: a parallel-group, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind two-center trial was conducted in Perth, Western Australia from March 2010 to October 2014. Patients with recent stroke were screened during hospitalization and, if diagnosed with apathy 8-36 weeks later, randomized to 12 weeks of nefiracetam 900 mg/day or placebo. The primary efficacy parameter was change in the 14-item Apathy Scale at 12 weeks.

Results from the abstract: 2514 patients were screened; 377 were eligible after first screening; 144 were assessed for apathy; 20 of 106 with complete psychiatric assessment had apathy; 13 were randomized. The Apathy Scale score decreased by a mean of 7.0 points with a 95% CI of -14.6 to 0.6, but there was no significant between-group difference at week 12, with P > .14.

Conclusion from the abstract: nefiracetam did not prove more efficacious than placebo for post-stroke apathy. The trial was limited by very small randomized sample size, illustrating the difficulty of drug trials for post-stroke behavioral symptoms.

Interpretation for the wiki: this is the most direct modern-ish human trial anchor for nefiracetam, and it is negative/noninformative rather than a cognition-positive result.
