# PubMed 41836095 brain glutathione cognition MRS note

2026 Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience article titled "The role of glutathione in cognition, cognitive effort, and cognitive endurance in young and older adults."

The study measured brain glutathione with HERMES magnetic resonance spectroscopy in 40 young adults aged 18-35 and 40 older adults aged 60-85, sampling inferior frontal and inferior parietal cortices at baseline and during cognitive task performance. It related glutathione levels and modulation to cognitive integrity, cognitive effort, sustained cognitive effort, BOLD response, GABA+, and Glx.

Reported synthesis: inferior-parietal glutathione related in opposite directions to distinct memory tasks in young and older adults. Glutathione levels in both regions modulated during sustained cognitive performance, with age- and region-dependent direction. Glutathione modulation positively related to cognitive performance in young adults. The authors emphasized heterogeneity by age, region, and state.

Interpretation for the wiki: this is a mechanistic brain-biomarker anchor, not supplementation evidence. It supports why glutathione biology is cognition-relevant while also warning against simplistic "more glutathione equals better cognition" claims.
