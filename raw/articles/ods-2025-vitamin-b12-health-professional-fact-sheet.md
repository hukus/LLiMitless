# Vitamin B12 fact sheet for health professionals

- Publisher: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements
- Updated: 2025-07-02
- Source type: official nutrient status, dosing, safety, and interaction overview
- Checked: 2026-05-03
- Ingested: 2026-05-03

## Summary

ODS describes vitamin B12 as cobalamins required for central nervous system development, myelination, healthy red blood cells, DNA synthesis, methionine synthase, and methylmalonyl-CoA mutase. Serum B12 below about 200-250 pg/mL is generally considered subnormal, while methylmalonic acid is a sensitive confirmatory marker when serum B12 is borderline. Homocysteine rises as B12 declines but is nonspecific.

## Safety And Cognition Notes

- B12 deficiency can cause fatigue, neurological changes, numbness/tingling, palpitations, megaloblastic anemia, dementia, and irreversible neurological damage if untreated.
- Risk groups include older adults, people with pernicious anemia, gastrointestinal disorders or surgery, long-term acid suppression or metformin exposure, vegetarians/vegans, and infants of vegan mothers.
- ODS summarizes that RCT evidence does not show cognitive improvement from B12 alone or with folate/B6 in older adults with or without dementia/MCI/AD when deficiency treatment is not the question.
- Vitamin B12 has no established tolerable upper intake level because of low toxicity potential, but status diagnosis and cause of deficiency matter.
