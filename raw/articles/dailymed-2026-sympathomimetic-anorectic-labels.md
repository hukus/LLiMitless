## Captured takeaways

- Diethylpropion, phendimetrazine, and benzphetamine are labeled anorectic / weight-reduction agents, not cognitive enhancers.
- DailyMed labels for these agents repeatedly emphasize monotherapy or avoidance of combination with other anorectic products.
- Shared risks include primary pulmonary hypertension, valvular heart disease concern, cardiovascular stimulation, hypertension caution, alcohol / CNS-active-drug caution, abuse/dependence, tolerance, and withdrawal after prolonged high-dose use.
- Diethylpropion labeling notes a case-control epidemiological association between anorectic-agent use and pulmonary hypertension, described as rare but often fatal.
- Phendimetrazine and benzphetamine labels advise against use in patients with known heart murmur or valvular heart disease and recommend considering baseline cardiac evaluation for valvular disease or pulmonary hypertension.
- Benzphetamine labeling explicitly warns that amphetamines and related stimulant drugs have been extensively abused and that abuse potential should be considered before including the drug in weight reduction.
- Interpretation for the wiki: these are risk-first sibling comparators for phentermine. They do not have captured direct healthy-adult cognition evidence in this pass.
