# Virtual reality-based cognitive training in mild cognitive impairment: a systematic review

- DOI: 10.3389/fnagi.2026.1784911
- Source: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/aging-neuroscience/articles/10.3389/fnagi.2026.1784911/full
- Ingested: 2026-05-12

## Summary

Frontiers in Aging Neuroscience 2026 systematic review comparing virtual-reality cognitive training (VRCT) with conventional cognitive training in mild cognitive impairment. The search used Web of Science, Scopus, and PubMed through August 2025 and screened 2,256 records. Seven randomized controlled trials were included, covering 380 older adults with MCI and 348 analyzed participants. The included interventions used immersive, semi-immersive, and non-immersive VR systems, usually across 12-36 sessions of 20-60 minutes delivered two to five times per week. Outcomes included general cognition, learning and memory, executive function, complex attention, perceptual-motor function, language, activities of daily living, and psychoaffective status. The review concluded that VRCT appeared more effective than conventional cognitive training for general cognition, complex attention, and perceptual-motor function, but emphasized variability in protocols, outcome measures, and the need for higher-quality multicenter trials with long-term follow-up.
