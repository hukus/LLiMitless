# Homocysteine-lowering by B vitamins slows the rate of accelerated brain atrophy in mild cognitive impairment

- PMCID: PMC2935890
- DOI: 10.1371/journal.pone.0012244
- Published: 2010-09-08
- Trial: VITACOG, ISRCTN94410159
- Source type: randomized, double-blind controlled trial
- Intervention: folic acid 0.8 mg/day, vitamin B12 0.5 mg/day, vitamin B6 20 mg/day for 24 months
- Ingested: 2026-05-03

## Summary

VITACOG randomized older adults with mild cognitive impairment to homocysteine-lowering B vitamins or placebo. In the MRI subset, mean annual whole-brain atrophy was 0.76% in the active group and 1.08% in placebo (p = 0.001). Response depended on baseline homocysteine: participants with homocysteine above 13 micromol/L had 53% lower atrophy rate with active treatment. Serious adverse events did not differ by treatment group.

## Notes

- This is the strongest mechanistic anchor for the folate/B-vitamin cognition branch.
- It is a biomarker/atrophy result in MCI, not direct healthy-adult cognitive enhancement.
- Baseline homocysteine is the key gate; without that phenotype, the signal should be downgraded.
