# Chronotype as a potential risk factor for cognitive decline: The mediating role of sleep quality and health behaviours in a 10-year follow-up study

- PMID: 40221238
- Published: 2025-06
- Study type: longitudinal cohort
- Key finding: chronotype, measured as mid-point sleep corrected for sleep debt on workdays, was associated with cognitive decline in adults 40+, with sleep quality, alcohol intake, physical activity, and smoking acting as mediators.
- Why it matters: chronotype belongs on the scorecard as a risk/state variable, but its effect appears mediated through behavior rather than acting as a stand-alone biological switch.
