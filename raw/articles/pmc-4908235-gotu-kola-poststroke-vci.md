# PMC 4908235: Gotu kola extract after post-stroke vascular cognitive impairment

This 2016 Evidence-Based Complementary and Alternative Medicine article compared gotu kola extract with folic acid in post-stroke vascular cognitive impairment.

Design: quasi-experimental study in Yogyakarta, Indonesia. Forty-eight post-stroke cognitive-impairment patients completed the study: 17 received gotu kola extract 1000 mg/day, 17 received gotu kola extract 750 mg/day, and 14 received folic acid 3 mg/day for 6 weeks. MoCA-Ina was measured at baseline and after treatment. The extract was prepared from whole herbs in standardized dry form, with 70% ethanol extraction, 10:1 extract ratio, and a certificate of analysis from a GMP-certified facility.

Results: all groups improved over the 6-week study. There was no significant difference in mean change in MoCA-Ina among the three groups, meaning gotu kola was not superior to folic acid for global cognition. The authors report better delayed-memory-recall improvement for gotu kola than folic acid. One participant discontinued due to an allergic reaction; the study concluded that 750 and 1000 mg/day were well tolerated with minimal side effects.

Interpretation for the wiki: this is clinical-state support for delayed recall after stroke, but it is not placebo-controlled and not healthy-adult enhancement evidence.
