# Ingestion of beta-nicotinamide mononucleotide increased blood NAD levels, maintained walking speed, and improved sleep quality in older adults

- PMID: 38789831
- PMCID: PMC11336149
- DOI: 10.1007/s11357-024-01204-1
- Trial registry: UMIN000047871
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38789831/
- Ingested: 2026-04-28

## Summary

2024 GeroScience randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group study of beta-nicotinamide mononucleotide (NMN) in 60 older adults. Participants consumed 250 mg/day NMN or placebo for 12 weeks. The study primarily targeted walking function, with NAD metabolites, sleep, and stress as secondary/proxy outcomes.

Primary outcome: the NMN and placebo groups did not differ significantly on the stepping test at 4 or 12 weeks.

Secondary/proxy outcomes: after 12 weeks, the NMN group had shorter 4-meter walking time than placebo and higher blood NAD+ and related metabolites. Sleep quality improved on Pittsburgh Sleep Quality Index "Daytime dysfunction" and "Global PSQI" scores. No adverse effects related to test-substance consumption were reported.

Use in the wiki: strongest current proxy-positive source for [[nicotinamide-mononucleotide]]. It supports blood NAD+ movement, sleep-quality/drowsiness relevance, and walking-speed maintenance, but it did not test a direct cognition endpoint and should not be ranked as cognition efficacy.

Conflict / product note: several authors were employees of Meiji Holdings / Wellness Science Labs, so industry sponsorship and product identity should be retained in interpretation.
