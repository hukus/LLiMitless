# PubMed 25890139 - Extracellular Matrix in Plasticity and CNS Disease

## Citation

- Fawcett JW. "The extracellular matrix in plasticity and regeneration after CNS injury and neurodegenerative disease." Progress in Brain Research. 2015. DOI: 10.1016/bs.pbr.2015.02.001.
- Source type: peer-reviewed review.

## Extracted Claims

- Chondroitin sulfate proteoglycans restrict axon regeneration in glial scar tissue and restrict plasticity in perineuronal nets.
- Much of the mechanistic evidence comes from chondroitinase ABC digestion of glycosaminoglycan chains.
- After spinal cord injury, ChABC can permit axon regeneration, increase plasticity, promote sprouting, and digest PNNs, especially when combined with rehabilitation-like behavioral context.
- The review reports that perirhinal-cortex ChABC can greatly increase retention of object-recognition memory in animal models.
- The review also notes that in tauopathy Alzheimer-model animals, ChABC digestion can restore object-recognition memory.
- Sulfation motifs, OTX2 binding, and semaphorin-3A binding are presented as important for PNN biology.

## Cognition-Relevant Extraction

- The cognition signal is preclinical and mechanism-first: object-recognition memory can improve after local PNN/CSPG manipulation in animals.
- Disease context matters: spinal cord injury, neurodegenerative disease, and PNN biology are not the same as healthy-adult enhancement.
- Human enhancement evidence: none in this source.
- Safety interpretation for the wiki: broad PNN digestion is a high-burden intervention concept because ECM structure stabilizes mature circuits and interacts with regeneration, inflammation, and plasticity.
