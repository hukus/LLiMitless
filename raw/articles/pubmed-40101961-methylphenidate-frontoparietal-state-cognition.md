# Methylphenidate promotes a frontoparietal-dominant brain state improving cognitive performance: a randomized trial

- PMID: 40101961
- DOI: 10.1523/JNEUROSCI.1693-24.2025
- Journal: Journal of Neuroscience (2025 Mar 18;45(17):e1693242025)

## Captured takeaways

- Single-blind, counterbalanced, placebo-controlled crossover study in 37 healthy adults.
- Participants received a single 60 mg dose of methylphenidate or placebo.
- Multimodal imaging combined PET, fMRI, and behavioral tasks to connect dopamine signaling, brain-state dynamics, and task performance.
- Methylphenidate primarily affected frontoparietal-dominant activated (FPN+), somatomotor-dominant activated (SOM+), and visual-dominant suppressed (VIS-) brain states.
- Dwell time and fractional occupancy increased in FPN+ and VIS-, with the opposite trend in SOM+.
- The FPN+ change correlated positively with baseline striatal D1 receptor availability.
- The FPN+ change was associated with quicker response on the 2-ball-track task, but not clearly on the 3-ball-track task.
- Interpretation for the wiki: this supports a state-shaping mechanism for attention in healthy adults, but the performance benefit looks task-limited rather than a broad universal enhancement signal.
