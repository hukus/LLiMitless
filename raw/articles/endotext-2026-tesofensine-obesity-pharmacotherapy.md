# Endotext: Existing and Emerging Molecular Targets for the Pharmacotherapy of Obesity - Tesofensine section

## Captured takeaways

- Endotext describes tesofensine as a presynaptic inhibitor of norepinephrine, dopamine, and serotonin.
- It compares tesofensine with sibutramine as an appetite-suppressing monoamine reuptake intervention.
- Endotext summarizes the 203-participant obesity dose-ranging trial: mean weight reductions of -6.7, -11.3, and -12.8 kg for 0.25, 0.5, and 1.0 mg versus -2.2 kg for placebo after 24 weeks.
- It notes improved lipid profile and glycemic control, but also dose-dependent blood-pressure increase and a 7.4 bpm pulse-rate increase in the 0.5 mg/day group.
- It summarizes a 32-male trial in which tesofensine increased satiety and fat oxidation without showing broad cognition outcomes.
- Its table lists tesofensine as a phase III / triple monoamine reuptake inhibitor branch with adverse-event concerns including headache, mood alterations, potential heart-rate and blood-pressure increases, and psychiatric disorders.
- Interpretation for the wiki: useful class/status summary and safety backstop; do not infer cognition benefit from obesity efficacy.
