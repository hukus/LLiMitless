# Impact of vitamin D on cognitive functions in healthy individuals: a systematic review in randomized controlled clinical trials

- PMID: 36524160
- PMCID: PMC9745323
- DOI: 10.3389/fpsyg.2022.987203
- Published: 2022-11-29
- Source type: systematic review of randomized clinical trials
- Population: healthy non-athlete adults
- Ingested: 2026-05-03

## Summary

Systematic review of randomized clinical trials in healthy adults. The authors reported positive changes across some domains, including verbal memory/working memory, learning memory, attention, executive function, and general cognitive function, but also emphasized the need for more trials and better vitamin D level control. They suggested that lower doses around 400-600 IU/day appeared more effective than higher-dose ranges in the included studies, and that fortified foods need further study.

## Notes

- Useful for healthy-adult claims, but it should be subordinate to larger meta-analytic and direct trial counterweights.
- The review is qualitative and does not establish a robust nootropic effect.
- Treat as hypothesis-supporting for correcting status, not as proof of acute cognitive enhancement.
