# Efficacy and acceptability of S-adenosyl-L-methionine (SAMe) for depressed patients: A systematic review and meta-analysis

2024 systematic review and meta-analysis in Progress in Neuro-Psychopharmacology & Biological Psychiatry. The authors searched PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and ClinicalTrials.gov through July 3, 2023 for randomized trials comparing SAMe with placebo or antidepressants in depression.

The abstract reports 23 trials with 2,183 participants: 11 SAMe versus placebo trials, 5 SAMe plus antidepressant versus placebo plus antidepressant trials, and 7 SAMe versus antidepressant trials. SAMe was superior to placebo for depressive symptoms as monotherapy (SMD -0.58, 95% CI -0.93 to -0.23, I2 68%). Adjunctive SAMe plus antidepressants did not significantly differ from placebo plus antidepressants (SMD -0.22, 95% CI -0.63 to 0.19, I2 76%). SAMe did not significantly differ from antidepressants alone (SMD 0.06, 95% CI -0.06 to 0.18, I2 49%).

Dropout rates were not significantly different across comparison categories. The authors judged that moderate-certainty evidence suggests SAMe monotherapy may offer a moderate therapeutic benefit for depression, but that adjunctive efficacy and comparison with antidepressants remain unresolved.

Wiki use: this strengthens the formal mood-state side of [[s-adenosyl-l-methionine]], but it does not provide a direct healthy-adult cognition endpoint. Mood recovery can reduce subjective brain fog in depressed people, but attribution must stay separate from objective cognitive enhancement.
