# Auditory-evoked changes in slow oscillations and spindles correlate with memory consolidation in children with epilepsy and controls

- PMID: 41435615
- DOI: 10.1016/j.clinph.2025.2111475
- Journal: Clinical Neurophysiology (2026)
- Published: 2026-02-01
- Epub: 2025-12-07
- Design: prospective crossover study in children with Rolandic epilepsy and controls; two nap visits with auditory or sham stimulation.

## Captured takeaways

- 11 children with RE and 9 controls were enrolled; 8 RE children and 7 controls were included in the final analysis after nap exclusions.
- Auditory stimulation reliably evoked slow oscillations and SO-spindle complexes.
- Stimulation delivered during background activity increased SOs by 29.8% and SO-spindle complexes by 16.8% versus sham.
- Stimulation delivered near the peak of an ongoing SO upstate produced the largest effects, with 51.3% higher SOs and 32.3% higher SO-spindle complexes versus sham.
- Changes in frontal SO rate and SO-spindle complex rate positively predicted changes in sleep-dependent memory consolidation on a motor sequence typing task.

## Interpretation for the wiki

This is the cleanest newer primary paper showing that auditory stimulation during sleep is not just a physiology manipulation. The event rate itself appears to matter, and the best yield comes from background activity or SO upstate timing rather than generic cue delivery. The main limitation is that the sample is small, pediatric, and disease-context, so it strengthens phase-sensitive mechanistic reasoning more than it proves healthy-adult enhancement.
