# PubMed 11462769: Nefiracetam modulation of neuronal nicotinic acetylcholine receptors

This 2001 Annals of the New York Academy of Sciences article is titled "Post-stroke dementia. Nootropic drug modulation of neuronal nicotinic acetylcholine receptors."

The abstract describes nefiracetam as a pyrrolidone nootropic being developed for clinical use in post-stroke vascular-type and Alzheimer's-type dementia. It focuses on neuronal nicotinic acetylcholine receptors because they are related to learning, memory, and Alzheimer's disease dementia.

In rat cortical neurons, whole-cell patch-clamp experiments found alpha-bungarotoxin-sensitive alpha7-type currents and alpha-bungarotoxin-insensitive alpha4beta2-type currents. Nefiracetam weakly inhibited alpha7-type currents but potently potentiated alpha4beta2-type currents.

The abstract reports that 0.1 nM nefiracetam reversibly potentiated acetylcholine-induced currents to 200-300% of control. Very high concentrations around 10 uM also potentiated currents but less strongly, consistent with a bell-shaped dose-response pattern. The potentiation was not blocked by PKA, PKC, or Gi/Go pathway perturbation but was abolished by cholera toxin, suggesting Gs-protein mediation.

Interpretation for the wiki: the source is mechanistic and disease-context translational. It supports cholinergic/nicotinic plausibility, but it is not an objective human cognition trial.
