# Melatonin: What You Need To Know

- Publisher: National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health, NIH
- Source type: official health information and safety overview
- Checked: 2026-05-03
- Ingested: 2026-05-03

## Summary

NCCIH frames melatonin as a darkness-responsive hormone involved in circadian timing and sleep. Supplements may help jet lag, delayed sleep-wake phase disorder, some pediatric sleep disorders, and perioperative anxiety, but evidence is not strong enough to recommend melatonin for chronic insomnia. Short-term use appears safe for most people, while long-term safety is unclear.

## Safety Notes

- Potential side effects include headache, dizziness, nausea, and sleepiness.
- People taking medicines should consult clinicians; NCCIH specifically flags epilepsy medicines and blood thinners.
- Older adults may have longer melatonin activity and daytime drowsiness; AASM guidelines recommend against melatonin use by people with dementia.
- Pregnancy and breastfeeding safety evidence is lacking.
- In the United States, melatonin is regulated as a dietary supplement, and products may not contain what labels state.
- NCCIH also cites pediatric ingestion and gummy-labeling concerns, so child-access and product form are safety variables.
