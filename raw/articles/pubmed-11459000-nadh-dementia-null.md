# PubMed 11459000 NADH dementia null study

This 2000 Journal of Neural Transmission paper directly cautioned against overreading NADH dementia marketing. The authors noted that reduced nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NADH) was advertised as an over-the-counter product or dietary supplement for Alzheimer's disease.

Design: 3-month open-label oral NADH 10 mg/day in 25 patients with mild to moderate dementia of Alzheimer, vascular, and frontotemporal types, in addition to current cholinomimetic medication. Nineteen patients completed the study.

Result: the authors found no evidence for cognitive effect as defined by established psychometric tests. They concluded that NADH was unlikely to achieve the magnitude of cognitive improvement reported earlier and presented theoretical arguments against effectiveness in dementia disorders.

Interpretation for the wiki: this is an important negative counterweight to early AD enthusiasm. It is open-label rather than placebo-controlled, but it keeps NADH in a low-confidence and contested category.
