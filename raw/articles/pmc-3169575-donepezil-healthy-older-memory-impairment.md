# Donepezil impairs memory in healthy older subjects

PLOS ONE paper reporting two double-blind placebo-controlled experiments in healthy older adults, combining cognitive testing, EEG, and simultaneous EEG/fMRI. Experiment 1 tested donepezil 5 mg/day over 4 weeks; experiment 2 tested a single 5 mg donepezil dose in a crossover imaging design.

The study is a direct caution against casual acetylcholinesterase-inhibitor enhancement in healthy aging. In the 4-week experiment, placebo participants improved on continuous paired-associates learning while the donepezil group deteriorated slightly; the treatment effect was visible early and persisted. Four participants withdrew before completing two weeks because of severe nausea, diarrhea, or vomiting.

Interpretation for the wiki: this is the most important healthy-older counterweight for [[donepezil]]. It turns the branch from "maybe memory-enhancing" into a responder- and state-sensitive intervention with a real possibility of worsening memory in healthy people.
