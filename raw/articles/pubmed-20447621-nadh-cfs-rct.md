# PubMed 20447621 NADH chronic fatigue syndrome randomized trial

This 2010 Revista Clinica Espanola article tested oral NADH in chronic fatigue syndrome. The authors framed NADH depletion as a possible CFS mechanism and evaluated supplementation in a double-blind, placebo-controlled 3-month trial.

Design: 86 patients were enrolled and 77 completed the study. Patients received oral NADH 20 mg or placebo during the first 2 months. Outcomes included fatigue intensity, functional performance, mood state, functional impact of fatigue, quality of life, sleep quality, exercise capacity, functional reserve, stress testing, and investigator/patient efficacy impressions at baseline, 30, 60, and 90 days.

Result: most studied variables did not differ significantly at study end. NADH was associated with decreased anxiety condition (-1.0 points, p<0.05) versus -0.2 in placebo (not significant). Maximum heart rate after stress testing decreased by a mean of -8.1/min (p<0.05) in the NADH group and increased by +1.7/min in placebo (p=0.73). Investigator and patient efficacy perceptions did not differ between NADH and placebo.

Interpretation for the wiki: this is a partial CFS physiology/anxiety signal, not a robust global functional or cognitive signal. It downgrades any broad NADH cognition claim and reinforces state-bound interpretation.
