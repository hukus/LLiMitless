# Transdermal rivastigmine for HIV-associated cognitive impairment

Randomized pilot study in HIV-associated cognitive impairment on stable antiretroviral therapy. Twenty-nine participants were enrolled after screening; participants received transdermal rivastigmine 9.5 mg daily, lithium, or no new medication, with 48-week follow-up and NPZ-7 global cognitive score as the primary endpoint.

All groups improved, but there were no significant differences between arms on global cognition. Rivastigmine showed nonsignificant positive trends in information-processing speed and executive functioning, and two rivastigmine participants discontinued due to medication-related effects. The authors concluded that transdermal rivastigmine did not provide significant cognitive benefits in HAND, despite domain trends.

Interpretation for the wiki: rivastigmine has a disease-context pilot with trend signals but no significant cognition win. It should not be promoted as a brain-fog or healthy-adult enhancer.
