# Polygalae Radix: review of metabolites, pharmacological activities and toxicology

PubMed-indexed 2024 Frontiers in Pharmacology review of Polygalae Radix, the dried root of Polygala tenuifolia Willd. or Polygala sibirica L. DOI: 10.3389/fphar.2024.1420853. PMCID: PMC11169621.

The abstract frames Polygalae Radix as traditionally used to improve memory and cognitive function, and says modern pharmacological studies report in vitro and in vivo nervous-system, immune, cardiovascular, respiratory, antioxidant, and antiepileptic activities. It reports more than 160 identified metabolites, including triterpenoid saponins, xanthones, and oligosaccharide esters.

Safety/toxicology interpretation: the review says clinical traditional-Chinese-medicine practice recognizes throat irritation, and that large or long-term use can stimulate the digestive tract, with saponins named as the main toxic metabolite class. It states that processing or combination with other Chinese herbal medicines is used to reduce throat and gastrointestinal irritation.

Relevance: current review-level safety and product-chemistry source for Polygalae Radix. It supports safety/product-identity caution, not standalone human cognition efficacy.
