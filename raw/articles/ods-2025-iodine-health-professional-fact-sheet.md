# Iodine fact sheet for health professionals

- Publisher: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements
- Updated: 2025
- Source type: official nutrient status, dosing, safety, and interaction overview
- Checked: 2026-05-11
- Ingested: 2026-05-11

## Summary

ODS describes iodine as an essential mineral required for thyroid hormone production. Thyroid hormone regulates growth, metabolism, and neurological development. Severe iodine deficiency causes goiter and hypothyroidism, and iodine status is especially important in pregnancy and early development.

For adult brain-fog routing, iodine is best treated as a thyroid-status gate. Too little iodine can contribute to hypothyroid symptoms such as fatigue, slowed thinking, cold intolerance, and low mood through thyroid dysfunction, but direct adult cognition-enhancement evidence is weak.

## Safety Notes

- Adult recommended intake is 150 mcg/day, higher in pregnancy and lactation.
- Adult tolerable upper intake level is 1,100 mcg/day.
- Excess iodine can also trigger thyroid dysfunction, including iodine-induced hyperthyroidism or hypothyroidism in susceptible people.
- Kelp and seaweed supplements can contain highly variable iodine doses.
- Thyroid disease, amiodarone exposure, lithium, antithyroid drugs, and pregnancy/lactation make unsupervised iodine experimentation higher risk.
