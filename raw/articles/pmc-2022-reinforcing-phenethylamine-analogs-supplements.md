# 2022 reinforcing effects of phenethylamine analogs found in dietary supplements

This paper reviews and tests phenethylamine analogs found in dietary supplements, with emphasis on analogs such as AEPEA and DEPEA and on the public-health problem created when stimulant analogs appear in sports or weight-loss products. The article frames prior supplement-adulterant cases including BMPEA, DMAA, DMBA, higenamine, deterenol, oxilofrine, octodrine, and related stimulants as a recurring supplement-market risk.

Wiki use: class-level supplement-market caution for [[nn-dimethylphenethylamine-eria-jarensis]] and [[phenethylamine]]. It does not establish that N,N-DMPEA itself improves cognition or has the same pharmacology as the tested analogs; it supports a risk-first product-identity lens for phenethylamine-like supplement ingredients.
