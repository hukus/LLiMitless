# Evening Chronotype Is Associated With Daytime Impairment and Differential Sleep and Circadian Response to a Sleep Extension Manipulation in Short Sleeping Adolescents

- PMID: 40458955
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40458955/
- Ingested: 2026-04-27

## Summary

Chronotype moderates response to sleep extension in short-sleeping adolescents. Evening types had more daytime impairment and more chronic sleep restriction at baseline, and the sleep-extension week changed sleep timing differently by chronotype even though both chronotype groups extended sleep.

## Notes

- Both morning/intermediate and evening types increased sleep duration during sleep extension.
- Only evening types significantly delayed sleep offset.
- Only morning/intermediate types narrowed the DLMO-to-sleep-onset phase angle.
- This is a sleep-circadian response paper, not a cognition trial, but it is directly relevant to chronotype-stratified intervention design.
