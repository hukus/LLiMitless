# Cognitive and clinical outcomes of homocysteine-lowering B-vitamin treatment in mild cognitive impairment

- DOI: 10.1002/gps.2758
- Published: 2012
- Source type: randomized controlled trial secondary cognitive and clinical outcomes
- Trial: VITACOG
- Intervention: folic acid 0.8 mg/day, vitamin B12 0.5 mg/day, vitamin B6 20 mg/day for 2 years
- Ingested: 2026-05-03

## Summary

VITACOG cognitive-outcome report in 266 MCI participants age 70+. B-vitamin treatment lowered plasma homocysteine by about 30% relative to placebo and stabilized executive function on CLOX. In participants with baseline homocysteine above the median, treatment benefited MMSE, Hopkins delayed recall, and category fluency. In the upper homocysteine quartile, clinical dementia rating and IQCODE also favored treatment.

## Notes

- This source connects the atrophy biomarker signal to cognitive/clinical endpoints.
- The positive effect is not uniform; it is strongest in elevated-homocysteine subgroups.
- Treat as `MCI + homocysteine-enriched` evidence rather than a broad B-complex nootropic claim.
