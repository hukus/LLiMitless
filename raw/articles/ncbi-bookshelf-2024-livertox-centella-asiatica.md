# LiverTox 2024: Centella asiatica / gotu kola

LiverTox updated its Centella asiatica entry on 2024-04-24. It identifies Centella asiatica as gotu kola / Asian pennywort, a food and traditional-medicine herb used in over-the-counter capsules, tablets, powders, solutions, and skin creams.

Identity and constituents: LiverTox notes more than 100 constituents and highlights pentacyclic triterpenoid glycosides and aglycones including asiaticoside, centelloside, madecassoside, asiatic acid, and madecassic acid.

Use and dose: typical oral dosing is described as 60-120 mg/day of purified extract, but 600-1800 mg/day may be used for capsules or powders of dried leaves. LiverTox states that Centella is not approved in the United States as therapy for any medical condition and that memory/cognitive claims have not been proven in prospective controlled human trials.

Safety: Centella is generally regarded as safe and small trials report mild, transient nonspecific adverse effects such as headache, dizziness, bloating, diarrhea, and nausea. Rare allergic reactions have been reported, especially with topical therapy.

Hepatotoxicity: LiverTox states that Centella has been linked to rare clinically apparent acute liver injury with jaundice. It gives a likelihood score of C, meaning probable rare cause of clinically apparent liver injury. Published cases include three Argentinian women using Centella for weight loss and one Swiss adolescent using oral gotu kola for acne; latency was about 3-8 weeks, injury was hepatocellular, and recovery followed stopping. Rechallenge should be avoided.
