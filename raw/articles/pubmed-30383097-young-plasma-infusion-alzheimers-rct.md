# Safety, Tolerability, and Feasibility of Young Plasma Infusion in the Plasma for Alzheimer Symptom Amelioration Study

- PMID: 30383097
- Source: JAMA Neurology 2019
- ClinicalTrials.gov: NCT02256306
- Ingested: 2026-04-29

## Summary

Pilot randomized clinical trial of young fresh frozen plasma (yFFP) in mild to moderate Alzheimer's disease. The study primarily tested safety, tolerability, and feasibility rather than efficacy and found the approach was feasible, with no related serious adverse events, but no statistically significant benefit on the exploratory cognitive or mood outcomes.

## Notes

- 18 patients were enrolled at Stanford University: 9 in the crossover cohort and 9 in the open-label cohort.
- Donor plasma came from men age 18 to 30 and was infused once weekly for 4 weeks at about 250 mL per infusion.
- Common adverse events included hypertension, dizziness, sinus bradycardia, headache, and sinus tachycardia; one participant stopped for urticaria and one for an unrelated stroke during the placebo washout period.
- The exploratory analyses did not find significant changes in global cognition, mood, or default-mode connectivity.
- Caregiver-reported functional ability looked better, but the sample was too small for a confident efficacy claim.
