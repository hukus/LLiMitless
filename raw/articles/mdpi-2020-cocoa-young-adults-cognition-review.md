# Effect of cocoa and cocoa products on cognitive performance in young adults

- PMID: 33265948
- DOI: 10.3390/nu12123691
- Journal: Nutrients (2020)

## Captured takeaways

- Systematic review of acute and chronic cocoa administration on cognition and brain health in children and young adults.
- The review selected 11 intervention studies with 366 participants after PubMed and Web of Science searches.
- The abstract reports that acute and chronic cocoa intake had positive effects on several cognitive outcomes and that acute benefits were often accompanied by increased cerebral blood flow or cerebral oxygenation.
- The review explicitly notes that cocoa products are mixed exposures: flavanols, epicatechin/procyanidins, caffeine, theobromine, matrix, and dose can differ across studies.
- One table entry highlighted capsules matched for theobromine and caffeine, which is useful because it separates flavanol effects from methylxanthine confounds.
- Interpretation for the wiki: cocoa cognition evidence cannot be automatically assigned to theobromine. Use this as a product-confounding and boundary source between [[cocoa-flavanols]], [[caffeine]], and [[theobromine]].
