# FDA 2002 Cylert pemoline label liver-warning packet

## Captured takeaways

- The FDA label identifies CYLERT (pemoline) as a central nervous system stimulant and describes pemoline as an oxazolidine compound.
- The label indication is ADHD, but it says CYLERT should not ordinarily be first-line therapy because of life-threatening hepatic failure risk.
- The label says treatment should start only in people without liver disease and with normal baseline liver-function tests.
- The recommended liver-monitoring program was serum ALT at baseline and every two weeks thereafter, with discontinuation for clinically significant ALT elevation, ALT at least two times the upper limit of normal, or clinical signs suggesting liver failure.
- Patient information says pemoline therapy has been associated with liver abnormalities ranging from reversible liver-test increases to liver failure that may result in death.
- The label states there was no way to predict who was likely to develop liver failure, and it required written informed consent before treatment.
- Clinical pharmacology says pemoline has stimulant pharmacological activity similar to other CNS stimulants, has minimal sympathomimetic effects, and may act through dopaminergic mechanisms in animals, but the exact human mechanism was not established.
- The label gives an approximate serum half-life of 12 hours and notes that clinical improvement can be gradual, sometimes not evident until the third or fourth week.
- Interpretation for the wiki: pemoline is not a clean nootropic precedent. It is a historical prescription stimulant with a strong liver-risk status layer.
