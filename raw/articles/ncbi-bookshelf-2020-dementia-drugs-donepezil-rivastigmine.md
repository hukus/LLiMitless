# NCBI Bookshelf AHRQ review: donepezil and rivastigmine versus placebo

AHRQ / NCBI Bookshelf chapter on prescription drugs versus placebo for cognition, function, and quality of life in clinical Alzheimer's-type dementia.

For donepezil versus placebo, the review reported small statistically significant cognition improvements in mild-to-moderate and moderate-to-severe clinical Alzheimer's-type dementia, but also increased serious adverse events and withdrawals at standard 10 mg/day dosing in mild-to-moderate disease, and increased withdrawals in moderate-to-severe disease.

For rivastigmine, standard-dose oral 12 mg/day or 9.5 mg/day transdermal patch showed small improvements in cognition and other clinical domains in mild-to-moderate clinical Alzheimer's-type dementia, but evidence for attention was insufficient and withdrawals due to adverse events increased. Lower-dose rivastigmine showed no cognition difference.

Interpretation for the wiki: this is disease-state evidence for acetylcholinesterase inhibitors. It supports prescription clinical use in dementia contexts but does not establish healthy-adult enhancement or brain-fog clearance.
