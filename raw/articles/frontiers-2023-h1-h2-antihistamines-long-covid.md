# H1/H2 antihistamines for long-COVID symptoms attributed to mast-cell activation

2023 Frontiers in Cardiovascular Medicine retrospective study of H1 and H2 histamine receptor blockade in long-COVID symptoms attributed to mast-cell activation. Fourteen treated patients were compared with 13 untreated controls. The treated regimen was fexofenadine 180 mg/day before dinner and famotidine 40 mg/day at bedtime for 20 days.

Brain fog was explicitly defined as lack of concentration, dizziness or lightheadedness or confusion, memory loss, multitasking difficulty, and word-finding difficulty. At baseline, brain fog was present in 93% of treated patients and 85% of controls. After 20 days, unchanged brain fog was present in 43% of treated patients and 70% of controls. Brain fog improved or disappeared significantly within the treated group, while controls showed no significant symptom improvement. Six treated patients had brain fog disappear, and four of 14 treated patients had no long-COVID symptoms after 20 days. No treatment adverse effects were observed in this small cohort.

Interpretation for the wiki: H1/H2 blockade is one of the more direct low-friction post-viral brain-fog signals, but evidence remains small, retrospective, self-reported, phenotype-enriched, and mast-cell-attribution dependent. It should not be generalized to healthy cognition enhancement.
