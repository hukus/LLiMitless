# A phase 2, randomized, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of S-adenosyl methionine in participants with mild cognitive impairment or dementia due to Alzheimer's disease

2026 phase 2 multicenter randomized double-blind placebo-controlled trial in 63 participants with a clinical Alzheimer's disease diagnosis, including MCI or dementia due to AD. Participants received 400 mg/day oral SAMe or placebo for 180 days.

The primary endpoint was change in plasma p-tau217. Secondary endpoints included safety, tolerability, and cognitive outcomes. The abstract reports that mean percentage change in p-tau217 increased 53.22% in the SAMe group versus 25.34% in placebo, with no statistically significant difference (SMD 37.58, 95% CI -32.61 to 107.76; p = 0.288). No significant differences were observed in safety or other secondary endpoints.

The article notes preclinical rationale: SAMe supplementation in neurodegenerative mouse models has been reported to lower A-beta, increase acetylcholine, and improve cognition. The human trial did not convert that rationale into a biomarker or cognitive signal.

Wiki use: this is the most important current direct AD/MCI SAMe trial. It downgrades disease-modification or cognition claims for [[s-adenosyl-l-methionine]] until larger or different-dose trials show otherwise.
