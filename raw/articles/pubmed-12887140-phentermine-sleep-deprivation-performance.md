# Phentermine in sleep-deprivation cognitive and motor performance study

- PMID: 12887140
- DOI: 10.1080/1028415031000120552
- Journal: Nutritional Neuroscience (2003)

## Captured takeaways

- Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled parallel-arm study in healthy young men after overnight sleep deprivation.
- Compared placebo, tyrosine 150 mg/kg, caffeine 300 mg/70 kg, phentermine 37.5 mg, and d-amphetamine 20 mg.
- Cognitive and motor tests included visual scanning, running memory, logical reasoning, mathematical processing, Stroop, serial reaction time, tracking, visual vigilance, Trails B, and long-term memory.
- Sleep deprivation impaired several tasks, including visual scanning, running memory, logical reasoning, mathematical processing, Stroop, tracking, and vigilance.
- The abstract reports medication-related improvements after sleep deprivation in running memory, logical reasoning, mathematical processing, tracking, and visual vigilance.
- The authors concluded that all tested drugs improved at least some aspects of cognitive and motor performance after sleep deprivation, with d-amphetamine strongest and tyrosine less effective.
- A companion abstract in the NCBI Caffeine for the Sustainment of Mental Task Performance workshop reports that caffeine, amphetamine, and phentermine improved most target performance measures during sleep deprivation, especially response time, but phentermine and amphetamine impaired recovery sleep depth.
- Interpretation for the wiki: this is a fatigue / sleep-loss rescue signal, not a rested healthy-adult enhancement result. Recovery-sleep cost is a central confound.
