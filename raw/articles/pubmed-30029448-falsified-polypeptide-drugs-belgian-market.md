# Impurity profiling of the most frequently encountered falsified polypeptide drugs on the Belgian market

- PMID: 30029448
- DOI: 10.1016/j.talanta.2018.06.023
- Journal: Talanta, 2018
- Source type: analytical study of falsified peptide drugs
- Ingested: 2026-05-06

## Summary

Belgian analytical study of falsified peptide drugs bought from suspected illegal internet pharmacies. The abstract reports large variability in active-drug amount, low purity in several peptide products, and contamination with toxic elemental impurities including arsenic and lead.

## Key points

- The authors screened ten frequently encountered falsified peptide drugs from three suspected illegal internet pharmacies.
- Reported problems included high unit-to-unit variability, low purity, peptide-related impurities, and toxic elemental contamination.
- The study is not BPC-157-specific, but it is directly relevant to any wiki claim that gray-market peptide supply is usually human-grade.

## Signal Use

- Sourcing signal: strong counterweight to blanket assumptions that research-chemical / gray-market peptides are reliably high quality.
- Safety signal: contamination risk can dominate the pharmacology question.
- Use limit: broad peptide-market evidence, not molecule-specific efficacy or toxicity for BPC-157.
