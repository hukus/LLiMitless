# PMC 7025776: Nicergoline for dementia and age-associated cognitive impairment

This Cochrane review evaluated nicergoline for dementia and other age-associated forms of cognitive impairment.

Evidence base: 11 studies with extractable numerical data met inclusion criteria after a broad search of published and unpublished trials. The review notes many other studies were excluded because they lacked placebo control, had no control group, or used non-qualifying populations.

Main findings: ADAS-Cog data from two studies in Alzheimer's disease tended to favor nicergoline but were not statistically significant. Other cognitive measures tended to favor nicergoline with limited sample sizes. Clinical global impression across six studies and 857 patients favored nicergoline versus placebo with Peto OR 3.33, 95% CI 2.50-4.43. Tolerability across 1362 patients showed a modest adverse-effect increase, Peto OR 1.51, 95% CI 1.10-2.07.

Interpretation for the wiki: nicergoline has a real but legacy clinical-state evidence packet. It is not a modern healthy-adult nootropic proof, and the review explicitly notes that it was not evaluated with current MCI categories or alongside modern therapeutic agents.
