# Iron fact sheet for health professionals

- Source type: official nutrient status, dosing, safety, and interaction overview
- Checked: 2026-05-11

## Summary

ODS describes iron as an essential mineral needed for oxygen transport, energy metabolism, growth, neurological development, cellular functioning, and hormone synthesis. Body iron status is usually interpreted with hemoglobin, hematocrit, ferritin, transferrin saturation, serum iron, total iron-binding capacity, and clinical context. Iron deficiency progresses from depleted stores to iron-deficient erythropoiesis and iron-deficiency anemia.

For cognition routing, the important point is status dependence: deficiency can plausibly produce fatigue, weakness, poor concentration, exercise intolerance, mood symptoms, and impaired cognitive performance, but this does not mean iron improves cognition in iron-replete adults.

## Safety Notes

- Adult recommended intakes vary strongly by sex, menstruation, pregnancy, and age.
- Adult tolerable upper intake level is 45 mg/day from food and supplements unless higher-dose therapy is medically supervised.
- Acute iron poisoning is dangerous; excess can cause gastrointestinal injury, metabolic acidosis, shock, liver failure, coma, and death.
- Hemochromatosis, repeated transfusion, chronic liver disease, and unnecessary high-dose supplementation raise risk.
- Iron can interact with levothyroxine, levodopa, some antibiotics, and other drugs by reducing absorption.
- Ferritin can rise with inflammation, so iron status should not be inferred from one number without context.
