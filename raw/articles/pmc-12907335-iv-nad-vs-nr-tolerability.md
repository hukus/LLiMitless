# PMC 12907335 IV NAD+ versus IV NR real-world tolerability pilot

Reyna et al. 2026, Frontiers in Aging: "Intravenous infusion of nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD+) versus nicotinamide riboside (NR): a retrospective tolerability pilot study in a real-world setting." DOI 10.3389/fragi.2026.1652582.

The study retrospectively reviewed commercial clinic clients who received four consecutive days of 500 mg IV NAD+ or 500 mg IV NR, with 30-day follow-up. Six clients received IV NAD+ and eight received IV NR. Outcomes included reported infusion symptoms, infusion time, blood pressure, heart rate, liver/renal/inflammatory/thyroid biomarkers, metabolic biomarkers, and PROMIS-style survey measures including fatigue, sleep, and cognitive function.

Tolerability differed sharply. All six NAD+ clients reported moderate to severe abdominal cramping, diarrhea, nausea, vomiting, increased heart rates, throat pain, congestion, and chest pressure during infusion; symptoms stopped after infusion completion. Five of eight NR clients had minor tingling or cramping. Mean infusion time was much longer with NAD+ than NR, about 97 minutes versus 37 minutes across the four-day loading period.

The study found no significant changes in ALT, AST, hsCRP, BUN/creatinine, TSH, systolic or diastolic blood pressure over 30 days. Metabolic exploratory findings were variable and clinically uncertain. The authors emphasized retrospective design, tiny sample, commercial setting, lack of placebo or matched controls, and need for larger trials. This is not a cognition-efficacy study.

Interpretation for the wiki: IV NAD+ and IV NR belong in a route-gated safety/tolerability branch. The study provides short-term real-world tolerability and biomarker context, not proof that direct NAD+ products improve healthy-adult cognition or brain fog.
