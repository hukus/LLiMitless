# BMC Chemistry 2025: Thallium in Shilajit raw material and supplements

This 2025 BMC Chemistry paper analyzed thallium in Shilajit raw materials and commercially available supplements.

Methods and sample framing: the authors used ICP-MS to measure thallium after nitric acid digestion in 28 Shilajit-containing samples, including raw Himalayan material and marketed supplements.

Findings: the highest thallium concentration in raw varieties was 0.226 microgram/gram, while commercial supplements reached up to 0.5 microgram/gram. The paper concludes that supplements can contain higher thallium levels than raw Shilajit, raising quality-control and consumer-safety concerns.

Interpretation for the wiki: thallium is not the whole heavy-metal story, but this is a direct modern analytical anchor for product-specific risk. Shilajit claims should require batch COAs covering toxic metals, not just fulvic-acid percentage or branding.
