# L-methylfolate as adjunctive therapy for SSRI-resistant major depression

- Source type: PubMed abstract / randomized controlled trials
- PMID: 23212058
- Journal: American Journal of Psychiatry
- Published: 2012-12
- Checked: 2026-05-15 via PubMed E-utilities

## Summary

Papakostas et al. reported two multicenter, randomized, double-blind, sequential-parallel trials of adjunctive L-methylfolate in SSRI-resistant major depressive disorder. Trial 1 enrolled 148 outpatients and used 7.5 mg/day for 30 days followed by 15 mg/day for 30 days in the continuous-treatment arm; it found no significant difference between treatment groups. Trial 2 enrolled 75 outpatients and used 15 mg/day in both 30-day periods; adjunctive L-methylfolate showed significantly greater efficacy than continued SSRI plus placebo on response rate, change in depression score, and two secondary severity measures. The number needed to treat for response was approximately 6, and adverse-event rates were not different from placebo.

## Use In Wiki

- Supports a mood-state / depression-adjunct signal for L-methylfolate at 15 mg/day.
- Does not establish healthy-adult cognitive enhancement or direct brain-fog efficacy.
- Best routed beside SAMe, folate/B12/B6, and one-carbon metabolism rather than as a standalone nootropic proof.

## Caveats

- The positive result was in the second, smaller trial; the first trial was null.
- Participants had SSRI-resistant major depressive disorder, not healthy-adult cognitive complaints.
- Cognitive outcomes were not the main endpoint.
