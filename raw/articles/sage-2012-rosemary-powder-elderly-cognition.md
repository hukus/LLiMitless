# Rosemary powder acute cognition study in older adults

- Source type: randomized double-blind placebo-controlled repeated-measures crossover trial.
- Publication: Journal of Medicinal Food, 2012.
- Date checked: 2026-05-11.

This study tested acute dried rosemary leaf powder in 28 older adults with a mean age of about 75 years. Participants completed Cognitive Drug Research computerized testing 1, 2.5, 4, and 6 hours after placebo and four rosemary doses, with counterbalanced dosing and 7-day washouts.

The dose-response pattern was biphasic. The lowest 750 mg dose improved speed of memory versus placebo, while the highest 6000 mg dose impaired the same measure. Other cognitive effects included less consistent deleterious findings. The authors highlighted the low-dose signal nearest culinary consumption and the need for longer-term work.

Use in the wiki: rosemary should be product- and dose-gated. Low-dose oral powder has a narrow older-adult speed-of-memory signal, while high doses may impair performance.
