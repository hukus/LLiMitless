# GeroScience 2024 glycine administration in human adults systematic review note

Soh et al. published an open-access systematic review in GeroScience on the effect of glycine administration on physiological-system characteristics in human adults. The review was published online October 18, 2023 and appeared in GeroScience 46:219-239 in 2024.

The authors searched Embase, PubMed, Web of Science, and CENTRAL through April 29, 2022. They included 47 articles describing 50 studies: 18 in healthy populations, 34 in diseased populations, and 2 containing both healthy and diseased populations. Most studies were randomized controlled trials, and most used oral glycine. Healthy-population exposure was generally short, up to 14 days, while diseased-population exposure reached up to 4 months.

The review's nervous-system read was the most relevant to cognition. It reported that longer-term glycine administration improved sleep in healthy populations, but those sleep studies had small sample sizes and high risk of bias. It also reported more positive nervous-system findings in psychiatric populations, especially schizophrenia, but that disease-state signal should not be treated as healthy-adult enhancement.

The authors concluded that glycine may improve characteristics of multiple physiological systems, but evidence remains limited for prevention or broad healthy-population benefit. Larger, more robust, longer-term randomized trials in healthy populations are needed before glycine can be treated as a general geroprotector or cognition-enhancing molecule.

Wiki use: this is the broadest modern human-adult evidence anchor for [[glycine]]. It supports a low-confidence sleep-state / healthspan-hypothesis classification, not a direct nootropic classification.
