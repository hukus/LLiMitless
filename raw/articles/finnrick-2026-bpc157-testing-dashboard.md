## Summary

Public BPC-157 testing dashboard from Finnrick Analytics. On 2026-05-06, the page reported 528 tested BPC-157 samples sourced from 75 vendors between 2024-12-17 and 2026-04-28. The page summarized 5th-95th percentile purity at 96.49%-99.95% and quantity divergence up to +/-71% versus label claim at the 95th percentile.

## Key points

- The current dashboard suggests that many active-market BPC-157 lots reach high HPLC purity bands.
- The same dashboard shows wide quantity spread, so purity and dosing accuracy are not the same problem.
- Finnrick's methodology page says samples are sent to commercial labs, HPLC is the primary method, and purity/quantity drive the scoring system.
- The methodology also notes mixed sample provenance across direct procurement, vendor-supplied samples, and public submissions.

## Signal Use

- Market-quality signal: meaningful counterweight to the claim that all gray-market peptide supply is uniformly poor.
- Caveat: not peer-reviewed, not a sterility/endotoxin validation program, and not a proof that gray-market product is human-grade.
- Best use: low-confidence market-monitoring evidence showing that some peptide supply chains may achieve high purity while identity, dosing, contamination, and vendor-spread risk remain unresolved.
