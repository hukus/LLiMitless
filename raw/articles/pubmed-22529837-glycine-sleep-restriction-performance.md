# PubMed 22529837 glycine sleep-restriction daytime performance note

Bannai et al. tested 3 g glycine before bedtime in healthy male volunteers during partial sleep restriction. Sleep was restricted to 25% less than usual for three consecutive nights, and the following-day outcomes included visual-analog sleepiness/fatigue ratings, questionnaire measures, and computerized performance tests.

The abstract reports that glycine significantly reduced fatigue by VAS and showed a tendency toward reduced sleepiness. Questionnaire data pointed in the same direction. The computerized battery showed a significant improvement in psychomotor vigilance-test reaction time, while simple reaction, memory recognition, and number-addition tests were not significant.

The study also measured melatonin and circadian-related markers. Glycine did not significantly alter plasma melatonin or clock-gene expression, although it affected SCN neuropeptide expression in the animal component. The practical human interpretation is sleep-restriction state support rather than direct memory or broad cognition enhancement.

Wiki use: this is the best healthy-adult cognition-adjacent anchor for [[glycine]]. It belongs in the same state-support family as sleep/circadian, fatigue, and vigilance interventions. It should not be upgraded into a resting healthy-adult nootropic claim because the sample was tiny, the exposure was sleep-restriction-bound, and most cognitive tests were null.
