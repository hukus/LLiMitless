# 2008 high-dose glycine healthy-control cognition null note

Palmer et al. tested whether modulating the NMDA receptor glycine site with acute high-dose glycine improved cognition in healthy young subjects. The study was published in Human Psychopharmacology: Clinical and Experimental in 2008, volume 23 issue 2, pages 151-159, DOI 10.1002/hup.904, PMID 17972276.

The design was randomized, placebo-controlled, and repeated-measures. Participants received oral glycine at 0.8 g/kg or placebo, separated by a 5-day washout. Cognition was assessed with the Cognitive Drug Research computerized assessment system across working memory, declarative memory, attention, and perceptual processing domains.

The abstract reports no significant glycine effects on working memory, declarative memory, attention, or perceptual processing. The authors interpreted the result as casting doubt on acute high-dose glycine as a cognition enhancer in healthy subjects and suggested that dose and population context may matter.

Wiki use: this is the direct healthy-adult counterweight for [[glycine]] and for simplistic NMDA-coagonist nootropic claims. It should be linked to [[sarcosine]], [[d-serine]], and [[nmda-coagonist-glyt1-daao-cognition-2026-scan]] as an example where more glycine-site stimulation did not improve healthy cognition.
