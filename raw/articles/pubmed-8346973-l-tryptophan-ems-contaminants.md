# Contaminants in L-tryptophan associated with eosinophilia myalgia syndrome

- PMID: 8346973
- DOI: 10.1007/BF00230724
- Journal: Archives of Environmental Contamination and Toxicology (1993)
- Source type: contamination / toxicology investigation
- Checked: 2026-05-03

## Summary

This paper investigated contaminants in L-tryptophan associated with the late-1989 eosinophilia-myalgia syndrome outbreak. The PubMed abstract reports that the US epidemic involved several thousand cases and 36 deaths, and that New Mexico physicians linked the syndrome to ingestion of L-tryptophan. The investigators concluded that trace contaminants in L-tryptophan were likely causes of the outbreak.

For the wiki, this is not a cognition-efficacy source. It is a product-quality and historical safety anchor showing why serotonin-precursor supplements need sourcing, manufacturing, and contaminant caution.

## Interpretation For Cognition Claims

- Do not treat L-tryptophan food intake, L-tryptophan supplement capsules, 5-HTP, and melatonin as interchangeable.
- Safety history is product/manufacturing-sensitive, not just molecule-name-sensitive.
- The EMS outbreak is a cautionary source for supplement identity and QA, not evidence that normal dietary tryptophan improves cognition.
