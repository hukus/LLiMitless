# PubMed 10071523 NADH chronic fatigue syndrome crossover trial

The 1999 Annals of Allergy, Asthma & Immunology trial evaluated ENADA, a stabilized oral absorbable NADH form, in chronic fatigue syndrome (CFS). The background describes CFS as prolonged debilitating fatigue with symptoms including neurocognitive dysfunction, flu-like symptoms, myalgia, weakness, arthralgia, low-grade fever, sore throat, headache, sleep disturbance, and tender lymph nodes.

Design: randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover. Twenty-six eligible CFS patients completed the analysis. Participants received 10 mg NADH or placebo for 4 weeks, then had a 4-week washout and crossed to the alternate regimen for a final 4 weeks. Medical history, physical exam, labs, and questionnaires were collected at baseline and follow-up.

Result: no severe adverse effects related to study drug were observed. Eight of 26 patients (31%) responded favorably to NADH versus two of 26 (8%) to placebo. The authors concluded that the pilot result suggested possible adjunctive value in CFS and warranted further trials.

Interpretation for the wiki: this is an illness-state fatigue and neurocognitive-symptom context, not a healthy-adult cognition trial. It is positive enough to justify NADH coverage, but the endpoint is a broad symptom response rather than objective memory, attention, or executive-function improvement.
