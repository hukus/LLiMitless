# CSTI-500 first-in-human monoamine TRI study

- Source: Appel et al., Psychopharmacology 2026 issue, published online 2025; first-in-human study of CSTI-500 / BMS-866949.
- Compound identity: CSTI-500 was described as a novel monoamine triple reuptake inhibitor of SERT, NET, and DAT, primarily developed by Bristol-Myers Squibb for MDD. The source also notes later continued-development interest.
- Design: part A was a double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, single-ascending-dose study in healthy volunteers, with doses from 0.5 to 150 mg. Part B was an open-label PET study estimating striatal SERT and DAT occupancy after a single dose.
- Result: maximum tolerable acute single dose was 100 mg. No serious adverse events occurred.
- PK: median maximum concentrations occurred 1-2 hours after dose; estimated plasma half-life was 44-50 hours.
- Target engagement: after a single 100 mg dose, mean striatal SERT occupancy was 72% at 4-6 hours and 62% at 24 hours; mean striatal DAT occupancy was 36% at 4-9 hours and 31% at 24 hours.
- Tolerability caveat: after 150 mg, 5 of 6 active-treated subjects had acute moderate to severe nausea and dizziness, and one had severe vomiting. Heart rate increased markedly at 150 mg.
- Interpretation for the wiki: CSTI-500 is the cleanest healthy-volunteer target-engagement source in this TRI branch, but it is safety/PK/PET evidence, not a cognition-efficacy study.
