## Summary

2026 Cognitive Vitality review of Sceletium tortuosum / Zembrin / kanna. The review says historical use does not equal efficacy, that the plant contains compounds with serotonin-linked and other biological targets, and that a few small short-duration clinical trials suggest possible improvement in complex cognitive functions, but larger and longer trials are lacking.

The review identifies five randomized clinical trials in healthy adults and numerous preclinical mechanism studies. It highlights the 21-person Zembrin middle-aged healthy-adult crossover trial, where 25 mg/day for three weeks improved cognitive flexibility and executive function but did not improve verbal memory, visual memory, processing speed, psychomotor speed, reaction, or complex attention. It also highlights the 60-person recreationally trained young-adult trial, where 25 mg/day for eight days improved complex reactive performance and reactive agility but not simpler visual tracking, reaction-time, mood, alertness, or energy outcomes.

Safety framing: Zembrin looked generally well tolerated in short studies, including a three-month healthy-adult trial, but long-term safety has not been established. The review cautions against use with drugs that alter serotonin uptake or release and notes a theoretical interaction with PDE4 inhibitors such as roflumilast.

## Notes

- Useful current synthesis for separating standardized Zembrin from generic kanna products.
- Supports a `low` medicinal cognition signal: small, short, product-specific, and complex-task-limited.
- Supports a `moderate` anecdote/community signal when combined with ACX/SSC survey data, but not a high-confidence efficacy claim.
