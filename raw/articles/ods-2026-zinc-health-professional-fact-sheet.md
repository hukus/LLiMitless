# Zinc fact sheet for health professionals

- Publisher: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements
- Updated: 2026
- Source type: official nutrient status, dosing, safety, and interaction overview
- Checked: 2026-05-11
- Ingested: 2026-05-11

## Summary

ODS describes zinc as an essential mineral involved in catalytic, structural, and regulatory functions, including immune function, protein and DNA synthesis, wound healing, taste and smell, and cell division. Zinc status is difficult to assess because serum zinc can be influenced by inflammation, time of day, meals, and stress.

For cognition routing, zinc is a status-correction and interaction candidate. Low zinc status can plausibly contribute to fatigue, immunity problems, taste/smell changes, and nonspecific brain-fog narratives, but controlled cognition data in generally healthy adults are not strong enough to rank it as an enhancer.

## Safety Notes

- Adult tolerable upper intake level is 40 mg/day.
- Chronic high-dose zinc can cause copper deficiency, anemia, neutropenia, impaired immune function, and neurological problems.
- Zinc can interact with some antibiotics and penicillamine by reducing absorption.
- Zinc lozenges and high-dose protocols should be separated from daily cognition claims.
- Copper status and dose duration matter when zinc is used above ordinary dietary levels.
