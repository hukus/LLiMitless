# CX614 sustained BDNF signaling and AMPA receptor downregulation

Open-access paper on whether AMPAkines can sustain BDNF signaling without impairing AMPA receptor expression.

Key extracted points:

- The study frames AMPAkines as compounds that slow AMPA receptor deactivation and desensitization, thereby enhancing fast excitatory transmission.
- Prolonged 12-24 hour treatment with CX614 reduced AMPAR subunit GluR1-3 mRNA and protein levels in cultured rat hippocampal slices.
- The study also found sustained increases in BDNF content and TrkB receptor activation at excitatory synapses.
- The authors interpret AMPAR downregulation as a broad response to prolonged elevated neuronal activity rather than a clean enhancement-only profile.
- Interpretation for the wiki: CX614 illustrates why "BDNF up" and "plasticity" are incomplete nootropic claims; prolonged AMPAR modulation can trigger compensatory receptor and excitability changes.
