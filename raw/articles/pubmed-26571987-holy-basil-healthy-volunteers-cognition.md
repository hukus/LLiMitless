# Holy basil leaf extract cognition trial in healthy volunteers

- Source type: randomized double-blind placebo-controlled study.
- Publication: Indian Journal of Physiology and Pharmacology, 2015.
- Date checked: 2026-05-11.

This study tested 300 mg/day ethanolic leaf extract of Ocimum sanctum, also known as Ocimum tenuiflorum, for 30 days in healthy male volunteers aged 18-30 years. The extract was described as 70% ethanolic dried-leaf extract, with quality checks for solvent residues, heavy metals, pesticide residues, microbiological contamination, and ursolic acid content.

Intergroup comparisons favored holy basil over placebo on Sternberg reaction time and error rate, Stroop neutral reaction time, and Stroop interference reaction time and error rate. P300 latency improved in the extract group, while salivary cortisol and anxiety scores improved over time only within the extract group. Heart rate and galvanic skin response did not change.

The study is small and practice effects were visible in both groups, but it is a direct healthy-volunteer cognition signal for a defined holy-basil leaf extract.

Use in the wiki: holy basil deserves low-confidence standalone coverage as an Ayurvedic adaptogen with direct but small healthy-adult cognitive-task evidence.
