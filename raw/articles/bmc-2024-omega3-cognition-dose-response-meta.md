# Omega-3 cognition dose-response meta-analysis

2024 BMC Medicine systematic review and dose-response meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials testing n-3 polyunsaturated fatty acids in adults aged 40 or older without dementia.

Scope: 24 studies, 9,660 participants, follow-up 3 to 36 months. Interventions included n-3 PUFA, DHA, EPA, and ALA trials. Outcomes covered global cognition, episodic memory, executive function, processing speed, attention, and visuospatial function.

Main result: the random-effects models did not show a significant association with global cognition or five other domains overall. Dose-response modeling found an executive-function signal trending upward over the first 12 months, most prominently with daily n-3 PUFA above 500 mg and EPA up to about 420 mg/day. The authors also reported that effects were more evident in regions where blood DHA + EPA was not very low.

Use in wiki: supports a `low-moderate but domain-specific` read for omega-3 cognition. It is not broad enhancement proof; executive function, duration, EPA dose, baseline omega-3 status, and geography/status proxies matter.
