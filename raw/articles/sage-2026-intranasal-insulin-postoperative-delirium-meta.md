# Efficacy of intranasal insulin in reducing the incidence of postoperative delirium: A systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials

- Journal: Journal of Perioperative Practice
- First published online: 2026-02-22
- DOI: 10.1177/17504589261421004
- Source type: review article / systematic review and meta-analysis
- Authors: Arusha Desai, Rahul Bisht, Esteban Marcelo Mogrovejo Carrion, Vishakha Maheshwari, Sidharth Misra, Mitali Joshi, Anjali Bhardwaj, and colleagues
- Ingested: 2026-05-12

## Summary

Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials testing intranasal insulin for postoperative delirium prevention in surgical patients. The review searched PubMed, EMBASE, Cochrane Central, and Web of Science from inception through October 2025.

The abstract reports nine randomized controlled trials with 1039 total patients, including 574 patients assigned to intranasal insulin. Intranasal insulin lowered overall postoperative-delirium incidence with risk ratio 0.36 and 95 percent confidence interval 0.28 to 0.46. Subgroup analyses also favored intranasal insulin after orthopedic, gastrointestinal, and cardiac surgeries.

## Notes

This source extends the perioperative intranasal-insulin evidence beyond the earlier 2026 PubMed-indexed meta-analysis captured in `raw/articles/pubmed-41720686-intranasal-insulin-perioperative-delirium-meta.md`, which summarized seven studies and 732 postoperative patients.

The interpretation remains state-specific. This strengthens the perioperative / postoperative-delirium prevention lane, but it does not upgrade intranasal insulin as a broad healthy-adult cognition enhancer or chronic MCI/AD disease-modifying therapy. The abstract also says intranasal insulin is not standardized or part of treatment protocols, so protocol, dose, timing, and external replication remain important.
