# Short-term folate, B12, or B6 supplementation and memory in women

- PMID: 12042457
- Title: Short-term folate, vitamin B-12 or vitamin B-6 supplementation slightly affects memory performance but not mood in women of various ages
- Journal: Journal of Nutrition
- Publication date: 2002-06
- Source type: randomized controlled trial / nutrient-cognition signal
- Checked: 2026-05-16
- Ingested: 2026-05-16

## Summary

This randomized trial enrolled 211 healthy younger, middle-aged, and older women. Participants took one of four daily interventions for 35 days: 750 micrograms folate, 15 micrograms vitamin B12, 75 mg vitamin B6, or placebo.

The study tested cognitive processing resources, memory, executive function, verbal ability, and self-rated mood before and after supplementation.

The abstract reports that supplementation had a significant positive effect on some memory-performance measures only, with no effect on mood. Usual dietary intake status was associated with speed of processing, recall, recognition, and verbal ability.

## Wiki Use

Use as a weak, short-term B-vitamin cognition clue for [[vitamin-b6-p5p]] and [[folate-b-vitamins]]. It is not clean evidence for P5P, pyridoxine, folate, or B12 as standalone nootropics because the trial was short, form-specific attribution is limited, and the signal was selective to some memory measures.
