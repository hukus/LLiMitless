# Comparing adaptive tablet-based cognitive training and paper-and-pencil cognitive training: a pilot randomized controlled trial with community-dwelling stroke survivors

- PMID: 41070261
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41070261/
- Ingested: 2026-04-29

## Summary

Two-arm pilot RCT in 20 community-dwelling stroke survivors comparing adaptive tablet-based cognitive training inspired by activities of daily living with adaptive paper-and-pencil training, plus a non-randomized passive control group. Interventions used 12 bi-weekly 30-minute sessions.

The adaptive tablet arm showed more within-group cognitive gains than the paper-and-pencil arm and improved function, quality of life, and motivation. The authors describe the tablet program as the more ecologically valid route and the only one to generalize to functionality and quality of life at post-intervention and follow-up.

## Notes

The sample is tiny, but this is one of the cleanest task-ecology examples in the branch because the training is explicitly built around activities of daily living.
