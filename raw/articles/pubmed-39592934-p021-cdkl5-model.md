# P021 in cellular and mouse models of CDKL5 deficiency disorder

- PMID: 39592934
- DOI: 10.1186/s11689-024-09583-4
- Journal: Journal of Neurodevelopmental Disorders (2024)

## Summary

P021 improved proliferation, survival, maturation, and GSK3-beta signaling measures in a human SH-SY5Y CDKL5-knockout cell model. The in-vivo result was notably weaker: chronic treatment in Cdkl5-knockout mice did not increase BDNF, did not correct the main neuroanatomical defects, and produced only limited behavioral benefit.

## Evidence boundary

This is an important model-specific counter-signal to the Alzheimer and Down-syndrome mouse literature. It shows that the proposed BDNF/neurogenesis mechanism does not generalize automatically across CNS disorders. Khalid Iqbal, a coauthor, disclosed P021 patents and a Phanes Biotech role.
