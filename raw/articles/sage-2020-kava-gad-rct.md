# Kava for generalised anxiety disorder: A 16-week double-blind randomized placebo-controlled study

2020 Australian and New Zealand Journal of Psychiatry phase III randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of aqueous dried-kava-root extract in generalized anxiety disorder. The study enrolled 171 non-medicated anxious participants and used tablets standardized to 120 mg kavalactones twice daily for 16 weeks.

The abstract reports no significant anxiety-reduction advantage versus placebo. Poorer memory and tremor/shakiness were more frequent in the kava group, and liver-function-test abnormalities were also more frequent, although no participant met herb-induced hepatic injury criteria.

Relevance: direct counterweight to kava calm-focus anecdotes; cognition routing should include memory-adverse and liver-monitoring caveats.
