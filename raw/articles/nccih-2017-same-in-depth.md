# S-Adenosyl-L-Methionine (SAMe): In Depth

NCCIH fact sheet last updated January 2017, with the site currently served on 2026-05-15. It defines S-adenosyl-L-methionine as an endogenous methionine-derived compound also called SAMe, SAM-e, ademetionine, SAM, or AdoMet, and notes that it is sold in the United States as a dietary supplement.

The page says SAMe has mainly been studied for depression, osteoarthritis, and liver diseases, but that evidence is not conclusive for these uses. It also notes that SAMe has been studied for other conditions including fibromyalgia, migraine, schizophrenia, Alzheimer's disease, and ADHD, but evidence is insufficient to reach conclusions for those conditions.

Safety gates are central for this wiki branch. NCCIH flags limited long-term safety data, limited pregnancy safety data, bipolar/mania risk, possible interaction with levodopa, possible interaction with serotonin-increasing drugs or supplements such as antidepressants, L-tryptophan, and St. John's wort, and a theoretical Pneumocystis concern in immunocompromised people. Reported side effects are usually uncommon and mild, mainly nausea or digestive upset.

Wiki use: this is the conservative public-health baseline for [[s-adenosyl-l-methionine]]. It supports treating SAMe as a mood/one-carbon metabolism candidate with important medication and bipolar gates, not as a proven healthy-adult cognition enhancer.
