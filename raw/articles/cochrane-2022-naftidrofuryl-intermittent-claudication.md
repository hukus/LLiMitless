# Naftidrofuryl for intermittent claudication - Cochrane plain-language and abstract capture

Cochrane page for de Backer et al., "Naftidrofuryl for intermittent claudication," Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, published on the Cochrane site 1 April 2022, Art. No. CD001368.

The review evaluated oral naftidrofuryl versus placebo for pain-free walking distance in people with intermittent claudication. The Cochrane page describes collection of randomized trials plus individual patient data.

The review included seven studies with 1266 patients. The ratio of relative improvement in pain-free walking distance for naftidrofuryl versus placebo was 1.37, 95% CI 1.27 to 1.49. The absolute difference in responder rate was 22.3%, 95% CI 17.1% to 27.6%, with a calculated number needed to treat of 4.5.

Cochrane concluded that oral naftidrofuryl has a statistically significant and clinically meaningful, although moderate, effect on walking distance in the six months after therapy starts. The page gives the practical dose as naftidrofuryl 200 mg three times daily by mouth.

Relevance: confirms that naftidrofuryl has a real peripheral-artery-disease symptom signal, but this is a walking-distance / vascular symptom outcome, not a cognitive-enhancement endpoint.
