# Theobromine consumption does not improve fasting and postprandial vascular function

- PMID: 29330660
- PMCID: PMC6499748
- DOI: 10.1007/s00394-018-1612-6
- Journal: European Journal of Nutrition (2019)

## Captured takeaways

- Randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover study in 44 apparently healthy overweight or obese men and women with low HDL-C concentrations.
- Participants consumed 500 mg/day theobromine or placebo for 4 weeks.
- Outcomes included fasting and postprandial flow-mediated dilation, peripheral arterial tonometry, augmentation index, pulse wave velocity, blood pressure, and retinal microvasculature.
- The study found no improvement in fasting vascular function markers except a small central augmentation-index decrease, and no modification of the high-fat-meal postprandial endothelial-function effects.
- Theobromine increased postprandial brachial artery diameter and lowered selected augmentation-index measures, but the authors concluded the findings did not suggest that theobromine alone contributes to cocoa's proposed cardioprotective effects.
- Interpretation for the wiki: vascular proxy evidence for theobromine alone is mixed/mostly null and should not be used to upgrade cognition or brain-fog claims.
