# GlyNAC older-adults randomized aging-hallmarks trial

Double-blind placebo-controlled randomized trial in 24 older adults plus 12 young-adult comparators. Older adults were randomized to GlyNAC or isonitrogenous alanine placebo for 16 weeks; young adults received GlyNAC for 2 weeks. The registered trial was NCT01870193.

Dose and controls: GlyNAC used 100 mg/kg/day each of glycine and N-acetylcysteine; placebo used 200 mg/kg/day alanine. Supplements were quality checked, capsule matched, and dispensed by a pharmacist. The trial stopped nonvitamin nutritional supplements and lipid-lowering drugs before testing to reduce mitochondrial confounding.

Findings: older adults had glutathione deficiency, oxidative stress, mitochondrial dysfunction, inflammation, endothelial dysfunction, insulin resistance, multiple aging-hallmark defects, impaired physical function, higher waist circumference, and higher systolic blood pressure. GlyNAC, but not placebo, improved or corrected many of these defects. The paper reports that 16-week GlyNAC was safe and well tolerated.

Use in wiki: this is the strongest blinded human mechanistic/safety anchor, but it is not a cognition-efficacy trial. It should raise mechanistic plausibility for older metabolic-redox phenotypes without upgrading cognition confidence.
