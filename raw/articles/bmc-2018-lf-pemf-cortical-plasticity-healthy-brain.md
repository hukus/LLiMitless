# Modulation of Long-Term Potentiation-Like Cortical Plasticity in the Healthy Brain With Low Frequency-Pulsed Electromagnetic Fields

Source type: open-access BMC Neuroscience proof-of-concept human study.

Core design:
- Published 2018.
- N=10 healthy subjects.
- Randomized double-blind sham-controlled crossover.
- Intervention was CTU Mega 20 low-frequency pulsed electromagnetic field stimulation over the primary motor area.
- Active protocol: magnetic field up to 2 Tesla, intensity 90 J, impulse frequency 7 Hz, 15-minute duration.
- TMS motor-evoked potentials were recorded before and after stimulation as a cortical-excitability readout.

Main findings:
- Significant time-by-treatment interaction for motor evoked potential amplitude.
- Real LF-PEMF increased corticospinal excitability immediately and through 30 minutes post-stimulation; sham did not show the same pattern.
- No direct depolarizing effect was detected during active stimulation.

Wiki takeaway:
- This supports the claim that LF-PEMF can modulate human cortical excitability under controlled conditions.
- It is not a cognition-efficacy study; it belongs in the target-engagement / physiology layer.
- Device, field strength, impulse frequency, placement, and TMS readout must stay attached to the claim.

Limitations and credibility notes:
- Very small sample.
- Motor cortex physiology endpoint, not memory, attention, or brain fog.
- Funded by PERISO SA, which provided real and sham CTU devices and device technical description.
- Good for device-dose ontology; weak for enhancement ranking.
