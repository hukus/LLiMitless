# Chronotype and Affective Response to Sleep Restriction and Subsequent Sleep Deprivation

- PMID: 37539684
- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37539684/
- Ingested: 2026-04-27

## Summary

In healthy adults, later chronotypes were more vulnerable to negative affect during sleep restriction and subsequent total sleep deprivation. The influence of chronotype on positive affect depended on how chronotype was defined.

## Notes

- Sixteen healthy adults completed a 10-day protocol.
- Sleep restriction was followed by 28 hours of total sleep deprivation.
- Affect was sampled hourly with PANAS.
- This paper supports chronotype as a vulnerability marker for sleep-loss countermeasures, even though it is not a treatment trial.
