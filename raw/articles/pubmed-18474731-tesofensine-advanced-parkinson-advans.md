# Tesofensine (NS 2330), a monoamine reuptake inhibitor, in patients with advanced Parkinson disease and motor fluctuations: the ADVANS Study

- PMID: 18474731
- DOI: 10.1001/archneur.65.5.577
- Journal: Archives of Neurology (2008)
- Trial registration: NCT00148512

## Captured takeaways

- Phase 2 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel-group pilot trial in advanced Parkinson disease with levodopa-related motor fluctuations.
- Tesofensine / NS 2330 was described as a triple monoamine reuptake inhibitor; doses were 0.125, 0.25, 0.5, and 1 mg once daily for 14 weeks.
- Coprimary endpoints were UPDRS II+III total score and waking "off" time in diaries, not healthy-adult cognition.
- The 0.5 mg dose improved UPDRS II+III versus placebo by an adjusted mean difference of -4.7 points, while 0.25 mg improved off time by -7.1 percentage points / -68 minutes.
- No clear dose-response relationship was established.
- Gastrointestinal and neuropsychiatric adverse events were more frequent with tesofensine, especially at higher doses.
- The paper states a human half-life of approximately 8 days and notes sleep difficulty at high doses in prior healthy-volunteer tolerability work.
- Interpretation for the wiki: formal human CNS exposure exists, but it is a Parkinson motor-fluctuation signal, not a healthy-adult cognition or brain-fog trial.
