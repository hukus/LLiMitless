# Folate fact sheet for health professionals

- Publisher: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements
- Source type: official nutrient status, dosing, safety, and interaction overview
- Checked: 2026-05-03
- Ingested: 2026-05-03

## Summary

ODS defines folate as vitamin B9, covering food folates, folic acid, and supplemental 5-MTHF / methylfolate. Folate is required for one-carbon transfers, DNA/RNA synthesis, amino-acid metabolism, and homocysteine-to-methionine conversion. Serum folate above 3 ng/mL and erythrocyte folate above 140 ng/mL are commonly used adequacy markers, while homocysteine is functional but not specific because kidney function and B12 status also affect it.

## Safety Notes

- Adult supplements commonly provide 400-800 mcg folic acid.
- Excess folic acid can mask vitamin B12 deficiency by correcting megaloblastic anemia while neurological damage progresses.
- ODS notes possible concern about cognitive impairment in older adults with low B12 and high folic acid exposure.
- Folate status, B12 status, homocysteine, kidney function, form (folic acid vs 5-MTHF), and dose should be tracked together.
