# NCBI Bookshelf DARE 1999: Propentofylline in dementia

The NCBI Bookshelf DARE record "A review of the use of propentofylline in the treatment of dementia" summarizes a 1999 review by Chilcott, Perrett, Golightly, Sykes, and Whittingham. The stated objective was to assess propentofylline effectiveness in dementia.

The DARE summary reports randomized controlled trials and meta-analyses of phase III trials over 12 months, with pilot and phase II studies also considered. It describes statistically significant but modest differences in outcomes such as MMSE, global function, and activities of daily living.

Key DARE interpretation: the clinical relevance of the modest MMSE and global-function differences was very doubtful, no benefits were found in psychometric assessments between groups, and the authors said the trials showed only very modest improvements.

Adverse-event summary from DARE: adverse events were reported for 40% of the propentofylline group and 22% of the placebo group. Specific reported adverse events included nausea, dizziness, headache, and gastrointestinal pain.

Interpretation for the wiki: this source is a strong counterweight to optimistic phase-trial summaries because it highlights modest effect size, doubtful clinical relevance, and adverse-event imbalance.
