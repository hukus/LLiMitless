# PMC 8142643 DMG progressive multiple sclerosis randomized trial note

Wolfsegger et al. reported a one-year double-blind placebo-controlled randomized pilot trial of oral N,N-dimethylglycine in 30 randomized patients with progressive multiple sclerosis. The study was motivated by claims that DMG could improve oxygen utilization, endurance, fatigue, and performance.

Participants were randomized 2:1 to DMG 125 mg/day or placebo. Outcomes included disability, fatigue, cognitive performance, and gait measures; the cognitive measure included PASAT tracking. The trial found no treatment effects between placebo and DMG for disability, fatigue, cognitive, or gait parameters.

Interpretation for the wiki: this is the cleanest direct clinical counterweight for cognition/fatigue-adjacent DMG claims. It is disease-state and small, but it tested exactly the "fatigue / performance / cognition" story and did not find a benefit.
