# Selenium fact sheet for health professionals

- Publisher: NIH Office of Dietary Supplements
- Updated: 2025
- Source type: official nutrient status, dosing, safety, and interaction overview
- Checked: 2026-05-11
- Ingested: 2026-05-11

## Summary

ODS describes selenium as an essential trace element incorporated into selenoproteins involved in thyroid hormone metabolism, antioxidant defense, DNA synthesis, reproduction, and immune function. Selenium intake varies by food source and soil content.

For cognition routing, selenium is mostly a thyroid/redox/status and toxicity-gated candidate. It has some small clinical-state evidence and many deficiency/excess anecdotes, but the margin between useful and excessive exposure is narrower than many supplement discussions imply.

## Safety Notes

- Adult recommended intake is 55 mcg/day.
- Adult tolerable upper intake level is 400 mcg/day.
- Chronic high intake can cause hair and nail brittleness/loss, garlic breath odor, metallic taste, rash, nausea, diarrhea, fatigue, irritability, nervous-system abnormalities, and in extreme cases severe toxicity.
- Brazil nuts can contain highly variable selenium amounts.
- Selenium should not be stacked blindly with multivitamins, thyroid supplements, Brazil nuts, and high-dose standalone products.
