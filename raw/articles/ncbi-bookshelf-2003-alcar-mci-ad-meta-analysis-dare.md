# DARE Critical Abstract of 2003 ALCAR MCI and Mild AD Meta-Analysis

- Source: NCBI Bookshelf / Database of Abstracts of Reviews of Effects.
- Review assessed: Montgomery, Thal, and Amrein 2003 meta-analysis of double-blind randomized placebo-controlled acetyl-L-carnitine trials in mild cognitive impairment and mild Alzheimer's disease.
- PubMed: 12598816.
- Ingested: 2026-05-03.

## Summary

The DARE record says the 2003 meta-analysis included 21 RCTs with 1,479 participants, with 1,204 used in analyses. Included trials generally lasted 3 to 12 months and used ALCAR doses from 1.5 to 3.0 g/day. The pooled combined clinical and psychometric effect size favored ALCAR, and memory, attention/performance, and higher intellectual function categories were statistically significant in psychometric analyses.

The DARE commentary is the important quality gate: the review used many different scales, heterogeneity was present, study selection/extraction methods were not described, validity was not otherwise assessed, and most included studies were sponsored by Sigma-Tau with company input into statistical analysis. DARE judged the clinical importance of the statistically significant findings unclear.

## Interpretation

Use this as a positive-but-contested MCI / early-AD evidence anchor. It justifies ALCAR inclusion and disease-context tracking, but it should not override the separate Cochrane dementia caution or the healthy-adult null/insufficient evidence gate.
