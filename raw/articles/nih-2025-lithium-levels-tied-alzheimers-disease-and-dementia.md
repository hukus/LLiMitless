# Lithium levels tied to Alzheimer’s disease and dementia

- NIH Research Matters, published 2025-09-09
- Official NIH summary of the Nature paper, not new primary data

## Summary

The NIH summary reports that endogenous lithium was lower in prefrontal cortex from people with MCI and AD, that lithium was concentrated in amyloid plaques, and that lowering dietary lithium worsened AD pathology in mouse models. It also says low-dose lithium orotate outperformed lithium carbonate in the mouse work, but explicitly cautions that the effective and safe dose range in people is unknown and that a human trial is still needed before recommending lithium orotate.

## Key Points

- Human tissue signal: lower naturally occurring lithium in prefrontal cortex from MCI and AD samples.
- Mechanistic signal: lithium appears sequestered in amyloid plaques.
- Mouse signal: reduced dietary lithium worsened plaque, tau, and memory phenotypes.
- Replacement signal: low-dose lithium orotate helped the mouse models more than lithium carbonate.
- Human translation caveat: NIH says dose and safety in people still need to be established.
