# Cognitive Effects of Esketamine in Treatment-Resistant Depression: A Systematic Review

- PMID: 41873584
- DOI: 10.1097/JCP.0000000000002165
- Published online: 2026-03-24 in Journal of Clinical Psychopharmacology.
- Scope: systematic review of intranasal or intravenous esketamine in adults aged 18-80 with treatment-resistant depression, with cognitive assessments at least 1 month after treatment initiation.

## Captured takeaways

- Six studies met inclusion criteria.
- Cognitive domains were grouped as attention/processing speed, memory, and executive functions.
- The review found no cognitive deterioration in assessed domains across the included studies.
- Attention and processing speed showed the most frequent and robust improvements, while memory was stable in short-term designs and improved in longer follow-up studies.
- Executive-function gains were mainly seen in participants with baseline impairment and in longer-term assessments.
- The authors still called the evidence limited and requested harmonized cognitive batteries, larger samples, and longer follow-up.
- Interpretation for the wiki: esketamine deserves a state-bound cognitive-safety / possible recovery entry in treatment-resistant depression, but the signal should not be promoted as healthy-adult enhancement or same-day cognitive improvement.
