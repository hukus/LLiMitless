# ODS niacin / vitamin B3 fact sheet

Office of Dietary Supplements health-professional fact sheet for niacin / vitamin B3.

Key capture:

- Niacin is vitamin B3. The main forms discussed for supplements and fortified foods are nicotinic acid and nicotinamide / niacinamide.
- Niacin is a precursor for NAD and NADP, which are involved in hundreds of enzymatic reactions, including energy metabolism and redox biology.
- Dietary tryptophan can be converted into niacin equivalents, so B3 status intersects with protein intake and the tryptophan / kynurenine branch.
- Severe niacin deficiency causes pellagra, classically involving dermatitis, diarrhea, and dementia; untreated pellagra can be fatal. For this wiki, that makes deficiency correction medically real, but it does not make high-dose B3 a nootropic in replete adults.
- Adult RDAs are expressed as niacin equivalents. The adult tolerable upper intake level for niacin from supplements or fortified foods is 35 mg/day, primarily because of flushing.
- High supplemental or pharmacologic doses can cause clinically important adverse effects. Nicotinic acid is especially associated with flushing; higher-dose use can also involve liver toxicity, gastrointestinal effects, glucose/insulin effects, and other safety issues.
