# Population pharmacokinetic modelling of NS2330 (tesofensine) and its major metabolite in patients with Alzheimer's disease

- Source: British Journal of Clinical Pharmacology / PMC article
- Population: mild-to-moderate Alzheimer's disease proof-of-concept study

## Captured takeaways

- The article modeled tesofensine / NS2330 and metabolite M1 pharmacokinetics from a 14-week proof-of-concept study in Alzheimer's disease.
- The underlying trial was double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, and conducted at 63 U.S. and 10 Canadian sites.
- Patients with mild-to-moderate dementia of Alzheimer's type received placebo or tesofensine 0.25, 0.5, or 1 mg once daily for 14 weeks.
- The article states that a phase IIa mild-AD trial had shown significant cognitive-function improvement, but the paper itself is primarily a pharmacokinetic model rather than a full efficacy report.
- Parent and metabolite profiles were modeled as long half-life exposures: roughly 234 h for NS2330 and 374 h for M1 in the article's results.
- Covariate analysis identified creatinine clearance and sex as potentially important for exposure, with simulated higher exposure in females with lower creatinine clearance.
- Interpretation for the wiki: this source supports the old AD development history and long-half-life accumulation issue, but it should not be treated as modern healthy-adult cognition proof.
