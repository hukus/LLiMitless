# Melatonin for sleep and cognitive outcomes in older adults with cognitive impairment: a meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials

- PMID: 41240058
- DOI: 10.1093/ageing/afaf333
- Published: 2025-10-30
- Source type: systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
- Population: adults age 65+ with mild cognitive impairment or dementia
- Ingested: 2026-05-03

## Summary

Meta-analysis of 10 RCTs with 516 older adults with cognitive impairment. Melatonin increased total sleep time by about 12.4 minutes and improved MMSE scores by about 1.8 points versus placebo. Neuropsychiatric symptoms also improved modestly. Sleep efficiency, circadian markers, depression, and activities of daily living were small or nonsignificant.

## Notes

- Useful current anchor for the melatonin cognition page because it measures both sleep and cognition, rather than treating melatonin as a generic nootropic.
- The authors frame effects as modest and not definitive, with a need for larger RCTs with harmonized outcomes and pre-specified primary endpoints.
- Interpretation should stay phenotype-bound: older adults with cognitive impairment, not healthy acute enhancement.
