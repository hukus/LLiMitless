Label-level practical points:

- Tadalafil is labeled for erectile dysfunction and/or benign prostatic hyperplasia under Cialis/generic tadalafil tablet labels, with tadalafil also appearing in pulmonary arterial hypertension labeling under separate products/dosing contexts.
- ED/BPH labels use 5 mg daily for BPH or ED/BPH, 2.5-5 mg daily for once-daily ED use, and 10-20 mg as-needed ED dosing; tadalafil should not be treated as a cognition protocol.
- Contraindication / major interaction gate: organic nitrates. Combining tadalafil with nitrates can produce dangerous hypotension.
- Additional major interaction gate in current tadalafil labeling: soluble guanylate cyclase stimulators such as riociguat, because the combination can lower blood pressure.
- Alpha-blockers, antihypertensives, and substantial alcohol use can add hypotension or dizziness risk.
- CYP3A4 inhibitors can raise tadalafil exposure; renal and hepatic impairment change dosing and risk boundaries.
- Key warnings include cardiovascular status before sexual activity, hypotension, priapism, sudden vision loss, sudden hearing loss, and use in people with relevant cardiac or vascular risk.
- Common adverse reactions in label context include headache, dyspepsia, back pain, myalgia, nasal congestion, flushing, and limb pain.
