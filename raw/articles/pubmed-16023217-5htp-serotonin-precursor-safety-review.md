# Serotonin a la carte: supplementation with the serotonin precursor 5-hydroxytryptophan

- PMID: 16023217
- DOI: 10.1016/j.pharmthera.2005.06.004
- Journal: Pharmacology & Therapeutics (2006)
- Source type: narrative pharmacology review
- Checked: 2026-05-03

## Summary

This review frames 5-HTP as the immediate serotonin precursor downstream of tryptophan. It distinguishes endogenous 5-HTP availability from exogenous supplementation and notes that peripheral conversion to serotonin is governed by aromatic amino acid decarboxylase, with peripheral decarboxylase inhibitors such as carbidopa changing exposure.

The review focused mainly on depression rather than cognition. Its relevance to the cognition wiki is mechanistic and safety-oriented: serotonergic mood, sleep, appetite, and arousal effects can masquerade as cognitive benefit, and direct cognitive-enhancement evidence is not established by the depression literature.

## Safety Notes

- Safety discussion explicitly included eosinophilia-myalgia syndrome and serotonin syndrome.
- The review supports treating 5-HTP as interaction-sensitive rather than as a generic amino-acid nutrient.
- It should be routed separately from L-tryptophan, melatonin, SSRIs/SNRIs/MAOIs, St. John's wort, triptans, and kynurenine-pathway challenge studies.
