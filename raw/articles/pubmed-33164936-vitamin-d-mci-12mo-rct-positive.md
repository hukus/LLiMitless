# Vitamin D supplementation improves cognitive function through reducing oxidative stress regulated by telomere length in older adults with MCI

- PMID: 33164936
- DOI: 10.3233/JAD-200926
- Published: 2020
- Source type: double-blind randomized placebo-controlled trial
- Population: 183 Chinese adults age 65+ with mild cognitive impairment
- Intervention: vitamin D 800 IU/day for 12 months
- Ingested: 2026-05-03

## Summary

The trial randomized 183 older adults with MCI to vitamin D 800 IU/day or placebo for 12 months. The vitamin D group improved more than placebo on full-scale IQ and several Wechsler subtests including information, digit span, vocabulary, block design, and picture arrangement. The vitamin D group also showed biomarker changes interpreted as lower oxidative stress and longer leukocyte telomere length.

## Notes

- Useful positive RCT anchor for the vitamin D3 entity.
- Interpretation should stay phenotype-specific: older adults with MCI, long duration, and baseline/status context.
- Cognitive battery and biomarker mediation should not be generalized to healthy acute enhancement.
