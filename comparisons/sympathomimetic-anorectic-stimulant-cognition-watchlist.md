# Sympathomimetic Anorectic Stimulant Cognition Watchlist

This page covers classic sympathomimetic anorectic stimulants where focus, energy, food-noise, binge-eating, and ADHD-like anecdotes can be confused with direct cognitive enhancement. The main entity is [[phentermine]]. Older sibling labels such as [[diethylpropion]], [[phendimetrazine]], and [[benzphetamine]] are handled as risk-first comparators unless a direct cognition or strong anecdote signal appears.

This branch is adjacent to [[monoamine-reuptake-anorectic-stimulants-cognition-watchlist]], but it should not be collapsed into it. [[tesofensine]] is a long-half-life triple reuptake inhibitor with obesity and DAT-occupancy data; phentermine is an older sympathomimetic amine anorectic with Schedule IV controlled-substance status and visible ADHD-like anecdotes.

## Evidence Split

[[phentermine]] has the only meaningful direct cognition-adjacent evidence in this branch. A 2003 sleep-deprivation study found that tested drugs including phentermine improved some cognitive/motor performance measures after sleep deprivation, while the NCBI workshop abstract reports caffeine, amphetamine, and phentermine improved target measures especially through response time but phentermine impaired recovery sleep depth.^[raw/articles/pubmed-12887140-phentermine-sleep-deprivation-performance.md]

An older acute healthy-volunteer study found phentermine and prolintane most active on some mental tasks, while ephedrine had the strongest cardiovascular effects. That is a useful signal, but too old, acute, and task-specific to support broad cognition claims.^[raw/articles/pubmed-6471970-phentermine-ephedrine-fenfluramine-prolintane-mental-effects.md]

The prescription-label layer is stronger than the cognition layer. Phentermine is indicated for short-term weight reduction and carries warnings for primary pulmonary hypertension, valvular heart disease, hazardous-task impairment, tolerance, abuse/dependence, alcohol, MAOIs, and hypertension caution.^[raw/articles/dailymed-2024-phentermine-label.md]

## Safety And Interpretation Gates

- Do not treat appetite suppression, binge-eating control, or less food noise as cognition proof.
- Do not merge phentermine with phentermine/topiramate. Qsymia's label explicitly includes cognitive impairment, attention/memory/language problems, and word-finding events.^[raw/articles/dailymed-2025-qsymia-cognitive-impairment-label.md]
- Do not use fen-phen history loosely. [[fenfluramine]]'s current label still carries VHD/PAH and REMS monitoring, and old fenfluramine-phentermine reports are a safety precedent, not a clean phentermine-alone cognition argument.^[raw/articles/pubmed-9271479-fenfluramine-phentermine-valvular-disease.md]^[raw/articles/dailymed-2026-fenfluramine-fintepla-rems.md]
- For [[diethylpropion]], [[phendimetrazine]], and [[benzphetamine]], keep the current route risk-first until direct cognition, modern clinical, or strong specific anecdote evidence is captured.^[raw/articles/dailymed-2026-sympathomimetic-anorectic-labels.md]
- Route sympathomimetic anorectic anecdotes through [[safety-interaction-matrix-for-multi-modal-stacks]] so appetite suppression, caloric deficit, sleep, caffeine, HR/BP, tolerance, controlled-substance context, and combo-product confusion stay separate from cognition claims.

## Current Route

This branch should be used when a user asks about prescription diet-pill stimulants, phentermine focus, ADHD-like response to anorectics, or "similar to tesofensine" stimulant/anorectic chemicals. It sits below [[modafinil]], [[methylphenidate]], [[solriamfetol]], and [[caffeine]] as a cognition comparator because the practical evidence is mostly sleep-loss rescue, old acute tasks, and confounded anecdotes.
