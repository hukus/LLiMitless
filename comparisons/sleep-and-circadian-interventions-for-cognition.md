# Sleep and Circadian Interventions for Performance and Cognition

This comparison consolidates the former cognition-framed and performance-framed sleep/circadian pages. The common pattern is that these interventions usually help by removing cognitive drag from insomnia, circadian misalignment, or expected sleep loss rather than by acting as direct nootropics. The clearest effects are on insomnia severity, daytime alertness, reaction time, attention, and broader neurobehavioral performance; more durable memory or executive gains remain modest, phenotype-dependent, or unproven.^[raw/articles/pubmed-36543054-cbt-i-objective-short-sleep-duration.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40982264-cbt-i-chronic-disease-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-37002704-light-therapy-insomnia-disorder.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41892386-sleep-banking-cognitive-motor-impairments-sleep-restriction.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40984126-sleep-extension-cognitive-performance-military-aged-adults.md]

Circadian disruption is the background reason this family matters. Shift-work, astronaut, and post-stroke literatures all point in the same direction: when timing, light exposure, or sleep opportunity drift out of sync, cognition-adjacent performance usually worsens first, and interventions are most defensible when they restore that baseline rather than promise a separate enhancement layer.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41139761-shift-work-cognitive-motor-performance-nurses.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41277818-post-stroke-circadian-rhythm-disruption-prognosis.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41090408-circadian-disruption-astronauts-neurocognitive-consequences.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40890717-sleep-improvement-programs-shift-work-nurses.md]

| Intervention or design lever | Best use-case | Strongest outcome layer | Current wiki read |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[cbt-i-and-cognition]] | Chronic insomnia, hyperarousal, fragmented sleep | Sleep continuity and daytime function | Best-supported upstream intervention; cognition benefit is real but mostly indirect through sleep restoration |
| [[bright-light-therapy-and-cognition]] | Circadian delay, daytime alertness, seasonal or schedule-linked drift | Phase shifting, alertness, stress/autonomic setting | Most useful when timing is the problem; cognition effects are secondary to circadian stabilization |
| [[sleep-banking-and-sleep-extension]] | Predictable sleep loss, travel, training, shift-work blocks | Acute vigilance and reaction-time preservation | Strongest as a short-horizon performance buffer, not as retained cognitive enhancement |
| [[circadian-disruption-and-cognition]] | Shift work, irregular schedules, recovery from rhythm mismatch | Mechanistic and observational performance protection | Best treated as the conceptual and practical alignment bucket for schedule regularization |
| [[chronotype-specific-response-across-sleep-stimulation-interventions]] | Timing-heavy protocols where response may depend on synchrony | Protocol moderation rather than standalone efficacy | Useful design variable, but not a guarantee of larger effect sizes or a separate intervention class |

The practical split inside this family is between sleep restoration, circadian alignment, and acute performance buffering. CBT-I is the first-line insomnia branch. Bright light and schedule regularization matter most when the sleep-wake pattern is mistimed. Sleep banking and sleep extension help when sleep loss is predictable, but the evidence still reads as short-horizon protection rather than durable cognitive prevention.^[raw/articles/pubmed-33091586-cbt-i-enhance-cognitive-function-abeta-deposition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-42015088-bright-light-therapy-cognition-sleep-stress.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41892386-sleep-banking-cognitive-motor-impairments-sleep-restriction.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40984126-sleep-extension-cognitive-performance-military-aged-adults.md]

There is also a narrower circadian-autonomic hypothesis worth tracking. A blue-enriched bright-light HRV study and a circadian-stage taVNS HRV study make a morning-aligned light-plus-taVNS stack biologically plausible, but that remains an inference from separate physiology papers rather than a direct combination trial. For that branch, see [[bright-light-tavns-circadian-autonomic-scan]].^[raw/articles/pubmed-35209793-bright-light-hrv.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-36327249-taVNS-circadian-hrv.md]

Chronotype is best treated as a design variable, not a standalone therapy. Sleep-extension and sleep-loss studies show meaningful response heterogeneity, but much of that signal still looks like synchrony, sleep debt, or baseline misalignment rather than a simple morning-type versus evening-type rule. For the deeper moderator map, see [[chronotype-specific-response-across-sleep-stimulation-interventions]] and [[phase-locked-triad-for-cognition-interventions]].^[raw/articles/pubmed-40458955-chronotype-sleep-extension-manipulation-adolescents.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-37539684-chronotype-affective-response-sleep-restriction-deprivation.md]

The newer closed-loop sleep literature sits just downstream of this bucket. CLAS and targeted-memory-reactivation studies show that phase-locking inside the sleep window can matter: multi-night CLAS has a lab language/discovery-learning signal, up-phase TMR has a motor-retention signal, and spindle-phase work shows that the cue window is part of the dose. The translational caution is just as important: home/ecological CLAS can improve slow oscillations while leaving memory and vigilance flat. For that evidence, see [[closed-loop-and-adaptive-neuromodulation-for-cognitive-performance]] and [[closed-loop-acoustic-stimulation-and-targeted-memory-reactivation]].^[raw/articles/pubmed-41110657-auditory-stimulation-sleep-varies-thalamocortical-spindle-phase.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40626105-targeting-sleep-spindles-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39595901-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation-language-discovery-learning.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40102385-phase-specific-enhancement-motor-memory-consolidation-closed-loop-tmr.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41229102-closed-loop-auditory-stimulation-ecological-memory-null.md]

If poor sleep or rhythm mismatch is the main bottleneck, this is one of the most mature intervention families in the wiki. If the goal is durable enhancement in already well-slept adults, the evidence falls off quickly. That is why this page sits alongside [[healthy-adult-cognition-signals]], [[adjunctive-interventions-for-cognition]], and [[anecdotal-evidence-in-cognitive-intervention-research]], and why the single-table cross-family view in [[cognition-intervention-master-table]] is still useful.
