# Adjunctive Interventions for Cognition

This comparison page groups several interventions that are not classic stimulants or nootropics but still have credible cognition-relevant research: [[intranasal-insulin-and-cognition]], [[incretin-therapies-brain-health]], [[hyperbaric-oxygen-neurocognitive-recovery]], [[transcutaneous-auricular-vagus-nerve-stimulation]], [[creatine-cognitive-support]], [[bright-light-therapy-and-cognition]], [[cerebrovascular-reserve-interventions-2026-scan]], and the traditional non-drug treatment branch in [[traditional-mind-body-cognition-treatment-watchlist]].

The raw-note cluster behind the table is especially useful to open directly: `raw/articles/pubmed-41862354-glp1-neuropsychiatric-outcomes.md`, `raw/articles/pubmed-41979527-hyperbaric-oxygen-therapy-traumatic-brain-injury.md`, `raw/articles/pubmed-41717223-taVNS-cognitive-deficits-systematic-review.md`, `raw/articles/pubmed-39815765-taVNS-memory-pupil-dilation.md`, and `raw/articles/pubmed-39070254-creatine-cognition-adults-meta-analysis.md`.

Relevant raw PubMed notes for the metabolic-brain branch: `raw/articles/pubmed-40195962-intranasal-insulin-ad-systematic-review-meta-analysis.md` and `raw/articles/pubmed-36530613-hypoglycemic-drugs-ad-mci-network-meta-analysis.md`.

| Intervention | Why it matters for cognition | Evidence shape | Main uncertainty |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incretin therapies (GLP-1/GIP) | Now has direct human cognition data in T2DM and MDD, but AD/MCI trials are mixed or negative | Mixed RCT/meta-analysis plus phase 3 readouts; GIP remains mostly preclinical | Whether benefit is phenotype-specific or just metabolically mediated |
| HBOT | May aid recovery after TBI/post-concussion states | Positive pooled meta-analysis, but the older systematic review was cautious, the cleaner sham-controlled studies and trial-only subgroup are much less convincing, and the newest comprehensive review still describes mixed outcomes | Retraction-sensitive evidence base and heterogeneous protocols |
| taVNS | Non-invasive autonomic neuromodulation with memory/inflammation links | Small reviews, mixed healthy-volunteer RCTs, early disease-context work | Which phenotypes respond and whether the signal is cognitive or mostly autonomic |
| Creatine | Energy buffering, sleep-loss rescue, aging, menopause phenotype, and exercise synergy | Meta-analysis plus challenge-stress RCTs; 2026 menopause RCT plus 2025 older-adult review, 2025 safety review, and sleep-deprivation mechanistic papers now show phenotype- and state-dependence rather than a universal effect | Effect size likely depends on baseline status, phenotype, acute demand, and formulation |
| Bright light therapy | Circadian alignment, sleep, daytime alertness, stress | Pilot and clinical literature | Direct cognition effects vs indirect sleep/mood effects |
| Cerebrovascular reserve | Exercise, [[dietary-nitrate-beetroot]], and breath protocols can target reserve, nitric-oxide availability, autonomic tone, or acute arousal | Acute exercise meta-review, exercise brain-volume meta-analysis, beetroot cognition/vascular trials, slow-paced-breathing protocol, plus a 24-month exercise/vascular-risk null RCT | Whether physiology, reserve, arousal, or practice effects drive cognition; long-term prevention can be null |
| Traditional mind-body treatments | Acupuncture, electroacupuncture, Tai Chi, Baduanjin, and breath practices may act through pain, sleep, vascular reserve, movement, autonomic state, or adherence | MCI/VaMCI meta-analyses, sham-acupuncture pilot, Tai Chi follow-up, and Baduanjin reviews | Active comparator and sham quality, protocol heterogeneity, and recovery-vs-enhancement transfer |

The shared pattern is that these interventions may help cognition indirectly, through metabolism, recovery, sleep, movement, vascular reserve, pain reduction, or autonomic regulation, rather than acting as simple "brain boosters." Incretins are now the exception that proves the rule: they have moved from speculative brain-health chemistry into direct cognition trials, but the direct signal is still mixed and phenotype-dependent. Intranasal insulin is the clearest split case on this page: the latest MCI/AD meta-analysis is null, while perioperative RCTs and the 2026 meta-analysis support a delirium/cognitive-protection story in surgical patients. Creatine is in the same bucket: a real effect in some contexts, but most convincing when demand is high, reserve is low, or the cohort is older. The 2026 menopause RCT adds a formulation-sensitive note to that story, because the medium-dose creatine hydrochloride arm outperformed placebo on reaction time and frontal brain creatine. The newer sleep-deprivation papers make the creatine story even more state-dependent: one paper shows the same high-dose creatine partly balances hemispheric energy asymmetry, while another shows sleep deprivation itself raises serum creatine, and the newer older-adult review keeps the age-specific signal alive without pretending it is settled causality.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40520504-creatine-sleep-deprivation-hemispheric-asymmetry.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40988912-sleep-deprivation-circulating-creatine-pilot.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40971619-creatine-cognition-older-adults-systematic-review.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40854087-creatine-menopause-cognition-rct.md] The cerebrovascular-reserve branch now has the same state-dependent shape: acute exercise is the clearest broad human signal, [[dietary-nitrate-beetroot]] is a product-specific nitric-oxide/reserve candidate, and slow-paced breathing remains physiology-first while the dedicated review catches up. The 2026 exercise / vascular-risk RCT is the guardrail: plausible reserve logic can still miss 24-month cognition endpoints.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39883421-acute-exercise-meta-review.md]^[raw/articles/springer-2026-exercise-brain-volume-cognition-dose-response-meta.md]^[raw/articles/jama-2026-exercise-intensive-vascular-risk-cognition-null.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-37875637-beetroot-chewable-cognitive-performance.md]^[raw/articles/springer-2026-slow-paced-breathing-cognition-healthy-protocol.md] Traditional mind-body treatments now sit in that same adjunctive frame: [[acupuncture-and-cognition]] and the Tai Chi/Baduanjin branch are interesting mostly when MCI, vascular injury, metabolic disease, pain/sleep, frailty, or fatigue are part of the phenotype, and the sham/active-comparator burden is high.^[raw/articles/bmc-2025-acupuncture-mci-sham-pilot.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41196453-tai-chi-two-year-follow-up-t2d-mci.md] HBOT is the recovery-side outlier here: the literature is still interesting, but the strict-bias read is weaker than the headline meta-analysis suggests because the positive pooled estimate outruns the cleaner sham-controlled and trial-only reads, the older systematic review was cautious, the 2026 review still called the literature mixed, and the retraction of a key severe-TBI trial matters. That makes these interventions especially important to track alongside [[neuroinflammation-and-cognition]], [[transcranial-photobiomodulation]], [[focused-ultrasound-neuromodulation]], and [[peptide-neuroprotection]].
