# Pemoline Adjacent Gray Market Stimulant Watchlist

This watchlist catches [[pemoline]], [[cyclazodone]], [[n-methyl-cyclazodone]], and closely adjacent nootropic / research-chemical stimulant claims. The purpose is to keep functional-stimulant anecdotes visible without letting them inherit clean-eugeroic or prescription-ADHD evidence.

## Interpretation Rules

1. Do not use [[pemoline]]'s former ADHD indication to validate cyclazodone/NMC as cognitive enhancers.
2. Do not use forum reports of "clean stimulation" to erase liver, ECG, CK, psychiatric, or product-identity risks.
3. Separate [[cyclazodone]] from [[n-methyl-cyclazodone]] when writing evidence summaries; the direct toxicology case is NMC exposure with cyclazodone metabolite confirmation.
4. Route athlete or regulated-performance use through [[regulatory-and-sports-visibility-map]] because WADA S0/S6 logic and pemoline S6.B visibility make this branch status-sensitive.^[raw/articles/wada-2026-stimulant-list-afinils-pemoline-tesofensine.md]
5. Benchmark any focus claim against [[modafinil]], [[methylphenidate]], [[d-amphetamine]], [[solriamfetol]], and [[bupropion]] before treating it as notable.
6. Route cyclazodone/NMC anecdotes through [[safety-interaction-matrix-for-multi-modal-stacks]] so liver markers, ECG/QTc/QRS, CK/rhabdomyolysis, sleep timing, stimulant tolerance, psychiatric co-meds, and product identity stay separate from cognition claims.

## Verdict

This branch is worth tracking because the community signal is visible and because direct toxicity/status evidence exists. It should remain below prescription comparators and below low-risk supplements. The wiki route is risk-first: [[pemoline]] anchors why the class needs liver/status caution, [[cyclazodone]] anchors the functional-stimulant anecdote lane, and [[n-methyl-cyclazodone]] anchors the strongest modern toxicology concern.
