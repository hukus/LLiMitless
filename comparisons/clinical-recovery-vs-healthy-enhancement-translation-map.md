# Clinical-Recovery vs Healthy-Enhancement Translation Map

This page separates evidence that comes from disease, injury, or sleep-pressure contexts from evidence that plausibly generalizes to healthy, well-slept adults. The default rule is simple: a positive signal in stroke, TBI, MCI, insomnia, depression, or delirium is a recovery or correction signal until a healthy-adult cohort reproduces it.

Since 2024, the split has become more explicit rather than less. Cerebrolysin and Cortexin remain recovery-first peptide branches, but Cerebrolysin now has a more nuanced human split: AD and vascular-dementia signals belong to impaired older cohorts, the 2025 MoCA-linked stroke comparative-effectiveness signal still sits on the recovery side, the 2023 acute-stroke Cochrane update is a hard-outcome/safety counterweight, and the only healthy-human signal surfaced here is an old acute oral-dose study in healthy elderly people. HBOT is now more visibly comparator-sensitive and retraction-aware. rTMS is the strongest clinical stimulation family, but healthy-adult enhancement is still small and heterogeneous. tPBM now has an explicit recovery-state anchor in insomnia and mTBI, with the healthy-older-adult signal sitting next to it rather than replacing it. tDCS/tACS show the clearest task- and phenotype-sensitivity, with sleep-deprivation and post-stroke rehab studies belonging on the recovery side. The glymphatic branch is now a serious mechanistic and recovery-state story, but not a healthy-enhancement story. MSC/exosome/secretome interventions now belong here too: the human signal is an AD treatment/regenerative-medicine hypothesis with FDA safety warnings, not a healthy-adult cognition lane.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40851188-cerebrolysin-c-regs2-a-moderate-acute-ischemic-stroke.md]^[raw/articles/cochrane-2023-cerebrolysin-acute-ischaemic-stroke.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-10961443-oral-cerebrolysin-healthy-elderly-alpha-memory.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-6844361-cerebrolysin-vascular-dementia-cochrane-2019.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40325343-cerebrolysin-post-thrombectomy-followup.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41010922-cerebrolysin-acute-ischemic-stroke-observational.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-11552768-cerebrolysin-alz-28-week-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39435777-cerebrolysin-mci-dementia-prevention.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-38217421-hbot-severe-tbi-retraction-notice.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40676541-rtms-cognitive-impairment-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39866659-rtms-neuroenhancement-healthy-adults.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41125953-tpbm-insomnia-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40485299-tpbm-mtbi-cognitive-postconcussion-ptsd.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40822571-tpbm-systematic-review-sleep-wakefulness-cognition.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40633904-tdcs-variability-age-2025.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-33524838-tdcs-sleep-deprivation-recovery.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41593094-glymphatic-system-clears-amyloid-beta-and-tau-from-brain-to-plasma-in-humans.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-37859748-msc-exosome-ad-trial.md]^[raw/articles/fda-consumer-alert-regenerative-medicine-stem-cells-exosomes.md]

## Effect Size Snapshot

- Cerebrolysin: the 2001 AD RCT reported a 3.2-point ADAS-cog advantage and higher CGI responder rate; the 2011 dose-finding AD trial reported significant global-function and cognition improvements at 24 weeks; the 2019 vascular-dementia Cochrane update reported SMD 0.36 for cognition with very-low-quality evidence; the 2025 C-REGS2-A stroke cohort added a MoCA-linked signal; the 2024 aMCI study remains open/comparative rather than blinded; and the old healthy-elderly oral study remains low-confidence because it is acute, old, and not modern replicated enhancement evidence.^[raw/articles/pubmed-11552768-cerebrolysin-alz-28-week-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-20500802-cerebrolysin-dose-finding-ad-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-6844361-cerebrolysin-vascular-dementia-cochrane-2019.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40851188-cerebrolysin-c-regs2-a-moderate-acute-ischemic-stroke.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39435777-cerebrolysin-mci-dementia-prevention.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-10961443-oral-cerebrolysin-healthy-elderly-alpha-memory.md]
- HBOT: the 2025 meta-analysis reported large pooled gains in memory, attention, global cognition, executive function, information processing speed, and motor skills, but the sham-controlled studies and the retraction-aware read pull the confidence back down.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41180753-hbot-neurocognitive-deficits-traumatic-brain-injury.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-26403017-hbot-tbi-sham-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-24052094-hbot-persistent-postconcussion-symptoms.md]
- rTMS: the AD/PD meta-analysis reported MoCA MD 2.13 and MMSE MD 1.16; the stroke meta-analysis reported global cognition SMD 0.54 and iTBS SMD 0.94; the healthy-adult review remains heterogeneous enough that the exact healthy-adult effect should be treated as provisional.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40676541-rtms-cognitive-impairment-neurodegenerative-diseases.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40769285-rtms-stroke-cognitive-impairment.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39866659-rtms-neuroenhancement-healthy-adults.md]
- tPBM: the 2025 insomnia RCT showed sleep-quality and delta-power improvement, the 2024 MCI trial reported a larger MoCA change with active tPBM than placebo, the healthy-older-adult crossover improved 3-back accuracy and reaction time, and the mTBI crossover improved working memory, verbal learning, and symptom scores; the persistent-concussion RCT stayed null after adjustment.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41125953-tpbm-insomnia-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39423445-transcranial-photobiomodulation-cognition-bdnf.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40482941-tpbm-healthy-older-adults-working-memory-fnirs.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40485299-tpbm-mtbi-cognitive-postconcussion-ptsd.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-38109202-tpbm-concussion-null-rct.md]
- tDCS / tACS: the 2026 tACS meta in older cognitive impairment found memory and global-cognition benefit but not attention, the theta-tACS healthy-adult meta found a moderate WM signal, the sleep-deprivation tDCS recovery study and post-stroke rehab meta keep the recovery lane alive, and the 2025 tDCS age-variability meta found 21% higher response variability in healthy older adults than young adults.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41507453-tacs-cognitive-impairment-meta.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40933163-theta-tacs-working-memory-healthy-adults.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-33524838-tdcs-sleep-deprivation-recovery.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40567421-tdcs-combined-cognitive-rehabilitation-post-stroke.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40633904-tdcs-variability-age-2025.md]
- Glymphatic / TMS: the 2026 tracer study showed higher morning plasma A beta and tau after normal sleep than sleep deprivation, the older-adult TBS study reported glymphatic-memory correlations, and the LF-rTMS insomnia study linked glymphatic proxy change to cognition. These are strong mechanistic signals, but not healthy-enhancement proof.^[raw/articles/pubmed-41593094-glymphatic-system-clears-amyloid-beta-and-tau-from-brain-to-plasma-in-humans.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40746364-glymphatic-system-as-therapeutic-target-tms-induced-modulation-older-adults.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40121525-lf-rtms-glymphatic-cognition.md]
- MSC / exosome / secretome: the published exosome AD trial is small and open-label; it reported ADAS-cog down 2.33 points and MoCA up 2.38 points in the medium-dose arm at week 12, with no significant amyloid or tau separation. That is a fragile AD treatment signal, not healthy enhancement.^[raw/articles/pubmed-37859748-msc-exosome-ad-trial.md]

## Safety / Adverse Events

- Cerebrolysin is not a benign supplement; the 2024 anaphylaxis case report and the 2023 Cochrane non-fatal serious-adverse-event signal should stay attached to the branch, and the new stroke cohorts should not be read as proof of broad safety in unsupervised use.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39055722-cerebrolysin-anaphylaxis-case-report.md]^[raw/articles/cochrane-2023-cerebrolysin-acute-ischaemic-stroke.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-40851188-cerebrolysin-c-regs2-a-moderate-acute-ischemic-stroke.md]
- Cortexin remains an injectable biologic with limited modern human safety granularity outside older recovery cohorts.
- HBOT has a real adverse-effect burden, with ear discomfort the most common signal and higher risk at more than 10 sessions or pressures above 2.0 ATA.^[raw/articles/pubmed-37275378-hbot-adverse-effects-meta.md]
- rTMS, tPBM, tDCS, and tACS are generally tolerable in the published trials here, but their practical risk profile is dominated by protocol quality, blinding, and operator dependence rather than a single headline toxicity.
- The glymphatic branch spans low-risk light therapy, standard stimulation, and supervised ultrasound/drug-combination protocols; the latter should not be treated as casual enhancement tools.
- MSC/exosome/secretome branches carry a regulatory safety burden: FDA says there are no FDA-approved exosome products and warns against unapproved disease claims, including neurological claims.^[raw/articles/fda-consumer-alert-regenerative-medicine-stem-cells-exosomes.md]^[raw/articles/fda-public-safety-alert-unapproved-stem-cell-exosome-products.md]

## Responder Phenotypes

- Cerebrolysin: mild-to-moderate AD, vascular dementia, amnestic MCI, moderate ischemic stroke with baseline cognitive impairment, and stroke-recovery settings; healthy elderly remains a weak acute signal rather than a general enhancement phenotype.
- Cortexin: chronic cerebral ischemia / brain ischemia and other older vascular-recovery phenotypes.
- HBOT: chronic mTBI and persistent post-concussion symptoms, but no validated responder model.
- rTMS: AD, MCI, stroke, and protocol-defined disease cohorts; healthy adults show weaker and more variable effects.
- tPBM: insomnia, older adults with MCI, healthy older adults, and mTBI; the branch looks more state- and age-sensitive than age-agnostic.
- tDCS / tACS: baseline cognition, target site, sleep state, frequency band, and age-driven variability.
- Glymphatic / TMS: insomnia, sleep deprivation, older adults with MCI, APOE epsilon4 carriers, and subthreshold depression.
- MSC / exosome / secretome: mild-to-moderate AD, moderate dementia, and MCI/early-AD adjunct settings; no healthy-adult responder model.

## Generalization Rules

1. Disease or recovery cohorts do not automatically translate to healthy enhancement.
2. Biomarker movement is not enough on its own; behavioral endpoints still have to move.
3. Healthy older adults are not the same population as healthy young adults.
4. Add-on or state-restoration wins should stay labeled as add-on or state-restoration wins.
5. If a branch is only positive in recovery phenotypes, keep it out of the healthy-adult ranking unless a healthy cohort reproduces the signal.
