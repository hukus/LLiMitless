# Traditional Mind-Body Cognition Treatment Watchlist

This watchlist covers traditional nonpharmacologic treatments with cognition or brain-fog relevance. It is intentionally separate from molecule pages because protocols, practitioner skill, adherence, active comparators, and sham feasibility matter more than dose chemistry.

## Verdict

The mind-body treatment branch is strongest in impairment or recovery phenotypes, not in healthy well-slept adults. [[acupuncture-and-cognition]] has positive MCI and vascular-MCI meta-analytic signals but a sham-controlled 2025 pilot primary-endpoint miss. Tai Chi and Baduanjin have useful older-adult/MCI evidence, including durable Tai Chi follow-up in T2D plus MCI and current Baduanjin meta-analyses, but both are partly exercise, attention, balance, social-contact, adherence, and expectation interventions rather than clean nootropics. Breath/yoga protocols remain more convincing as physiology/state tools than as healthy-adult cognitive enhancers.^[raw/articles/frontiers-2023-acupuncture-mci-meta-analysis.md]^[raw/articles/bmc-2025-acupuncture-mci-sham-pilot.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41196453-tai-chi-two-year-follow-up-t2d-mci.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-39207292-baduanjin-mci-cognition-meta.md]^[raw/articles/springer-2026-baduanjin-mci-cognition-meta.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-32344403-unilateral-left-nostril-breathing-cognitive-function.md]

## Treatment Rows

| Treatment | Current evidence read | Best-fit phenotype | Main caveat |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[acupuncture-and-cognition]] | Low; MCI/VaMCI meta-analyses positive, sham pilot primary endpoint null | MCI, vascular MCI, pain/sleep/fatigue-linked brain fog | Sham, practitioner, point/stimulation, frequency, and safety variability |
| Electroacupuncture | Low but visible adjunctive vascular-MCI signal | Vascular MCI / post-stroke cognitive rehabilitation | Most evidence is adjunctive and protocol-heterogeneous |
| Tai Chi | Low-moderate for older impaired phenotypes; 2-year T2D+MCI follow-up showed MoCA preservation | Older adults with metabolic disease plus MCI | Exercise, social contact, balance, and adherence confounds |
| Baduanjin | Low; meta-analyses favor MoCA/global cognition, but heterogeneity and study quality limit certainty | Older adults with MCI or cognitive frailty | Protocol intensity and comparator quality vary; durability uncertain |
| Pranayama / breath protocols | Very low for healthy cognition; disease/autonomic contexts may be more plausible | Autonomic imbalance, stress regulation, Parkinson's adjunct, reserve physiology | Healthy yoga-naive left-nostril breathing did not beat breath awareness |

## Safety / Protocol Notes

Acupuncture requires the clearest safety gate because it involves needles and sometimes electrical stimulation. NCCIH notes that FDA-regulated acupuncture needles should be sterile and labeled single-use, and that improper delivery can cause infection, punctured organs, or central nervous system injury.^[raw/articles/nccih-2026-acupuncture-effectiveness-safety.md]

Mind-body exercise safety is lower-risk but not zero: falls, orthostatic symptoms, overexertion, pain flares, breath-hold presyncope, and panic-like overbreathing can all confound a "brain fog" experiment. For measurement and protocol discipline, route through [[practical-cognitive-measurement-stack]], [[self-experimentation-stop-rules]], and [[active-sham-and-inertness-taxonomy]].

## Signal Judgment

Traditional treatments deserve coverage because they may help brain fog through sleep, pain, vascular reserve, autonomic regulation, frailty, movement, or stress-state pathways. They should not be ranked as direct cognitive enhancers unless they beat an active or credible sham comparator on objective cognition with delayed follow-up.
