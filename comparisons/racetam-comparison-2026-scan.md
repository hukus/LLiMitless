# Racetam Comparison 2026 Scan

This page is the class-level comparator row set for the legacy racetam lane and the adjacent actoprotective controls that keep showing up in the same nootropic conversation. The key discipline is to stop flattening all of them into one "racetam" bucket. Aniracetam, noopept, fonturacetam / phenylpiracetam, bromantane, and PRL-8-53 land in different evidence, phenotype, and regulatory lanes.

For [[piracetam]], [[oxiracetam]], [[fasoracetam]], [[coluracetam]], and [[sunifiram]], use [[racetam-long-tail-watchlist]]. Those branches now have standalone conservative entity pages, but the watchlist remains the class-level triage layer.

## Summary

- Aniracetam is the oldest and most phenotype-bound human racetam here: placebo-controlled dementia / psychosyndrome trials are mixed to negative, while comparator and open comparative work is more favorable.
- Fonturacetam / phenylpiracetam has the strongest current modern human rescue signal in the set, but that signal is asthenia / fatigue / recovery, not rested-baseline enhancement.
- Bromantane reads as a state-adjacent actoprotective / stimulant-like comparator with a small healthy-volunteer signal and stronger rescue literature than enhancement proof.
- Noopept is human-real but thin: the branch has a review, a comparative study, and an EEG study in vascular / post-traumatic impairment, plus stronger preclinical plausibility than human proof.
- PRL-8-53 remains a one-study curiosity that deserves formal control status, not class leadership.

## What Changed Since 2024

- Fonturacetam now has a 2025 meta-analysis across 11 studies / 549 patients, which makes the asthenia-rescue reading much harder to ignore.
- WADA now explicitly names both `Bromantan` and `Fonturacetam [4-phenylpiracetam (carphedon)]` in the in-competition stimulant lane, so this family now has a clearer sports-status overlay than it did before.
- Noopept has been easier to separate from racetam folklore because the human evidence still clusters in vascular / traumatic impairment rather than generic enhancement.
- PRL-8-53 is still a single-study branch; nothing in this pass upgraded it beyond "replication-worthy curiosity."
- The overall split is now clearer: old rescue / disease-context human signals are real, but they do not collapse into a broad healthy-adult nootropic class.

## Safety / Adverse Events

- Aniracetam: confusion interrupted treatment in the older placebo-controlled SDAT trial, and the elderly PK study showed prolonged metabolite exposure.
- Fonturacetam: pooled adverse effects averaged 5.5% and were described as transient in the 2025 meta-analysis.
- Bromantane: the healthy-volunteer paper did not show a dramatic subjective change in untired men, but the branch still needs sleep / tolerance caution because actoprotective arousal can be misread as cognition.
- Noopept: human safety data are sparse; the biggest practical issue is product identity, salt/form, and route variability.
- PRL-8-53: safety interpretation remains limited by the single-study evidence base and identity ambiguity.

## Responder Phenotypes

- Aniracetam: dementia, vascular impairment, solvent-related psychosyndrome, and older adults with exposure-sensitivity issues.
- Fonturacetam / phenylpiracetam: asthenia, fatigue, work-capacity decline, and other rescue states.
- Bromantane: fatigue, neurasthenia, actoprotective / vigilance states, and likely sleep-loss or load states.
- Noopept: vascular or post-traumatic cognitive impairment rather than rested healthy adults.
- PRL-8-53: unknown; the human signal is too sparse to enrich responders confidently.

## Protocol Parameters

- Aniracetam: 1 g/day in the older placebo-controlled trials; 1500 mg/day in vascular mental deterioration; 200 mg single-dose PK probe in elderly cerebrovascular patients.
- Fonturacetam / phenylpiracetam: 200 mg/day, typically over about 30 days in the pooled asthenia studies.
- Bromantane: healthy-volunteer data are too sparse for a clean standard; clinical asthenia use in the captured sources is 50-100 mg/day for 28 days.
- Noopept: older review notes 5 mg and 10 mg tablets; the captured human-study snippets do not support a modern standardized dose recommendation.
- PRL-8-53: the original paper used low oral doses, but the branch still lacks a modern protocol standard.

## Open Contradictions

- Aniracetam has the oldest and most mixed placebo-controlled human record, yet comparator and open studies look more favorable.
- Fonturacetam is the strongest modern human branch here, but the signal is phenotype-bound to asthenia, not baseline cognition.
- Bromantane looks useful for activation and fatigue relief, but the same state shift can be mistaken for cognition enhancement.
- Noopept has real human data, but the branch is still too dependent on older Russian studies and product QA to generalize cleanly.
- PRL-8-53 is one of the few single-study branches that still looks interesting, but one study is still one study.
