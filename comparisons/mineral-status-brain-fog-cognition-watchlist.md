# Mineral Status Brain Fog Cognition Watchlist

This watchlist covers mineral candidates where brain-fog or cognition claims often come from correcting a limiting status variable: [[iron]], [[iodine]], [[zinc]], [[magnesium]], [[selenium]], and the product-specific comparator [[magnesium-l-threonate]]. The shared rule is simple: mineral deficiencies can matter a lot, but extra mineral exposure is not automatically cognitive enhancement in replete healthy adults.

## Medicinal Signal

| Candidate | Main formal signal | Signal strength | Best route | Main caveat |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[iron]] | Young-women RCT where cognitive improvement tracked iron-status improvement; strong biological plausibility for low ferritin / iron deficiency | `low-moderate/status-gated` | Deficiency correction / menstruation or blood-loss phenotype | Excess iron risk, inflammation-confounded ferritin, and no replete-adult enhancement proof |
| [[magnesium-l-threonate]] | Product-specific 2026 Magtein RCT in sleep-dissatisfied adults with NIH cognition and reaction-time signals | `low-moderate/product-specific` | Sleep-dissatisfied / product-specific branch | Does not transfer to generic magnesium; Raven's and several sleep outcomes were null |
| [[magnesium]] | 2024 adult review supports observational associations but limited RCT inference | `low/generic` | Sleep, migraine, deficiency, metabolic or stress-state support | Form, dose, bowel effects, kidney function, and product identity dominate |
| [[zinc]] | ZENITH older-adult cognition anchor plus status biology | `low/status-sensitive` | Low intake/status, immune/taste-smell context, older adult watch | Copper depletion and high-dose lozenge confusion are central safety gates |
| [[selenium]] | Small Brazil-nut MCI pilot with selenium-status and selected cognitive measures | `low/clinical-state` | Low selenium / thyroid-redox status watch | Brazil-nut dose variability and excess toxicity narrow the margin |
| [[iodine]] | Adult evidence captured here is status/thyroid-response oriented, not cognition-positive | `low/indirect` | Thyroid-status gate | Both deficiency and excess can worsen thyroid state; kelp products vary widely |

The strongest practical signal is [[iron]] when deficiency or low ferritin is plausible. The cleanest supplement-product cognition signal remains [[magnesium-l-threonate]], but it is not generic magnesium evidence. [[iodine]] and [[selenium]] are mostly thyroid/status gates; [[zinc]] is a low-confidence older-adult/status candidate with a copper-safety brake.^[raw/articles/pubmed-17344500-iron-treatment-cognition-young-women.md]^[raw/articles/frontiers-2026-magnesium-l-threonate-cognition-sleep-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pmc-11362647-magnesium-cognitive-health-meta.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-17010236-zinc-zenith-cognition-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-25567069-brazil-nut-selenium-mci-rct.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-26891118-iodine-mild-deficiency-adults-rct.md]

## Product And Lab Gates

- [[iron]]: ferritin, hemoglobin/CBC, transferrin saturation, inflammation context, menstruation/blood loss, diet, GI tolerance, and follow-up labs.
- [[iodine]]: iodized salt, seaweed/kelp exposure, thyroid labs, thyroid antibodies, pregnancy/lactation, amiodarone/lithium/antithyroid drugs, and total iodine dose.
- [[zinc]]: dose, duration, copper/ceruloplasmin if high-dose or chronic, CBC if symptomatic, diet, immune context, and antibiotic/penicillamine timing.
- [[magnesium]]: form, elemental dose, timing, kidney function, bowel tolerance, sleep, migraine/cramp/anxiety context, and interacting drugs.
- [[selenium]]: Brazil-nut count/origin, multivitamin overlap, standalone dose, thyroid labs, hair/nail/GI symptoms, and total selenium exposure.
- Multivitamin/mineral stacks are not clean evidence. They blur active units and can accidentally stack zinc, selenium, iodine, iron, copper, vitamin D, and B vitamins above intended exposure.

## Current Read

The mineral lane belongs downstream of [[molecule-intake-triage-table]] and beside [[nutrient-supplement-cognition-signal-map]]. It is a status-correction branch, not an enhancement branch. If labs or diet suggest a mineral deficit, the signal can be practically important; if labs and diet look replete, the evidence drops quickly and safety burden rises.

For self-study, the minimum design is not a blind supplement trial first. It is a status audit: diet, labs when relevant, dose/form, total exposure across products, sleep, caffeine, GI effects, and a clear stop rule for thyroid activation, iron overload, copper-deficiency symptoms, diarrhea/hypotension, or selenium toxicity.
