# Circadian and Sleep Interventions for Performance and Cognition

This comparison groups the practical ways to improve cognition-adjacent performance by reducing sleep loss or circadian misalignment. The strongest signal is usually acute alertness, reaction time, and attention rather than long-term disease modification.

The new chronic-disease CBT-I meta-analysis adds a stronger sleep-stabilization anchor for the behavior-first branch: CBT-I reliably improves insomnia severity and sleep continuity, which makes it a practical upstream move when poor sleep is the main performance limiter, even though the evidence still reads as sleep restoration rather than direct enhancement.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40982264-cbt-i-chronic-disease-meta-analysis.md]

Relevant raw PubMed notes: `raw/articles/pubmed-41892386-sleep-banking-cognitive-motor-impairments-sleep-restriction.md|sleep banking review`, `raw/articles/pubmed-40984126-sleep-extension-cognitive-performance-military-aged-adults.md|sleep extension review`, `raw/articles/pubmed-41139761-shift-work-cognitive-motor-performance-nurses.md|shift-work meta-analysis`, `raw/articles/pubmed-40890717-sleep-improvement-programs-shift-work-nurses.md|shift-work sleep programs`, `raw/articles/pubmed-41277818-post-stroke-circadian-rhythm-disruption-prognosis.md|post-stroke review`, and `raw/articles/pubmed-41090408-circadian-disruption-astronauts-neurocognitive-consequences.md|astronaut review`.

| Intervention | Main use-case | Evidence shape | Wiki takeaway |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[cbt-i-and-cognition]] | Chronic insomnia | Strong sleep evidence; modest cognition signal | Best first-line insomnia treatment and the clearest upstream sleep-stabilization step |
| [[circadian-disruption-and-cognition]] | Shift work, irregular schedules, circadian misalignment | Mechanistic + observational + some intervention support | Best conceptual bucket for performance losses from rhythm disruption |
| [[bright-light-therapy-and-cognition]] | Phase shifting, daytime alertness | Pilot and clinical studies, especially older adults | Indirect cognition benefit via sleep/circadian stabilization |
| [[sleep-banking-and-sleep-extension]] | Planned protection against later sleep loss | Short-term performance support, limited long-term data | Useful for short-term vigilance, not proven cognitive prevention |

The common pattern is simple: fixing sleep and rhythm can help people think better, but that is not the same thing as a true nootropic effect. That distinction matters across this wiki, especially when comparing sleep-related interventions with [[repetitive-transcranial-magnetic-stimulation-and-cognition]] and [[intranasal-insulin-and-cognition]].

There is also a narrower circadian-autonomic hypothesis worth tracking. A blue-enriched bright-light study in healthy adults changed HRV in the afternoon, and a taVNS HRV study found stronger effects in the morning and in people with lower baseline vagal tone. That makes a morning-light plus morning-taVNS stack biologically plausible, but it remains an inference from separate physiology papers rather than a direct combo trial.^[raw/articles/pubmed-35209793-bright-light-hrv.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-36327249-taVNS-circadian-hrv.md]

Chronotype is the next moderation layer, but the current evidence is uneven. Sleep extension and sleep-loss studies suggest that evening types may respond differently to the same schedule change, while chronotherapy papers in depression show that timing can matter a lot in some settings and almost not at all in others. That means timing-by-chronotype should be treated as a design variable, not a guarantee of larger effect sizes.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40458955-chronotype-sleep-extension-manipulation-adolescents.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-37539684-chronotype-affective-response-sleep-restriction-deprivation.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-32924897-bright-light-evening-chronotype-mdd.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-31495294-morningness-eveningness-day-treatment-streams.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-29764214-chronotype-antidepressant-chronotherapeutics-bipolar.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-27259079-chronotype-light-therapy-fixed-time-sad.md]
