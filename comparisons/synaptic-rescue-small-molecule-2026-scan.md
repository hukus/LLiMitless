# Synaptic-Rescue Small-Molecule 2026 Scan

This branch gathers the small-molecule programs that are trying to rescue cognition by nudging synapses, trophic signaling, or upstream degenerative pathways rather than by classic symptomatic stimulation. The common pattern is not "one clean winner"; it is a mix of biomarker movement, phenotype-stratified subgroup signals, and several sponsor-heavy narratives that still stop short of a reproducible class-wide cognition effect.

The main rule for reading this branch is simple: keep target engagement, biomarker movement, exploratory subgroup effects, and primary cognitive/functional endpoints separate. If those layers are merged, the branch looks stronger than it is.

## What The Evidence Says

[[ct1812-zervimesine]] looks like the best-developed synaptic-displacement program in the set. The published human package now includes qEEG and CSF biomarker engagement in AD, a DLB phase 2a trial that was safe and exploratory-positive, and an active early-AD trial that is still the only place a longer-horizon cognition answer can come from. The mechanism remains plausible, but the branch is still short of a clean, durable clinical win.^[raw/articles/pubmed-40547328-ct1812-sequel-biomarkers.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41416579-zervimesine-dlb.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct05531656-ct1812-early-ad.md]

[[lm11a-31]] is the clearest example of a branch that does what it says mechanistically without yet proving a cognitive effect. The phase 2a AD trial met safety/tolerability and showed biomarker and imaging shifts, but it did not move cognitive tests. That makes it useful as a p75NTR target-engagement reference, not as a validated enhancer.^[raw/articles/pubmed-38760589-lm11a31-phase-2a-ad.md]

[[neflamapimod]] is the most phenotype-sensitive member of the set. AscenD-LB produced a DLB proof-of-principle package with a posted-results registry record, and a later ptau181 analysis suggested stronger responses in people without clear AD co-pathology. RewinD-LB then narrowed the branch in the other direction: the larger phase 2b did not meet the CDR-SB primary endpoint or the key secondary endpoints, and the company said target plasma concentrations were not achieved during the double-blind phase. A 2026 systematic review found too few eligible studies for meta-analysis and still judged primary cognitive effects uncertain. The right conclusion is not "failed" or "won"; it is "possible enrichment signal, but the later phase 2b materially weakens confidence."^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct04001517-ascend-lb-neflamapimod.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct05869669-rewind-lb-neflamapimod.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-41510854-neflamapimod-systematic-review.md]^[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37657939/]

[[fosgonimeton]] is the critical HGF/MET comparator for Dihexa. The preclinical literature is strong enough to justify the mechanism, but the human LIFT-AD record matters more: the parent AD trial did not meet the primary GST endpoint or the secondary ADAS-Cog11 and ADCS-ADL23 endpoints, and the registry now shows the study completed with results posted. In other words, the HGF/MET story has a real human test now, and it has not yet cleared the bar.^[raw/articles/pmc-12701236-fosgonimeton-lift-ad.md]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct04488419-fosgonimeton-lift-ad.md]^[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38599894/]^[https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04886063]

[[blarcamesine]] is the most optimistic-looking published human result in the branch, but it is also one of the most sponsor-linked. The published phase IIb/III report claims benefit on ADAS-Cog13 and CDR-SB, with ADCS-ADL not significant, plus biomarker movement and transient dizziness. That is enough to keep it on the map, but not enough to treat it as independently settled.^[raw/articles/pubmed-39800452-blarcamesine-anavex2-73-ad-004.md]

[[bryostatin-1]] stays in the mixed-evidence bucket. The older placebo-controlled phase II trial did not meet the prespecified primary endpoint in the full analysis set, even though later analyses were more favorable. The 2024 registry record for NCT04538066 shows the 122-participant moderately severe AD study as completed with results posted, and the 2023 6-month paper keeps the signal alive mainly in the moderately severe cohort. This is still a case where cohort-specific and post hoc success should not be allowed to erase the primary-readout problem.^[https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30530975/]^[raw/articles/clinicaltrials-nct04538066-bryostatin-ad.md]^[raw/articles/pubmed-37742657-bryostatin-6-month-dlb.md]

## Dihexa Comparison

Dihexa should remain in the preclinical-only bucket. The point of this scan is not to upgrade it by analogy with other "synaptic rescue" compounds. If anything, fosgonimeton is the better comparison because it is the humanized HGF/MET analogue, and its negative primary AD result lowers confidence in the idea that HGF/MET modulation automatically translates into cognition benefit. See [[dihexa]].

The practical conclusion is narrow but important: Dihexa can be kept as a mechanistic hypothesis node, but it should not inherit credibility from the rest of the synaptic-rescue cluster.

## What Changed Since 2024

- [[ct1812-zervimesine]] moved from mostly preclinical / early biomarker discussion into a fuller human package: qEEG and CSF biomarker engagement in AD, plus a published DLB phase 2a with exploratory-positive trends.
- The early-AD [[ct1812-zervimesine]] phase 2 remains the key unresolved efficacy readout, so the branch still needs a durable clinical endpoint before it can be ranked as more than hypothesis-generating.
- Neflamapimod split the DLB story into two layers: an earlier phenotype-sensitive signal and a later RewinD-LB phase 2b miss, which is the clearest reminder in this branch that enrichment can still fail when exposure or disease stage is wrong.
- Bryostatin stayed mixed rather than becoming a clean positive; the newer registry record and 6-month paper keep the moderately severe cohort signal alive, but the full-analysis primary-endpoint problem is still the right anchor.
- Blarcamesine moved from registry-only anticipation into a published phase IIb/III claim of cognitive benefit, but the branch should still be read as sponsor-linked because one functional endpoint stayed null.
- [[lm11a-31]] is now a completed human phase 2a example of biomarker and imaging movement without cognitive benefit.
- Fosgonimeton now has a completed negative phase 2/3 result, which weakens the idea that HGF/MET modulation automatically translates into cognition benefit.
- The branch now has standalone entity pages for [[ct1812-zervimesine]], [[fosgonimeton]], [[lm11a-31]], [[neflamapimod]], [[bryostatin-1]], and [[blarcamesine]], each with medicinal and anecdotal signal separated.
- The overall pattern is unchanged: biomarker movement does not guarantee cognitive efficacy, and the branch should be read as a comparator set rather than a win list.

## Reading Rule

When this branch is cited elsewhere in the wiki, the following order should be preserved:

1. Mechanism and target engagement.
2. Biomarkers and imaging.
3. Exploratory subgroup effects.
4. Primary cognition or function.
5. Independence of the publication.

If a program only reaches step 2 or 3, it is still hypothesis-generating.
